# O&G Notes — Section 4: Medical Disorders of Pregnancy
*Australian-anchored: SOMANZ 2023 (hypertension), SOMANZ 2023 (ICP), SOMANZ 2020 (NVP), ADIPS 2025 (GDM), Queensland Clinical Guidelines (HDP, VTE), RANZCOG C-Obs 46, Therapeutic Guidelines, Australian Prescriber. Scope = written finals: recognition, thresholds, drug-of-choice, timing-of-birth decisions. UK/NICE divergences are flagged where they are exam-relevant.*

---

## 4.1 Hypertensive disorders — the classification comes first

Every hypertensive vignette is answered by placing the woman into one of four boxes. Hypertension in pregnancy = **sBP ≥140 and/or dBP ≥90** on two occasions (or once if severe), Korotkoff V for diastolic.

| Box | Defining feature | Discriminator |
|---|---|---|
| Chronic (pre-existing) HTN | HTN **before 20/40** or pre-dating pregnancy | Present at booking; doesn't resolve by 3/12 postpartum |
| Gestational HTN | New HTN **after 20/40**, **no** end-organ features | Diagnosis of exclusion; **~1 in 4 progress to pre-eclampsia**, so it is never "reassure and discharge" |
| Pre-eclampsia (PE) | New HTN after 20/40 **plus ≥1 new maternal organ or uteroplacental feature** | **Proteinuria is not required** ⚑ — a normotensive-protein-free woman with new thrombocytopenia + RUQ pain + FGR is still PE |
| Superimposed PE | Chronic HTN **plus** new organ dysfunction / step-up in BP / new proteinuria | Hardest to call — look for a *new* feature, not just worse numbers |

**White-coat HTN** (raised in clinic, normal on ABPM/HBPM) still carries elevated PE risk — monitor, don't dismiss.

**Organ features that make it pre-eclampsia** (SOMANZ 2023 / QCG):
- **Renal** — protein:creatinine ratio **≥30 mg/mmol**, creatinine **≥90 µmol/L**, oliguria
- **Haematological** — thrombocytopenia **<150 ×10⁹/L** (SOMANZ 2023 / QCG diagnostic threshold — note this is *not* the <100 used to define **severe** disease below), haemolysis, DIC
- **Hepatic** — transaminases **>40 IU/L** (or >2× ULN), severe persistent RUQ/epigastric pain
- **Neurological** — new severe headache, visual scotomata/photopsia, hyperreflexia with **sustained clonus**, altered GCS, stroke, eclampsia
- **Pulmonary** — pulmonary oedema
- **Uteroplacental** — FGR, abnormal umbilical artery Dopplers, abruption, stillbirth

**Severe hypertension = ≥160 systolic and/or ≥110 diastolic.** This is a *number*, not a syndrome — a woman can have severe HTN without severe PE and vice versa. Treat the number urgently regardless.

**The short version**
- Proteinuria is optional; end-organ involvement is not.
- Gestational HTN is a holding diagnosis — 25% become PE.
- ≥160/110 is severe hypertension and needs treatment within minutes, not hours.

## 4.2 Prevention and prediction

- **Aspirin** — the single highest-yield intervention. **Start before 16/40**, take at **night**, continue to ~36 weeks. SOMANZ 2023 says **150 mg daily**; QCG allows **100–150 mg**. ⚑ The old "75–100 mg" advice was superseded by 150 mg on ASPRE data — quote 150 mg for a single number. Indicated for previous PE, chronic HTN, pre-gestational diabetes, CKD, SLE/APS, or **≥2 moderate risks** (nulliparity, age ≥40, BMI ≥30, family history, multiple pregnancy). Aspirin started at 24 weeks is of little value.
- **Calcium** 1.2–2.5 g/day if dietary intake **<1 g/day**.
- **sFlt-1/PlGF ratio** — a **rule-out** test: ratio **≤38** makes PE within the next week very unlikely (high NPV). ⚑ Not universally available in Australia; never use it to *diagnose* PE.

## 4.3 Pre-eclampsia with severe features, eclampsia and HELLP

**Severe PE** = PE plus any of: sBP ≥160/dBP ≥110 despite treatment, platelets <100, transaminases >2× ULN with RUQ pain, creatinine rising, pulmonary oedema, persistent neurological signs, eclampsia. Severe PE <34 weeks is the classic "stabilise, steroids, magnesium, transfer to tertiary, then deliver" stem.

**Eclampsia** — tonic-clonic seizure in a woman with PE. Clues that a seizure is eclamptic rather than epileptic: gestation >20/40 or **within 48 h postpartum**, preceding headache/visual change/RUQ pain, hyperreflexia and clonus, raised BP (though **up to ~15% are normotensive at the time of fit** — do not exclude eclampsia on a normal BP). Management sequence:
1. ABC, left lateral, oxygen, protect airway — **do not** give diazepam first-line.
2. **MgSO₄** 4 g IV over 20 min, then 1 g/h.
3. Recurrent seizure → **further 2 g IV over 5 min** (QCG allows repeat after 2 min).
4. Control BP to <160/110.
5. Deliver **once the mother is stable** — the fetus is not the emergency; maternal stabilisation is. Delivery is the definitive treatment.

**HELLP** — Haemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets. It is a severe variant of PE, and **12–18% are normotensive**, which is what makes it a trap.
- Haemolysis: ↑LDH, ↓haptoglobin, **schistocytes/fragments on film**, ↑unconjugated bilirubin
- ↑AST/ALT (>2× ULN)
- Platelets **<100 ×10⁹/L**
- Presentation: RUQ/epigastric pain, nausea/vomiting, malaise — frequently misfiled as gastritis or biliary colic. Complication to know: **subcapsular hepatic haematoma → rupture** (sudden shoulder-tip pain, shock).
- Management = MgSO₄ + BP control + **delivery**. Expectant management is dangerous (QCG cites ~6.3% maternal mortality). **Corticosteroids do not improve maternal outcomes in HELLP** ⚑ — give betamethasone only for fetal lung maturation, not to "treat the platelets".

**Confusable trio at term with RUQ pain + deranged LFTs**

| | Discriminator |
|---|---|
| **HELLP** | Platelets ↓↓, haemolysis, hypertension usually present, glucose normal |
| **Acute fatty liver of pregnancy** | **Hypoglycaemia**, ↑ammonia, coagulopathy out of proportion, ↑urate, often *nauseated + polydipsic*; Swansea criteria |
| **Obstetric cholestasis** | **Itch without rash** dominates, bile acids ↑↑, BP and platelets normal |

**The short version**
- Normotensive HELLP and normotensive eclampsia both exist — pattern beats blood pressure.
- Magnesium prevents/treats seizures; it is not an antihypertensive.
- Delivery cures PE. Everything else buys time.

## 4.4 Drugs: antihypertensives and magnesium

**Acute severe hypertension (≥160/110) — treat within 15–30 min.** Target sBP 130–150, dBP 80–90; avoid precipitous drops (uteroplacental hypoperfusion).

| Agent | Dose | Onset | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nifedipine IR** oral | 10–20 mg, repeat at 45 min, max 80 mg/day | 30–45 min | Practical first choice on a ward; **do not** give sublingual |
| **Labetalol** IV | 20 mg, then 40–80 mg q10 min, max 300 mg/24 h | ~5 min | Avoid in asthma, decompensated heart failure |
| **Hydralazine** IV | 5–10 mg over 3–10 min, repeat q20 min, max 30 mg | ~20 min | Preload-dependent — **co-load with fluid**; causes reflex tachycardia and headache (mimics PE symptoms) |

**Ongoing oral therapy** — start when BP persistently ≥140/90; SOMANZ 2023 target **≤135/85** ⚑ (tighter than the older "treat only if ≥160/100" teaching, following CHIPS/CHAP).

| Agent | Typical dosing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Labetalol** | 100 mg BD → 200–400 mg BD–QID (max 2.4 g/day) | Usual first line; asthma is the limiter; watch neonatal hypoglycaemia/bradycardia |
| **Methyldopa** | 125–250 mg BD → max 2 g/day | Longest safety record; sedation, depression — **avoid postpartum** (depression risk); slow onset |
| **Nifedipine SR** | 20–30 mg daily → max 120 mg/day | Headache, flushing, ankle oedema |
| **Hydralazine oral** | 25 mg BD → max 200 mg/day | Add-on |

**Contraindicated / avoid**: **ACE inhibitors and ARBs** (fetal renal dysgenesis, oligohydramnios, skull hypoplasia — safe postpartum and in breastfeeding, e.g. enalapril/captopril), **atenolol** (FGR with prolonged use), routine **diuretics** (plasma volume already contracted), **direct renin inhibitors**.

**Magnesium sulfate**
- **Indications**: eclampsia (treatment), severe PE (seizure prophylaxis), and **fetal neuroprotection when birth is imminent <30 weeks** (Australian practice; some centres extend to <32 weeks — the ANZ guidance is <30). ⚑ Neuroprotective use is a separate indication with the same drug.
- **Regimen**: 4 g IV over 20 min → **1 g/h for 24 h** (24 h from the last seizure or from birth, whichever is later). Renal impairment → maintenance 0.5 g/h with levels.
- **Monitoring**: hourly respiratory rate, deep tendon reflexes, urine output; continuous BP/pulse initially. Therapeutic range ~1.7–3.5 mmol/L.
- **Toxicity sequence** (useful MCQ ladder): loss of patellar reflexes → respiratory depression → cardiac arrest. **Antidote: calcium gluconate 1 g IV.** First sign to be lost is the deep tendon reflex — this is why reflexes are checked hourly.
- Magnesium **lowers BP only modestly** — never rely on it for BP control.

**Postpartum**: BP typically **peaks day 3–6**, so discharge with a BP plan. NSAIDs are usually avoided in PE with renal impairment or persistent hypertension. Women with PE carry roughly a **2–4× lifetime cardiovascular risk** — arrange 6–12 week review, and lifelong CV risk-factor management (SOMANZ 2023 places new emphasis on this).

## 4.5 Timing of birth in hypertensive disease

| Scenario | Australian approach |
|---|---|
| Gestational HTN, well controlled | Birth from ~**37–38 weeks** |
| Pre-eclampsia **≥37+0** | **Deliver** (SOMANZ 2023: initiate birth) |
| Pre-eclampsia 34+0–36+6 | Individualise; expectant with close surveillance is reasonable if stable |
| Pre-eclampsia **<34 weeks**, stable | Expectant in a tertiary unit + **betamethasone**; prolongs gestation, improves neonatal outcome |
| **Severe PE / eclampsia / HELLP / AFLP** at any gestation | Deliver — stabilise first, but **do not delay** |
| Non-reassuring fetal status, abruption, uncontrollable BP, pulmonary oedema, deteriorating renal or hepatic function | Deliver |

Mode of birth is obstetric, not dictated by PE per se — vaginal birth is appropriate if the cervix is favourable and the fetus is well.

## 4.6 Gestational diabetes — ADIPS 2025 ⚑⚑ (highest-yield change in this section)

**Screening (ADIPS 2025)**
- **First antenatal visit**: HbA1c if risk factors (previous GDM, obesity, first-degree family history, age ≥30, PCOS, hypothyroidism, adverse obstetric history, high-risk ethnicity, corticosteroid/antipsychotic use).
- **Early 75 g OGTT before 20 weeks (ideally 10–14 weeks)** if: previous GDM, **or** early HbA1c **6.0–6.4% (42–47 mmol/mol)**. ⚑ Early OGTT is now *targeted*, no longer offered broadly.
- **Universal 75 g OGTT at 24–28 weeks** for everyone not already diagnosed.

**Diagnostic thresholds — 75 g OGTT, any ONE value diagnoses GDM**

| | **ADIPS 2025 (current)** ⚑ | ADIPS 2014 (superseded) |
|---|---|---|
| Fasting | **≥5.3 mmol/L** (5.3–6.9) | ≥5.1 mmol/L (5.1–6.9) |
| 1 hour | **≥10.6 mmol/L** | ≥10.0 mmol/L |
| 2 hour | **≥9.0 mmol/L** (9.0–11.0) | ≥8.5 mmol/L (8.5–11.0) |

*Every threshold went **up**. Mnemonic for the new set: **5.3 / 10.6 / 9.0** — note 10.6 is exactly double 5.3.* The rationale was overdiagnosis under IADPSG-derived criteria, informed by **TOBOGM** (early treatment benefits the higher-glycaemia band) and **GEMS** (higher thresholds did not worsen outcomes overall). Older textbooks and many hospital handbooks still print 5.1/10.0/8.5 — if an exam question is dated, read the stem for which criteria it is using.

**Overt diabetes in pregnancy (overt DIP)** — not GDM, manage as pre-existing diabetes: **FPG ≥7.0**, or **2 h ≥11.1**, or **HbA1c ≥6.5% (≥48 mmol/mol)**.
**Early GDM** = GDM criteria met **before 20 weeks**.

**Management ladder**
1. **Lifestyle first** — dietitian-led medical nutrition therapy (carbohydrate quantity/quality/distribution), physical activity, gestational weight-gain targets, plus **self-monitored BGL** 4×/day (fasting + post-prandial). Roughly 70–80% are managed on lifestyle alone.
2. **Pharmacotherapy if targets are not met** (typically after 1–2 weeks of lifestyle). **In Australia, insulin is first-line pharmacotherapy** — usually basal (isophane/detemir) for fasting hyperglycaemia, rapid-acting prandial for post-prandial peaks, or both.
3. **Metformin** — crosses the placenta; ADIPS does not endorse it for routine first-line use, reserving it for individualised situations (e.g. severe insulin refusal/inability, marked insulin resistance) usually **with** insulin. ⚑ **This is where Australia diverges from the UK: NICE recommends metformin first-line, adding insulin if targets are unmet.** If an exam stem is British in flavour, metformin is the answer; in an Australian paper, insulin is.
4. **Glibenclamide** is essentially obsolete in Australian practice.

**Glycaemic targets** — sources genuinely disagree; know the tighter set and be aware of the looser:
- Traditional ADIPS/Australian hospital targets: fasting **≤5.0**, 1 h post-prandial **≤7.4**, 2 h **≤6.7 mmol/L**.
- *Australian Prescriber* (2025) quotes fasting **<5.3**, 1 h **<7.8**, 2 h **<6.7 mmol/L**.
The 2 h figure (6.7) is consistent across both; the fasting and 1 h figures differ. Local protocol wins in practice.

**Consequences**: macrosomia with **asymmetric growth (abdominal circumference)**, polyhydramnios, shoulder dystocia and brachial plexus injury, pre-eclampsia, preterm birth, stillbirth, caesarean; neonatal **hypoglycaemia**, hyperbilirubinaemia, polycythaemia, respiratory distress. Serial growth scans from ~28–30 weeks if insulin-requiring or poorly controlled.

**Timing of birth**: diet-controlled and well grown → expectant to **40–41 weeks**; insulin-requiring or complicated → **38–39 weeks**. Intrapartum, keep BGL ~4–7 mmol/L; insulin requirements collapse the moment the placenta delivers, so **stop GDM insulin at birth**.

**Postpartum**: **75 g OGTT at 6–12 weeks** using non-pregnant criteria, then HbA1c/FPG/OGTT **every 1–3 years lifelong** — 50–60% develop type 2 diabetes within a decade and CVD risk roughly doubles.

**The short version**
- 2025 numbers: **5.3 / 10.6 / 9.0** — all raised from 5.1/10.0/8.5. ⚑
- HbA1c ≥6.5% or fasting ≥7.0 or 2 h ≥11.1 = overt diabetes, not GDM.
- Australia = insulin first; NICE = metformin first.

## 4.7 VTE in pregnancy

Pregnancy is prothrombotic throughout and **maximally in the first 6 weeks postpartum**. VTE remains a leading direct cause of maternal death in Australia.

**Recognition**
- **DVT**: ~85% are **left-sided** and **iliofemoral** (compression of the left iliac vein by the right iliac artery and gravid uterus) — whole-leg swelling, back/buttock pain. Left leg swelling in pregnancy is DVT until excluded.
- **PE**: dyspnoea, pleuritic pain, tachycardia, hypoxia, collapse. **D-dimer is not useful** — physiologically raised in pregnancy; a negative result does not exclude PE and a positive result adds nothing. Go straight to imaging.
- Imaging: **compression duplex ultrasound** first for suspected DVT (and if positive with suspected PE, treat — no chest imaging needed). For suspected PE with normal leg dopplers: CXR first, then **V/Q (or perfusion-only) scan vs CTPA**. V/Q gives lower maternal breast dose; CTPA gives lower fetal dose and is better if the CXR is abnormal. Both fetal doses are far below teratogenic thresholds — **do not withhold imaging**.

**Treatment**: **LMWH (enoxaparin)** — does not cross the placenta, no routine monitoring. Antenatal therapeutic dose **1 mg/kg SC BD**; postnatal **1.5 mg/kg SC daily**. Continue for the remainder of the pregnancy **and at least 6 weeks postpartum**, minimum 3 months total. **Warfarin is teratogenic** (chondrodysplasia punctata, nasal hypoplasia at 6–12 weeks; fetal haemorrhage later) — stop before 6 weeks' gestation; it *is* safe in breastfeeding. **DOACs: avoid in pregnancy and breastfeeding.**

**Prophylaxis** (QCG risk scoring): antenatal LMWH if score ≥4, from the first trimester if previous VTE or high-risk thrombophilia; postnatal LMWH **7 days** if score ≥3, extended to **6 weeks** for previous unprovoked VTE, high-risk thrombophilia or recurrent VTE. Standard prophylactic enoxaparin is **40 mg daily** at booking weight 50–90 kg, scaled up by weight band.

**Neuraxial timing** (frequently examined): prophylactic LMWH — **12 h** before block; therapeutic LMWH — **24 h** before block.

## 4.8 Anaemia and iron

Physiological haemodilution drops Hb; the thresholds are trimester-specific.

| | Anaemia if Hb below |
|---|---|
| 1st trimester | **110 g/L** |
| 2nd trimester | **105 g/L** |
| 3rd trimester | 110 g/L (many Australian units use 105 g/L through the 2nd and 3rd) |
| Postpartum | **100 g/L** |

**Ferritin <30 µg/L = iron deficiency** (treat). Ferritin is an acute-phase reactant — a normal ferritin with raised CRP does not exclude deficiency; use transferrin saturation. Screen with FBC + ferritin at **booking, 24–28 weeks and ~36 weeks**.

**Treatment**: oral elemental iron 100 mg — **alternate-day dosing** is preferred where tolerated ⚑ (a dose triggers a hepcidin surge that blocks absorption for ~24 h, so alternate-day gives equal or better uptake with fewer GI effects). Take with vitamin C, away from tea/coffee/calcium/PPI; recheck at 2–4 weeks. **IV iron** (ferric carboxymaltose/derisomaltose) for oral intolerance or failure, or when time is short — restricted to the **2nd and 3rd trimesters**; causes **transient hypophosphataemia** and skin staining on extravasation.

Consider **thalassaemia trait** if MCV is low with normal/raised ferritin and a normal or high red cell count.

## 4.9 Thyroid disease in pregnancy

**Physiology**: hCG cross-stimulates the TSH receptor → **TSH falls in T1** while free T4 transiently rises; oestrogen raises thyroxine-binding globulin so total T4/T3 rise (use **free** T4). Iodine requirement rises — **all pregnant and breastfeeding women in Australia should take iodine 150 µg/day**.

**Reference ranges** (RANZCOG C-Obs 46): T1 upper limit is ~0.5 mU/L below the non-pregnant range; T2/T3 as non-pregnant. A pragmatic single figure is an upper limit of **4 mIU/L** throughout pregnancy.

**Hypothyroidism**
- **Overt** = ↑TSH with ↓free T4, **or TSH >10** with normal free T4 → treat.
- **Pre-existing on thyroxine**: increase the dose **as soon as pregnancy is confirmed** — the practical instruction is **two extra doses per week** (~a 25–30% increase; RANZCOG quotes 30–50%). Check TSH at least once per trimester; aim for the lower half of the trimester-specific range. Return to pre-pregnancy dose immediately postpartum.
- **Subclinical hypothyroidism (SCH)** — **genuine guideline disagreement**: **RANZCOG C-Obs 46 does not recommend treating SCH** (large RCTs showed no maternal or neonatal benefit). The ATA and *Australian Prescriber* support levothyroxine when TSH is 2.5–4 mIU/L **with positive TPO antibodies**, or TSH ≥4 regardless of antibodies. In an Australian written paper, follow RANZCOG; be able to state the alternative.
- **Screening**: **targeted, not universal** (symptoms, T1DM, personal/family thyroid history, previous neck irradiation, recurrent miscarriage). Routine TPO antibody screening is not recommended.

**Hyperthyroidism**
- Distinguish **gestational transient thyrotoxicosis** (hCG-driven, peaks 8–14 weeks, associated with hyperemesis, **no goitre, no eye signs, TRAb negative**, resolves by ~20 weeks — supportive care only) from **Graves' disease** (goitre, orbitopathy, **TRAb positive**, pre-dates pregnancy).
- **Drug choice by trimester**: **propylthiouracil in the first trimester** (carbimazole/methimazole → aplasia cutis, choanal/oesophageal atresia — "carbimazole embryopathy"); switch to **carbimazole from the second trimester** to avoid PTU hepatotoxicity. Use the lowest dose keeping free T4 at the upper limit of normal. **Do not** use block-and-replace in pregnancy. Radioiodine is absolutely contraindicated.
- **TRAb crosses the placenta** — measure in women with current or past Graves' (including post-thyroidectomy/radioiodine) at ~18–22 and 30–34 weeks; high titres → risk of **fetal/neonatal thyrotoxicosis** (fetal tachycardia >160, goitre, FGR) → fetal surveillance and neonatal thyroid checks.
- **Thyroid storm** is precipitated by labour, infection, caesarean — a life-threatening pregnancy emergency.

**Postpartum thyroiditis**: 5–10% of women, higher with TPO antibodies or T1DM. **Painless**, classically thyrotoxic phase at 1–4 months → hypothyroid at 4–8 months → usually euthyroid by 12 months. **Distinguish from Graves'**: postpartum thyroiditis has **low radioiodine uptake**, negative TRAb, no orbitopathy. Beta-blocker for the thyrotoxic phase (never antithyroid drugs — it is destructive, not overproductive); thyroxine if symptomatic hypothyroidism. Up to 20–30% become permanently hypothyroid — check TSH annually.

## 4.10 Epilepsy and teratogenic antiseizure medications

**Principles**: most women with epilepsy have uncomplicated pregnancies. **Seizures — especially generalised tonic-clonic — are more dangerous than most modern ASMs**, and SUDEP risk rises with non-adherence. Never stop an ASM abruptly because a woman is pregnant. Optimise **pre-conceptually**: monotherapy, lowest effective dose, **folic acid 5 mg daily** (high dose for all women on ASMs) from at least 1 month pre-conception through T1.

| Drug | Pregnancy risk | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Sodium valproate** | Major congenital malformation **~3.3% at <1100 mg/day rising to ~30% at ≥1100 mg/day**; neural tube defects; **fetal valproate syndrome**; ↓IQ ~8–10 points; autism (OR 1.7–4.4), ADHD (OR 1.4–1.8), intellectual disability (OR 2.4–4.5) | **Avoid in anyone of childbearing potential** unless no alternative; TGA requires effective contraception be co-prescribed and risks documented. Dose-dependent, no safe dose ⚑ |
| **Topiramate** | **Oral clefts**; FGR/small for gestational age; emerging neurodevelopmental signal | Avoid — including when used for migraine or weight management ⚑ (a recently tightened warning that predates many textbooks) |
| **Carbamazepine** | Modest ↑ malformation (NTD, cardiac) | Second-tier; acceptable if effective and alternatives failed |
| **Phenytoin, phenobarbitone** | Fetal hydantoin syndrome; cognitive effects | Avoid where possible |
| **Lamotrigine** | Lowest malformation rates | **Preferred**. Levels fall substantially in pregnancy (oestrogen-induced glucuronidation) → **monitor levels and increase dose**, then reduce promptly postpartum to avoid toxicity |
| **Levetiracetam** | Lowest malformation rates | **Preferred**. Clearance also rises — monitor |

Also: detailed anomaly scan ± fetal echo; **all ASMs are compatible with breastfeeding**; postnatal safe-care advice (shower rather than bathe, change nappies on the floor, avoid co-sleeping and sleep deprivation). A seizure in the second half of pregnancy in a woman with no epilepsy history is **eclampsia until proven otherwise**.

## 4.11 Obstetric cholestasis (intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy, ICP)

**Clue → diagnosis**: **third-trimester itch, worst on palms and soles, worse at night, no primary rash** (excoriations only) + raised bile acids. Jaundice is uncommon (<10%). Risk factors: previous ICP (recurrence up to 90%), family history, multiple pregnancy, hepatitis C, IVF conception.

**Diagnosis (SOMANZ 2023)** — pruritus + elevated **total serum bile acids (TSBA)**, with other causes excluded.
- **Non-fasting TSBA ≥19 µmol/L** is the diagnostic threshold. ⚑ (Fasting ≥10 µmol/L is an alternative but is insensitive; the old textbook "any elevation above the lab range" is outdated.)
- Transaminases are often raised; **check for other causes** — viral hepatitis serology, EBV/CMV, liver autoantibodies, liver ultrasound, pre-eclampsia bloods.

**Severity and risk — the number that matters is 100** ⚑

| Band | Peak TSBA | Stillbirth risk | Planned birth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mild | 19–39 µmol/L | Background | Not indicated for ICP alone; usually term |
| Severe | 40–99 µmol/L | ~0.28% (≈ background) | Consider **from 38+0** |
| Very severe | **≥100 µmol/L** | **~3.4%** (HR ~30), concentrated **35–36 weeks** | Consider **from 36+0** |

**Management**
- Repeat TSBA at least weekly in severe disease — decisions follow the **peak** value.
- **Ursodeoxycholic acid** — modest pruritus benefit and biochemical improvement, but **PITCHES showed no reduction in the composite perinatal outcome** ⚑: offer it for symptoms, do not promise it prevents stillbirth. Dose 500 mg/day (mild) up to 1500–2000 mg/day (severe, ~20 mg/kg). Second-line for refractory itch: rifampicin (under evaluation in TURRIFIC), plus emollients/menthol and a sedating antihistamine at night.
- **Vitamin K** 10 mg if PT/INR ≥1.4 or APTT ≥40 s (fat-soluble vitamin malabsorption → PPH and neonatal haemorrhage).
- CTG and ultrasound do **not** predict ICP stillbirth — reassuring surveillance must not delay planned birth in very severe disease.
- **Postnatal**: itch resolves within days; recheck **TSBA and ALT at 6 weeks** and investigate if still abnormal. Avoid the COCP until LFTs normalise. Counsel on high recurrence and long-term hepatobiliary risk.

**The short version**
- Itch without rash + bile acids **≥19** = ICP.
- **≥100 µmol/L** is the stillbirth-risk threshold → birth from 36 weeks.
- UDCA treats the mother's symptoms, not the fetal risk.

| Treat subclinical hypothyroidism in pregnancy? | **RANZCOG: no**; ATA/*Aust Prescriber*: yes if TPO+ or TSH ≥4 — sources disagree |
| Antithyroid drug by trimester | **PTU in T1** (carbimazole embryopathy), **carbimazole from T2** (PTU hepatotoxicity) |
| Hyperemesis + thyrotoxic biochemistry at 10/40, no goitre, TRAb negative | **Gestational transient thyrotoxicosis** — supportive only |
| Painless thyrotoxicosis 3 months postpartum, low uptake | **Postpartum thyroiditis** — beta-blocker, not carbimazole |
| Safest ASMs in pregnancy | **Lamotrigine and levetiracetam** — and both need dose *increases* as pregnancy progresses |

| Folic acid dose on any ASM | **5 mg daily** |
| Third-trimester itch of palms and soles, no rash | **ICP** — TSBA **≥19 µmol/L** (non-fasting) |

# O&G Notes — Section 5: Early Pregnancy Problems
*Australian-anchored: RANZCOG C-Gyn 38 (miscarriage, recurrent miscarriage, ectopic), Queensland Clinical Guidelines (Early pregnancy loss), RANZCOG GTD statement, SOMANZ NVP/HG guideline, state termination-of-pregnancy legislation. Scope: bleeding and pain <20 weeks, plus HG and TOP.*

---

## 5.1 The first move in any early-pregnancy bleed

Three questions, in order: **is she shocked**, **where is the pregnancy**, **is it viable**. Everything else follows.
- Resuscitate first. **Cervical shock** — bradycardia and hypotension from products in the cervical os — is relieved by removing the tissue with sponge forceps; it mimics haemorrhagic shock but the pulse is *slow*.
- **Rh D negative** → anti-D. **250 IU up to 12 weeks; 625 IU beyond 12 weeks**, within 72 h. ⚑ RANZCOG C-Gyn 38 states anti-D is **no longer routinely recommended for threatened miscarriage or miscarriage before 10 weeks** on evidence grounds — many Australian units still give it, so know both. Anti-D **should** be offered for surgically managed ectopic pregnancy.

**Ultrasound definitions of failed pregnancy** (transvaginal; conservative thresholds — when in doubt, rescan in 7–10 days):
- **MSD ≥25 mm with no embryo** = failed pregnancy
- **CRL ≥7 mm with no fetal heart** (observed ≥30 s) = failed pregnancy
- No embryo ≥2 weeks after a scan showing a sac without yolk sac, or ≥11 days after a sac with yolk sac
Never diagnose miscarriage on a single borderline scan, and never on hCG alone.

---

## 5.2 Miscarriage

Pregnancy loss before **20 weeks** (Australian definition; the UK uses 24 weeks ⚑). Occurs in ~15–20% of clinically recognised pregnancies; **50–60% are chromosomal**, mostly autosomal trisomy.

| Type | Bleeding | Pain | Os | Ultrasound | Discriminator |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Threatened** | Light | Mild/none | **Closed** | Viable IUP, FH present | Pregnancy still alive; ~50% continue |
| **Inevitable** | Heavy | Cramping | **Open** | Sac may still be in utero | Open os = inevitable, whatever the scan shows |
| **Incomplete** | Ongoing | Cramping | Open | **Retained products** | Bleeding persists because tissue remains |
| **Complete** | Settling | Settling | Closed | **Empty uterus, thin endometrium**, with prior confirmed IUP | Beware — an "empty uterus" without prior confirmed IUP may be an **ectopic** |
| **Missed (early fetal demise)** | None/spotting | None | Closed | CRL ≥7 mm, no FH, or MSD ≥25 mm empty | Often an incidental scan finding; symptoms absent |
| **Septic** | ± offensive discharge | Tender uterus | Open | RPOC | Fever, tachycardia, peritonism — resuscitate, IV antibiotics, **urgent evacuation** |

**Management — all three options are acceptable for a stable woman with incomplete or missed miscarriage; the choice is hers.**

| | Regimen | Best for | Caveats |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Expectant** | Wait 7–14 days (longer if she wishes), rescan | Incomplete miscarriage (~80% success) | Unpredictable; only ~50% success in missed miscarriage |
| **Medical** | **Missed**: mifepristone 200 mg then misoprostol 600–800 µg 24–48 h later ⚑ (mifepristone pre-treatment is now standard — MifeMiso). **Incomplete**: misoprostol 600–800 µg alone, repeatable | Avoiding theatre | Cramping, bleeding, diarrhoea, fever/chills; ~5–15% need surgery |
| **Surgical** | **Suction aspiration** with prophylactic **doxycycline ± metronidazole** | Heavy bleeding, sepsis, instability, molar suspicion, choice | Perforation, infection, **Asherman syndrome** — **avoid sharp curettage** ⚑ |

**Aftercare**: histopathology (excludes molar disease), anti-D per above, immediate contraception if wanted, and explicit acknowledgement of grief. Fertility returns immediately — no evidence-based reason to delay conceiving.

**Recurrent miscarriage** — RANZCOG defines it as **two or more** losses <20 weeks, consecutive or not ⚑ (older texts say three).
- **Investigate**: **lupus anticoagulant and anticardiolipin antibodies** (antiphospholipid syndrome — the only reliably treatable cause), TSH ± TPO antibodies, **3D pelvic ultrasound** for uterine anomaly.
- **Case-by-case**: parental karyotype, ANA, sperm DNA fragmentation.
- **Not routine**: inherited thrombophilia screen, NK cell/HLA/cytokine testing, vitamin D, prolactin, homocysteine, routine karyotyping of products.
- **Treat APS** with **low-dose aspirin + prophylactic LMWH** — live birth rate improves substantially. Otherwise supportive care in a dedicated early pregnancy clinic materially improves outcomes; ~75% of women with unexplained recurrent loss go on to a live birth.

**The short version**
- Open os = inevitable/incomplete; closed os with no FH = missed.
- Mifepristone before misoprostol is now standard for missed miscarriage. ⚑
- Recurrent = **two** losses in Australia; the must-do test is antiphospholipid antibodies.

---

## 5.3 Ectopic pregnancy

**1–2% of pregnancies; ~95% tubal (ampulla commonest).** Remains a cause of maternal death — the fatal error is anchoring on "miscarriage".

**Risk factors** (all reduce tubal function or bypass it): previous ectopic (10–15% recurrence), previous PID/chlamydia, tubal surgery including **sterilisation and reversal**, **IUD in situ** (reduces absolute risk of any pregnancy, but a pregnancy that occurs is proportionally more likely ectopic), **IVF/ART**, endometriosis, smoking, progestogen-only contraception, increasing age. **Up to a third have no risk factors** — never exclude on risk profile.

**Presentation**: 6–8 weeks' amenorrhoea, **unilateral lower abdominal pain preceding light dark "prune juice" bleeding**, cervical excitation, adnexal tenderness, uterus small for dates. Ruptured: sudden severe pain, **shoulder-tip pain** (diaphragmatic irritation), syncope, shock, peritonism. Atypical: diarrhoea, urge to defecate, dizziness. **Any woman of reproductive age with abdominal pain and collapse gets a pregnancy test.**

**Investigation**
- **Urine/serum hCG** then **transvaginal ultrasound** — the definitive test. Look for an **adnexal mass separate from the ovary** ("bagel"/"blob" sign), free fluid, empty uterus. A **pseudo-sac** (central, no double decidual ring) is not a pregnancy.
- **hCG discriminatory zone** — the level above which an intrauterine pregnancy *should* be visible on TVUS. Quoted between **1500 and 3500 IU/L** with **no single international consensus**; higher figures (3500) are used deliberately to avoid interrupting a wanted early IUP. ⚑ In multiple pregnancy the zone is higher. An hCG above the zone with an empty uterus = ectopic until proven otherwise.
- **48-hour serial hCG** in a stable woman with a PUL (below).

**Management** — driven by stability, hCG, mass size and fetal cardiac activity.

| | Criteria | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Expectant** | Stable, minimal/no symptoms, **hCG <1500 IU/L** and falling, mass **<35 mm** with **no fetal heart**, reliable follow-up | Serial hCG to non-pregnant level; safety-net explicitly |
| **Methotrexate** | Stable, no significant pain, mass **<35 mm**, **no fetal cardiac activity**, no haemoperitoneum, normal FBC/LFT/U&E, no breastfeeding, no MTX contraindication, able to attend follow-up | **RANZCOG**: single dose if **hCG <3000 IU/L or mass <20 mm**; **two doses 4–7 days apart** if **hCG 3000–5000 IU/L or mass 20–35 mm** ⚑. Older texts quote a single "hCG <5000" cut-off — a real source difference; the RANZCOG two-tier version is the current Australian answer |
| **Surgery** | Haemodynamic instability, significant pain, **rupture**, hCG high or rising, mass ≥35 mm, **fetal cardiac activity**, MTX contraindicated/failed, unable to follow up | **Laparoscopic salpingectomy** is preferred. **Salpingostomy** only if the contralateral tube is absent/damaged and future fertility is a priority — higher failure and needs hCG surveillance for **persistent trophoblast** |

**Methotrexate practicalities**: 50 mg/m² IM, then **hCG on days 4 and 7** — expect a **≥15% fall from day 4 to day 7**; if not, second dose or surgery. Weekly hCG thereafter until <5 IU/L. Counsel: avoid alcohol, NSAIDs, folate supplements and sun exposure; expect transient "separation pain" around days 2–3 (must be distinguished from rupture); **avoid conception for ~4 months**. Side effects: stomatitis, transaminitis, marrow suppression.

**Non-tubal ectopics**: **interstitial/cornual** (presents later, ~8–10 weeks, catastrophic bleeding), **cervical**, **caesarean scar**, **ovarian**, and **heterotopic** — rare spontaneously (~1:30,000) but **~1:100 after IVF**, so a confirmed IUP does *not* exclude an ectopic after ART ⚑.

---

## 5.4 Pregnancy of unknown location (PUL)

**Definition**: positive hCG, **no intrauterine and no extrauterine pregnancy visible** on TVUS. It is a *state*, not a diagnosis — the three destinations are early IUP, failing/failed pregnancy, and occult ectopic. Roughly 7–20% of PULs turn out to be ectopic.

**Serial hCG at 0 and 48 h**:

| hCG behaviour over 48 h | Interpretation | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Rise ≥63–66%** | Likely viable IUP (but **ectopic not excluded**) | Rescan in ~7–10 days / when hCG >discriminatory zone |
| **Fall ≥50%** | Likely failing pregnancy | Urine hCG at 2 weeks to confirm negative |
| **Suboptimal rise (<63%) or slow fall (<50%) — the "plateau"** | **Highest ectopic risk** | Senior review, close surveillance, low threshold for laparoscopy |

Doubling time in a normal IUP is ~1.4–2.1 days. Progesterone <20 nmol/L supports a failing pregnancy and >60 nmol/L a viable one, but neither **localises** the pregnancy. **Manage clinically, not numerically** — pain and haemodynamic status override any hCG trend. Every PUL leaves with written safety-net advice and a named follow-up point.

## 5.5 Hyperemesis gravidarum

**Nausea and vomiting of pregnancy (NVP)** affects up to 80%; **HG** is the severe end. SOMANZ definition: pregnancy-caused nausea/vomiting with significant reduction in oral intake and **weight loss of at least 5%** of pre-pregnancy weight (typically with dehydration and electrolyte or ketotic disturbance). Onset **before 9 weeks** — new-onset vomiting after ~10–11 weeks should prompt a search for another cause (UTI, gastroenteritis, hepatitis, pancreatitis, appendicitis, thyrotoxicosis, raised ICP, DKA).

**Assess with PUQE-24** (nausea hours + vomits + retches): **mild 4–6, moderate 7–12, severe ≥13**. Check weight, ketones, U&E (hypokalaemia, hypochloraemic metabolic alkalosis), LFTs, TFTs, urine MCS, and **ultrasound — exclude multiple pregnancy and molar pregnancy**, both of which raise hCG and drive vomiting.

**Treatment ladder (SOMANZ)**
1. **Non-pharmacological**: small frequent dry carbohydrate meals, avoid triggers, **ginger up to 1200 mg/day** or **pyridoxine (B6) up to 200 mg/day**.
2. **First-line drugs**: antihistamine/dopamine antagonists — **doxylamine (often combined with pyridoxine)**, metoclopramide, prochlorperazine.
3. **Second-line**: **ondansetron 4–8 mg TDS**. Early registry signals of a small absolute increase in **cleft palate** with first-trimester exposure have not been confirmed in updated pooled analyses ⚑ — the current position is no significant increase in major malformations, but document a risk–benefit discussion for T1 use.
4. **Third-line/refractory**: corticosteroids (prednisolone 40–50 mg/day or hydrocortisone 100 mg IV BD), acid suppression (ranitidine alternatives/PPI), and consideration of enteral nutrition.

**Fluids and the Wernicke trap — this is the exam point.**
- Rehydrate with **normal saline** (1–2 L, ~1 L/h initially), with potassium replacement as needed. **Avoid dextrose-containing fluids until thiamine has been given** — a glucose load in a thiamine-depleted woman can precipitate **Wernicke's encephalopathy**.
- **Give thiamine to any woman vomiting >3 weeks or requiring IV rehydration** — oral 100 mg daily, or IV/IM thiamine (commonly 100 mg IV daily for 3 days, higher doses if Wernicke's is suspected) **before** any glucose.
- **Wernicke's triad**: confusion, ophthalmoplegia/nystagmus, ataxia — often incomplete; a low threshold to treat.
- Also avoid over-rapid correction of hyponatraemia (**central pontine myelinolysis**) and watch for **refeeding syndrome** (hypophosphataemia) when nutrition restarts.

**Also remember**: **VTE prophylaxis** (dehydration + immobility), dental care (acid erosion), and mental health — HG carries substantial psychological morbidity and is a recognised reason women terminate wanted pregnancies. Recurrence is common (~70–80%); pre-emptive treatment before symptom onset helps.

## 5.6 Gestational trophoblastic disease

A spectrum from benign molar pregnancy to malignant **gestational trophoblastic neoplasia (GTN)**. Risk factors: **extremes of maternal age (<16, >45)**, previous molar pregnancy, Asian ethnicity.

| | **Complete mole** | **Partial mole** |
|---|---|---|
| Genetics | **Diploid, entirely paternal (46,XX ~75–80%)** — empty ovum fertilised by one sperm that duplicates, or two sperm | **Triploid (69,XXX / 69,XXY)** — normal ovum + two sperm sets |
| Fetal tissue | **Absent** | **Present** (often abnormal fetus/fetal red cells) |
| Villi | Diffuse hydropic swelling, circumferential trophoblast proliferation | Focal, scalloped villi |
| **p57 immunostain** | **Negative** (no maternal genome) | **Positive** |
| hCG | Very high | Modest/normal |
| Progression to GTN | **~15–25%** | **~0.5–4%** |

**Presentation**: first-trimester bleeding (often with passage of "grape-like" vesicles), **uterus large for dates**, **hCG far higher than expected**, **exaggerated pregnancy symptoms — hyperemesis**, theca lutein cysts, and the classics that appear *before 20 weeks*: **early-onset pre-eclampsia** and **hyperthyroidism** (hCG cross-reacts at the TSH receptor). Ultrasound: **"snowstorm"/cluster-of-grapes** heterogeneous mass with cystic spaces, no fetal parts (complete). Many are now diagnosed histologically after an apparently ordinary miscarriage — **which is why all products of conception go to histopathology**.

**Management**: **suction evacuation** under ultrasound guidance — **avoid medical management, and avoid oxytocin until evacuation is under way** (theoretical trophoblast embolisation). Anti-D if Rh D negative. Send tissue for histology **with p57 immunostaining** and register with a GTD centre where available.

**Surveillance**: **partial mole** — weekly hCG until **three consecutive normal results**, then stop. **Complete mole** — hCG to normal, then **monthly for 6 months**. Avoid pregnancy until surveillance is complete; **COCP is acceptable once hCG has normalised** (avoid an IUD until then).

**GTN** — defined by hCG behaviour, not histology alone: **plateau across four values over 3 weeks**, **rise >10% across three values over 2 weeks**, persistent elevation ≥6 months, histological choriocarcinoma, or metastases (**lung commonest** — haemoptysis or a new lung lesion after a molar pregnancy). The malignant group is invasive mole, choriocarcinoma, placental site and epithelioid trophoblastic tumour. FIGO/WHO scoring: **low risk → single-agent methotrexate (with folinic acid) or actinomycin D; high risk → multi-agent EMA-CO**. Cure rates are excellent and fertility is usually preserved.

**The short version**
- Complete = no fetus, all-paternal diploid, p57 negative, 15–25% → GTN.
- Large-for-dates uterus + hyperemesis + pre-eclampsia before 20 weeks = mole.
- GTN is defined by an hCG plateau or rise, not by symptoms.

## 5.7 Termination of pregnancy — Australian legal framework in brief

**Abortion is decriminalised in every Australian state and territory.** ⚑ (Queensland 2018, NSW 2019, SA 2021, WA's reform 2023 — the older "unlawful procurement" framing in textbooks is obsolete.) What varies is the **gestational limit for termination on request** and what is required beyond it.

| Jurisdiction | On request up to | Beyond that limit |
|---|---|---|
| ACT | **No gestational limit** | — |
| NSW | **22 weeks** | Two specialist medical practitioners |
| NT | 24 weeks | Two doctors (one must assess the woman) |
| Qld | **22 weeks** | Two doctors |
| SA | 22 weeks 6 days | Two doctors (risk to life/health or serious fetal anomaly) |
| Tas | **16 weeks** (most restrictive) | Two doctors, one an O&G specialist |
| Vic | **24 weeks** | Two doctors |
| WA | 23 weeks | Two doctors |

Common to all jurisdictions: **informed consent**; **counselling offered, not mandated**; **safe access zones** (typically 150 m); and a **conscientious objection** provision permitting refusal to perform the procedure but imposing a **duty to declare the objection promptly and transfer care or name a non-objecting practitioner**, with an overriding obligation to act in an **emergency**. Exam framing: a doctor may decline to perform a termination but may **not** obstruct access or withhold the information needed to obtain one.

**Methods**
- **Medical (MS-2 Step)** — **mifepristone 200 mg** then **misoprostol 800 µg** 36–48 h later, approved to **63 days (9 weeks)**. PBS-listed and, since 2023, prescribable by qualified nurse practitioners and endorsed midwives without the old certification scheme ⚑ — a substantial widening of rural access. Confirm completion at ~2 weeks.
- **Surgical** — suction aspiration to ~12–14 weeks; **dilatation and evacuation** with cervical preparation beyond.
- **Always**: exclude ectopic before medical abortion, **anti-D if Rh D negative**, screen/treat chlamydia, and start contraception immediately — fertility returns within ~2 weeks.
