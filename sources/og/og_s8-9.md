## 8.1 Postpartum haemorrhage

The single highest-yield obstetric emergency in written finals. Questions almost always test either **(a) which of the 4 Ts the stem is describing**, or **(b) the next step on the escalation ladder given a specific contraindication**.

**Definitions (Australian).** Primary PPH = blood loss ≥500 mL within 24 h of birth. Severe/major PPH = ≥1000 mL, **or any volume with haemodynamic compromise** — the second half of that clause is the exam point, because a 700 mL loss in a 50 kg anaemic woman who is tachycardic and hypotensive is a major PPH. Massive/critical haemorrhage ≥2500 mL. Secondary PPH = excessive bleeding from **24 h to 6 weeks** postpartum.

⚑ Note the Australian convention: QCG and RANZCOG apply the **same ≥500 mL threshold to both vaginal and caesarean birth**, whereas older textbooks (and much UK teaching) use 500 mL vaginal / 1000 mL caesarean. If a stem gives 800 mL after LSCS, Australian practice calls that a PPH.

**Visual estimation systematically underestimates loss** — weigh swabs, use graduated drapes. Pregnancy-expanded plasma volume means a woman can lose 1500 mL before BP falls: **tachycardia and narrowed pulse pressure precede hypotension**, which is a late and ominous sign.

### The 4 Ts — clue → cause

| T | Share | Clue in the stem | Confirm / treat |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tone** (atony) | ~70–80% | **Boggy, high, poorly contracted uterus**; bleeding is steady dark loss; prolonged/augmented labour, multiple pregnancy, polyhydramnios, macrosomia, grand multiparity, chorioamnionitis, tocolytics/halogenated anaesthetics | Palpate fundus → rub up a contraction; uterotonic ladder |
| **Trauma** | ~20% | **Well-contracted, firm uterus but continuing bright red bleeding**; instrumental birth, episiotomy, precipitate labour, big baby | Inspect under good light ± lithotomy/theatre; repair. Consider concealed: broad ligament / paravaginal haematoma (pain + shock out of proportion to visible loss), uterine rupture, uterine inversion |
| **Tissue** | ~10% | Placenta incomplete on inspection (**missing cotyledon or ragged membranes**), uterus stays boggy despite uterotonics, previous LSCS/placenta praevia (accreta spectrum) | Explore uterus / manual removal in theatre; USS for retained products |
| **Thrombin** | <1% | Oozing from cannula sites, non-clotting blood, **abruption, severe pre-eclampsia/HELLP, IUFD, amniotic fluid embolism, sepsis, known bleeding disorder or therapeutic anticoagulation** | Coags, fibrinogen, ROTEM; targeted product replacement |

**Hook:** *Firm uterus + bleeding = look for a tear. Boggy uterus + bleeding = squeeze and drug it.* Uterine inversion is the trap — **fundus not palpable abdominally + a mass at the introitus + shock disproportionate to loss**; replace immediately (manual, or hydrostatic O'Sullivan technique) and do **not** give a uterotonic until it is back in.

### Prevention — active management of the third stage

Roughly halves PPH risk. Prophylactic oxytocin: **10 IU IM** (vaginal birth, no IV access), or **10 IU IV slowly over 3–5 min**, or **3–5 IU IV over 1–2 min at caesarean**. Plus controlled cord traction with counter-traction (guarding the uterus), and post-birth fundal assessment. ⚑ Early cord clamping is no longer part of active management — deferred clamping ≥60 s is standard and does not increase PPH.

### The escalation ladder

Run mechanical, pharmacological, and resuscitative arms **in parallel**, not in sequence. Call for help early.

1. **Mechanical / first 60 seconds** — rub up the fundus, **empty the bladder** (a full bladder splints the uterus open), bimanual compression if torrential. Two large-bore cannulae, **FBC, coags, fibrinogen, group & hold/crossmatch**, warmed fluids, oxygen.
2. **Oxytocin 5 IU IV over 1–2 min**, repeatable once (max 10 IU bolus), then infusion **30–40 IU in 500 mL crystalloid at ~5–10 IU/h**. Watch hypotension with rapid bolus and hyponatraemia with large cumulative volumes.
3. **Ergometrine 250–500 microgram IM or slow IV**, repeat 5-minutely to **maximum 1 mg**. **⚠ CONTRAINDICATED IN HYPERTENSION** — any hypertension, pre-eclampsia or eclampsia. An ergot alkaloid and potent vasoconstrictor: risks hypertensive crisis, stroke, myocardial ischaemia. Also contraindicated in severe cardiac/renal/hepatic/vascular disease, severe sepsis, and retained placenta. Causes marked vomiting — give an antiemetic. **The most examined contraindication in obstetrics**: PPH + BP 165/105 is testing whether you skip it.
4. **Misoprostol 800–1000 microgram SL or PR** — heat-stable, safe in hypertension and asthma, useful without refrigeration or IV access. Causes pyrexia (can exceed 40 °C), shivering, diarrhoea. Do not repeat within 2 h.
5. **Carboprost (15-methyl PGF2α) 250 microgram IM**, repeat every 15 min (up to 90 min intervals) to **maximum 2 mg = 8 doses**; 500 microgram intramyometrial off-label in theatre. **⚠ Avoid in asthma** (bronchospasm) and significant cardiac/pulmonary disease.
6. **Tranexamic acid 1 g IV over 10 min, as early as possible and within 3 h of onset** — mortality benefit falls off sharply after that (WOMAN trial). Repeat 1 g if bleeding continues after 30 min or recurs within 24 h. ⚑ TXA is given for **all** causes of PPH, not just suspected coagulopathy, and belongs early alongside the uterotonics, not as a rescue.
7. **Intrauterine balloon tamponade** (Bakri/condom catheter) — the next step if bimanual compression works, i.e. a positive "tamponade test". Fill ~300–500 mL, watch the drainage port for ongoing loss or blockage. Often avoids laparotomy.
8. **Laparotomy — conservative surgery**: **B-Lynch/compression sutures**, bilateral uterine artery ligation, internal iliac ligation; or **arterial embolisation** if stable and the service exists.
9. **Hysterectomy** — definitive. Decide early; delay is a recurring theme in maternal mortality reports. Subtotal unless the source is the lower segment/cervix (praevia/accreta).

**Transfusion.** 2 units RBC (group-specific or O-negative) if actively bleeding; in massive haemorrhage transfuse near-**1:1:1 (RBC:FFP:platelets), not below 2:1:1**. **Cryoprecipitate/fibrinogen concentrate when fibrinogen ≤2.5 g/L** — a far higher trigger than the trauma target of 1.5–2 g/L, because term fibrinogen is physiologically 4–6 g/L and a "normal-looking" 2.5 g/L already signals consumption. Keep platelets >50, temperature >36 °C, ionised calcium and pH corrected.

**The short version**
- Firm uterus + bleeding → trauma; boggy uterus + bleeding → tone.
- Ergometrine is banned in hypertension/pre-eclampsia; carboprost is the one to avoid in asthma.
- TXA 1 g IV early (<3 h) is standard for every PPH, not a rescue drug.
- Escalate mechanical → pharmacological → tamponade → compression sutures → hysterectomy, and do not delay the last step.

## 8.2 Retained placenta

Placenta undelivered **≥30 min after birth with active management** (≥60 min with physiological third stage). Incidence ~2–3%. It matters because it is both a *cause* of PPH (Tissue) and a *reason your uterotonics are failing*.

Empty the bladder (an IDC alone delivers a surprising number), re-attempt controlled cord traction, encourage skin-to-skin/nipple stimulation. **Oxytocin 10 IU IV/IM** may help; ⚑ intra-umbilical vein oxytocin is no longer recommended. **Do not give ergometrine** — tonic contraction entraps the placenta. If still undelivered → **manual removal in theatre under regional/general anaesthesia** with prophylactic antibiotics (endometritis risk).

**Suspect placenta accreta spectrum** if no cleavage plane can be found — clue is **previous caesarean(s) plus anterior placenta praevia**, or previous uterine surgery/curettage. Do not force separation: stop, call for help, prepare for haemorrhage/hysterectomy.

**The short version**
- 30 min (active) / 60 min (physiological) defines it.
- Empty the bladder, re-try CCT, oxytocin — never ergometrine.
- Previous LSCS + anterior praevia + non-separating placenta = accreta until proven otherwise.

## 8.3 Puerperal sepsis and secondary PPH

**Puerperal sepsis** = life-threatening organ dysfunction from a dysregulated response to a genital-tract or pregnancy-related infection in the puerperium. Historically the leading cause of maternal death and still a leading *direct* cause worldwide.

**Organisms.** *E. coli* is the most frequent isolate, but **Group A Streptococcus (S. pyogenes) causes the majority of sepsis deaths** — the killer to recognise. Also GBS, anaerobes, *Staph aureus*. GAS is often acquired from a household contact with pharyngitis — the stem may say so.

**Clue → source**

| Clue | Source |
|---|---|
| Tender bulky uterus, **offensive lochia**, subinvolution, fever after day 2 | Endometritis (commonest) |
| Fever + **wound erythema/discharge/dehiscence** post-LSCS or perineal repair | Wound infection; if pain out of proportion, crepitus, dusky skin → **necrotising fasciitis** |
| Fever + unilateral loin pain + dysuria | Pyelonephritis |
| Fever + **wedge-shaped tender red breast** | Mastitis (8.4) |
| Fever + calf/leg pain, or pleuritic chest pain/dyspnoea | VTE — remember PE mimics sepsis |
| Fever + **severe pain out of all proportion, diarrhoea/vomiting, rash, rapid collapse** | **Invasive GAS / toxic shock** |

**Red flags** (any two of): temp <36 or >38 °C, HR >110 sustained, RR >24 sustained, MAP <65, abnormal WCC, altered conscious state, poor perfusion. Beware: **maternal sepsis can present without pyrexia**, and physiological tachycardia masks early deterioration.

**Management.** Cultures (**two sets** blood cultures, plus HVS/wound/urine, throat swab if GAS suspected), **lactate**, FBC/U&E/LFT/coags, and **IV antibiotics within 60 min** — do not delay for cultures. Fluids, source control (evacuate retained products, drain collections, debride necrotic wound), early ICU involvement. **Empirical Australian regimen for unknown source postpartum: IV ampicillin + gentamicin + metronidazole. Add clindamycin if GAS or toxic shock is suspected** — it suppresses exotoxin production at the ribosome, and this is an exam favourite. IVIG in severe streptococcal toxic shock.

**Secondary PPH** (24 h – 6 weeks): **retained products of conception and endometritis**, usually together. Clue: bleeding restarts or worsens around day 7–14, offensive lochia, crampy pain, open os. FBC/CRP, cultures if febrile, **pelvic ultrasound** (clot and RPOC look similar early). Antibiotics ± uterotonics; **surgical evacuation only if bleeding is heavy or persistent**, after antibiotic cover — the puerperal uterus perforates easily and vigorous curettage risks Asherman syndrome. If bleeding persists, consider **gestational trophoblastic disease**: check βhCG and send tissue for histology.

## 8.4 Lactational mastitis and breast abscess

Affects roughly **1 in 5 Australian breastfeeding women in the first 6 months**, peaking at **weeks 2–3**. About **3–11% progress to abscess**.

**Clue → diagnosis.** A **unilateral, wedge-shaped (segmental) area of erythema, heat, firmness and tenderness with fever and flu-like myalgia** in a breastfeeding woman. Bilateral, diffuse, non-tender fullness in the first week is **engorgement**, not mastitis. A localised tender lump without systemic upset is a **blocked duct**.

**Organism:** *Staph aureus* predominates (MRSA in healthcare-associated cases); also streptococci and *S. epidermidis*.

**Management — the two examinable points**

1. **Flucloxacillin (or dicloxacillin) 500 mg PO qid for at least 5 days** is first-line in Australia. Penicillin allergy: **cephalexin 500 mg qid** (non-severe/delayed hypersensitivity) or **clindamycin 450 mg tds** (immediate/severe hypersensitivity, and the choice if MRSA is a concern — alternatively trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, though avoid in the first weeks in jaundiced/G6PD-deficient/preterm infants). **Avoid tetracyclines, ciprofloxacin and chloramphenicol while breastfeeding.**
2. **Continue breastfeeding from the affected breast** — start this at diagnosis, do not stop. There is no evidence of harm to a healthy infant feeding from an infected breast, and abrupt cessation worsens stasis and increases abscess risk. If direct feeding is too painful, express. ⚑ Modern guidance (ABM Protocol #36, 2022) has **reversed several long-taught measures**: use **cold** packs rather than heat for inflammation, avoid **deep/vigorous massage** (it worsens oedema and tissue injury), and **do not "empty" the breast** with extra pumping — feed to appetite, since over-removal drives more milk production and more stasis. Older texts advising heat, firm massage and full expression are out of date.

Adjuncts: NSAIDs (anti-inflammatory as well as analgesic), paracetamol, rest, attachment/positioning review, lactation consultant referral.

**Abscess.** **Review at 24–48 h. If not improving after 48 h of appropriate antibiotics → ultrasound** to distinguish persisting cellulitic mastitis from a collection. Clues: **discrete fluctuant tender mass**, swinging fevers, overlying skin thinning/necrosis. Treatment is **ultrasound-guided needle aspiration under local anaesthetic** (often repeated) — this has replaced formal incision and drainage as first-line (better cosmesis, preserves breastfeeding, outpatient). Reserve **incision and drainage** for large, multiloculated or skin-compromised abscesses. Continue antibiotics, send pus for MC&S, continue feeding.

**Do not miss: inflammatory breast carcinoma.** Suspect if "mastitis" **fails to resolve after adequate antibiotics**, especially with peau d'orange, nipple retraction, a persisting mass or axillary nodes. Non-resolving breast inflammation needs triple assessment — never accept a third course of flucloxacillin.

**The short version**
- Wedge-shaped unilateral erythema + fever = mastitis; flucloxacillin 500 mg qid ≥5 days **plus keep feeding**.
- No better after 48 h of antibiotics → ultrasound for abscess → needle aspiration first, not incision.
- ⚑ Cold not heat, no deep massage, no aggressive "emptying".
- Non-resolving mastitis = exclude inflammatory breast cancer.

## 8.5 Perinatal mental health

The commonest complication of childbirth and a leading cause of maternal death in the year after birth (suicide). Written finals test the **three-way discrimination** and **which one is an emergency**.

| | **Baby blues** | **Postnatal depression** | **Puerperal (postpartum) psychosis** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 50–80% | ~10–16% | **~1–2 per 1000 births** |
| Onset | **Days 3–5**, peaks day 5 | Usually within 6 weeks–3 months; can begin antenatally or up to 12 months | **Abrupt, usually days 3–14; most within 2 weeks** |
| Duration | **Self-limiting, ≤2 weeks** | Weeks–months if untreated | Days–weeks with treatment |
| Core features | Tearfulness, lability, irritability, mild anxiety, **insight and function preserved** | Persistent low mood/anhedonia, guilt, poor sleep beyond baby's needs, appetite change, hopelessness, **impaired functioning and bonding** | **Confusion/perplexity, fluctuating course, delusions (often infant-focused), hallucinations, mania, disorganised behaviour, loss of insight** |
| Risk factors | Ubiquitous | Past depression/anxiety, lack of support, IPV, adverse life events, unplanned pregnancy, birth trauma, preterm/NICU | **Bipolar affective disorder (~20% risk per birth), previous puerperal psychosis (~50% recurrence), first pregnancy, family history** |
| Action | Reassure, support, sleep, review | GP/mental health referral, psychological therapy ± SSRI | **PSYCHIATRIC EMERGENCY — same-day psychiatric assessment, usually admission, ideally to a mother–baby unit; never manage in the community** |

**Discriminators to say out loud:** duration >2 weeks or functional impairment moves you from blues to depression. **Confusion, fluctuation and loss of insight** move you to psychosis. A mother with bipolar disorder who becomes elated, sleepless and disorganised on day 5 is not "blues" — that is puerperal psychosis until proven otherwise. **Exclude organic causes** in any acute postpartum confusion: sepsis, hypoglycaemia, hyponatraemia (oxytocin-related), thyroid storm/postpartum thyroiditis, drug/alcohol withdrawal, eclampsia, intracranial event, Sheehan syndrome.

**EPDS (Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale).** 10 items, self-report, scored 0–30. **Score ≥13 → further assessment/referral** in Australian practice. Antenatally, COPE advises **repeating the EPDS in 2–4 weeks** if the score is ≥13, and referring if it remains ≥13; postnatally, arrange referral/ongoing care at ≥13. **Item 10 asks about self-harm — any non-zero response (1, 2 or 3) triggers an immediate safety assessment of the woman and children in her care**, regardless of the total score. Screen **at least once, preferably twice, antenatally and postnatally (postnatal ideally 6–12 weeks)**. It detects anxiety symptoms too. ⚑ Lower cut-offs may be more appropriate for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander women and culturally and linguistically diverse women — the EPDS is a screening tool, not a diagnosis, and a low score never overrides clinical concern.

**Treatment.** CBT/IPT first-line for mild–moderate PND. **Sertraline** is the usual first-line SSRI in breastfeeding (low milk transfer, good safety data); continue an already-effective antidepressant rather than switching to a "safer" one that has not worked. In psychosis: antipsychotics ± lithium under specialist care; ECT is highly effective where rapid response is needed. Assess **risk to self, risk to infant, and capacity to care** at every contact. Paternal perinatal depression affects ~5–10%.

## 8.6 Postpartum contraception and lactational amenorrhoea

**Timing matters because ovulation can precede the first period.** In a non-breastfeeding woman ovulation can return from **day 21–28**; contraception is therefore recommended **from day 21**. In fully breastfeeding women, return is later and more variable. Short inter-pregnancy intervals (<18 months) carry increased risk of preterm birth, SGA and PPH — so contraception is a routine part of postnatal care, ideally discussed antenatally.

**LAM — lactational amenorrhoea method.** ~98% effective **only if all three criteria hold simultaneously**:
1. **Amenorrhoeic** (no bleeding after day 56 postpartum), **and**
2. **Fully/nearly fully breastfeeding** — no supplements, feeds day and night, **intervals ≤4 h day and ≤6 h night**, **and**
3. **<6 months postpartum**.

**If any one fails, LAM fails** — that is the exam point. Returning to work, introducing formula, or the baby sleeping through the night all break it.

| Method | When it can start | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **POP / progestogen implant / DMPA** | **Immediately, any time postpartum**, breastfeeding or not | No effect on milk supply/volume; no need to wait. Implant and POP immediately; DMPA immediate is acceptable (some services delay to reduce irregular bleeding) |
| **COC** | **≥21 days** if not breastfeeding; **≥6 weeks** if breastfeeding | Oestrogen is prothrombotic — VTE risk is highest in the first 3 weeks. Delay further if additional VTE risk factors. UK/UKMEC and Australian practice broadly agree here |
| **Cu-IUD or LNG-IUD** | **Within 48 h of birth (postplacental)** or **from 4 weeks** | Insertion between 48 h and 4 weeks is avoided (perforation/expulsion risk). Immediate postplacental insertion has higher expulsion but excellent continuation |
| **Condoms** | Immediately | Backup / STI protection |
| **Sterilisation** | At LSCS, or ≥6 weeks postpartum | Counsel about regret — higher when decided peripartum; vasectomy is safer and more effective |
| **Emergency contraception** | From day 21 | Ulipristal: express and discard for 7 days. Levonorgestrel and Cu-IUD compatible with breastfeeding |

## 8.7 Postpartum VTE prophylaxis

**The puerperium is the highest-risk VTE window of pregnancy** — peaking in week 1, elevated for ~6 weeks. VTE remains a leading direct cause of maternal death. Every woman needs a **documented risk assessment at booking, on admission, and after birth**.

**Risk factors:** previous VTE (strongest), high-risk thrombophilia (antithrombin deficiency, antiphospholipid syndrome, homozygous factor V Leiden), **caesarean — especially emergency in labour**, BMI ≥30, age >35, parity ≥3, immobility, pre-eclampsia, PPH >1 L or transfusion, mid-cavity instrumental birth, multiple pregnancy, sepsis, smoking, IVF, medical comorbidity (SLE, IBD, sickle cell).

**Stratified approach (Australian units):**

| Risk tier | Example | Prophylaxis |
|---|---|---|
| Lower | Elective LSCS, no other risk factors | Mobilise, hydrate, TEDs; no LMWH |
| Intermediate | Emergency LSCS in labour, **or** elective LSCS with ≥2 other risk factors | **LMWH for ~10 days** (extend to 6 weeks if risk factors persist) |
| High | **Previous VTE**, antenatal LMWH required, high-risk thrombophilia | **LMWH for at least 6 weeks** |

**Enoxaparin prophylaxis: 40 mg SC daily**, weight-adjusted at extremes of BMI (e.g. reduced <50 kg, increased or twice-daily ≥90–100 kg — follow local protocol).

**Timing rules that get examined:**
- First dose **≥4 h after vaginal birth**, once bleeding risk is acceptable.
- **Do not give LMWH within 4 h of a spinal or of epidural catheter removal.**
- **Do not site/remove a neuraxial catheter within 12 h of a prophylactic LMWH dose** (24 h for treatment dose) — spinal haematoma risk.
- **LMWH and warfarin are both safe in breastfeeding**; no need to discard milk. **DOACs are not recommended in breastfeeding.**
- Mechanical measures (TEDs, calf compression, early mobilisation, hydration) for everyone.

Suspected postpartum VTE: compression duplex for DVT; CTPA or V/Q for PE. ⚑ **D-dimer is not useful in pregnancy or the early puerperium** — physiologically elevated, and a normal result does not exclude VTE. Treat empirically with therapeutic LMWH while awaiting imaging if suspicion is high.

## 9.1 Abnormal uterine bleeding and PALM-COEIN

⚑ **Terminology has changed.** FIGO abolished *menorrhagia, metrorrhagia, menometrorrhagia, oligomenorrhoea* and *dysfunctional uterine bleeding* (DUB). Describe bleeding instead by four parameters, and name the cause using PALM-COEIN. If a stem or answer stem uses "DUB", it is dated — the modern equivalents are **AUB-O** (ovulatory dysfunction) or **AUB-E** (endometrial).

**Normal menstrual parameters (FIGO):**

| Parameter | Normal |
|---|---|
| **Frequency** | **24–38 days** (frequent <24; infrequent >38) |
| **Duration** | **≤8 days** (prolonged >8) |
| **Regularity** | cycle-to-cycle variation **≤7–9 days** (age-dependent) |
| **Volume** | patient-determined — **volume that interferes with quality of life is heavy** |

Plus intermenstrual bleeding (IMB) and postcoital bleeding (PCB) as separate symptom descriptors.

**PALM-COEIN.** PALM = **structural**, assessable by imaging/histology. COEIN = **non-structural**.

| | Cause | Clue in the stem |
|---|---|---|
| **P** | Polyp | **IMB/PCB**, regular but "spotting between periods"; focal lesion on TVUS/saline sonohysterography |
| **A** | Adenomyosis | **Heavy + progressively painful periods**, bulky **globular tender** uterus, multiparous 35–50; MRI or TVUS (myometrial heterogeneity, junctional zone thickening) |
| **L** | Leiomyoma (fibroid) | **Bulky irregular firm uterus**, pressure/bulk symptoms; submucosal ones cause the bleeding (see 9.6) |
| **M** | Malignancy/hyperplasia | **Postmenopausal bleeding**, persistent IMB, obesity/PCOS/tamoxifen/Lynch, age >45 |
| **C** | Coagulopathy | **HMB since menarche**, bleeding after dental extraction/surgery, epistaxis, easy bruising, family history — **von Willebrand disease is the big one (~13% of HMB)** |
| **O** | Ovulatory dysfunction | **Irregular, unpredictable cycles**; extremes of reproductive life, **PCOS**, thyroid disease, hyperprolactinaemia, athletes/eating disorder, stress |
| **E** | Endometrial (primary disorder of local haemostasis) | **Regular, predictable, cyclical but heavy** bleeding with normal imaging and normal ovulation — diagnosis of exclusion |
| **I** | Iatrogenic | **Depot/implant/POP/IUD breakthrough bleeding**, missed pills, anticoagulants, tamoxifen, SSRIs, herbal supplements |
| **N** | Not otherwise classified | AVM, caesarean scar niche/isthmocele, chronic endometritis |

**Hook:** *Regular and heavy → structural or endometrial. Irregular and unpredictable → ovulatory.* And in any woman of reproductive age with abnormal bleeding, **exclude pregnancy first** — that is the free mark.

## 9.2 Heavy menstrual bleeding: investigation and management

**Definition (Australian/NICE-aligned):** excessive menstrual blood loss that **interferes with physical, social, emotional and/or material quality of life** — patient-defined, not a 80 mL measurement. ⚑ The old ">80 mL per cycle" definition is research-only and is no longer the clinical standard.

**Assessment.** History: cycle parameters, flooding/large clots, hourly protection changes, nocturnal soiling, iron symptoms, IMB/PCB, dyspareunia, cervical screening status, fertility and contraception plans, **and a bleeding-disorder screen** (since menarche? post-dental bleeding? PPH? family history?). Examination: abdominal, speculum ± bimanual (bulky/irregular = fibroids; bulky/tender/globular = adenomyosis; do not miss a cervical lesion).

**Investigations**
- **βhCG** — always.
- **FBC + ferritin** — treat iron deficiency in its own right (oral, or IV iron if severe/intolerant). ⚑ Ferritin is explicitly part of first-line assessment in the 2024 Australian Clinical Care Standard.
- **TFTs, coagulation/von Willebrand studies** only if the history suggests — test for vWD **if HMB since menarche or a personal/family bleeding history**.
- **Pelvic ultrasound — transvaginal preferred, ideally days 5–10.** For abnormal examination, suspected structural pathology, failed medical therapy, or recent-onset HMB in an older woman.
- **Endometrial sampling (Pipelle ± hysteroscopy)** for **persistent IMB, treatment failure, or hyperplasia risk factors**: unopposed oestrogen (obesity, PCOS, anovulation), diabetes, nulliparity, tamoxifen, Lynch syndrome, prior breast/gynae cancer. ⚑ **Sources disagree on the age threshold alone** — Australian unit guidelines commonly sample from **>40** with recent-onset or persistent IMB; NICE uses **≥45**. Safest answer: sample on risk factors and persistence, and lower your threshold from 40 onwards.
- **Hysteroscopy** for suspected focal pathology or non-diagnostic blind sampling — diagnostic and therapeutic.

**Management ladder.** Start medical treatment while investigating — do not wait for the ultrasound.

| Rung | Option | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **LNG-IUD 52 mg** | **Most effective medical treatment** (>80% reduction at 3 months, >90% at 12); also contraceptive and endometrial-protective. Default first choice unless the cavity is distorted or pregnancy is wanted now |
| 2 | **Tranexamic acid 1 g tds–qid, up to 5 days of menses** | ~40–60% reduction; non-hormonal, **taken only during bleeding** — ideal when trying to conceive |
| 3 | **NSAID — mefenamic acid 500 mg tds** or naproxen | 20–40% reduction **and treats dysmenorrhoea** — the choice when heavy *and* painful |
| 4 | **COC** | ~50% reduction; can be run continuously to skip bleeds |
| 5 | **Cyclical progestogen** (norethisterone 5 mg tds days 5–26) | Up to ~80% reduction, poorly tolerated; **luteal-phase-only regimens are ineffective** — classic distractor |
| 6 | **DMPA / implant / GnRH agonist + add-back** | GnRH short-term only (bone loss); mainly to shrink fibroids pre-op |
| 7 | **Surgery** — hysteroscopic resection, **endometrial ablation**, UAE, **hysterectomy** | Ablation: ~50% amenorrhoea, but **irreversible and requires reliable contraception**. Hysterectomy definitive, last-line |

**Acute severe HMB:** resuscitate, βhCG, FBC/coags/group & hold, **tranexamic acid 1 g IV or PO**, plus **high-dose oral progestogen** (norethisterone 5–10 mg tds or MPA 10–20 mg); EUA if uncontrolled; treat the anaemia.

**The short version**
- HMB is defined by impact on life, not millilitres.
- FBC + ferritin + βhCG in everyone; imaging and biopsy by indication.
- LNG-IUD is the most effective medical option and the default first-line.
- Tranexamic acid ± NSAID is the answer when the woman is trying to conceive.

## 9.3 Dysmenorrhoea

**Primary:** no underlying pathology. Begins **6–24 months after menarche, once cycles become ovulatory**. Crampy suprapubic pain **starting with or just before the flow, lasting 8–72 h**, radiating to back/thighs, with nausea, diarrhoea and headache. Mechanism: **endometrial prostaglandin F2α → myometrial hypercontractility and ischaemia** — hence NSAIDs work so well. Examination normal.

**Secondary:** underlying pathology. Clues that should stop you calling it primary — **onset years after menarche, or new/worsening pain**; pain **beginning days before the period and persisting after it**; deep dyspareunia; non-cyclical pelvic pain; cyclical bowel/bladder symptoms; subfertility; abnormal examination (nodularity, fixed retroverted uterus, adnexal mass); or **failure of NSAIDs plus hormonal therapy**. Causes: **endometriosis** (commonest), adenomyosis, fibroids, PID, ovarian cysts, cervical stenosis, copper IUD, and obstructive Müllerian anomalies (**severe cyclical pain with primary amenorrhoea in a young girl** — imperforate hymen/transverse septum).

**Management:** **NSAIDs first-line** (start at or just before onset, take regularly not PRN), then **hormonal suppression** — COC (continuous/extended), LNG-IUD, POP, DMPA, implant. Adjuncts: heat, exercise, TENS. Refer if secondary features, failure after **3–6 months**, or fertility affected.

## 9.4 Polycystic ovary syndrome

Commonest endocrinopathy in women of reproductive age (~8–13%, with up to 70% undiagnosed). Australia leads this guideline — the **2023 International Evidence-Based Guideline for the Assessment and Management of PCOS** (Monash-led, endorsed by RANZCOG/ESHRE/ASRM) is the reference standard and supersedes the 2018 version.

### Diagnostic criteria (Rotterdam, as modified 2023)

**Adults: any 2 of 3.**
1. **Clinical or biochemical hyperandrogenism** — hirsutism (Ferriman–Gallwey), acne, androgenic alopecia; or raised **calculated free testosterone / free androgen index / bioavailable testosterone**. (Total testosterone alone is insensitive; assays are unreliable on the COC — **stop the pill for ≥3 months before testing**.)
2. **Ovulatory dysfunction** — irregular cycles: <21 or >35 days, or <8 cycles/year; in the first year post-menarche irregularity is normal, and 1–3 years post-menarche <21 or >45 days counts.
3. **Polycystic ovarian morphology (PCOM) on ultrasound, ⚑ *or* an elevated serum AMH.**

**⚑ Key 2023 changes**

| Change | Detail |
|---|---|
| **AMH may substitute for ultrasound** in adults | Serum AMH can define PCOM instead of TVUS. But **not as a standalone diagnostic test**, **not in adolescents**, and **do not do both** — using both drives overdiagnosis. Thresholds are assay- and lab-specific, so quote the principle, not a number |
| **Ultrasound threshold** | **FNPO ≥20 in at least one ovary** on a high-frequency (≥8 MHz) transvaginal probe; on older/transabdominal imaging use **ovarian volume ≥10 mL or FNPS ≥10**. The old "≥12 follicles" is superseded |
| **Adolescents** | **Both hyperandrogenism AND ovulatory dysfunction required** — **no ultrasound, no AMH** (multifollicular ovaries are normal in adolescence). New "**at increased risk**" category for partial features: re-evaluate by full reproductive maturity (~8 years post-menarche) |
| **Metabolic screening** | **75 g OGTT is the most accurate test**; HbA1c and fasting glucose less accurate in PCOS. Repeat **every 1–3 years** by risk. **Lipids at diagnosis in everyone** regardless of age/BMI; **BP annually** |
| **Obstructive sleep apnoea** | New emphasis — screen symptomatically; prevalence raised **independent of BMI** |
| **Psychological** | Strengthened to "should": **screen all for depression and anxiety**; new attention to **body image distress and eating disorders — regardless of weight**; minimise weight stigma, seek permission before weighing |
| **Endometrial cancer** | Hyperplasia/carcinoma risk **markedly increased** but absolute risk low → **no routine screening**; prevent with cycle regulation/progestogen, investigate abnormal bleeding |

**Diagnosis is one of exclusion.** Before labelling PCOS, exclude: **thyroid disease (TSH), hyperprolactinaemia (prolactin), non-classical congenital adrenal hyperplasia (17-hydroxyprogesterone)**, and — if virilisation is rapid or testosterone is markedly raised — androgen-secreting tumour and Cushing syndrome. **Rapid virilisation (clitoromegaly, deepening voice, male-pattern balding over months) is never PCOS.**

⚑ LH:FSH ratio >2–3 is **no longer a diagnostic criterion** — it appears in old textbooks and is not in the guideline. Similarly, "string of pearls" is descriptive, not diagnostic, and the ovaries are not truly cystic (they are arrested antral follicles) — hence the ongoing debate about renaming the condition.

### Management — by what the woman wants

- **Lifestyle for all.** 5–10% weight loss restores ovulation in many; **no one dietary pattern is superior**; target metabolic and psychological health, not weight alone.
- **Cycle regulation / hyperandrogenism, not seeking pregnancy: COC is first-line**, no specific preparation recommended, **prefer lower-dose oestrogen** — 35 µg EE + cyproterone acetate is **second-line only** (VTE risk). Add **spironolactone** after ≥6 months if hirsutism persists, always with reliable contraception (feminisation of a male fetus).
- **Endometrial protection if oligo/amenorrhoeic:** COC, cyclical progestogen or LNG-IUD — a withdrawal bleed **at least every 3–4 months**.
- **Metformin:** consider **with lifestyle in adults with BMI ≥25** (max ~2.5 g/day; 2 g adolescents); weaker evidence at BMI <25; useful for adolescent cycle regulation where COC is unsuitable. **Not routinely recommended in pregnancy** — does not prevent GDM, miscarriage or hypertensive disease. **Inositol:** limited benefit, metformin superior; experimental for infertility.
- **Infertility: ⚑ letrozole is first-line for ovulation induction** — higher live-birth rate than clomiphene, especially with obesity. Then clomiphene ± metformin; gonadotrophins or laparoscopic ovarian drilling second-line; IVF third-line (**OHSS risk is highest in PCOS** — antagonist protocol, agonist trigger, freeze-all).
- **Pregnancy risks:** GDM, pre-eclampsia, preterm birth. Screen for GDM **early and again at 24–28 weeks**.

**The short version**
- Adults: 2 of 3 (hyperandrogenism, ovulatory dysfunction, PCOM **or AMH**). Adolescents: need both clinical criteria, **no imaging, no AMH**.
- ⚑ FNPO ≥20 (not 12); AMH is an alternative to ultrasound, never both, never alone, never in adolescents.
- COC first-line for cycle/androgens; **letrozole first-line for ovulation induction**.
- 75 g OGTT beats HbA1c; screen lipids at diagnosis, BP annually, mood in everyone, and consider OSA.

## 9.5 Endometriosis

Endometrial-like tissue outside the uterus; ~1 in 7–9 Australian women. Mean diagnostic delay historically 6–8 years — reducing it is the explicit aim of the Australian guideline.

**Clue → diagnosis.** **Cyclical pelvic pain that predates the period by days, deep dyspareunia, dysmenorrhoea unresponsive to NSAIDs + COC, dyschezia/dysuria that is cyclical, cyclical rectal bleeding or haematuria, chronic fatigue, and subfertility.** Examination may show a **fixed retroverted uterus, uterosacral nodularity, or a tender adnexal mass (endometrioma)** — but ⚑ **a normal examination does not exclude endometriosis**, and neither does a normal ultrasound.

**⚑ Diagnostic paradigm shift.** Laparoscopy is **no longer the required first step or the sole gold standard**. The Australian living evidence guideline (RANZCOG-endorsed) recommends:
1. **Symptom-based clinical diagnosis** and **empirical medical treatment** without waiting for surgical confirmation.
2. **Transvaginal ultrasound as first-line imaging** (specialist/advanced technique detects endometriomas and deep endometriosis); **MRI** where TVUS is inappropriate or deep disease is suspected.
3. **Laparoscopy reserved** for diagnostic uncertainty, failed medical therapy, or where surgical treatment is planned — **preferably after a treatment trial, even if imaging is normal.**

Staging (rASRM I–IV) correlates **poorly** with pain severity — a small superficial lesion can be agonising and a large endometrioma silent.

**Management.**
- **Hormonal suppression is first-line**: COC (continuous/extended), progestogens (**LNG-IUD**, oral, implant, DMPA). **Trial 3–6 months before switching.** Plus NSAIDs, pelvic floor physiotherapy, psychological input and multidisciplinary persistent-pain management — central sensitisation is real and undertreated.
- **Second-line: GnRH agonists/antagonists with add-back HRT** on specialist advice, limited by bone loss.
- **Surgery:** laparoscopic excision or ablation if medical therapy fails; counsel that **pain-relief evidence is limited, recurrence is common, and repeat surgery may be needed**. Endometrioma excision costs ovarian reserve.
- **Fertility:** ⚑ **do not offer hormonal treatment (alone or with surgery) to improve spontaneous conception — it does not work** and wastes fertile time. Refer for fertility assessment/ART.
- **Hysterectomy is not curative** — disease outside the uterus persists.

**Adenomyosis** — the confusable pair. **Endometriosis: nulliparous/younger, pain out of proportion, uterus normal-sized but fixed/nodular. Adenomyosis: multiparous, 35–50, heavy *and* painful periods, uterus diffusely bulky, globular and tender, "boggy".** Adenomyosis responds to LNG-IUD and is cured by hysterectomy; endometriosis is not.

## 9.6 Fibroids (uterine leiomyomas)

Benign monoclonal smooth-muscle tumours; oestrogen- and progesterone-dependent (grow in pregnancy, regress after menopause). Most common pelvic tumour; higher incidence and earlier onset in **women of African ancestry**. Often asymptomatic.

**FIGO subclassification (0–8) — position determines the symptom.**
- **0** pedunculated intracavitary; **1** submucosal <50% intramural; **2** submucosal ≥50% intramural — **these cause the bleeding and the fertility/implantation problems**.
- **3** intramural contacting endometrium; **4** intramural; **5** subserosal ≥50% intramural.
- **6** subserosal <50%; **7** subserosal pedunculated; **8** other (cervical, parasitic) — **these cause the bulk/pressure symptoms**.
- Hybrid lesions get two numbers (e.g. 2–5).

**Clue → diagnosis:** **HMB with a bulky, firm, irregular, non-tender uterus**, plus pressure symptoms — urinary frequency, retention or hydronephrosis, constipation, abdominal distension ("my clothes don't fit"), and iron-deficiency anaemia. **Pain is not typical** — acute pain suggests **red (carneous) degeneration in pregnancy** (constant pain, low-grade fever, localised tenderness — manage conservatively with analgesia) or **torsion of a pedunculated fibroid**.

**Investigations:** TVUS first; **saline infusion sonohysterography or hysteroscopy** for suspected submucosal lesions; MRI to map before myomectomy/UAE. **⚠ Rapid growth, especially postmenopausal, raises leiomyosarcoma** — rare (<1 in 300–1000) but the reason morcellation is now restricted and all tissue goes to histology.

**Management by goal.** **Asymptomatic → no treatment, regardless of size.** Bleeding with an undistorted cavity → **LNG-IUD**, tranexamic acid, NSAIDs, COC/progestogen. **Submucosal (FIGO 0–2) → hysteroscopic resection** — most effective for bleeding and the fertility-sparing option. **GnRH agonist ± add-back** for 3–6 months pre-op to shrink and correct anaemia (⚑ oral GnRH antagonist + add-back combinations now allow longer-term medical control). **UAE** preserves the uterus but is a **relative contraindication if future pregnancy is planned**. **Myomectomy** if fertility is desired (recurrence, adhesions, conversion risk). **Hysterectomy** is definitive.

## 9.7 Ovarian cysts and ovarian torsion

**Physiological cysts** (follicular, corpus luteum) dominate in premenopausal women; **most simple cysts resolve within 2–3 cycles**.

**RCOG Green-top 62 thresholds (widely used in Australian practice) for a *simple* cyst in a premenopausal woman:**
- **<50 mm — no follow-up required** (almost certainly physiological).
- **50–70 mm — yearly ultrasound surveillance.**
- **>70 mm — further imaging (MRI) or surgical assessment**, because they are hard to characterise completely on ultrasound and carry higher torsion risk.

**Risk assessment.** **RMI = U × M × CA-125**, where U = ultrasound score (0/1/3 for multilocularity, solid areas, bilaterality, ascites, intra-abdominal metastases) and M = 1 premenopausal, 3 postmenopausal. **RMI ≥200 → refer to a gynaecological oncology service.** IOTA simple rules/ADNEX models are increasingly used and out-perform RMI in specialist hands. ⚑ **In women under 40 with a complex ovarian mass, measure LDH, AFP and βhCG** — germ cell tumours, not epithelial cancer, are the concern at that age. CA-125 is unhelpful in simple cysts and is falsely raised by endometriosis, fibroids, PID, pregnancy, menstruation, and any peritoneal irritation.

**Clue → cyst type**

| Clue | Cyst |
|---|---|
| Thin-walled, anechoic, unilocular, <5 cm, resolves | **Follicular** |
| Cyst with "ring of fire" peripheral vascularity, luteal-phase pain | **Corpus luteum** (may rupture with haemoperitoneum) |
| **"Ground-glass"/homogeneous low-level echoes**, cyclical pain, dyspareunia, subfertility | **Endometrioma ("chocolate cyst")** |
| **Fat, hair, teeth, calcification; hyperechoic with acoustic shadowing**; young woman; commonest benign ovarian tumour of reproductive age | **Mature cystic teratoma (dermoid)** — highest torsion risk |
| Large, unilocular, thin-walled, can be enormous | **Serous or mucinous cystadenoma** (mucinous → pseudomyxoma peritonei if ruptured) |
| **Postmenopausal, solid components, septations, ascites, raised CA-125** | **Malignancy** until proven otherwise |

**Ovarian torsion — the emergency.** **Clue: sudden severe unilateral colicky lower abdominal pain with nausea and vomiting**, often after exertion or position change, in a woman with a **known cyst ≥5 cm** (dermoids and IVF/hyperstimulated ovaries are classic); intermittent pain suggests recurrent torsion/detorsion. Right side more often affected. Examination: localised adnexal tenderness ± mass; peritonism if necrotic; fever is late.

**βhCG first (exclude ectopic — the key differential)**, FBC, **TVUS with Doppler**. ⚑ **Preserved Doppler flow does NOT exclude torsion** — dual blood supply and intermittent twisting mean flow is often present. Most reliable finding is an enlarged oedematous ovary with peripherally displaced follicles ("whirlpool sign" of the twisted pedicle). **Management: urgent laparoscopy with detorsion and ovarian conservation**, even if the ovary looks dusky or black — ⚑ most recover function and oophorectomy is no longer the default; cystectomy at the same sitting or as an interval procedure. Differentials: ectopic, ruptured/haemorrhagic cyst, appendicitis, PID/TOA, renal colic.

**The short version**
- Simple cyst <5 cm premenopausal: leave it alone.
- RMI ≥200 → gynae-oncology; <40 with a complex mass → LDH/AFP/βhCG.
- Torsion: sudden severe unilateral pain + known cyst; **normal Doppler does not exclude it**; laparoscopic detorsion, conserve the ovary.

## 9.8 Amenorrhoea

**Definitions.** **Primary:** no menses by **age 15 with normal secondary sexual characteristics**, or **by age 13 with no secondary sexual characteristics**, or **>3 years after thelarche**. **Secondary:** cessation of previously established menses for **≥3 months if previously regular, or ≥6 months if previously irregular**. **Pregnancy is the commonest cause of both** — βhCG is always the first test.

### Investigation algorithm

**Step 1 — βhCG.** Then history (weight change, exercise, stress, galactorrhoea, headache/visual field defects, hot flushes, virilisation, drugs, chemo/radiotherapy, recent uterine instrumentation) and examination (BMI, Tanner staging, hirsutism, acanthosis nigricans, visual fields, thyroid, external genitalia).

**Step 2 — bloods: FSH, LH, oestradiol, prolactin, TSH** (+ free androgen index if hyperandrogenic; 17-OHP if CAH suspected).

**Step 3 — interpret the FSH:**

| Pattern | Category | Causes |
|---|---|---|
| **↑FSH, ↑LH, ↓oestradiol** | **Hypergonadotrophic hypogonadism** (ovarian failure) | **Premature ovarian insufficiency**; **Turner syndrome 45,X** (primary amenorrhoea, short stature, webbed neck, widely spaced nipples, coarctation/bicuspid aortic valve); gonadal dysgenesis; chemo/radiotherapy; autoimmune oophoritis; fragile X premutation (*FMR1*) |
| **↓/normal FSH, ↓LH, ↓oestradiol** | **Hypogonadotrophic hypogonadism** (hypothalamic–pituitary) | **Functional hypothalamic amenorrhoea** (low weight, excessive exercise, stress, eating disorder — the classic athlete/RED-S picture); **hyperprolactinaemia** (prolactinoma, antipsychotics, metoclopramide); **Kallmann syndrome (anosmia + delayed puberty)**; Sheehan syndrome (**postpartum haemorrhage → failure to lactate → amenorrhoea**); infiltrative/pituitary tumour; chronic illness |
| **Normal FSH/LH, ± ↑androgens, LH often ↑ relative to FSH** | **Eugonadotrophic / normogonadotrophic** | **PCOS** (commonest cause of secondary amenorrhoea after pregnancy), thyroid disease, hyperprolactinaemia |
| **Normal hormones, no withdrawal bleed on progestogen** | **Outflow tract** | **Asherman syndrome** (intrauterine adhesions after curettage/endometritis — clue: amenorrhoea *after* a D&C or after severe endometritis), cervical stenosis |

**Step 4 — imaging.** **Pelvic ultrasound** in all primary amenorrhoea to confirm **a uterus is present**. **Karyotype** if primary amenorrhoea with raised FSH, short stature, or absent uterus. **MRI pituitary** if prolactin is persistently raised or a hypogonadotrophic pattern comes with headache/visual symptoms. **DEXA** if prolonged hypo-oestrogenism.

**Primary amenorrhoea: the uterus + breasts grid** — the highest-yield discriminator in the whole topic.

| Breasts | Uterus | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| **Present** | **Absent** | **Müllerian agenesis (MRKH)** — 46,XX, **normal testosterone, normal pubic/axillary hair**, normal ovaries; vs **complete androgen insensitivity syndrome (CAIS)** — **46,XY, male-range testosterone, scant/absent pubic and axillary hair**, intra-abdominal testes (gonadectomy for malignancy risk) |
| **Present** | **Present** | Outflow obstruction (**imperforate hymen** — cyclical pain + bulging blue membrane + haematocolpos; transverse vaginal septum), PCOS, hyperprolactinaemia, pregnancy |
| **Absent** | **Present** | Hypogonadism — **Turner syndrome** (↑FSH), constitutional delay, **Kallmann** (↓FSH + anosmia), functional hypothalamic |
| **Absent** | **Absent** | Rare — 46,XY gonadal dysgenesis / disorders of sex development |

**Hook for the classic pair:** *No uterus + hair present = MRKH (46,XX). No uterus + no hair = CAIS (46,XY).*

**Premature ovarian insufficiency:** amenorrhoea/oligomenorrhoea **before age 40** with **FSH >25 IU/L on two occasions ≥4 weeks apart** plus low oestradiol. Screen for cause (karyotype, *FMR1* premutation, adrenal/thyroid autoantibodies). **Treat with HRT (or COC) until at least the average age of menopause (~51)** for bone and cardiovascular protection — not optional, and a common exam point. Spontaneous conception still occurs in ~5%, so contraception is still needed if pregnancy is unwanted.

**Management principles:** treat the cause. **Restore energy availability** in functional hypothalamic amenorrhoea (weight restoration, reduced training load, psychological input) — the COC is not the answer, and masking it with a withdrawal bleed does not protect bone. **Cabergoline** for prolactinoma; oestrogen replacement for prolonged hypo-oestrogenism; cyclical progestogen/COC for endometrial protection in PCOS; hysteroscopic adhesiolysis for Asherman; surgery for obstructive anomalies.
