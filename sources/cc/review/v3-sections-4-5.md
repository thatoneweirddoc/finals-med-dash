## HEADLINE ADJUDICATION — the "SSC 2026" claim

**The claim is TRUE. It is not fabricated authority.**

*Surviving Sepsis Campaign: international guidelines for management of sepsis and septic shock 2026* exists, is published in **Intensive Care Medicine vol. 52, pp. 1–73, online first 23 March 2026**, DOI 10.1007/s00134-026-08361-1 (Prescott HC, Antonelli M, Alhazzani W, Coopersmith CM et al., 69-member committee; SCCM/ESICM). ESICM's own announcement gives the release date as 23 March 2026. The adult guideline contains 129 statements, 46 of them new.

The specific antimicrobial-timing claim is also correct, verbatim:

- Probable/definite **septic shock** → "administering antimicrobial therapy immediately, ideally within 1 h" (**strong**, very low certainty)
- Probable/definite **sepsis without shock** → "administering antimicrobial therapy immediately, ideally within 1 h" (**strong**, very low certainty)
- **Possible sepsis without shock** → "a time-limited course of rapid investigation and if concern for infection persists, the administration of antimicrobial therapy within 3 h" (conditional)
- **Low likelihood of infection** → "deferring antimicrobial therapy while continuing to closely monitor" (conditional)

**And the ACSQHC cross-check confirms the section, not the previous library error.** The *Sepsis Clinical Care Standard* (ACSQHC, June 2022), Quality Statement 3, states verbatim: *"Start antimicrobials within 60 minutes of recognising **infection-related organ dysfunction**, and as soon as possible if shock is present."* The 60-minute clock is tied to **organ dysfunction**, not to shock. The section's ⚑ "Antibiotic timing is tied to sepsis/organ dysfunction, NOT to shock" is **correct and should be preserved verbatim**. ACSQHC was reachable (safetyandquality.gov.au served the full PDF), contrary to the blocked-site list.

Source: [Intensive Care Med 2026;52:1–73](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00134-026-08361-1); [ESICM release](https://www.esicm.org/2026-surviving-sepsis-campaing-guidelines/); [SSC 2026 full text PDF](https://www.stairs-sepsis.com/wp-content/uploads/Surviving-Sepsis-Campaign_international-guidelines-for-management-of-sepsis-and-septic-shock-2026_Intensive-Care-Med.pdf); [ACSQHC Sepsis CCS 2022](https://www.safetyandquality.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-06/sepsis_clinical_care_standard_2022.pdf)

## Verified correct

Checked against a retrieved primary source and found accurate. Do not change these.

**§4 — Sepsis**

- **SSC 2026 exists, dated 23 March 2026, Intensive Care Med vol. 52.** See headline adjudication. The source-list entry "Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2026. *Intensive Care Med*, online 23 March 2026 (SCCM/ESICM)" is exact.
- **The 1-hour tier applies to probable/definite sepsis with or without shock, strong recommendation.** Verbatim in SSC 2026.
- **ACSQHC ties the 60-minute target to infection-related organ dysfunction, not shock.** The section's ⚑ is correct and is the safest formulation in the library. The 3-hour outer tier for diagnostic uncertainty is also correct.
- **Sepsis-3 definitions.** "Life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection"; operational criterion acute SOFA rise ≥2; mortality ~10% for sepsis, "in excess of 40%" for septic shock. All exact.
- **Septic shock definition, exactly, and all five worked examples in the §4.3 table.** Sepsis + vasopressor required to maintain MAP ≥65 mmHg + lactate >2 mmol/L, both despite adequate fluid resuscitation. Every row's verdict is right, including the two traps the 2025 past paper turns on: vasopressor-dependent with lactate 1.6 is **not** septic shock, and a patient on noradrenaline at MAP 58 **is** in septic shock and below target.
- **qSOFA components and cut-offs** (RR ≥22, GCS <15, SBP ≤100) and its status as prognostic rather than a screen.
- **SSC 2021 and 2026 both strongly recommend against qSOFA as a single screening tool, preferring NEWS/NEWS2/MEWS/SIRS.** SSC 2026 verbatim: "we recommend using NEWS, NEWS2, MEWS, or SIRS over qSOFA as a single tool to screen for sepsis" (strong).
- **ACSQHC recommends LqSOFA, NEWS or MEWS**, and the SIRS divergence from SSC is real (see Finding 11 for the one wording fix).
- **NEWS2** — RCP UK, December 2017, +2 for supplemental oxygen, thresholds 1–4 / 3-in-one-parameter / 5–6 / ≥7. Correctly labelled a UK instrument.
- **ACSQHC seven quality statements**, in the order and with the titles given, including statements 5–7 (education, transitions, survivorship). Blood cultures: two sets in adults, one set in children — verbatim.
- **SA Health Adult Sepsis Pathway (August 2025):** screening off the RDR chart plus "could it be sepsis?"; escalation to senior clinician or MER call; bundle "Oxygen, Blood Cultures, IV antibiotics & IV fluids within 60mins"; applies to adults ≥16 years, not pregnant/postpartum, not neutropenic. All exact. NSW pathway ≥16 years confirmed.
- **30 mL/kg crystalloid in the first 3 h is a *conditional*, low-certainty SSC 2026 recommendation.** Verbatim. Balanced crystalloid over 0.9% saline is conditional, moderate certainty.
- **PLUS (NEJM 2022):** n=5037, 90-day mortality 21.8% vs 22.0%.
- **ARISE FLUIDS outcome figures** (see Finding 8 — only the site description needs fixing).
- **MAP ≥65 strong/moderate certainty, and the SSC 2026 conditional suggestion of an initial MAP range 60–65 mmHg in adults aged ≥65.** Verbatim: "an initial MAP range of 60–65 mmHg over higher ranges (conditional recommendation, low certainty)". The §4.8 handling — that this lowers the floor to 60 but does not make 58 acceptable, and that Australian pathways have not adopted a geriatric target — is sound.
- **Noradrenaline first line over dopamine, adrenaline or selepressin (strong); add vasopressin on escalating noradrenaline (conditional); add adrenaline third (conditional); suggest against terlipressin (conditional) and beta-blockers (conditional); insufficient evidence for methylene blue and midodrine.** All verbatim.
- **Peripheral initiation of vasopressors before central access — SSC 2026 conditional.** Verbatim.
- **Source control early over late, ideally within 6 h — conditional.** Verbatim.
- **Corticosteroids: SSC 2026 "For adults with septic shock, we suggest using IV corticosteroids" (conditional, low certainty)** — and correctly restricted to shock, not sepsis without shock.
- **ADRENAL outcome figures and regimen** (see Finding 9 for the enrolment number).
- **LOVIT (NEJM 2022):** death or persistent organ dysfunction at day 28 44.5% (191/429) vs 38.5% (167/434), RR 1.21 (1.04–1.40), P=0.01 — and **SSC 2026 "we suggest against using IV vitamin C"** (conditional, low certainty). **SSC 2026 also suggests against vitamin D** (conditional, very low certainty). Both confirmed.
- **Lactate: SSC 2026 makes both "measuring blood lactate" and "using serial lactate measurements to guide resuscitation" conditional, low-certainty.** Verbatim, exactly as the section's ⚑ states.
- **De-escalation strong when susceptibilities are available; conditional when no pathogen is identified on final cultures.** Both verbatim — including the "negative cultures are a reason to narrow" point.
- **Procalcitonin asymmetry.** Verbatim both ways: "we suggest using clinical evaluation alone over procalcitonin plus clinical evaluation to decide whether to **start**"; and "where optimal duration of therapy is unclear, we suggest using procalcitonin AND clinical evaluation to decide when to **discontinue**". **Shorter over longer duration** is a conditional SSC 2026 recommendation. The §4.11 handling is exactly right.
- **Febrile neutropenia, Australian adult definition (WACHS, 1 November 2021):** "Temperature of at least 38.3°C (or at least 38°C on two occasions) and neutrophil count of less than 0.5 x 10⁹ cells/L" — the section's version is exact. Time targets ("delay of more than 1 hour is associated with inferior outcomes"; 30 minutes for a ward patient with signs of sepsis) confirmed. Empirical piperacillin–tazobactam 4 g/500 mg IV 6-hourly confirmed; severe allergy vancomycin load + **ciprofloxacin 400 mg IV 12-hourly** confirmed — the 12-hourly frequency in this row is right, and is genuinely different from the 8-hourly used elsewhere in the section for other indications.

- **TSANZ target saturations, and the edition.** The current Australian authority is the **TSANZ Position Statement on Acute Oxygen Use in Adults, Respirology 2022** (Barnett et al.), which is correctly cited in the section's source list. Verbatim: *"88%–92% is the recommended target range (i.e. administer O2 when SpO2 < 88%) in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, morbid obesity, obesity hypoventilation syndrome, bronchiectasis, cystic fibrosis, neuromuscular disease and chest wall deformities such as severe kyphoscoliosis"* and *"92%–96% is the recommended target range in other clinical situations (i.e. administer O2 when SpO2 < 92%)"*. The section's at-risk group list matches the guideline's list. "Start oxygen below the lower bound" is exactly the guideline's parenthetical. **BTS 2017 confirmed at 94–98% and 88–92%.** The only defect is the over-reaching "only genuine split" claim — Finding 2.
- **NIV trigger thresholds, and the flagged COPD-X vs ERS/ATS divergence — the divergence is real and both sides are stated correctly.** COPD-X Handbook defines hypercapnic respiratory failure requiring NIV as *"PaCO2 > 45 mmHg and a pH < 7.35 (respiratory acidosis)"*; ERS/ATS 2017 states *"We recommend bilevel NIV for patients with ARF leading to acute or acute-on-chronic respiratory acidosis (pH ⩽7.35) due to COPD exacerbation"* and, for implementation, *"pH is ⩽7.35, PaCO2 is >45 mmHg and the respiratory rate is >20–24 breaths·min⁻¹ despite standard medical therapy"*. So COPD-X `<7.35` vs ERS/ATS `≤7.35` is genuine, and the section's rendering of the PaCO₂ side (>45 mmHg in both) is right.
- **§5.12 exam stem 2 (pH 7.26, PaCO₂ 68, PaO₂ 58 on 28%, alert) — the answer "start bilevel NIV" is correct**, as is every element of the reasoning: threshold met on both pH and PaCO₂; alert and protecting the airway so no immediate intubation; do not increase FiO₂; ABG at 1 hour; pH <7.26 marks the higher-risk band so pre-plan escalation. The ERS/ATS recommendations quoted for cardiogenic pulmonary oedema (either bilevel or CPAP, strong), de novo hypoxaemic failure (no recommendation possible), asthma (no recommendation possible), prevention vs treatment of post-extubation failure — all verbatim correct.

The §5.9 exam stem 3 answer is right, including the explicit exclusion of pneumothorax, pulmonary oedema and ARDS progression (which raise plateau too), and the correct action sequence. - **§5.12 88–92% in AECOPD, COPD-X and TSANZ agreeing.** COPD-X Handbook verbatim: "target SpO2 88% to 92%".
- **Cross-conflict 6 — MAP targets. This is NOT an inconsistency; leave all three as they are.** ANZCOR Guideline 11.7 states verbatim an "initial target mean arterial blood pressure (MAP) of at least 60 to 65 mm Hg, or a systolic pressure greater than 100 mmHg" **post-ROSC** — a genuinely different recommendation from the septic shock target, for a different physiology (myocardial stunning, cerebral autoregulation). §3.5 and §4.8's ≥65 is the SSC septic shock target (strong, moderate certainty). §6.12's "SBP ≥90 mmHg or MAP ≥60 mmHg" is a **precondition for brain-death determination**, not a resuscitation target — a third, unrelated use of a blood pressure number. Three different questions, three correct answers.
