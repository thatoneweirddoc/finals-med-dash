## HEADLINE ADJUDICATIONS (requested)

### 1. Prothrombinex-VF vs Beriplex — **§3.14 IS CORRECT. THE SURGERY NOTES ARE WRONG AND MUST BE CHANGED.**

Established against four independent Australian sources:

- **TGA/NSW Health**: "Prothrombinex VF contains three coagulation factors (II, IX, X)", both Beriplex variants contain "four factors (II, VII, IX, X)"; the products are "NOT interchangeable"; dose weight "should not exceed 100 kg". (NSW Health Safety Notice SN-017, 1 July 2024, https://www.health.nsw.gov.au/sabs/Documents/2024-sn-017.pdf)
- **National Blood Authority**: Prothrombinex-VF supply continued "until around June 2024", then Beriplex P/N (imported), then Beriplex AU. (https://www.blood.gov.au/update-transition-prothrombinex-vf)
- **THANZ**: "Australia's prothrombin complex concentrate was replaced mid-2024 with 4-factor PCC (Factors II, VII, IX, X) BERIPLEX P/N"; from February 2025 transition to BERIPLEX AU, the earlier formulation discontinued. (https://www.thanz.org.au/use-of-the-new-4-factor-pcc-beriplex/)
- **THANZ/MJA position statement (Robinson et al., MJA 2025;222(1))**: "Prothrombinex-VF … contains factors II, IX and X, and only small amounts of factor VII"; and decisively — **"4FPCC has higher factor VII concentrations and, therefore, co-administration with FFP is no longer required."** (https://www.mja.com.au/system/files/issues/222_01/mja252538.pdf)
- **Lifeblood**: "When Beriplex® is used in life-threatening warfarin-related bleeding (in conjunction with IV Vitamin K), FFP is not required for reversal of the warfarin effect." (https://www.lifeblood.com.au/health-professionals/clinical-practice/clinical-indications/warfarin-reversal)

**Verdict:** Prothrombinex-VF *was* 3-factor (that part of the Surgery note is historically right), but it is **no longer the Australian product** and **FFP alongside PCC is no longer recommended for warfarin reversal**. Any Surgery-notes statement that the current Australian position is "Prothrombinex-VF 3-factor given with FFP for factor VII" is **superseded and dangerous** (it would lead a candidate to give an unnecessary plasma volume load, and to name a product no longer stocked). **Act on this: correct the Surgery notes to Beriplex 4-factor PCC, FFP not required.** §3.14's "Exam safety note" hedge (state both positions, name the product in front of you) is the correct framing and should be carried across.

Doses in §3.14 verified exactly: 50 IU/kg for life-threatening/critical-organ bleeding at INR ≥1.5; 25–50 IU/kg for clinically significant non-life-threatening bleeding at INR ≥2.0; vitamin K 5–10 mg IV in both; weight capped at 100 kg (max 5,000 IU).

### 2. Andexanet alfa ARTG removal — **VERIFIED TRUE, 20 May 2026.**

TGA news article "Lapse of provisional registration for Andexxa (andexanet alfa)"; provisional registration lapsed because the sponsor "is unable to complete the required confirmatory trials within the legislated timeframe", and "available evidence was not sufficient to confirm the benefits of ANDEXXA outweigh the risks". Removal date **20 May 2026** confirmed. NSW CEC separately confirms the NSW Medicines Formulary Committee declined to list it — "no clear advantage … in terms of effectiveness, safety or cost-effectiveness … compared to usual care" — citing **10.3% vs 5.6%** thrombotic events within 30 days in acute ICH. **The Australian factor Xa inhibitor reversal option is therefore PCC-based (Beriplex), plus stop-the-drug / time-of-last-dose / renal function / surgical haemostasis / charcoal if recent ingestion.** §3.14 and the Rapid-Recall row are correct.
Sources: https://www.tga.gov.au/news/news-articles/lapse-provisional-registration-andexxa-andexanet-alfa-used-factor-xa-inhibitor-apixabanrivaroxaban-reversal ; https://cec.health.nsw.gov.au/therapeutics-safety/resources/andexanet-alfa

### 3. Maximum peripheral IV potassium rate — **§6.9 IS RIGHT; §3.7 IS WRONG.**

Queensland Health *Prescribing guidelines for HYPO-electrolyte disturbances in adults* (SW1232 v7.00, 10/2023) states verbatim:
- "Maximum CONCENTRATION peripherally = 40 mmol/L to prevent phlebitis."
- "If potassium level above 2.5 mmol/L = 10 mmol/hr"; "With burette = 10 mmol/hr"; "**If potassium level below 2.5 mmol/L and with infusion pump = 20 mmol/hr**".
- Central access is required only where the **concentration** exceeds 40 mmol/L, or where "maximum rates or concentration are exceeded".
- "Plasma levels below 3.0 mmol/L may result in arrhythmias. Consider cardiac monitoring."

Queensland Health *Management of diabetic ketoacidosis in adults (16 years and over)* states: "With burette = 10 mmol/hr" and "**With infusion pump = 20 mmol/hr**"; central access ("CVC, venous access port, PICC") plus cardiac monitoring "in a high acuity bed" is required only "**if maximum rates or concentration are exceeded**".

**Australian position:** 10 mmol/h is the ceiling with a burette; **20 mmol/h is permitted peripherally with a volumetric infusion pump** (in the general electrolyte guideline, restricted to K⁺ <2.5 mmol/L; in the DKA guideline, not so restricted), at a peripheral concentration ≤40 mmol/L. **Central access is not the trigger — the pump is.** The DKA exception in §6.9 is real. §3.7's "with central access" is an invented requirement.
Sources: https://www.health.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0027/1006587/electrolyte-prescribe-gline-adult.pdf ; https://www.health.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0019/621640/diabetic-ketoacidosis.pdf

## Verified correct

Checked against a live source and found accurate — do not change:

**Anticoagulant reversal (§3.14)** — Prothrombinex-VF 3-factor (II, IX, X, minimal VII); Beriplex 4-factor (II, VII, IX, X) from June 2024, Beriplex AU from February 2025; FFP no longer required; 50 IU/kg for life-threatening bleed at INR ≥1.5 + vitamin K 5–10 mg IV; 25–50 IU/kg for significant non-life-threatening bleed at INR ≥2.0; 100 kg weight cap = max 5,000 IU; recheck INR at 12–24 h. Andexanet ARTG removal 20 May 2026; NSW CEC thrombotic figures 10.3% vs 5.6%; PCC-based Australian alternative. Idarucizumab 5 g as two 2.5 g/50 mL infusions ≤15 min apart, effect may re-emerge up to 24 h, repeat dose may be needed, no renal dose adjustment recommended (Australian Prescriber). PATCH — platelet transfusion worse in antiplatelet-associated spontaneous ICH.

**Transfusion (§3.11)** — NBA Module 4 R1 "restrictive transfusion strategy should be employed (Grade B)"; PP3 bands <70 / 70–90 / >90 g/L verbatim; PP2 single unit then reassess; PP5 routine FFP not advised; PP7 INR ≤2 may not benefit; PP11 platelets <20 × 10⁹/L; PP12 ≥50 × 10⁹/L for invasive procedures. The Hb 68 g/L stem answer (transfuse one unit, reassess) is correct, as is the list of exceptions (active bleeding, ACS, cardiac surgery per Module 2, neurocritical care unquantified). TRISS 2014 n = 1,005 septic shock, 70 vs 90 g/L, no difference. TRICC lineage correct.

**Critical bleeding (§3.12)** — NBA/THANZ MJA 2024;220(4) replaces Module 1 (2011); ratio "no lower than 2:1:1" = 8 RBC : 4 FFP : 1 adult platelet dose; meta-analysis showed no mortality effect of 1:1:1 vs 2:1:1; fibrinogen critical derangement <2.0 g/L; replacement "3–4 g of fibrinogen which may be achieved using FgC or cryoprecipitate"; TXA early, within 3 h of injury (trauma) or of onset of bleeding (obstetric).
