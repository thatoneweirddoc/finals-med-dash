```
QUOTE: The classic "400-fold increase" is an overestimate — pooled data give ~**1.2% cardiac anomalies** (OR ~2), **dose-dependent** (>900 mg/day roughly triples risk vs ≤600 mg/day).
FILE + HEADING: 02-mood-disorders.md — §2.6 Management of bipolar disorder → "Lithium… *Pregnancy*"
CORRECTION: The classic "400-fold increase" is a gross overestimate. SOURCE: Patorno E et al., *Lithium Use in Pregnancy and the Risk of Cardiac Malformations*, N Engl J Med 2017;376:2245–54, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28591541/ ; Fornaro M et al., Am J Psychiatry 2020;177:76–92, https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2019.19030228
SEVERITY: dangerous
```

## Cross-section adjudications (brief items 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 9, 11)

**1 — TFT at first presentation. §1.5 is CORRECT; the "2026 ANZ" claim is real.**
Warren N, O'Gorman C, Dark F, et al., *Initial physical health assessment for psychosis in Australia and New Zealand: 2026 recommendations*, ANZJP 2026, doi:10.1177/00048674261435740, states verbatim: *"Testing thyroid function is therefore only recommended when history and examination indicate clinical concern (prominent mood/anxiety, goitre, unexplained change in weight, heat sensitivity, sleep, menstrual cycle, hair/skin or heart rate)"* and *"universal thyroid function testing is not supported currently in routine care."* Its recommended baseline panel is HbA1c/glucose, lipids, U&E, LFT, FBC, **prolactin**, ECG, urine drug screen, targeted infectious screen, βhCG.
**However the recommendation is scoped to first-presentation psychosis, not to all psychiatric presentations.** §2.2 (depression workup), §3.1 (anxiety) and §4.4 must be reconciled as follows: TSH stays in the **depression** baseline panel (hypothyroidism is the classic depressive mimic and no Australian source has removed it there); TSH comes **out** of the routine **first-episode psychosis** panel and becomes targeted; and TSH is **mandatory** before and during lithium regardless. Add the scoping sentence to §1.5 so the apparent conflict is resolved on the page. Two further gaps: §1.5's routine table **omits prolactin**, which Warren 2026 includes at baseline and at 3 months then annually; and the ⚑ should name the paper, since "2026 ANZ recommendations" unattributed reads like an invention.

**2 — Antipsychotic metabolic monitoring. §1.7 is closest to correct; §4.5 and §7.2 are both wrong.**
The Australian standard is the Adult Positive Cardiometabolic Health Resource (Curtis/Lester algorithm, Mindgardens/Equally Well, 2023 ed.): **weight weekly-to-fortnightly for the first 6–8 weeks, then 3, 6, 9, 12 months, then 6-monthly**; and **waist, BP, glucose/HbA1c and lipids at baseline, 3, 6, 9, 12 months, then 6-monthly**. Fix all three sections to this single schedule. §4.5's "baseline, ~6 weeks, 3 months, then at least annually" and §7.2's "baseline, 3 months, then annually" are both under-monitoring relative to the Australian resource and should be deleted, not reconciled. (Warren 2026 separately adds prolactin at baseline, 3 months, then annually.)

**4 — Maximum escitalopram dose. §2.3 and §7.2 are CORRECT; §3.6 is wrong.**
The Australian PI caps escitalopram at **20 mg/day** for major depression **and for OCD**, with **10 mg/day the maximum maintenance dose over 65**. §3.6's "escitalopram 10–40 mg" in OCD exceeds the registered maximum, and its framing of citalopram as "the exception to go higher" is wrong — escitalopram carries the same dose-dependent QT signal and the same class of cap. Off-label high-dose escitalopram in OCD is not correctly framed anywhere in the notes; if it is retained it must be labelled explicitly as off-label, specialist-initiated, with ECG monitoring. See the DANGEROUS block above.

**6 — Discontinuation syndrome duration. Both §2.3 and §7.2 are wrong; neither figure is defensible.**
Australian Prescriber (Aust Prescr 2026;49:99–103) gives onset **"within a day or 2"** and duration as **conditional, not fixed**: mild and brief after short-term (6–12 weeks) therapy, but "severe and protracted" after longer treatment. Delete "1–2 weeks" (§2.3) and "1–3 weeks" (§7.2) and use duration-agnostic wording. The examinable discriminator from relapse is **onset within days + physical/neurological symptom profile + resolution within hours of reinstatement**, not duration.

**8 — Lithium target level. Not a genuine contradiction; §2.6's ⚑ is honest and §2.6 stands.**
§2.4's 0.5–0.8 is for **antidepressant augmentation** and §2.6's 0.6–0.8 is for **bipolar maintenance** — different indications. Both cited Australian sources exist and do disagree, exactly as the ⚑ claims: Australian Prescriber gives maintenance **0.6–0.8**, acute mania **0.6–1.0**, depression **0.4–0.8**; WA Country Health Service (2023) gives acute mania **0.8–1.2**, maintenance **0.4–1.0**, elderly **0.4–0.6**. §2.6's synthesised "safe answer" (maintenance ~0.6–0.8, mania up to ~1.0–1.2, elderly 0.4–0.6) is defensible against both. Only change needed: §2.4's augmentation target should read **0.4–0.8** to match the Australian Prescriber figure for lithium in depression.

**9 — Atypical age of onset. §1.5 (<14 or >40) is CORRECT; §4.4 (<15 or >40) is wrong.**
Warren 2026 states the thresholds verbatim as *"childhood (<14 years) or older age (>40 years) onset of symptoms"*, and separately that "a workup … should occur for anyone presenting for the first time over the age of 40 years". Change §4.4 to **<14 or >40**.

**11 — QTc rise threshold. §4.5 (>60 ms) is the conventional form; §1.5 (≥60 ms) should change.** See the STYLISTIC block above. Neither is sourced to an Australian authority; Australian Prescriber in fact argues against fixed QTc thresholds in favour of the QT nomogram.

**§2.5 / §2.6**
