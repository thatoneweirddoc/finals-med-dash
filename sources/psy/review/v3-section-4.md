## ADJUDICATIONS REQUESTED

**1. TFT at first presentation — §1.5 is CORRECT; this section's §4.4 is wrong.**
The "2026 ANZ" claim is **real**: Warren N, O'Gorman C, Dark F, Every-Palmer S, … Siskind D, *Initial physical health assessment for psychosis in Australia and New Zealand: 2026 recommendations*, ANZJP 2026, doi:10.1177/00048674261435740. It explicitly moves thyroid function testing to targeted use — indicated only "when history and examination indicate clinical concern (prominent mood/anxiety, goitre, unexplained change in weight, heat sensitivity, sleep, menstrual cycle, hair/skin or heart rate)". CRP/ESR ("nonspecific, insensitive markers") and B12/folate are likewise targeted, and HIV/syphilis serology is opportunistic and risk-based, not universal. Fix §4.4 per Finding 3.

**2. Antipsychotic metabolic monitoring — §1.7 is CORRECT (Australian standard); §4.5 and §7.2 are both wrong.**
The Australian standard is the *Positive Cardiometabolic Health Resource* (Curtis/Newall/Samaras; 2023 adult update): baseline full panel → weight weekly-to-fortnightly for the first 6–8 weeks after initiation or change → full panel at 3 months and 6 months → weight at 9 and 12 months → all parameters 6-monthly thereafter. §4.5's "baseline / ~6 weeks / 3 months / annually" is the **INTEGRATE 2025 international** schedule (weekly BMI, waist and BP for 6 weeks, full panel at 3 months then annually) — a type-A import. §7.2's "baseline, 3 months, then annually" is the thinnest of the three and should be replaced with §1.7's wording.

**3. NMS temperature — NEITHER side is right; §4.7 is closer.**
The consensus diagnostic threshold is **>38.0 °C on at least two occasions, measured orally** (Gurrera international Delphi consensus 2011, carried into DSM-5-TR). §7.2's ">40 °C" is the dangerous one — it would exclude NMS in most real cases; correct it to ">38.0 °C on ≥2 occasions, often much higher". §4.7's ">38.5 °C" is also above the criterion and should be corrected per Finding 5.

**9. Atypical age of onset — NEITHER; use the ANZ 2026 framing.**
Neither <14 (§1.5) nor <15 (§4.4) is what the Australian source says. Warren et al. 2026 flags **childhood onset ≤12 years** (up to 12.5% organic pathology) and **first presentation >40 years** (work up for delirium and dementia). Harmonise both sections to ≤12 / >40. See Finding 13.

**10. Cannabis-associated psychosis — §4.3 is wrong and self-contradictory; §5.8 is roughly right.**
Actual figures: **Moore 2007** (Lancet meta-analysis) — ever use adjusted OR **1.41** (1.20–1.65), i.e. ~40% increase, *not* a doubling; most frequent use OR **2.09** (1.54–2.84). **Di Forti 2019** (EU-GEI) — daily use OR **~3.2**; daily high-potency (THC ≥10%) use OR **~4.8** (~5-fold). So §5.8's "heavy use roughly doubles-to-quadruples risk" is defensible; §4.3's "any use roughly doubles risk (OR ~1.4)" fuses the wrong verb to the right number. Fix per Finding 1.

**11. QTc rise threshold — §4.5 is CORRECT.**
The convention is an **increase of >60 ms from baseline** (and an absolute QTc >500 ms) prompting dose reduction, switch or cessation. §1.5's "≥60 ms" should be changed to ">60 ms".

## Verified correct

Checked against live Australian sources and found accurate; do not change:

- **Clozapine haematological monitoring schedule and the traffic-light table.** FBC before starting, weekly for 18 weeks, then ≥4-weekly for the duration. Green WCC ≥3.5 **and** ANC ≥2.0 → continue; amber WCC 3.0–<3.5 and/or ANC 1.5–<2.0 → continue with twice-weekly FBC until green; red WCC <3.0 and/or ANC <1.5 → cease/withhold, haematology. (WA Health clozapine guideline; SA Health *Clozapine Management Clinical Guideline* v2.0 2017; Australian Prescriber *Clozapine in primary care*.) The Rapid-Recall rows for both the schedule and the ANC cut-off match the prose.
- **The Delphi/EMA caveat is correctly handled.** The note that the 2025 *Lancet Psychiatry* global Delphi consensus (Northwood, Siskind et al.) and the EMA's 2025 revision are **not** Australian practice, and that Australian requirements remain as tabulated, is exactly the right separation — this is the single most-often-botched item in this topic and it is done correctly. (EMA DHPC 2025; WA/SA Health schedules unchanged.)
- **Clozapine myocarditis monitoring.** Baseline troponin, CRP and echocardiogram, then troponin and CRP at days 7, 14, 21 and 28 with ECG and clinical review; cease and refer to cardiology if troponin >2× ULN **or** CRP >100 mg/L; peak incidence in the first 3–4 weeks. This is the Australian-specific requirement and it is stated accurately. (Ronaldson KJ et al., ANZJP 2011; WA Health; SA Health.)
- **Clozapine constipation as the leading cause of death.** Clozapine-related GI hypomotility carries higher mortality than agranulocytosis; ~60% affected; blunted visceral sensation; prophylactic aperients; treat as ileus/toxic megacolon until excluded. (Medsafe NZ *Prescriber Update*, "Clozapine: fatal 'constipation' more common than fatal agranulocytosis"; Every-Palmer et al.)
- **Clozapine maximum dose 900 mg/day** (TGA-endorsed, per SA Health), inpatient start 12.5 mg day 1, plasma level ~350–600 ng/mL for response with seizure risk above ~1000 ng/mL, and the smoking/CYP1A2 warning including forced abstinence on admission. (SA Health; Australian Prescriber.)
- **Agranulocytosis = ANC <0.5**, ~0.8% incidence, highest in the first 18 weeks (SA Health: 85% of cases in the first 18 weeks); fever or sore throat → urgent FBC regardless of schedule; red result → non-rechallengeable registration; benign ethnic neutropenia as the legitimate reason for individualised thresholds agreed with haematology.
- **Clozapine seizure risk ~3–5% cumulative at one year, dose- and level-dependent.** (Devinsky et al., Neurology 1991 — ~5% at 1 year, 10% at 3.8 years, dose-stratified 1%/2.7%/4.4%.)
- **Acute dystonia management.** Benztropine 1–2 mg IM/IV, response within ~5 minutes and symptom-free by 15, repeatable; promethazine 25–50 mg IV/IM as alternative; then 2–3 days of oral benztropine 1–2 mg bd. (Australian Prescriber, *The management of acute dystonic reactions*.) The "response within minutes is diagnostic" framing is sound.
- **EPS time courses** — acute dystonia 50% <48 h and 90% <5 days; akathisia within ~4 weeks of start or dose rise; parkinsonism over weeks to months; TD after >3 months exposure (>1 month in the elderly), persisting after withdrawal. Consistent with Australian Prescriber *Drug-induced movement disorders*.
- **Anticholinergics worsen TD and do not treat akathisia; clozapine has the best evidence for TD.** Australian Prescriber confirms clozapine is preferred in psychotic disorders with TD, and that there is no evidence for prophylactic anticholinergics.
- **QTc >500 ms or a rise >60 ms → stop/switch, correct K⁺/Mg²⁺**, with haloperidol (esp. IV), droperidol, ziprasidone, pimozide and amisulpride as the high-risk agents.
- **NMS mortality now under 10%, resolution over 1–2 weeks (longer after a depot), rechallenge ≥2 weeks after full resolution with a lower-potency/second-generation agent at low dose.** All confirmed.
- **NMS vs serotonin syndrome vs malignant catatonia discriminators** — onset over days vs hours vs days-on-catatonic-background; lead-pipe rigidity vs lower-limb clonus and hyperreflexia vs waxy flexibility/posturing; hyperreflexia as the best single discriminator; mydriasis and hyperactive bowel sounds in serotonin syndrome; cyproheptadine for severe serotonin syndrome; lorazepam then ECT for malignant catatonia with antipsychotics contraindicated. The Rapid-Recall rows match the §4.7 table on every one of these.
- **Catatonia**: ≥3 signs, Bush–Francis as the standard screen, lorazepam challenge 1–2 mg (0.5–1 mg elderly/frail) with scheduled dosing to 6–16 mg/day, ECT first-line without delay in malignant catatonia, response ~80–90%, antipsychotics can precipitate or worsen it.
- **Duration-of-untreated-psychosis evidence claim.** Longer DUP predicts poorer remission, symptom severity, social functioning and global outcome — supported by Penttilä M et al., *Duration of untreated psychosis as predictor of long-term outcome in schizophrenia: systematic review and meta-analysis*, Br J Psychiatry 2014;205:88–94. The hedge that neurotoxicity itself is contested is appropriate.
- **headspace** (national youth centres, 12–25, free/low-cost, no referral barrier) and the **EPPIC model** (2–5 years specialised early psychosis care, vocational recovery a core outcome, gains attenuate on early discharge to standard care). Consistent with Orygen/headspace material.
- **LAIs should be discussed early including at first episode** — endorsed by both INTEGRATE 2025 ("discuss long-acting injectable formulations early") and the ANZJP 2026 GRADE guidelines.
- **Duration criteria (type E check).** Schizophrenia ≥6 months total with ≥1 month active-phase; schizophreniform 1–6 months, functional decline not required; brief psychotic disorder ≥1 day to <1 month with full return to premorbid function; delusional disorder ≥1 month with function preserved outside the delusional theme; schizoaffective requiring ≥2 weeks of psychosis in the absence of a major mood episode plus mood episodes for the majority of total illness duration; substance-induced resolving within ~1 month of abstinence. All four Rapid-Recall duration rows match the §4.2 table exactly. No slippage found.
- **Neuroimaging is not routine at first episode; MRI beats CT; EEG and LP/autoimmune antibodies only when indicated.** Aligns with Warren et al. 2026 ("for individuals with a first episode psychosis and no atypical features, neuroimaging is less clearly indicated"; EEG not to be requested without clinical indication; autoimmune antibodies not universal).
- **Zuclopenthixol acetate (Acuphase) is short-acting (2–3 days) and not a maintenance depot** — correct, and correctly flagged as a trap.
- **Arithmetic (type G).** Prognosis 20% / 50% / 20–30% sums to 90–100% — loose but not erroneous.
