## Cross-section adjudications

### 4. Maximum escitalopram dose — §7.2 and §2.3 are right; §3.6 is wrong as framed

**TGA-approved PI: escitalopram maximum 20 mg/day in adults, 10 mg/day maximum maintenance if >65 years.** So §7.2 ("caps at 20 mg/day, 10 mg if >65") is exactly the label, and §2.3 ("10–20 mg") is the correct clinical range.

§3.6's "escitalopram 10–40 mg" is a verbatim lift from Australian Prescriber's OCD article (Brakoulias 2015), so it is not invented — but it is **above the TGA maximum and off-label**, and §3.6 does not say so. It is framed **unsafely**: the very next sentence nominates citalopram as "*the* exception to go higher", which tells the reader that escitalopram is one of the SSRIs you *can* push. The Lexapro PI documents dose-dependent QTc prolongation with escitalopram (4.3 msec at 10 mg, 10.7 msec at 30 mg — 30 mg was the highest dose ever studied), and escitalopram is the active S-enantiomer at roughly twice the per-mg potency of citalopram, so 40 mg escitalopram sits well beyond the citalopram ceiling the note is warning about. **Fix: state that escitalopram is capped at 20 mg (10 mg >65) alongside citalopram, and label the 10–40 mg OCD range explicitly as off-label, specialist-supervised, ECG-monitored.** See Finding 1.

### 5. Benzodiazepine taper rate — §3.6 is closer; §5.6's "10%" is not the Australian figure

No Australian source gives a single mandated rate. RACGP explicitly says "clear evidence for the optimal rate of tapering is lacking… individualise", bounding the whole taper at a minimum of 6 weeks (BNF) to a maximum of 6 months (Lader). The commonly quoted Australian operational figures are:

- Primary Health Tasmania: "a dose reduction of **25% every 1 to 2 weeks**"; "majority will tolerate tapering by 15–20% per step over 6–8 weeks"
- NSW TAG: "wean gradually by **25% of the daily dose every 1–4 weeks**", slowing to **~12.5%** for the final step
- Primary Health Tasmania notes the **10–20% every 2 weeks** figure is explicitly attributed to **UK guidelines**

**Verdict: §3.6's "~10–25% every 1–2 weeks with slower final steps" is defensible and brackets the Australian range. §5.6's "~10% of current dose every 1–2 weeks" is the UK/hyperbolic-taper figure presented as Australian (taxonomy A) and should be reconciled upward.** Best single line for both sections: *"No evidence-based optimum — individualise. A common Australian approach is ~25% of the daily dose every 1–2 weeks, slowing to ~10–12.5% for the final steps; total taper typically 6 weeks to 6 months."* Separately, both sections should drop the routine diazepam switch (Finding 7).

### 6. Antidepressant discontinuation syndrome duration — neither §2.3 nor §7.2 is currently right

Section 3 itself only says "discontinuation symptoms" in the SNRI row, so it makes no claim to adjudicate — but the two sides break down as:

- **Onset:** the Australian source says symptoms "may start within 1–10 days, but usually within three days of stopping treatment." §7.2's "1–4 days" is narrower than the source; §2.3 gives none. Correct figure: **onset usually within 3 days, range 1–10 days.**
- **Duration:** the "resolve spontaneously within 1–2 weeks" figure (§2.3) comes from Schweitzer & Maguire, *Aust Prescr* 2001 — now **superseded**. The current Australian position is duration-conditional: *"Withdrawal symptoms can be mild and brief, especially after short-term (6 to 12 weeks) therapy, but with longer-term therapy withdrawal symptoms can sometimes be severe and protracted."* §7.2's flat "1–3 weeks" is not sourced to anything Australian and is equally wrong as a blanket claim.

**Verdict: neither. Both sections should read:** *"Onset usually within 3 days of stopping or dose reduction (range 1–10 days). Duration depends on treatment length: brief (days to ~2 weeks) after short-term therapy, but potentially severe and protracted (months) after long-term use — up to 58% of people on antidepressants ≥9 months fail a standard taper. Venlafaxine and paroxetine are worst; fluoxetine rarely causes it. Standard Australian taper (TG Psychotropic 2025): reduce by 25–50% every 1–4 weeks to half the lowest unit strength, hold 2 weeks, stop; use hyperbolic tapering ('lower dose, go slower') for long-term users."*
Sources: Wallis K, *Stopping antidepressants: when and how*, Aust Prescr 2026, citing TG: Psychotropic 2025, Kendrick 2024 and Maudsley 2023: https://australianprescriber.tg.org.au/articles/stopping-antidepressants-when-and-how.html | Schweitzer & Maguire, Aust Prescr 2001: https://australianprescriber.tg.org.au/articles/stopping-antidepressants-1.html

## Verified correct

Checked against a live source and found right:

**Diagnostic timeframes and criteria (all DSM-5-TR/ICD-11 verified, psychdb.com):**
- GAD ≥6 months, most days, worry experienced as uncontrollable, with the six-symptom somatic tail.
- Panic attack = discrete surge peaking within minutes, a descriptor not a diagnosis; panic disorder = recurrent **unexpected** attacks + **≥1 month** of anticipatory fear or maladaptive avoidance.
- Social anxiety ≥6 months; performance-only subtype; blood-injection-injury phobia's biphasic vasovagal response and **applied tension** (not relaxation) as its treatment.
- OCD **>1 h/day** working threshold; insight as a **specifier** (good/fair, poor, absent-delusional) not a requirement; ego-dystonic vs ego-syntonic as the OCD/OCPD discriminator; compulsions including **mental acts**.
- Hoarding disorder as a **standalone DSM-5 diagnosis, not an OCD subtype**. BDD's mirror-checking/cosmetic-procedure-seeking and high suicide risk.
- **ASD 3 days–1 month; PTSD >1 month; "with delayed expression" = full criteria first met ≥6 months after the event.** All exact.
- PTSD criterion A4 media exclusion: *"Criterion A4 does not apply to exposure through electronic media, television, movies, or pictures, unless this exposure is work related."* Exact.
- The four PTSD clusters (intrusion / avoidance / negative alterations in cognition and mood / arousal and reactivity), plus the dissociative subtype specifier.
- **ICD-11 complex PTSD**: full PTSD + the three DSO clusters (affect dysregulation, negative self-concept, interpersonal disturbance), **with at least one symptom from each** — confirmed against BJPsych Advances, "Complex post-traumatic stress disorder: a new diagnosis in ICD-11".
- SSD's core reframe — the symptom **may be medically explained**; the criterion is the disproportionate thoughts/feelings/behaviours, typically >6 months.

**Scales:** GAD-7 range **0–21**; cut-points **5 / 10 / 15** = mild/moderate/severe; **≥10** as the threshold for further assessment (sens 89%, spec 82%). Verified against Spitzer et al., *Arch Intern Med* 2006, full text. No item text is reproduced in the note — compliant.

**PTSD management (Phoenix Australia v6.0, published 20 Dec 2021, NHMRC-approved 23 Jun 2020 — the note's "2020, updated 2021" dating is correct and it is still the current edition; it is a living guideline but no recommendation has changed since Dec 2021):**
- The five **strong** recommendations for adults: trauma-focused CBT, CPT, cognitive therapy, prolonged exposure, EMDR. Exact match.
- Debriefing: **conditional recommendation AGAINST** individual *and* group psychological debriefing, with the alternative worded as "information, emotional support, and practical assistance". The note's "not recommended and may increase PTSD risk" and its attribution to Rose (Cochrane 2002) both hold.
- SSRIs (sertraline, paroxetine, fluoxetine) and venlafaxine each carry a **conditional** recommendation — matching the note's "sertraline/paroxetine/fluoxetine also support PTSD" and "venlafaxine has PTSD evidence".

**Anxiety pharmacotherapy (Australian Prescriber; RANZCP CPG 2018 content):**
- **Beta blockers: "Beta blockers have little evidence to support their use in anxiety disorders, including social anxiety disorder"** — the note's §3.3 quote is near-verbatim and correctly attributed. Propranolol's Australian PI indications confirm no anxiety-disorder indication (only "anxiety tachycardia" under cardiac dysrhythmias), so "off-label" for performance anxiety is right.
- Onset in anxiety **4–6 weeks**, slower than in depression; continue **12 months** after response; gradual discontinuation. All exact.
- Pregabalin effective in GAD but **"not subsidised for this indication in Australia"** — exact.
- Quetiapine: effective in trials but **"long-term use of antipsychotics is inadvisable"** (metabolic, TD) — exact.
- Benzodiazepines "not first-line", reserved for short-term use or refractoriness; RANZCP adds "restricted to short-term **regular (not PRN)** use" in panic — a nuance the note could add but does not misstate.
- **OCD pharmacology, all exact against Brakoulias, Aust Prescr 2015:** adequate trial = maximum tolerated dose for **≥12 weeks**; full effect may take 12 weeks; continue **≥12 months**; **relapse >50% within 12 weeks** of stopping; clomipramine "usually used after two failed trials of an SSRI due to problems with tolerability"; fluoxetine 40–80, sertraline 50–200, paroxetine 20–60, fluvoxamine 50–300. (Only the escitalopram and clomipramine figures fail against the PI — Findings 1 and 2.)
- **Citalopram capped at 40 mg, 20 mg if >65 y** — exact against the Cipramil Australian PI, which also caps at 20 mg in hepatic impairment and CYP2C19 poor metabolisers (worth adding).

**Benzodiazepine harms and the Australian position (RACGP Part B — every number checked):**
- "Long-term use, **beyond 4 weeks**, should be uncommon" — exact.
- **"60–80% increase in the risk of traffic accidents"** and **"alcohol and benzodiazepines together increases accident risk more than seven times"** — both exact.
- **"After 4–6 weeks of use in approximately 15–30% of patients"** — the number is exact (context caveat at Finding 11).
- **"As of February 2014, alprazolam has been rescheduled by the TGA to a Schedule 8 drug"** — exact, including the misuse/harm rationale.
- Real-time prescription monitoring (SafeScript/national RTPM) — current and correct.

**Chronic pain (ANZCA FPM PS01(PM) 2025, updated October 2025 — the note's edition and year are correct):** every figure in §3.9 matches Appendix 1 verbatim — **"Trial duration 6-8 weeks"**, **"Limit dose to ~100 mg/day oral morphine equivalent"**, the **5 As** (analgesia, activity, adverse effects, affect, aberrant behaviour), **"Interval: weekly initially, no longer than monthly"**, **"Involve another colleague in decision to continue treatment"**, **"At least annual peer or specialist review is recommended"**, tapered termination if goals unmet, and the risk factors (age <18 or >65, respiratory/liver/kidney disease, household members with SUD or suicidal ideation). Opioids not first-line — "the first line of therapy for CNCP involves engaging the person to develop pain self-management skills". Long-term benefit — "insufficient evidence to determine the effectiveness of long-term opioid therapy". The **June 2020 TGA opioid reforms** (smaller pack sizes, tightened indications, boxed warnings) are real and correctly dated. Chronic pain >3 months matches the ICD-11/IASP definition.

**Conceptual claims verified as sound:** FND as a rule-in rather than exclusion diagnosis; la belle indifférence and secondary gain as unreliable; malingering as a description of behaviour rather than a psychiatric disorder; SSD not requiring an unexplained symptom; the PTSD risk-factor hierarchy (peritraumatic distress/dissociation and perceived life threat > post-trauma social support and later life stress > pre-trauma demographics) consistent with Brewin 2000 and Ozer 2003 as cited; "most trauma-exposed people do not develop PTSD".
