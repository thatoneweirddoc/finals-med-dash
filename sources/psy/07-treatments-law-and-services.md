# Psychiatry Notes — Section 7: Treatments, Law and Services
*Sources: RANZCP ECT guidelines (2019), Therapeutic Guidelines: Psychotropic, AMH, NSW Mental Health Act 2007, Victorian Mental Health and Wellbeing Act 2022, MBS Online, PBS, ACSQHC/AIHW, National Mental Health and Suicide Prevention Agreement (2022). Scope: the psychotherapies, the drug reference, ECT and neurostimulation, the law authorising involuntary care, restrictive practice, confidentiality and the service system.*

## 7.1 The psychotherapies

**The short version**
- Three registers: **insight-oriented** (psychodynamic — makes the unconscious conscious), **short-term focussed** (CBT/IPT — changes a defined maintaining mechanism now), **supportive** (holds function, does not destabilise).
- **CBT has the broadest evidence base** — the default answer unless the stem points elsewhere.
- Match therapy to **mechanism**: avoidance → exposure; role transition/grief → IPT; emotional dysregulation and self-harm → DBT; ambivalence → MI.

**CBT.** Model: **event → automatic thought → emotion, physiology, behaviour**; the behaviour (avoidance, safety behaviour) prevents disconfirmation and maintains the belief. Layers: automatic thoughts → intermediate rules → **core beliefs (schemas)**; distortions include catastrophising, all-or-nothing thinking and mind-reading. Course: shared formulation → **thought records** (situation, emotion, automatic thought, evidence for/against, balanced alternative, re-rate) → **behavioural experiments** → **behavioural activation** → **exposure** → relapse-prevention blueprint. Structured and agenda-set; **homework is the active ingredient**; **typically 8–20 sessions**.

- **Behavioural activation** — withdrawal → lost reinforcement → worse mood, reversed by **scheduling activity by value and mastery, not mood**. Effective standalone; deliverable by non-specialists.
- **Exposure** — graded, repeated, **without safety behaviours** (which preserve the belief that escape averted catastrophe). In vivo, imaginal, **interoceptive** (panic), **ERP** for OCD. ⚑ Inhibitory-learning framing has displaced "stay until anxiety halves" — the goal is **violated expectancy**.

**IPT.** Time-limited; targets the **interpersonal context**, not cognitions, through one or two of four foci: **grief, role transition, role dispute, interpersonal deficits**.

**Psychodynamic psychotherapy.** Current distress repeats **unconscious conflict and early relational patterns**. Tools: **transference** (feelings for earlier figures displaced onto the therapist), **countertransference** (the therapist's reaction, used as data), **defences** — mature (humour, sublimation), neurotic (repression, displacement, intellectualisation), immature (**splitting, projection, projective identification, denial, acting out** — the first and third mark borderline organisation) — and **interpretation** producing **insight**. Needs ego strength; **relatively contraindicated in acute psychosis, mania or crisis**.

**DBT.** For **BPD with chronic self-harm**. Individual therapy + **skills group** (mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation, interpersonal effectiveness) + phone coaching + consultation team. Dialectic = **acceptance and change**; targets ranked life-threatening → therapy-interfering → quality-of-life-interfering.

**ACT.** Targets **psychological flexibility**, not thought content: acceptance, **defusion**, present-moment contact, self-as-context, **values**, committed action.

**Supportive therapy.** Not uncovering — ventilation, reassurance, advice, psychoeducation, problem-solving, environmental intervention. ⚑ It **strengthens defences rather than dismantling them** — the pivot away from psychodynamic work.

**Motivational interviewing.** Guiding style for **ambivalence**; evokes **change talk**, avoids the righting reflex. Technique in **§5**. Exam point: MI does not treat depression — it is the **precursor** that gets someone to accept a treatment.

**Family therapy.** **Family-based (Maudsley) treatment for adolescent anorexia** — first-line, parents control refeeding (§6); **family psychoeducation in schizophrenia**, cutting relapse by lowering **expressed emotion** (criticism, hostility, over-involvement) (§4).

| Therapy | Targets | Duration | Evidence | Suits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CBT** | Maintaining cognitions and behaviours | 8–20 sessions | Strongest, broadest | Concrete, does homework |
| **Behavioural activation** | Withdrawal, lost reinforcement | 8–16 | Non-inferior to full CBT in depression | Non-specialist delivery |
| **IPT** | Grief, transition, dispute, deficits | 12–16 | Strong — depression, bulimia | Clear interpersonal trigger |
| **Psychodynamic** | Conflict, defences, transference | 16–30 to years | Moderate; BPD (MBT) | Ego strength; **not** acute psychosis |
| **DBT** | Emotional dysregulation, self-harm | ~12 months | Strong in BPD | Recurrent self-harm |
| **ACT** | Psychological flexibility, values | 8–16 | Moderate; chronic pain/illness | Symptoms unlikely to remit |
| **Supportive** | Function, coping, adaptive defences | Open-ended | Modest but real | Chronic psychosis, frailty, crisis |
| **MI** (§5) | Ambivalence | 1–4 | Strong — substance use, adherence | Precontemplative |
| **Family** | Family system, expressed emotion | 10–20 | FBT first-line in adolescent AN | Adolescents, high-EE households |

## 7.2 Psychopharmacology overview

**The short version**
- **SSRI first-line**; efficacy across classes is similar, so **the distinguishing hazard chooses the drug**.
- **Serotonin toxicity is fast, hyperreflexic, clonic; NMS is slow, rigid, hyporeflexic; anticholinergic toxicity is dry.**
- Interactions that kill: **MAOI with serotonergics or tyramine**; **lithium with NSAIDs/ACEi/thiazides/dehydration**; tramadol or St John's wort on an SSRI.
- Antidepressants **do not cause dependence** — but stopping abruptly causes a **discontinuation syndrome**.

| Class | Mechanism | Key adverse effects | Distinguishing hazard |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SSRI** (sertraline, escitalopram, fluoxetine, paroxetine, citalopram, fluvoxamine) | 5-HT reuptake block | GI upset, headache, sleep change, **sexual dysfunction 40–60%**, early anxiety spike | **Hyponatraemia** in elderly; **bleeding** with NSAID/anticoagulant; **citalopram QT**; fluoxetine long t½ (5-week MAOI washout); paroxetine worst discontinuation; fluvoxamine ↑ clozapine |
| **SNRI** (venlafaxine, desvenlafaxine, duloxetine) | 5-HT + NA reuptake | As SSRI + sweating, tachycardia | **Venlafaxine: dose-related ↑BP >150–225 mg, severe discontinuation, high overdose toxicity** |
| **TCA** (amitriptyline, nortriptyline, clomipramine) | Reuptake + antimuscarinic, antihistaminic, α1 block | Dry mouth, constipation, retention, sedation, postural drop | **Lethal in overdose — Na⁺-channel block → broad QRS, VT, seizures**; bicarbonate is the antidote |
| **MAOI** (phenelzine, tranylcypromine); **RIMA** (moclobemide) | Blocks MAO-A/B | Postural hypotension, insomnia, weight gain | **Hypertensive crisis with tyramine**; **serotonin toxicity** with any serotonergic. Moclobemide reversible → far safer |
| **Mirtazapine** | α2 antagonist + 5-HT2/3 block | **Sedation — paradoxically worse at low dose** (relative H1 dominance); **weight gain and increased appetite at all doses** | Rare agranulocytosis; chosen for insomnia + weight loss in the frail elderly |
| **Agomelatine** | MT1/MT2 agonist, 5-HT2C antagonist | Headache; weight- and sexually neutral | ⚑ **Hepatotoxicity — LFTs at baseline, ~3, 6, 12, 24 weeks and each dose rise** |
| **Vortioxetine** | 5-HT reuptake + multimodal receptor action | Dose-related nausea; less sexual dysfunction | Signal for cognitive symptoms; PBS-restricted |

**Mood stabilisers** — **§2**. Recall: lithium level **12 h post-dose**; lithium alone has an anti-suicide effect; valproate effectively barred in childbearing potential; lamotrigine **halved** with valproate, any rash → stop.

**Antipsychotics** — **§4**. Typicals — D2 block, EPSE, hyperprolactinaemia; atypicals — D2 + 5-HT2A, dominated by **metabolic** harm; **clozapine** for treatment resistance (agranulocytosis, myocarditis in the first 4 weeks, seizures, mandatory monitoring). **Metabolic monitoring — see §1.7 for the full monitoring table.** Australian schedule (Adult Positive Cardiometabolic Health Resource, Mindgardens/Equally Well 2023): **weight and waist weekly for the first 6–8 weeks** after starting or changing an antipsychotic, and **all parameters — weight/waist, BP, glucose or HbA1c, lipids — at baseline, 3, 6, 9 and 12 months, then 6-monthly**.

**Anxiolytics and hypnotics.** **Benzodiazepines** — GABA-A positive modulation; effective short-term but cause **tolerance, dependence, withdrawal seizures, falls, cognitive impairment, and respiratory depression with opioids/alcohol**. Prescribe for **days to weeks with an exit plan**; avoid in PTSD and substance dependence. Long t½ (diazepam) for tapering; **oxazepam** (no active metabolites) in the elderly and hepatic impairment. **Z-drugs** — same dependence risk plus a ⚑ TGA warning on **complex sleep behaviours**. CBT-I first-line for insomnia; **propranolol** for autonomic performance anxiety only.

| Feature | Serotonin toxicity | NMS | Anticholinergic toxicity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Onset** | **Hours (<24 h)** | **Days (1–3)** | Hours |
| **Neuromuscular** | **Hyperreflexia, clonus, legs > arms** | **Lead-pipe rigidity, hyporeflexia** | Normal |
| **Pupils** | **Mydriasis** | Normal | **Mydriasis** |
| **Bowel sounds** | **Increased**, diarrhoea | Normal/reduced | **Absent — ileus, retention** |
| **Skin** | Diaphoretic | Diaphoretic | **Dry, flushed, hot** |
| **Temperature** | Moderate ↑ (usually <38.5 °C) | **↑ — diagnostic threshold >38.0 °C on at least 2 occasions**; frequently >38.5 °C and may exceed 40 °C | ↑ |
| **CK** | Normal–mild ↑ | **Markedly ↑**; rhabdomyolysis → AKI | Normal |
| **Management** | Cease, **benzodiazepines**, cooling, fluids; **cyproheptadine** if moderate–severe | Cease antipsychotic, ICU-level support, cooling, fluids; **dantrolene ± bromocriptine** if severe | Cease, supportive care, benzodiazepines; **physostigmine** only on toxicology advice |

Hook: **serotonin = hyper and fast; NMS = rigid, hot, slow, high CK; anticholinergic = dry.** Also on the differential: malignant hyperthermia (suxamethonium/volatiles, masseter spasm, ↑EtCO₂) and sympathomimetic toxicity (sweaty, no rigidity). Triggers: serotonergic added or interacting; dopamine blocker started (or agonist withdrawn); antimuscarinic load.

**Discontinuation syndromes.** Onset **1–4 days** after stopping or a large dose cut (later with fluoxetine): flu-like malaise, dizziness, nausea, insomnia, vivid dreams, **"brain zaps."** Worst with **paroxetine and venlafaxine**; minimal with fluoxetine. ⚑ **Duration is not the discriminator from relapse** — it is brief (days to a few weeks) only after **short-term treatment of 6–12 weeks**; after longer treatment withdrawal can be **severe and protracted, lasting months** (Aust Prescr 2026;49:99–103). Use the **onset window** instead: discontinuation begins **within 1–4 days** of the dose change, is physical/neurological, and settles within hours of reinstatement; relapse takes weeks and reproduces the illness. Taper **gradually, scaled to treatment duration and half-life — 2–4 weeks may suffice after a short course, but after long-term use taper over months**, using **hyperbolic (proportional) dose reductions** for paroxetine, venlafaxine and duloxetine. ⚑ Not dependence — no craving, no dose escalation. Benzodiazepine withdrawal *is* dependence, with seizure risk.

**Hyponatraemia/SIADH.** SSRIs and venlafaxine. Risk: **age >65, female, low body weight, thiazide, low baseline Na⁺, carbamazepine**. Usually within **2–4 weeks**: nausea, lethargy, falls, confusion, seizures — easily blamed on the depression or on dementia. **Check Na⁺ at baseline and 2–4 weeks in older patients.** Mild and stable — continue and monitor; moderate–severe — cease, fluid restrict, **correct slowly (osmotic demyelination)**.

**QT prolongation.** Highest risk: **citalopram and escitalopram** (dose-dependent), **haloperidol** (especially IV), **droperidol**, ziprasidone, amisulpride, pimozide, TCAs, methadone. ⚑ TGA caps: **citalopram 40 mg/day, 20 mg if >65, hepatic impairment or CYP2C19 poor metaboliser; escitalopram 20 mg/day, 10 mg if >65.** ECG before and after starting with cardiac disease, electrolyte disturbance or other QT drugs; correct **K⁺ and Mg²⁺**.

**Interactions that matter.** **MAOI + tyramine** (aged cheese, cured meat, fermented soy, tap beer, yeast extract) → hypertensive crisis. **MAOI + any serotonergic** (SSRI, SNRI, TCA, triptan, tramadol, pethidine, linezolid, St John's wort) → serotonin toxicity; washout **2 weeks either way, 5 weeks after fluoxetine**. **Tramadol** — serotonergic *and* proconvulsant. **St John's wort** — serotonergic **and** a CYP3A4/P-gp inducer failing the OCP, warfarin and antiretrovirals. **Lithium** — levels rise with **NSAIDs, ACEi/ARBs, thiazides, dehydration, low salt intake, AKI**. **Clozapine** — ↑ with fluvoxamine, ciprofloxacin and **smoking cessation**.

## 7.3 ECT — practicalities, consent and the law

**The short version**
- A modified generalised seizure under **general anaesthesia with muscle relaxation**; **2–3 per week, 6–12 per course** (indications in §2).
- **Right unilateral ultrabrief** = fewest cognitive effects; **bitemporal** = fastest and most effective — speed versus memory.
- Dosing is relative to **seizure threshold** (RUL ≈6×, bitemporal ≈1.5×); adequacy is **EEG seizure quality (≥~25 s with post-ictal suppression)**, not a good-looking convulsion.
- Consent is the examinable part: **capacity-specific, informed, written, revocable**; without capacity a **tribunal** must authorise.

**How it is given.** Pre-anaesthetic and cognitive baseline workup; IV **propofol or thiopentone** at lowest effective dose (both raise threshold), **suxamethonium**, bite block, pre-oxygenation and bag-mask ventilation; EEG and motor seizure recorded; monitored recovery. Threshold is titrated at the first treatment and **rises through the course**. **Benzodiazepines and anticonvulsant mood stabilisers raise the threshold and blunt efficacy** — reduce or cease where safe; **lithium** risks prolonged seizure and post-ECT delirium (withhold on treatment days); antipsychotics are compatible. Seizure >120 s is terminated pharmacologically.

**Relative contraindications:** raised ICP or space-occupying lesion, recent MI or stroke, unstable aneurysm/AVM, phaeochromocytoma, retinal detachment, prohibitive anaesthetic risk. **Pregnancy is not one.**

**Side effects by time course.** Immediate — anaesthetic risk, headache, myalgia, nausea; **post-ictal confusion over 15–60 min**. Early — **anterograde amnesia**, usually recovering within **2–4 weeks** of the course ending. Persistent — **retrograde autobiographical amnesia around the treatment period**, permanent in a minority, more likely with bitemporal placement. Mortality is that of a brief GA — approximately **2.1 deaths per 100,000 treatments (~1 in 50,000)**.

**Consent and involuntary ECT.** ⚑ **State-based — name the jurisdiction.** Capacity is presumed and assessed **for this decision**.
- **NSW (MHA 2007).** An **ECT consent inquiry** confirms a voluntary patient has capacity and has consented in writing. An **ECT administration inquiry** covers those who cannot consent, involuntary patients and under-16s: the Mental Health Review Tribunal must find the person lacks capacity and that ECT is **reasonable and proper** and **necessary or desirable for their safety or welfare**, on written certification by **two medical practitioners, at least one a psychiatrist**. It authorises a **specified number, usually up to 12**.
- **Victoria (MHWA 2022).** An adult with capacity may consent in writing. ⚑ **No substitute decision-maker — guardian, parent or family — may consent to ECT for an adult.** Where capacity is absent, the authorised psychiatrist applies to the **Mental Health Tribunal**, which must find **no less restrictive way to treat** and weigh the person's views and preferences and any **advance statement of preferences**. It specifies **up to 12 treatments within a maximum 6 months**. **All under-18s need a Tribunal determination even if consenting.** Urgent hearings exist where ECT is needed to save life.
- ⚑ ***PBU & NJE v Mental Health Tribunal* [2018] VSC 564** — a capable person may refuse ECT even if the refusal seems unwise; **lack of insight is not automatically incapacity.**

**Myths to correct.** Not given awake or unmodified; no structural brain damage on imaging; **not a last resort** (§2); not wholesale memory erasure, though retrograde loss must be disclosed; most Australian ECT is given **with consent**. Relapse without maintenance is high, so **ECT always hands over to continuation pharmacotherapy**.

## 7.4 Other neurostimulation

**The short version**
- **rTMS**: awake, no anaesthetic, no induced seizure, no cognitive impairment; **MBS-funded since 1 November 2021**.
- ⚑ MBS rules are strict: **18+, ≥2 antidepressant classes adequately trialled, prescribed and mapped by a trained psychiatrist**; **35 initial + 15 retreatment sessions — a lifetime cap of 50, once only**; any prior rTMS (public or private) disqualifies.
- **Esketamine** is TGA-registered for treatment-resistant depression and ⚑ **PBS-listed from 1 May 2025**, supervised with post-dose observation.

**rTMS.** Focal alternating magnetic field over the **left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex**; weekday sessions over **4–6 weeks**, shortened by theta-burst protocols. Patient awake, drives home. Scalp discomfort and headache are common; the serious risk is **seizure (<1 in 10,000 sessions)**. Contraindicated with **ferromagnetic implants near the coil**, cochlear implants or deep brain stimulators. No systemic or cognitive effects — its advantage over ECT — but weaker in severe or psychotic depression. Items **14216/14217** (prescription/mapping, initial delivery) and **14219/14220** (retreatment).

**Ketamine/esketamine.** NMDA antagonists with downstream glutamatergic/AMPA effects. **Intranasal esketamine** is given with an oral antidepressant, self-administered **under supervision in an approved setting with ≥2 hours' observation** for sedation, dissociation and BP rise; no driving that day; twice-weekly induction then tapering. **Racemic ketamine** (IV/SC) is off-label and specialist-only. Onset is **rapid (hours–days), including on suicidal ideation, but wanes without repeat dosing** — a bridge, not maintenance. Concerns: dissociation, hypertension, cystitis, abuse liability, thin long-term data. Not a substitute for ECT in psychotic depression or catatonia.

## 7.5 Mental health legislation

**The short version**
- ⚑ **Each state and territory has its own Act — name the state before quoting any threshold or time limit.**
- The universal test: **mental illness/disorder present; risk of serious harm or serious deterioration; no less restrictive option reasonably available.** Diagnosis alone never suffices; risk alone never suffices.
- These Acts authorise treatment of **the mental illness only** — physical treatment runs on consent and guardianship law.
- ⚑ Both Acts require a **statement of rights** to be given and explained, and guarantee representation and free communication.
- ⚑ A **statutory patient-initiated right to a second psychiatric opinion** exists in **Victoria** (and the ACT, Qld and WA) but **not in NSW**, where a second opinion is a matter of clinical courtesy rather than legislation.

**Never grounds by themselves:** political, religious or philosophical belief; sexual preference or gender identity; immoral or illegal conduct; substance use alone; intellectual disability alone; economic or social status.

**NSW — Mental Health Act 2007.** A **mentally ill person** has a condition seriously impairing mental functioning with characteristic symptoms (delusions, hallucinations, serious thought disorder, severe mood disturbance) *and* needs care to prevent **serious harm** — physical, financial, reputational or self-neglect. A **mentally disordered person** need not have a diagnosable illness but behaves so irrationally that temporary care is needed to prevent **serious physical harm**; ⚑ that detention is capped at **3 days excluding weekends and public holidays**, reviewed 24-hourly, **maximum 3 per month**.

Pathway: a medical practitioner or accredited person completes a **mental health certificate (Schedule 1)** after examination; police and ambulance may also bring a person in. An **authorised medical officer must examine within 12 hours** of arrival, and continued detention needs **two examinations by two doctors, at least one a psychiatrist**. The person then goes before a **mental health inquiry conducted by the Mental Health Review Tribunal** as soon as practicable — the Tribunal, not the treating team, decides. An **involuntary patient order** made at inquiry runs **up to 3 months**; review is **at least 3-monthly for the first 12 months**, then at least 6-monthly. **CTOs** run **up to 12 months**, the Tribunal being satisfied it is the **least restrictive alternative**, that a workable treatment plan exists, and (where there is a history) that treatment has been refused with consequent deterioration. Breach permits the person to be taken to a facility for treatment; ⚑ a CTO **does not authorise forcible injection at home** — enforcement runs through recall.

**Victoria — Mental Health and Wellbeing Act 2022** (replaced the 2014 Act; commenced 1 September 2023, implementing Royal Commission recommendations). All orders require mental illness, need for **immediate treatment** to prevent serious deterioration or harm, and no less restrictive means:

| Order | Made by | Maximum duration |
|---|---|---|
| **Assessment order** (community or inpatient) | Medical practitioner or authorised mental health practitioner, having examined within the previous 24 h | Community **24 h**; inpatient **24 h from arrival or 72 h from making, whichever is earlier**; extendable **twice by 24 h** |
| **Temporary treatment order** | **Authorised psychiatrist** — ⚑ not the person who made the assessment order | **28 days**; Tribunal must hear before expiry |
| **Treatment order** (community or inpatient) | **Mental Health Tribunal only** | **6 months** adult; **3 months** under 18 |

⚑ The divergence students get wrong: **in Victoria only the Tribunal makes the ongoing treatment order**, whereas in NSW the initial order is made at a Tribunal inquiry flowing from clinical certification. Victorian orders default to **community** unless the person cannot be treated there. The Act is explicitly **supported decision-making**: **views and preferences** and any **advance statement of preferences** considered, a **nominated support person** recognised, and **opt-out non-legal advocacy (IMHA)** notified when an order is made.

**Interface with guardianship and consent law.** ⚑ A mental health order authorises treatment **of the mental illness** — not a laparotomy or dialysis. For physical treatment in someone lacking capacity use consent and substitute-decision law: advance care directive, enduring guardian/medical treatment decision maker, the "person responsible" hierarchy, or the guardianship tribunal (**NCAT** in NSW, **VCAT** in Victoria); emergency treatment is permitted where delay risks life. Sterilisation and psychosurgery always need tribunal approval. **Being an involuntary patient does not itself mean incapacity for other decisions.**

## 7.6 Seclusion, restraint and acute behavioural disturbance

**The short version**
- **Least restrictive practice always** — a last resort after de-escalation genuinely fails, for the shortest time possible.
- **De-escalate first**: environment, space, one communicator, offer of oral medication, food, a phone call.
- **Oral before parenteral** — **olanzapine wafer 5–10 mg** or **diazepam 5–10 mg** are standard Australian first-line options.
- ⚑ **Do not give a parenteral benzodiazepine within 1 hour of IM olanzapine** — excessive sedation, cardiorespiratory depression and, rarely, death. The rule runs **one way**: if a parenteral benzodiazepine has already been given, IM olanzapine may follow only after careful clinical evaluation with close monitoring.

**De-escalation.** Reduce stimulation, remove the audience, two exits and no entrapment, one nominated communicator, calm voice, acknowledge the grievance, offer choices, set limits without threats. Always exclude organic causes: hypoglycaemia, hypoxia, head injury, delirium, intoxication or withdrawal, sepsis, pain, retention.

**Drugs (adults).** ⚑ Real variation between jurisdictions and settings — quote the local protocol.
- **Oral (preferred):** **olanzapine wafer 5–10 mg** (max 30 mg/24 h), **diazepam 5–10 mg**, or **lorazepam 1–2 mg** (max 8 mg/24 h), repeatable ~2-hourly.
- **Parenteral:** ⚑ **droperidol 10 mg IM/IV**, repeatable once after 15 min (commonly capped at 20 mg/24 h), is the dominant Australian **emergency department** first-line (DORM trials); **IM olanzapine 5–10 mg** is the main alternative. ⚑ **Parenteral benzodiazepines (e.g. IM midazolam 5–10 mg), alone or added to droperidol, are *not* recommended in adults** — increased adverse events and more repeat sedation (Qld Health, *Management of patients with acute severe behavioural disturbance*; same position stated in §5.7). Midazolam survives in some local ED protocols as a fallback, but it is not the guideline first- or second-line. ⚑ Several **inpatient mental health** guidelines (e.g. Queensland Health MHAOD) instead use **IM lorazepam 1–2 mg first-line with IM olanzapine second-line and explicitly do not recommend droperidol or midazolam** — an examinable ED-versus-unit divergence.
- **Older, frail or delirious:** halve doses, avoid benzodiazepines (paradoxical worsening), treat the cause. **Avoid haloperidol in stimulant toxicity or hyperthermia**; QT caution with droperidol and haloperidol.

**Monitoring after sedation.** Continuous visual observation, frequency escalating with depth: awake — observations 15-minutely for an hour, then half-hourly; drowsy but rousable — half-hourly vitals; difficult to rouse — **5–10-minutely**; unresponsive — **call the MET**. Record RR, continuous SpO₂, BP, HR, temperature and conscious level until ambulant; watch for **dystonia and akathisia**.

**Governance.** **Seclusion** = confinement alone in a room the person cannot leave; **physical restraint** = staff using hands or body; **mechanical restraint** = devices; **chemical restraint** = medication given primarily to control behaviour rather than treat illness. All need legal authority, documented justification, near-continuous observation, attention to hydration, toileting and privacy, regular review, carer notification, reporting and a **debrief with the consumer**. ⚑ Prone restraint is discouraged nationally (positional asphyxia). Reducing and where possible **eliminating** restrictive practice is national policy under the **National Mental Health and Suicide Prevention Agreement (in effect March 2022)**, carrying forward the safety priorities of the Fifth National Mental Health and Suicide Prevention Plan (2017–2022), and under the **NSQHS Standards**: ⚑ the AIHW-reported seclusion rate has **more than halved in a decade — ~14 events per 1,000 bed days in 2009–10 to ~6 in 2024–25**.

## 7.7 Confidentiality and its limits

**The short version**
- Confidentiality is strong but **not absolute** — it yields to serious risk, statutory mandate and court order.
- Exceptions: **risk of serious harm to an identifiable other**, **mandatory reporting of children**, statutory notifications, subpoena, treatment-team sharing.
- ⚑ Mental health Acts create a **statutory carve-out** — designated carers must be told certain things without case-by-case consent.

**Risk to others.** A credible threat against an **identifiable person** may justify warning that person, notifying police, intensifying treatment or admission. Australia has not adopted *Tarasoff* wholesale, but privacy law permits disclosure to lessen a **serious and imminent threat to life, health or safety**. Disclose the **minimum necessary** and document the reasoning. Analogous duties: **fitness to drive**, notifiable diseases, and **mandatory notification to Ahpra** for an impaired practitioner placing the public at substantial risk.

**Children.** ⚑ Medical practitioners are **mandatory reporters** in every jurisdiction. NSW — report **risk of significant harm** to the Child Protection Helpline (*Children and Young Persons (Care and Protection) Act 1998*). Victoria — the *Children, Youth and Families Act 2005*, plus Crimes Act offences of **failing to disclose** and **failing to protect**. The trigger is **reasonable suspicion, not proof**; good-faith reports attract protection from liability. Exam point: a parent's illness (puerperal psychosis with infanticidal ideation) triggers consideration of the **children's** safety regardless of the parent's consent.

**Family and carers.** ⚑ Under the **NSW Act** a patient may nominate up to **two designated carers**, and an authorised medical officer may identify a **principal care provider**; they must be notified of **detention** (⚑ the commonly quoted **"within 24 hours"** timeframe is **UNCONFIRMED** — it could not be verified against s78 of the Act, the NSW Health carers page or the Schedule 3 statement of rights; the *events* requiring notification are confirmed, the *deadline* is not), an upcoming inquiry, absconding, transfer, discharge, reclassification to voluntary, ECT applications and Tribunal matters. A designated carer may be excluded by the patient — but not a parent/guardian of someone under 18, and not where exclusion creates serious risk. Victoria recognises a **nominated support person**. Even without consent you may always listen, give **general** information about illness, treatment and services, discuss crisis and discharge planning in general terms, and refer to carer supports. Carers' involvement in assessment, care and discharge planning is recognised in **state carers legislation** — the *Carers (Recognition) Act 2010* (NSW) (NSW Carers Charter: carers are partners in care) and the *Carers Recognition Act 2012* (Vic) — and, operatively, in the carer-notification and consultation provisions of each mental health Act. ⚑ The Commonwealth *Carer Recognition Act 2010* binds **Australian Public Service agencies and Commonwealth-funded providers, not state health services**, and creates no enforceable individual right.

## 7.8 Services and access

**The short version**
- Most mental health care happens in **general practice**, not psychiatry.
- A **GP mental health treatment plan** unlocks **Better Access**: **10 individual + 10 group** subsidised sessions per **calendar year** (⚑ item structure changed 1 November 2025).
- **headspace** = **12–25 years**, self-referral, free or low cost.
- **Barriers** are examinable in themselves: stigma, cost, distance, culture, language, workforce.

**Team.** **Psychiatrist** — diagnosis, complex pharmacology, ECT, legal instruments. **Psychologist** — assessment, psychometrics, structured therapy. **Mental health nurse** — monitoring, depot clinics, physical health; often the **case manager**, who usually holds the relationship. **Social worker** — housing, income, child protection, discharge planning. **Occupational therapist** — function, vocational and cognitive rehabilitation, sensory modulation. Plus **peer/lived-experience workers**, pharmacist, Aboriginal mental health worker, interpreter. The **GP** is the coordinating hub.

**Settings.** **Inpatient** — acute units, high-dependency areas, specialist units (mother–baby, older persons, CAMHS, eating disorders, forensic). **Community teams** — case management, depot clinics, CTO delivery, assertive outreach. **Crisis teams** — 24-hour triage and home-based treatment as an admission alternative. **Consultation–liaison psychiatry** — delirium, capacity assessment, self-harm review, somatic symptom disorders. **PECC/PECU** — short-stay units co-located with EDs (~48–72 h). **NGO/psychosocial** — housing, day programmes, NDIS. **Digital/telephone** — Lifeline 13 11 14, Beyond Blue, 13YARN, online CBT.

**Primary care and funding.** A **GP mental health treatment plan** requires assessment with an outcome measure (K10), agreed goals, treatment and referral, a crisis plan and scheduled **review**. It opens **Better Access** — Medicare-subsidised therapy by psychologists and eligible social workers and occupational therapists, capped at **10 individual and 10 group services per calendar year**, referred in blocks (commonly up to 6, then GP review, then the balance). ⚑ **From 1 November 2025 the MHTP review items (2712 and equivalents) and ongoing mental health consultation items (2713 and equivalents) were removed** — GPs now use **time-tiered general attendance items** to review, refer and provide ongoing mental health care, letting physical and mental health be addressed in one consultation. **Dedicated MHTP preparation items remain**, and are now tied to the patient's MyMedicare-registered practice or usual medical practitioner. **Session caps unchanged.** Also PHN-commissioned stepped care and psychiatrist item **291**.

**Populations.** **headspace** — **12–25**, self-referral, four streams (mental health, physical/sexual health, AOD, vocational/education), with early-psychosis and eheadspace arms. **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander** care runs through ACCHOs and social and emotional wellbeing frameworks (§1). **CALD** patients need **professional interpreters — never family, never children**. ⚑ **Rurally**, psychiatrist and psychologist supply falls steeply while suicide rates rise with remoteness: longer waits, more GP and ED presentations, long-distance involuntary transfers by police or air, no local specialist units. Mitigations — **telepsychiatry** (Medicare telehealth now permanent), outreach, GP upskilling, the RFDS, workforce incentives.

**Barriers to care.** **Stigma** — public, self- and structural, including from health professionals. **Cost** — gap fees, the session cap, medication, lost income. **Distance and transport. Waiting lists. Cultural** — mistrust from historic and continuing harms, differing explanatory models, fear of child removal. **Language** — interpreters under-used. **The illness itself** — impaired insight, avolition, paranoia and hopelessness are structural barriers. **Systemic** — workforce shortage, fragmentation between Commonwealth-funded primary care and state-funded specialist services, and the **"missing middle"**: too unwell for Better Access, not unwell enough for state services.

**Consumers and carers with lived experience.** Built into the system rather than consulted about it: **peer workers** in teams; consumer and carer consultants in governance; **advance statements of preferences** and nominated support persons in Victorian law; **official/community visitors** with statutory access to units; lived-experience representation on tribunals and curricula. The **recovery model** (hope, identity, meaning, personal responsibility, connectedness) is the stated framework of the **National Mental Health and Suicide Prevention Agreement** and the National Mental Health Strategy. Carers' own burden, financial strain and grief are a legitimate target of assessment.

## Rapid-Recall Discriminators

| Cue | Answer |
|---|---|
| Therapy that dismantles defences vs the one that strengthens them | **Psychodynamic** (insight, transference) vs **supportive** (function, adaptive defences) |
| Only therapy reliably reducing self-harm in BPD | **DBT** — individual + skills group + phone coaching, ~12 months |
| Antidepressant with dose-dependent hypertension | **Venlafaxine** — check BP above 150–225 mg/day |
| Antidepressant needing scheduled LFTs | **Agomelatine** — baseline, ~3, 6, 12, 24 weeks and each dose rise |
| Maximum citalopram and escitalopram doses over 65 | **20 mg** and **10 mg**/day — QT prolongation |
| Confused, hyponatraemic 78-year-old 3 weeks after starting sertraline on a thiazide | **SSRI-induced SIADH** — check Na⁺ at baseline and 2–4 weeks; correct slowly |
| Hours, clonus, hyperreflexia, mydriasis, diarrhoea, sweating | **Serotonin toxicity** — cease, benzodiazepines, cyproheptadine if moderate–severe |
| Days, lead-pipe rigidity, hyporeflexia, CK 12,000, 40 °C | **NMS** — cease antipsychotic, ICU support, dantrolene ± bromocriptine. ⚑ 40 °C is a *severe* case, **not** the threshold: the diagnostic criterion is **>38.0 °C on at least 2 occasions**, so 38.7 °C with rigidity is still NMS |
| Washout between fluoxetine and an MAOI | **5 weeks** (2 weeks for other antidepressants, either direction) |
| Brain zaps and dizziness 2 days after stopping paroxetine | **Discontinuation syndrome** — not relapse, not dependence. ⚑ The discriminator is **onset (1–4 days of the dose change)**, *not* duration: after long-term treatment withdrawal can last months. Taper scaled to treatment duration — weeks after a short course, **months after long-term use**, hyperbolic reductions for paroxetine/venlafaxine/duloxetine |
| Drugs that raise the seizure threshold and blunt ECT | **Benzodiazepines and anticonvulsant mood stabilisers**; lithium → delirium |
| ECT placement with fewest cognitive effects vs fastest | **Right unilateral ultrabrief** vs **bitemporal** |
| Who may consent to ECT for an adult without capacity in Victoria | ⚑ **Nobody by substitution — only the Mental Health Tribunal**, ≤12 treatments over ≤6 months |
| NSW involuntary order at inquiry; NSW CTO; "mentally disordered person" limit | **3 months**; **up to 12 months**; **3 days** excluding weekends/public holidays |
| Victorian assessment, temporary treatment and treatment orders | Assessment order: **community 24 h; inpatient — the earlier of 24 h from arrival at the service or 72 h from the order being made**, extendable twice by 24 h; TTO **28 days** (authorised psychiatrist); treatment order **6 months adult, 3 months under 18** — the last by the **Tribunal only** |
| Sequence rule for IM olanzapine and a parenteral benzodiazepine | ⚑ **No parenteral benzodiazepine within 1 hour after IM olanzapine** — excessive sedation, cardiorespiratory depression, rarely death. **One-way rule**: benzodiazepine first, olanzapine only after careful evaluation and close monitoring |
| Australian ED parenteral first-line for severe behavioural disturbance | **Droperidol 10 mg IM/IV**, repeat once at 15 min (⚑ some inpatient guidelines prefer IM lorazepam) |
| rTMS MBS eligibility and lifetime cap | **18+, ≥2 antidepressant classes failed**; **35 + 15 = 50 sessions, once per lifetime** |
| Better Access sessions per year; headspace ages | **10 individual + 10 group**; **12–25** |
| Who in NSW must be told when a person is detained | The **designated carer** (up to two nominated) and/or **principal care provider** — a statutory exception to consent. ⚑ The often-quoted **24-hour** deadline is **unconfirmed** against the Act — name the duty, not the timeframe |
