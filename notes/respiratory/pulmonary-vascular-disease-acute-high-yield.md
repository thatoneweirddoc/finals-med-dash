## Pulmonary Vascular Disease (acute — high yield)


### Respiratory Master Notes — Pulmonary Vascular Disease (acute — high yield)

#### 6.1 Venous Thromboembolism (PE)

Usually embolus from a **DVT** (Virchow's triad: stasis, endothelial injury, hypercoagulability). Presentation ranges from silent to pleuritic chest pain + dyspnoea + haemoptysis, to **haemodynamic collapse** (massive PE). Signs: tachycardia, tachypnoea, hypoxia, signs of DVT; ABG classically **type 1 failure with low CO₂ (respiratory alkalosis)**. ECG: sinus tachycardia (commonest); **S1Q3T3** is classic but uncommon; RV strain (T-wave inversion V1–V4).

**Diagnostic pathway (probability-first):**
- **Two-level Wells score** → **PE likely (>4)** vs **unlikely (≤4)**.
- **PE unlikely → D-dimer**: if negative, PE excluded (use an **age-adjusted cutoff = age × 10 µg/L for patients >50** to reduce false positives ⚑). If positive → imaging.
- **PE likely → CTPA** directly (don't delay for D-dimer). **V/Q scan** if CTPA contraindicated (renal impairment, contrast allergy, pregnancy — where a low-dose perfusion scan is often preferred).
- Interim/therapeutic anticoagulation should be started while awaiting imaging if there's any delay.

**Risk-stratify (drives disposition):** **sPESI** and haemodynamics —
- **High-risk (massive)** = **haemodynamic instability** (hypotension/shock) → **systemic thrombolysis** (e.g. alteplase), or embolectomy/catheter-directed therapy if lysis contraindicated.
- **Intermediate/low-risk** = anticoagulate; consider outpatient management if sPESI 0 and stable.

**Anticoagulation** ⚑: **DOACs (apixaban, rivaroxaban) are first-line** for most — no bridging needed, oral from the start. Exceptions: **antiphospholipid syndrome (use warfarin)**, severe renal impairment, and **pregnancy/cancer considerations** (LMWH in pregnancy; DOACs or LMWH in cancer-associated VTE).
- **Duration:** provoked by a **transient major risk factor → 3 months**; **unprovoked or ongoing risk factor → ≥3–6 months, often long-term** (weigh bleeding risk); **cancer-associated → continue while active**.

**PE in pregnancy:** avoid D-dimer (physiologically raised); use compression Doppler first if DVT symptoms; **V/Q or CTPA** per local protocol; treat with **LMWH** (DOACs and warfarin avoided).

#### 6.2 Pulmonary Hypertension

⚑ **Definition updated (2022 ESC/ERS):** **mean pulmonary arterial pressure (mPAP) >20 mmHg at rest** (previously ≥25). Sub-classify with right heart catheter:
- **Pre-capillary PH:** mPAP >20, **PAWP ≤15 mmHg**, **PVR >2 Wood units** (the PVR threshold also fell, from >3) ⚑.
- **Post-capillary PH (left-heart disease):** PAWP **>15 mmHg**.

**The 5 WHO groups:**

| Group | Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — PAH** | Pulmonary **arterial** | Idiopathic, heritable, connective-tissue disease (systemic sclerosis), congenital shunts, drugs |
| **2** | **Left heart disease** (commonest overall) | HFrEF/HFpEF, valve disease |
| **3** | **Lung disease/hypoxia** | COPD, ILD, OSA (→ cor pulmonale) |
| **4** | **Chronic thromboembolic (CTEPH)** | Organised PE — potentially **curable by pulmonary endarterectomy** |
| **5** | Multifactorial/unclear | Sarcoid, haematological, metabolic |

**Clinical:** exertional dyspnoea, fatigue, syncope, and signs of RV failure (raised JVP, **loud P2**, RV heave, tricuspid regurgitation, peripheral oedema). **Echo** screens (estimates PA pressure, RV size/function); **right heart catheter confirms**. **Cor pulmonale** = right heart failure due to lung disease/pulmonary hypertension.

**Treatment:** treat the underlying cause (groups 2/3 — optimise heart/lung disease; **do not give PAH-specific vasodilators in group 2/3**, can worsen). **Group 1 PAH** gets targeted therapy: **endothelin receptor antagonists (bosentan/ambrisentan/macitentan), PDE-5 inhibitors (sildenafil/tadalafil), prostacyclin analogues (epoprostenol), riociguat**; **CCBs only if vasoreactive**. **CTEPH (group 4)** → assess for **pulmonary endarterectomy** ± riociguat.

#### 6.3 Other Pulmonary Vascular Disease

**Pulmonary AVMs** — cause right-to-left shunt (hypoxia unresponsive to O₂), risk of paradoxical embolism/stroke and brain abscess; associated with **hereditary haemorrhagic telangiectasia (Osler–Weber–Rendu)**. **Pulmonary vasculitis** (GPA/EGPA) — see the systemic-disease overlap. **Fat embolism** (post-long-bone fracture: dyspnoea + petechiae + confusion) and **amniotic fluid embolism** are non-thrombotic embolic syndromes.

- pCO2: 4.5-6.0 kPa

- Assess oxygenation: hypoxia, alveolar-arterial gradient
- pH status
- Respiratory component (respiratory acidosis= ↓pH, ↑PaCO2; respiratory alkalosis= ↑pH, ↓PaCO3). If PaCO2 doesn’t agree with pH, ignore until step 5

- Metabolic compensation: ↓HCO3- production by kidney (will result in ↓HCO3-)

**Pathology**

LARGE AIRWAYS:

- Mucous gland enlargement
- Goblet cell hyperplasia
- Columnar cells replaced by squamous cells (potential carcinogenesis, ↓ mucociliary clearance)

SMALL AIRWAYS:

- Goblet cell hyperplasia
- Replacement of surfactant-secreting clara cells with mucous secreting cells
- Mononuclear cells
- Smooth muscle thickening

PARENCHYMAL DESTRUCTION:

- Reduced surfactant: compounding
- Reduced elastic recoil → hyperinflation → dynamic airway collapse
- Fibrosis: decreased gas exchange
- Reduced SA for gas exchange
- Luminal narrowing: mucus, oedema, infiltration

**Presentation**

**CHRONIC BRONCHITIS (Blue Bloater)**

- Hypertrophy and hyperplasia of mucus-secreting glands in bronchi (Reid index > 50%)
- Symptoms
- Daily productive cough
- Cor pulmonale (RHF, pulmonary oedema/HTN, hepatosplenomegaly)
- Frequent infective episodes
- Cyanotic and bloated (central obesity)
- Investigations
- ↓PaO2, mid-high PaCO2
- Auscultation: coarse crackles, expiratory wheeze
- Cor pulmonale: ↑ JVP, ascites, peripheral pitting oedema
- CXR: ↑ bronchovascular markings, enlarged heart
- Note: In the short-term, high CO2 will stimulates respiration however patient’s will become insensitive to CO2 and depend on hypoxemia to drive ventilation (Haldane effect). Hence, administrating O2 may make situation worse in late-stage

**EMPHYSEMA (Pink Puffer)**

- Respiratory effort is maintained so CO2 excretion is not impaired to the same extent
- Symptoms:
- Severe dyspnoea (even at rest) and worsening exercise tolerance
- Weight loss with cachexia
- Malaise/fatigue
- Hyperinflation and barrel chest
- Infrequent infections (severe when they occur)
- Investigations
- Normal PaO2
- Pursed lip breathing, accessory muscle use, Hoover’s sign
- Auscultation: hyperresonant percussion note, ↓ air entry
- CXR: flat hemidiaphragm, ↓ vascular markings

**Investigations**

- PHYSICAL EXAM: hyper-resonant lungs, ↓ diaphragmatic excursion,
- AUSCULTATION:  early inspiratory coarse crackles and wheezing, prolonged expiratory phase, ↓ breath sounds (silent in advanced COPD), soft/distant heart sounds
- SPIROMATORY: FEV1/FVC, residual volume, confirm if asthma vs COPD (irreversible)
- ABG: hypoxemia and hypercapnia in advanced cases
- CXR: hyper-expanded lungs > 10 ribs, flattened diaphragms, ↓ lung markings, blackened bulla signs, prominent hila

**Management**

**C**: CONFIRM DIAGNOSIS AND ASSESS SEVERITY

- Risk factor evaluation e.g. smoking
- Exercise stress test: determine severity by quantifying limitation from breathlessness
- Diagnosis via spirometry

**O**: OPTIMISE FUNCTION

- Bronchodilators: anti-cholinergic, B-agonist
- Anti-inflammatory: corticosteroid
- Both: methyl-xanthines/PDE inhibitors, theophylline, selective PDE-4 inhibitor (roflumilast)
- Mucolytics (selected patients): oral bromhexine, nebulised acetyle cycteine
- Pulmonary rehabilitation and correct inhaler technique
- Identifying and treating aggravating factors e.g. OSA, alcohol, reflux

**P**: PREVENT DETERIORATION.

- Smoking cessation: NRT, Bupropion, Varencicline
- Education, counselling and regular follow-up
- Vaccinations: influenza (yearly), pneumococcal (every 5 years)
- Antibiotics
- Assess and treat comorbidity: CVD, osteoporosis

**D**: DEVELOP SUPPORT NETWORKS AND SELF-MANAGEMENT PLANS.

- Continued pulmonary rehabilitation
- Encourage self-management
- Emergency plan
- Advanced care directives

**X**: MANAGE EXACERBATIONS

- Bronchodilators: inhaled B2 agonists and anticholinergics e.g. salbutamol and ipratropium bromide via nebulizers × 3 back-to-back q15min
- Glucocorticoids: no more than 2 week tapering course
- Antibiotics e.g. amoxicillin, cephalosporin
- Controlled O2 therapy (target 88-92%): minimise excessive O2 to avoid worsening hypercapnia
- Assisted ventilation

**Outcomes**

↓ prognostic markers: age, worsening airflow limitation (↓FEV1), dyspnoea severity, complications

**Complications**

Exacerbations, cor pulmonale, pulmonary cachexia, spontaneous pneumothorax (ruptured bullae)
