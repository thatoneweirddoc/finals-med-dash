## Pulmonary Vascular Disease (acute — high yield)


### Respiratory Master Notes — Pulmonary Vascular Disease (acute — high yield)

#### 6.1 Venous Thromboembolism (PE)

Usually embolus from a **DVT** (Virchow's triad: stasis, endothelial injury, hypercoagulability). Presentation ranges from silent to pleuritic chest pain + dyspnoea + haemoptysis, to **haemodynamic collapse** (massive PE). Signs: tachycardia, tachypnoea, hypoxia, signs of DVT; ABG classically **type 1 failure with low CO₂ (respiratory alkalosis)**. ECG: sinus tachycardia (commonest); **S1Q3T3** is classic but uncommon; RV strain (T-wave inversion V1–V4).

**Diagnostic pathway (probability-first):**
- **Two-level Wells score** → **PE likely (>4)** vs **unlikely (≤4)**.
- **PE unlikely → D-dimer**: if negative, PE excluded (use an **age-adjusted cutoff = age × 10 µg/L for patients >50** to reduce false positives ⚑). If positive → imaging.
- **PE likely → CTPA** directly (don't delay for D-dimer). **V/Q scan** if CTPA contraindicated (renal impairment, contrast allergy, pregnancy — where a low-dose perfusion scan is often preferred).
- Interim/therapeutic anticoagulation should be started while awaiting imaging if there's any delay.

**Risk-stratify (drives disposition):** **sPESI** and haemodynamics —
- **High-risk (massive)** = **haemodynamic instability** (hypotension/shock) → **systemic thrombolysis** (e.g. alteplase), or embolectomy/catheter-directed therapy if lysis contraindicated.
- **Intermediate/low-risk** = anticoagulate; consider outpatient management if sPESI 0 and stable.

**Anticoagulation** ⚑: **DOACs (apixaban, rivaroxaban) are first-line** for most — no bridging needed, oral from the start. Exceptions: **antiphospholipid syndrome (use warfarin)**, severe renal impairment, and **pregnancy/cancer considerations** (LMWH in pregnancy; DOACs or LMWH in cancer-associated VTE).
- **Duration:** provoked by a **transient major risk factor → 3 months**; **unprovoked or ongoing risk factor → ≥3–6 months, often long-term** (weigh bleeding risk); **cancer-associated → continue while active**.

**PE in pregnancy:** avoid D-dimer (physiologically raised); use compression Doppler first if DVT symptoms; **V/Q or CTPA** per local protocol; treat with **LMWH** (DOACs and warfarin avoided).

#### 6.2 Pulmonary Hypertension

⚑ **Definition updated (2022 ESC/ERS):** **mean pulmonary arterial pressure (mPAP) >20 mmHg at rest** (previously ≥25). Sub-classify with right heart catheter:
- **Pre-capillary PH:** mPAP >20, **PAWP ≤15 mmHg**, **PVR >2 Wood units** (the PVR threshold also fell, from >3) ⚑.
- **Post-capillary PH (left-heart disease):** PAWP **>15 mmHg**.

**The 5 WHO groups:**

| Group | Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — PAH** | Pulmonary **arterial** | Idiopathic, heritable, connective-tissue disease (systemic sclerosis), congenital shunts, drugs |
| **2** | **Left heart disease** (commonest overall) | HFrEF/HFpEF, valve disease |
| **3** | **Lung disease/hypoxia** | COPD, ILD, OSA (→ cor pulmonale) |
| **4** | **Chronic thromboembolic (CTEPH)** | Organised PE — potentially **curable by pulmonary endarterectomy** |
| **5** | Multifactorial/unclear | Sarcoid, haematological, metabolic |

**Clinical:** exertional dyspnoea, fatigue, syncope, and signs of RV failure (raised JVP, **loud P2**, RV heave, tricuspid regurgitation, peripheral oedema). **Echo** screens (estimates PA pressure, RV size/function); **right heart catheter confirms**. **Cor pulmonale** = right heart failure due to lung disease/pulmonary hypertension.

**Treatment:** treat the underlying cause (groups 2/3 — optimise heart/lung disease; **do not give PAH-specific vasodilators in group 2/3**, can worsen). **Group 1 PAH** gets targeted therapy: **endothelin receptor antagonists (bosentan/ambrisentan/macitentan), PDE-5 inhibitors (sildenafil/tadalafil), prostacyclin analogues (epoprostenol), riociguat**; **CCBs only if vasoreactive**. **CTEPH (group 4)** → assess for **pulmonary endarterectomy** ± riociguat.

#### 6.3 Other Pulmonary Vascular Disease

**Pulmonary AVMs** — cause right-to-left shunt (hypoxia unresponsive to O₂), risk of paradoxical embolism/stroke and brain abscess; associated with **hereditary haemorrhagic telangiectasia (Osler–Weber–Rendu)**. **Pulmonary vasculitis** (GPA/EGPA) — see the systemic-disease overlap. **Fat embolism** (post-long-bone fracture: dyspnoea + petechiae + confusion) and **amniotic fluid embolism** are non-thrombotic embolic syndromes.

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators — Section 6

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Wells "PE likely" (>4) next step | **CTPA** (skip D-dimer) |
| D-dimer cutoff in patient >50 | **Age × 10 µg/L** (age-adjusted) ⚑ |
| ABG in acute PE | Type 1 failure, **low** CO₂ (respiratory alkalosis) |
| ECG "classic" (uncommon) PE sign | **S1Q3T3** |
| First-line anticoagulant for PE | **DOAC** (apixaban/rivaroxaban) ⚑ |
| PE + antiphospholipid syndrome | **Warfarin** (not DOAC) |
| Anticoagulant in pregnancy | **LMWH** |
| Massive (high-risk) PE with shock | **Thrombolysis** |
| Provoked PE anticoagulation duration | 3 months |
| Updated mPAP threshold for PH | **>20 mmHg** (was ≥25) ⚑ |
| Pre-capillary PH numbers | mPAP >20, PAWP ≤15, **PVR >2 WU** ⚑ |
| Commonest cause of PH overall | Group 2 (left heart disease) |
| Potentially curable PH group | **Group 4 (CTEPH)** → endarterectomy |
| Loud P2 + RV heave + raised JVP | Pulmonary hypertension / cor pulmonale |
| Hypoxia not correcting on O₂ + telangiectasia | Pulmonary AVM (HHT) |
