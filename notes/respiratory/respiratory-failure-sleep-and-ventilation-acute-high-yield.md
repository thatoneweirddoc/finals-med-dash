## Respiratory Failure, Sleep & Ventilation (acute — high yield)


### Respiratory Master Notes — Respiratory Failure, Sleep & Ventilation (acute — high yield)

#### 10.1 Respiratory Failure and Oxygen Therapy

**Definition:** PaO₂ <8 kPa (60 mmHg) on room air. **Type 1** = hypoxaemia with normal/low CO₂ (oxygenation/**V-Q** failure — pneumonia, oedema, PE, ILD). **Type 2** = hypoxaemia **+ PaCO₂ >6.0 kPa (45 mmHg)** (ventilatory/**pump** failure — COPD, exhausted asthma, opioids, neuromuscular, chest wall, obesity). (See Section 3.3 for acute vs chronic.)

**Oxygen as a drug — targets (BTS):**
- **Most acutely unwell patients: SpO₂ 94–98%.**
- **At risk of hypercapnic (type 2) failure: SpO₂ 88–92%** — start with a **28% Venturi** and check an ABG; titrate to avoid both hypoxia and CO₂ narcosis.
- Devices by delivery: nasal cannulae (low flow, variable FiO₂) → simple mask → **Venturi** (fixed FiO₂, ideal for retainers) → **non-rebreathe reservoir mask** (~15 L, high FiO₂ for the critically hypoxic) → **high-flow nasal oxygen (HFNO)** (heated, humidified, high flow — some CPAP-like effect).

**LTOT** (chronic — see Section 4.2): survival benefit only in **chronic hypoxaemia** (PaO₂ ≤7.3 kPa, or 7.3–8.0 with cor pulmonale/polycythaemia), ≥15 h/day.

#### 10.2 Acute Non-Invasive and Invasive Ventilation

**Match the mode to the failure:**

| Mode | What it does | Core indications |
|---|---|---|
| **CPAP** | Continuous pressure (splints airways/alveoli open) — improves **oxygenation** | **Type 1** failure: **cardiogenic pulmonary oedema**, OSA; some pneumonia/COVID |
| **BiPAP (NIV)** | Two pressures (IPAP > EPAP) — augments **ventilation**, clears **CO₂** | **Type 2** failure: **AECOPD with respiratory acidosis**, neuromuscular/chest-wall hypoventilation, OHS |

**The classic exam indication:** **NIV (BiPAP) for AECOPD with pH <7.35 and PaCO₂ >6.0 kPa persisting despite ≥1 h of maximal medical therapy and controlled oxygen.** If pH <7.25 or NIV fails → consider intubation/ICU. Contraindications to NIV: reduced consciousness, vomiting/aspiration risk, facial trauma, untreated pneumothorax, haemodynamic instability.

**ARDS** — acute, diffuse inflammatory lung injury → non-cardiogenic pulmonary oedema and refractory hypoxaemia. Causes: **sepsis/pneumonia** (commonest), aspiration, pancreatitis, trauma, transfusion (TRALI).

⚑ **ARDS — New Global Definition (2023)** updates the 2012 Berlin definition:
- **Timing:** within **1 week** of a known insult or new/worsening respiratory symptoms.
- **Imaging:** **bilateral opacities** on CXR/CT **or ultrasound**, not fully explained by effusions/collapse; oedema not fully explained by cardiac failure/fluid overload.
- **Oxygenation:** may now use **SpO₂/FiO₂ (≤315, with SpO₂ ≤97%)** as an alternative to PaO₂/FiO₂.
- **New category — non-intubated ARDS:** diagnosable in patients on **HFNO ≥30 L/min** or **CPAP/NIV with PEEP ≥5 cmH₂O** (Berlin required intubation/PEEP ≥5 with a PaO₂/FiO₂ ≤300).
- **Intubated severity (PEEP ≥5):** **mild** PaO₂/FiO₂ 200–300, **moderate** 100–200, **severe** ≤100.
- **Resource-limited settings:** SpO₂/FiO₂ ≤315 without a PEEP/flow requirement.

**Management of ARDS:** treat the cause; **lung-protective ventilation** (**low tidal volume ~6 mL/kg ideal body weight**, plateau pressure <30 cmH₂O, permissive hypercapnia), adequate PEEP, conservative fluids, **prone positioning** for moderate–severe, neuromuscular blockade in selected cases, and ECMO in refractory cases.

#### 10.3 Sleep-Disordered Breathing and Hypoventilation

**Obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA)** — repetitive upper-airway collapse in sleep → apnoeas/hypopnoeas, fragmented sleep. Presentation: **loud snoring, witnessed apnoeas, daytime somnolence** (Epworth score), morning headache, obesity, hypertension; increases cardiovascular and RTA risk. Screen with **STOP-BANG** (Snoring, Tiredness, Observed apnoea, Pressure/BP, BMI >35, Age >50, Neck >40 cm, male). Diagnose with **polysomnography** (AHI ≥5 with symptoms). Treatment: weight loss, avoid alcohol/sedatives, **CPAP** (first-line for moderate–severe), mandibular device for mild.

**Obesity hypoventilation syndrome (OHS)** = obesity + **awake daytime hypercapnia** (not explained by other cause), often with OSA → type 2 respiratory failure; treat with weight loss and **NIV/CPAP**. **Neuromuscular/chest-wall causes** (Guillain–Barré, motor neurone disease, myasthenia, muscular dystrophy, kyphoscoliosis) cause **restrictive** ventilatory failure — monitor with **FVC** (falling FVC, e.g. <15 mL/kg or <1 L, signals impending failure — a key GBS/myasthenic-crisis alarm).

#### 10.4 Respiratory Emergencies (consolidated)

| Emergency | One-move recognition | First action |
|---|---|---|
| **Tension pneumothorax** | Distress + hypotension + trachea deviated away + hyper-resonance | **Immediate needle decompression** (don't image) |
| **Massive PE** | Collapse/shock + hypoxia + RV strain | **Thrombolysis** |
| **Life-threatening asthma** | Silent chest, SpO₂ <92%, **normal/rising CO₂** | Back-to-back nebs, IV magnesium, steroids, ICU |
| **Acute type 2 failure (AECOPD)** | ↑CO₂, pH <7.35 despite therapy | Controlled O₂ + **NIV (BiPAP)** |
| **Massive haemoptysis** | Large-volume bleeding, asphyxiation risk | Airway protection, lie **bleeding side down**, resus, bronchial artery embolisation |
| **Upper-airway obstruction** | **Inspiratory stridor**, distress | Secure airway; anaphylaxis → IM adrenaline |
| **SVC obstruction** | Facial/arm swelling, distended veins | Urgent imaging, steroids/stent, oncology |

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators — Section 10

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| SpO₂ target, most acute patients | 94–98% |
| SpO₂ target, CO₂ retainer | 88–92% (28% Venturi) |
| CPAP treats… | **Oxygenation** (type 1) — cardiogenic oedema, OSA |
| BiPAP/NIV treats… | **Ventilation/CO₂** (type 2) — AECOPD acidosis |
| NIV threshold in AECOPD | pH <7.35 + PaCO₂ >6.0 despite therapy |
| Commonest ARDS cause | Sepsis/pneumonia |
| ARDS tidal volume | ~6 mL/kg ideal body weight |
| New in the 2023 ARDS definition | SpO₂/FiO₂ allowed; **non-intubated (HFNO) ARDS** category ⚑ |
| Intervention improving survival in moderate–severe ARDS | Prone positioning |
| OSA screening tool | STOP-BANG (diagnose with polysomnography) |
| Obesity + awake daytime hypercapnia | Obesity hypoventilation syndrome |
| Monitoring in GBS/myasthenic crisis | **Serial FVC** (not peak flow) |
| Haemoptysis positioning | Bleeding side **down** |
