## Obstructive Airway Disease


### Respiratory Master Notes — Obstructive Airway Disease

#### 4.1 Asthma

Chronic **reversible** airway inflammation → variable airflow obstruction, bronchial hyperresponsiveness, wheeze, cough, chest tightness, breathlessness (diurnal, triggered by allergens/exercise/cold/viruses).

**Diagnosis (objective confirmation — the pathway has changed)** ⚑: don't diagnose on symptoms alone. Supportive objective tests: **spirometry showing obstruction with bronchodilator reversibility**, **raised FeNO** (eosinophilic inflammation), **PEF variability >10%**, or bronchial challenge. *(NICE and GINA differ slightly on the order/first test — the shared principle for finals is "confirm objectively, don't treat on history alone.")*

⚑ **Chronic management — GINA 2025 "Track 1" (preferred):** the biggest change from older teaching. **SABA-only treatment is no longer recommended** (it increases severe exacerbation and death risk). Instead, everyone gets **ICS-containing treatment**, using **low-dose ICS–formoterol as the reliever**:
- **Steps 1–2:** *as-needed* low-dose **ICS–formoterol** (AIR — anti-inflammatory reliever).
- **Steps 3–5:** low-dose then medium-dose **ICS–formoterol as both maintenance and reliever (MART)**, escalating to add **LAMA**, then **biologics** / high-dose ICS for severe disease.
- **Track 2** (alternative, when Track 1 not possible): ICS whenever SABA taken / regular ICS + SABA reliever.

**Acute asthma — severity stratification (BTS/SIGN), memorise the thresholds:**

| Severity | Key features |
|---|---|
| **Moderate** | PEF 50–75% best/predicted, no features of severe |
| **Acute severe** | **PEF 33–50%**, RR ≥25, HR ≥110, **can't complete sentences** |
| **Life-threatening** | **PEF <33%**, **SpO₂ <92%**, silent chest, cyanosis, poor effort, exhaustion, arrhythmia, hypotension, altered consciousness, **normal PaCO₂** |
| **Near-fatal** | **Raised PaCO₂** ± need for ventilation |

*The single most important trap: a **normal or rising CO₂ in acute asthma means the patient is tiring — call ICU.*** A silent chest is ominous (no air moving), not reassuring.

**Acute management — "O SHIT ME" / stepwise:** high-flow **Oxygen** (target 94–98%), nebulised **Salbutamol** (SABA, back-to-back/continuous), **Ipratropium** (SAMA) if severe/life-threatening, **steroids** (oral prednisolone or IV hydrocortisone), and — if not responding — **IV magnesium sulfate**, consider IV salbutamol/aminophylline and ICU. **Antibiotics only if infection**. Escalate to NIV/intubation for exhaustion/rising CO₂.

**Special phenotypes:** **occupational asthma** (symptoms better on days off/holidays — ask about work); **aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease** (asthma + nasal polyps + aspirin/NSAID sensitivity = **Samter's triad**); **ABPA** (asthma + fleeting infiltrates + **very high IgE, Aspergillus precipitins, eosinophilia, central bronchiectasis**).

#### 4.2 COPD

Persistent airflow limitation from **noxious exposure** (smoking >>; also biomass, occupational dust, **α₁-antitrypsin deficiency** in the young/non-smoker with **basal/panacinar** emphysema and liver disease). Chronic bronchitis (clinical: productive cough ≥3 months over 2 years) and emphysema (pathological) coexist.

**Diagnosis:** **post-bronchodilator FEV₁/FVC <0.70** (GOLD). Grade airflow limitation by FEV₁ % predicted (GOLD 1 ≥80%, 2 = 50–79%, 3 = 30–49%, 4 <30%).

⚑ **GOLD "ABE" assessment (2023 onward — replaced the old ABCD):** combine **symptoms** (mMRC/CAT) with **exacerbation history**:
- **Group A** — low symptoms, 0–1 exacerbation (no hospitalisation): **a bronchodilator**.
- **Group B** — high symptoms, 0–1 exacerbation: **LABA + LAMA**.
- **Group E** — **≥2 moderate or ≥1 hospitalised exacerbation** (regardless of symptoms): **LABA + LAMA**, and **add ICS if blood eosinophils ≥300 cells/µL**.
- (Add/withhold ICS by eosinophils; **avoid ICS if eos <100** or frequent pneumonia. Roflumilast/azithromycin for selected frequent exacerbators.)

**Non-pharmacological (mortality/quality benefit):** **smoking cessation** (the only intervention that slows FEV₁ decline), **pulmonary rehabilitation**, vaccination (influenza, pneumococcal, COVID), and **LTOT** where criteria met.

**Acute exacerbation of COPD (AECOPD):**
- **Controlled oxygen — target SpO₂ 88–92%** with a **28% Venturi** in known/at-risk retainers; get an ABG.
- Nebulised **salbutamol + ipratropium**; **oral prednisolone 30 mg for 5 days**; **antibiotics if increased sputum purulence/volume or consolidation**.
- **NIV (BiPAP)** for **persistent respiratory acidosis (pH <7.35 with PaCO₂ >6.0) despite maximal medical therapy** — a core exam indication. Intubation if NIV fails/contraindicated.

**Chronic complications:** chronic type 2 respiratory failure, **cor pulmonale**, secondary polycythaemia, weight loss, pneumothorax (bullae).

**LTOT criteria** ⚑ (assessed when stable, ≥2 ABGs 3 weeks apart, non-smoker): **PaO₂ ≤7.3 kPa (55 mmHg)**; **or 7.3–8.0 kPa** *with* cor pulmonale/pulmonary hypertension/secondary polycythaemia/nocturnal hypoxaemia. Used ≥15 h/day. (LTOT improves survival **only in chronic hypoxaemia**; it does not help isolated exertional or mild desaturation.)

#### 4.3 Bronchiectasis

Permanent, abnormal **bronchial dilatation** from the **"vicious cycle"** of infection → inflammation → airway damage → impaired clearance → more infection. Causes: **post-infective** (childhood pneumonia, TB, whooping cough), **CF**, immunodeficiency, **ABPA**, primary ciliary dyskinesia (Kartagener = situs inversus + bronchiectasis + sinusitis + infertility), connective-tissue disease, and traction (from fibrosis).

**Clinical:** chronic cough with **copious purulent/mucopurulent sputum**, recurrent infections, haemoptysis, coarse crackles. **HRCT is diagnostic**: **signet-ring sign** (airway wider than its accompanying vessel), **tram-track lines**, lack of tapering. Common colonisers: *Haemophilus influenzae* (commonest), and ***Pseudomonas aeruginosa*** (marks more severe disease/worse prognosis). Management: **airway clearance/physiotherapy**, treat exacerbations with **prolonged (often 14-day) targeted antibiotics**, long-term **azithromycin** for frequent exacerbators, treat the underlying cause, vaccination.

#### 4.4 Cystic Fibrosis

**Autosomal recessive**, mutation in the **CFTR** chloride channel (commonest **ΔF508**), → thick secretions across exocrine organs.
- **Respiratory:** bronchiectasis, chronic infection (**Staph aureus early; Pseudomonas and Burkholderia cepacia later**), nasal polyps, ABPA.
- **GI/other:** pancreatic insufficiency (malabsorption, steatorrhoea, fat-soluble vitamin deficiency), **meconium ileus** in neonates, CF-related diabetes, biliary disease, **male infertility (absent vas deferens)**, salt loss.
- **Diagnosis:** newborn screening (raised immunoreactive trypsinogen), confirmed by the **sweat test (chloride >60 mmol/L)** and/or genetics.

⚑ **CFTR modulator therapy** has transformed prognosis: **elexacaftor/tezacaftor/ivacaftor** ("triple therapy", *Trikafta/Kaftrio*) for eligible genotypes markedly improves lung function and reduces exacerbations — a major shift from purely supportive care. Alongside: airway clearance, mucolytics (**dornase alfa, hypertonic saline**), pancreatic enzyme replacement + vitamins, aggressive infection treatment, and MDT care.

**Differentials**

Bronchitis, acute exacerbation of COPD, inhaled foreign body, cystic fibrosis, PE, pneumothorax

**Acute management**

- Adrenergic agonist e.g. salbutamol (inhaled β2-agonist via MDI or spacer)
- Steroids (PO or IV)
- Theopyine
- Hydration
- Masked O2, IV MgSO4
- Anticholinergic , Adrenaline (if anaphylaxis)

**Long-term management**

GOALS: **↓** symptoms, preserve lung function, prevent permanent lung damage, ↓ drug SE

- Corticosteroid and/or other asthma prophylactic agents – usually through combination devices
- β2 agonist prior to exposure to a known trigger
- Review and adjust drug treatment according to step approach
- Provide information and tools for self-management (asthma management plan)
- Training in correct inhaler technique (MDI)
- Canister shaken
- Patient exhales to FRC
- Aerosol nozzle placed to open mouth
- Patient inhales
- Breath held ~10 second

+ Educate benefits of a spacer

- Information to maximize adherence
- Information about avoiding triggers
- Lifestyle modifications e.g. smoking cessation, diet, immunization and weight control
- Managing flare-ups when they occur
- Regularly reassess: control, spirometry, inhaler technique, adherence, triggers, comorbidities

**Complications**

- STATUS ASTHMATICUS: extreme asthma exacerbation that does not respond to bronchodilators
- AIRWAY REMODELLING: predispose to COPD development

### Respiratory (ANU) — asthma medications — Obstructive Airway Disease

| β2-adrenergic agonists e.g.  salbutamol (SABA) | Selective β2-adrenoreceptor agonists in smooth muscle → smooth muscle relaxation, bronchodilation | SE: tachycardia, arrhythmias, hypokalemia |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anticholinergics ipratropium (short-acting), tiotropium (long-acting) | Competitive antagonists of Ach in bronchial smooth muscles → blocks muscarinic (M3) receptors, → ↓ smooth muscle contraction and airway constriction | SE: dry mouth, nausea, headache |
| Phosphodiesterase inhibitors e.g. theophylline | Inhibits phosphodiesterase → ↑ cAMP levels and smooth muscle relaxation →bronchodilation | SE: arrhythmias, CNS (headache, sleeplessness, seizure, nervousness), ↑ gastric acid secretion, ↑ GORD |
| Glucocorticoid e.g. prednisolone (oral) hydrocortisone (IV) | For prevention not acute attacks- suppresses inflammation and immune response | SE: Cushing’s syndrome, osteoporosis, immunosuppression |
| Leukotriene inhibitors e.g. Montelukast, Zafirlukast, Zileuton | Block cysteinyl leukotrienes in smooth muscles of the airways →inhibits inflammation responsible | SE: Abdominal pain, GI disturbance, Headache |
