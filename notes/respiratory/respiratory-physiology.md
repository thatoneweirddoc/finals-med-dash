## Respiratory Physiology


### Respiratory Master Notes — Respiratory Physiology

#### 1.1 Ventilation and Mechanics

**Lung volumes** — four volumes that don't overlap and four capacities (sums of volumes):
- **Tidal volume (TV)** ~500 mL; **IRV** ~3000 mL; **ERV** ~1100 mL; **residual volume (RV)** ~1200 mL.
- **Capacities**: IC = TV+IRV; **FRC = ERV+RV** (volume at end of quiet expiration — the relaxed equilibrium); VC = TV+IRV+ERV; **TLC = VC+RV**.
- **Discriminator:** spirometry **cannot measure RV, FRC or TLC** (anything containing RV) — you need **body plethysmography or gas dilution**. This is why a "restrictive picture" on spirometry needs lung volumes to confirm.

**Compliance** = ΔV/ΔP (distensibility). High compliance (floppy, easy to inflate) = **emphysema**; low compliance (stiff) = **fibrosis, oedema, ARDS**. The lung's elastic recoil comes from tissue elastin *and* surface tension.

**Surfactant** (type II pneumocytes) lowers alveolar surface tension. By **Laplace (P = 2T/r)** small alveoli would otherwise empty into large ones; surfactant lowers tension *more in small alveoli*, stabilising them and preventing collapse. It also increases compliance and keeps alveoli dry. **Memory hook: surfactant = the lung's "anti-collapse detergent."** Deficiency → neonatal RDS; disruption → part of ARDS.

**Airway resistance** is highest in the **medium-sized bronchi**, not the tiny ones — because the huge *total cross-sectional area* of the many small airways in parallel drops resistance (this is why small-airway disease is "silent" early). During forced expiration the **equal pressure point** moves the point of dynamic collapse into the airways; in emphysema (loss of radial traction) this causes **dynamic airway collapse** and air trapping.

#### 1.2 Gas Exchange and Transport

**Alveolar gas equation:** PAO₂ = FiO₂(P_atm − P_H₂O) − PaCO₂/R ≈ (on room air at sea level) **150 − PaCO₂/0.8**.

**A–a gradient** = PAO₂ − PaO₂. Normal ≈ **5–10 mmHg** in the young; rises with age (rough rule: ≈ age/4 + 4). **This is the single most useful number for classifying hypoxaemia** (see 1.3).

**Oxyhaemoglobin dissociation curve** — sigmoid. **P50 ≈ 27 mmHg** (the PO₂ at 50% saturation).
- **Right shift (offloads O₂ to tissues)**: ↑CO₂, ↑H⁺/↓pH (**Bohr effect**), ↑temperature, ↑2,3-BPG. *Memory: "exercise conditions" — hot, acidic, high CO₂ — dump O₂ where it's needed.*
- **Left shift (holds O₂)**: the opposites, plus **carbon monoxide, methaemoglobin, and fetal Hb (HbF)**.

**CO₂ transport**: ~70% as **bicarbonate** (via carbonic anhydrase in RBCs, chloride shift), ~23% as **carbamino compounds**, ~7% dissolved. **Haldane effect**: deoxygenated Hb carries more CO₂ — so oxygenation in the lung *promotes* CO₂ release (the mirror of the Bohr effect).

**Diffusion** follows **Fick's law** (∝ area × ΔP / thickness). **DLCO/TLCO** (CO uptake) is the clinical measure of the alveolar–capillary membrane.
- **↓DLCO:** emphysema (destroyed surface area), **ILD/fibrosis**, PE/pulmonary vascular disease, anaemia.
- **↑DLCO:** **alveolar haemorrhage** (extra Hb in alveoli soaks up CO), polycythaemia, left-to-right shunt, asthma.
- **Discriminator:** obstruction with **low DLCO = emphysema**; obstruction with **normal/high DLCO = asthma**.

#### 1.3 Ventilation–Perfusion (V/Q) Matching

Ideal V/Q ≈ **0.8** overall. Gravity makes both ventilation and perfusion greater at the **base**, but perfusion increases more — so **V/Q is high at the apex** (more ventilation relative to perfusion → alveolar dead-space-like) and **low at the base** (shunt-like). **V/Q = ∞** is pure dead space (ventilated, not perfused, e.g. PE); **V/Q = 0** is pure shunt (perfused, not ventilated, e.g. consolidation, collapse).

**The five causes of hypoxaemia** — and how to separate them:

| Cause | A–a gradient | Corrects with 100% O₂? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hypoventilation** | **Normal** | Yes | Opioids, T2RF, neuromuscular |
| **Low inspired PO₂** | **Normal** | Yes | Altitude |
| **Diffusion limitation** | Raised | Yes (largely) | ILD, exercise |
| **V/Q mismatch** | Raised | Yes | COPD, asthma, PE, pneumonia |
| **Right-to-left shunt** | Raised | **No** (poorly) | Consolidation, collapse, cardiac shunt, AVM |

**Two discriminators worth memorising:** (1) a **normal A–a gradient** points to hypoventilation or low FiO₂ — the lungs themselves are fine; (2) **failure to correct with high-flow oxygen = shunt**, because shunted blood never meets the alveolar gas.

#### 1.4 Control of Breathing

- **Central chemoreceptors** (medulla) respond to **CSF pH / CO₂** — the *dominant minute-to-minute driver*.
- **Peripheral chemoreceptors** (carotid + aortic bodies) respond mainly to **PaO₂**, but only kick in hard when **PaO₂ falls below ~60 mmHg** (the flat part of the sat curve) — plus CO₂ and pH.

**Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction (HPV):** unique to the lung — hypoxia *constricts* pulmonary vessels (systemically hypoxia dilates). It diverts blood from poorly-ventilated areas, improving V/Q matching. **Chronic, global HPV → pulmonary hypertension and cor pulmonale** (the mechanism in COPD and OSA).

⚑ **The "hypoxic drive" caveat:** the old teaching that giving oxygen to a CO₂-retainer "removes their drive to breathe" is **oversimplified and largely wrong**. Hypercapnia after O₂ in COPD is mostly due to **worsened V/Q matching (reversal of HPV) and the Haldane effect**, not loss of central drive. The practical point stands — **target SpO₂ 88–92%** in known/at-risk CO₂ retainers — but the *mechanism* examiners now expect is V/Q, not "knocking out the drive."

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators — Section 1

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Which volumes can spirometry **not** measure? | Anything containing **RV**: RV, FRC, TLC |
| Stiff lung, low compliance | Fibrosis / ARDS / oedema |
| Floppy lung, high compliance | Emphysema |
| Right shift of O₂ curve (offloads O₂) | ↑CO₂, ↑H⁺, ↑temp, ↑2,3-BPG |
| Left shift | CO, metHb, HbF, alkalosis, cold |
| Obstruction + **low** DLCO | Emphysema |
| Obstruction + **normal/high** DLCO | Asthma |
| Raised DLCO | Alveolar haemorrhage (also polycythaemia, L→R shunt) |
| Hypoxaemia with **normal** A–a gradient | Hypoventilation or low FiO₂ |
| Hypoxaemia that **won't** correct on 100% O₂ | Right-to-left shunt |
| Hypoxia *constricts* these vessels | Pulmonary (HPV) — drives cor pulmonale |
| SpO₂ target in a CO₂ retainer | 88–92% |
