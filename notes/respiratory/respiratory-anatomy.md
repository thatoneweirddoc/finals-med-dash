## Respiratory Anatomy


### Respiratory Master Notes — Respiratory Anatomy

*Written out as prose + tables (not image-only) — pair with `respiratory_anatomy_sheet.svg`.*

#### 2.1 Upper and Lower Airways

Air passes nose/mouth → pharynx → **larynx** (the protective sphincter and voice box; vocal cords at C5–6) → **trachea** (C6 to the **carina at ~T4/5, sternal angle**) → main bronchi → lobar → segmental bronchi → bronchioles → **terminal bronchioles** (end of the **conducting zone**, ~generation 16) → respiratory bronchioles → alveolar ducts → **alveoli** (the **respiratory zone**, gas exchange).

Structural gradient as you descend (worth a table):

| Feature | Trachea/large bronchi | Bronchioles | Alveoli |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cartilage | Yes (C-rings/plates) | **None** (collapse-prone) | None |
| Smooth muscle | Present | **Prominent** (site of bronchospasm) | None |
| Epithelium | Pseudostratified ciliated columnar | Cuboidal, fewer cilia | Type I (gas exchange) + Type II (surfactant) pneumocytes |
| Goblet/mucus | Yes | Club (Clara) cells | None |

The **mucociliary escalator** (cilia + mucus) clears debris upward; smoking, CF and primary ciliary dyskinesia paralyse it → recurrent infection and bronchiectasis. The **conducting zone is anatomical dead space (~150 mL)** — ventilated but no gas exchange.

#### 2.2 Lobes, Fissures and Bronchopulmonary Segments

- **Right lung: 3 lobes** (upper, middle, lower), divided by the **oblique (major)** and **horizontal (minor)** fissures.
- **Left lung: 2 lobes** (upper, lower) + the **lingula** (the middle-lobe equivalent) + a **cardiac notch**. Only an oblique fissure.
- **Discriminator — the right main bronchus is wider, shorter and more vertical**, so **aspirated material and inhaled foreign bodies lodge on the right** (posterior segment of RUL if supine, basal segments if upright). *Memory: "things fall down the right."*

Each lung has ~10 **bronchopulmonary segments** — functionally independent units (own segmental bronchus and artery) that can be resected individually; they explain **postural drainage** positioning and the segmental distribution of aspiration pneumonia.

#### 2.3 Blood Supply, Lymphatics and the Pleura

**Dual circulation:**

| Circulation | Origin | Job | Pressure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pulmonary** | RV → pulmonary arteries (carry **deoxygenated** blood) | **Gas exchange** | Low (~25/8 mmHg) |
| **Bronchial** | Aorta/intercostals | **Nutritive** supply to airway walls | Systemic (high) |

Bronchial arteries are the usual source of **massive haemoptysis** (systemic pressure) — relevant to bronchial artery embolisation. The pulmonary circulation is **low-pressure, low-resistance and high-compliance**, and recruits/distends to accept increased flow — which is why pulmonary hypertension appears late.

**Lymphatic drainage** ascends through **intrapulmonary → hilar → mediastinal (paratracheal, subcarinal) → scalene/supraclavicular** nodes — the anatomical basis of **lung-cancer N-staging** (and why a supraclavicular node is sampled).

**Pleura:** the **visceral** pleura hugs the lung (insensate); the **parietal** pleura lines the chest wall (**pain-sensitive**, somatic + phrenic innervation) — hence **pleuritic pain** localises to the chest wall or refers to the shoulder tip (diaphragmatic/phrenic, C3–5). The **pleural space** holds a thin film of fluid; it becomes an effusion, pneumothorax, empyema or haemothorax when air/fluid/blood collect.

#### 2.4 The Mediastinum, Diaphragm and Surface Anatomy

**Mediastinal compartments** and the exam-favourite masses:

| Compartment | Key contents | Classic mass |
|---|---|---|
| **Anterior** | Thymus, fat, nodes | **The 4 Ts**: Thymoma, Teratoma (germ cell), (terrible) Lymphoma, Thyroid (retrosternal) |
| **Middle** | Heart, great vessels, trachea, nodes | Lymphadenopathy, bronchogenic cyst, aneurysm |
| **Posterior** | Oesophagus, aorta, sympathetic chain | **Neurogenic tumours**, oesophageal lesions |

**Diaphragm:** the principal muscle of inspiration, innervated by the **phrenic nerve (C3, 4, 5 — "keeps the diaphragm alive")**. Three key openings: **T8 IVC** (vena cava), **T10 oesophagus** (+ vagi), **T12 aorta** (+ thoracic duct, azygos). *Memory: "I 8 10 EGGS AT 12" → IVC 8, oEsophagus 10, Aorta 12.* Accessory muscles (sternocleidomastoid, scalenes, intercostals) recruit in distress — visible accessory-muscle use is a sign of severe respiratory compromise.

**Surface anatomy for procedures:**
- **Oblique fissure** ≈ line from T2 spinous process to the 6th rib in the midclavicular line; **horizontal fissure** ≈ 4th rib.
- **Chest drain "triangle of safety"**: bordered by the lateral edge of **pectoralis major**, the anterior edge of **latissimus dorsi**, and a horizontal line at the **nipple (5th intercostal space)** — the base is the axilla apex. Drains and aspiration go **just above a rib** to avoid the **neurovascular bundle** running below each rib.

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators — Section 2

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Aspirated object — which side? | **Right** (wider, shorter, more vertical main bronchus) |
| Carina level | T4/5 (**sternal angle of Louis**) |
| End of the conducting zone / dead space volume | Terminal bronchiole; ~150 mL |
| Pain-sensitive pleura | **Parietal** (visceral is insensate) |
| Shoulder-tip pain from the chest | Diaphragmatic/phrenic irritation (C3–5) |
| Source of **massive** haemoptysis | **Bronchial** (systemic-pressure) arteries |
| Anterior mediastinal mass | **4 Ts** — Thymoma, Teratoma, Lymphoma, Thyroid |
| Posterior mediastinal mass | Neurogenic tumour |
| Phrenic nerve roots | **C3, 4, 5** |
| Diaphragm openings | IVC T8 · oEsophagus T10 · Aorta T12 |
| Needle/drain position relative to rib | **Above** the rib (avoid the sub-costal neurovascular bundle) |
| Triangle of safety borders | Pec major (ant), lat dorsi (post), 5th ICS/nipple line (inf) |
