## Clinical Assessment & Investigation


### Respiratory Master Notes — Clinical Assessment & Investigation

#### 3.1 History and Examination

**Cardinal symptoms and their discriminators:**
- **Dyspnoea** — grade with the **mMRC scale (0–4)**: 0 = only on strenuous exercise; 1 = hurrying/slight hill; 2 = walks slower than peers/stops on the level; 3 = stops after ~100 m; 4 = breathless dressing/housebound. Orthopnoea and PND suggest cardiac, but severe COPD/large effusions cause them too.
- **Cough** — dry (ILD, ACE inhibitor, asthma) vs productive (COPD, bronchiectasis, pneumonia). **>8 weeks = chronic**; think asthma, GORD, post-nasal drip (the "big three"), ACEi, and always exclude cancer/TB in a smoker.
- **Sputum** — copious purulent/mucopurulent daily = **bronchiectasis**; "rusty" = pneumococcal pneumonia; pink frothy = pulmonary oedema; **haemoptysis** demands exclusion of cancer, TB, PE, bronchiectasis.
- **Wheeze** (expiratory, polyphonic = airflow obstruction) vs **stridor** (inspiratory, monophonic = **upper-airway obstruction — an emergency clue**).

**Signs by pathology — the classic exam table** (percussion / breath sounds / vocal resonance / mediastinal shift):

| Pathology | Percussion | Breath sounds | Vocal resonance | Trachea/mediastinum |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Consolidation** | Dull | **Bronchial** + crackles | **Increased** | Central |
| **Pleural effusion** | **Stony dull** | Reduced/absent | Reduced | Pushed **away** (if large) |
| **Pneumothorax** | Resonant/hyper-resonant | Reduced/absent | Reduced | Central (**away** if tension) |
| **Collapse (lobar)** | Dull | Reduced | Reduced | Pulled **towards** |
| **Fibrosis** | Normal/dull | **Fine end-inspiratory ("velcro") crackles** | Normal/increased | Central |

*Two discriminators to lock in:* **effusion pushes, collapse pulls** (the mediastinum); and **consolidation increases vocal resonance, effusion decreases it** — the fastest way to separate the two "dull bases."

**Peripheral signs:** finger **clubbing** (lung cancer, ILD/IPF, bronchiectasis, empyema, CF — *not* uncomplicated COPD or asthma), **cyanosis** (central = <~85% sats), **CO₂-retention flap/bounding pulse/warm peripheries**, and cor pulmonale (raised JVP, peripheral oedema, loud P2, RV heave).

#### 3.2 Pulmonary Function Tests

**Spirometry — the master pattern:**

| Pattern | FEV₁ | FVC | **FEV₁/FVC** | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Obstructive** | ↓↓ | ↓ or normal | **<0.7** (↓) | Asthma, COPD, bronchiectasis |
| **Restrictive** | ↓ | ↓↓ | **≥0.7** (normal or ↑) | ILD/fibrosis, chest-wall, neuromuscular, obesity |
| **Mixed** | ↓↓ | ↓↓ | ↓ | Combined disease |

⚑ **Fixed 0.7 vs the lower limit of normal (LLN):** GOLD still uses a **post-bronchodilator FEV₁/FVC <0.70** to define COPD, but **ATS/ERS 2022 recommend the LLN (z-score < −1.645)** because the fixed ratio over-diagnoses obstruction in the elderly and under-diagnoses in the young. Know both; quote 0.7 for the COPD definition and mention LLN as the more physiological cut-off.

**Reversibility** ⚑ — the ATS/ERS 2022 criterion for a significant bronchodilator response changed to a **>10% change relative to predicted** FEV₁ or FVC (the older "≥12% *and* ≥200 mL" is still widely quoted and taught). Marked reversibility favours asthma; COPD is by definition not fully reversible.

**Lung volumes** (plethysmography/gas dilution — *not* spirometry): **restriction is confirmed by a low TLC**; **obstruction shows raised RV and TLC (gas trapping/hyperinflation)**.

**DLCO/TLCO** adds the "why":
- **Obstruction + low DLCO → emphysema**; **obstruction + normal/high DLCO → asthma**.
- **Restriction + low DLCO → ILD/parenchymal** (intrinsic lung disease).
- **Restriction + normal DLCO → extra-parenchymal** (chest wall, neuromuscular, obesity — the lungs are fine, they just can't be moved).
- **Isolated low DLCO with normal spirometry → pulmonary vascular disease (e.g. PAH), early emphysema, or anaemia.**

**Flow–volume loops:**
- **Obstruction** — scooped-out (concave) expiratory limb.
- **Fixed obstruction** (e.g. tracheal stenosis, goitre) — **flattening of *both* inspiratory and expiratory limbs**.
- **Variable extrathoracic** (e.g. vocal-cord dysfunction) — flat **inspiratory** limb.
- **Variable intrathoracic** (e.g. tracheomalacia) — flat **expiratory** limb.

**Peak expiratory flow (PEF)** — effort-dependent; used for **asthma diagnosis (diurnal variability >10%) and monitoring/exacerbation severity**, expressed as % of personal best or predicted.

#### 3.3 Arterial Blood Gas Interpretation *(the single highest-yield data skill)*

**A systematic approach:**
1. **Oxygenation** — PaO₂ and the **A–a gradient** (PAO₂ ≈ 150 − PaCO₂/0.8 on room air).
2. **pH** — acidaemia (<7.35) or alkalaemia (>7.45).
3. **Respiratory component** — PaCO₂ (>6.0 kPa/45 mmHg = retention).
4. **Metabolic component** — HCO₃⁻/base excess.
5. **Compensation** and whether the picture is **mixed**.

**Respiratory failure types:**

| | **Type 1** | **Type 2** |
|---|---|---|
| PaO₂ | **Low** (<8 kPa / 60 mmHg) | **Low** |
| PaCO₂ | Normal or low | **High** (>6.0 kPa / 45 mmHg) |
| Core problem | **V/Q mismatch / oxygenation failure** | **Ventilatory (pump) failure** |
| Causes | Pneumonia, PE, oedema, ILD, early asthma | COPD, exhausted asthma, opioids, neuromuscular, chest wall, OHS |

**Acute vs chronic type 2 failure** — the exam trap: look at the **pH and bicarbonate**.
- **Acute** T2RF: high CO₂, **low pH, normal HCO₃⁻** (no time to compensate) — e.g. tiring asthmatic (a **normal or rising CO₂ in acute asthma is a red flag** — the patient is exhausting).
- **Chronic** T2RF: high CO₂, **near-normal pH, high HCO₃⁻** (renal compensation) — e.g. stable COPD.
- **Acute-on-chronic**: high CO₂, low pH **and** high HCO₃⁻.

#### 3.4 Imaging and Other Investigations

**Chest X-ray — read systematically** (patient details → adequacy [RIPE: Rotation, Inspiration ≥6 anterior ribs, Penetration, Exposure] → ABCDE: Airway/trachea, Breathing/lung fields, Cardiac, Diaphragm/costophrenic angles, Everything else/bones/soft tissue).
- **Silhouette sign** — loss of a normal border localises pathology: a lost **right heart border = right middle lobe**; lost **left heart border = lingula**; lost **hemidiaphragm = lower lobe**.
- **Consolidation** = air bronchograms, no volume loss. **Collapse** = volume loss (raised hemidiaphragm, tracheal/mediastinal shift *towards*, crowded ribs). **Effusion** = homogeneous opacity with a **meniscus**, blunted costophrenic angle. **Pneumothorax** = a lung edge with absent lung markings peripherally; **tension** = mediastinal shift *away* + depressed hemidiaphragm (but tension is a **clinical** diagnosis — don't wait for the film).

**CT:**
- **HRCT** — the investigation for **ILD** (reticulation, honeycombing, ground-glass) and **bronchiectasis** (signet-ring, tram-track).
- **CTPA** — first-line for **PE**.
- **Staging CT (chest/abdomen ± PET)** for lung cancer.

**Solitary pulmonary nodule pathway** — features favouring malignancy: **larger size, spiculated/irregular margins, upper lobe, older age, smoker, growth over time**; benign clues: **fat (hamartoma), popcorn/laminated/central calcification, stability >2 years**. Size-based CT surveillance vs PET vs biopsy per the risk (British Thoracic Society/Fleischner algorithms).

**Other:** **bronchoscopy** (± **EBUS-TBNA** for mediastinal node sampling/staging), **pleural aspiration/biopsy** (see Section 8), sputum MC&S/AFB/cytology, and blood biomarkers (NT-proBNP to exclude cardiac dyspnoea, D-dimer for PE probability, eosinophils to guide ICS, precipitins/autoantibodies for ILD).

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators — Section 3

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Dull base, **increased** vocal resonance, bronchial breathing | Consolidation |
| **Stony** dull base, **reduced** breath sounds & resonance | Pleural effusion |
| Mediastinum pushed **away** | Large effusion / tension pneumothorax |
| Mediastinum pulled **towards** | Lobar collapse |
| Fine end-inspiratory "velcro" crackles | Pulmonary fibrosis |
| Inspiratory **stridor** | Upper-airway obstruction (emergency) |
| Clubbing + lung disease | Cancer, IPF, bronchiectasis, empyema, CF (**not** COPD/asthma) |
| FEV₁/FVC <0.7 | Obstructive |
| FEV₁/FVC ≥0.7 with low FVC & low TLC | Restrictive |
| Restriction + **low** DLCO | Intrinsic (ILD) |
| Restriction + **normal** DLCO | Extra-parenchymal (chest wall/neuromuscular/obesity) |
| Isolated low DLCO, normal spirometry | Pulmonary vascular disease / early emphysema / anaemia |
| Flat **both** loop limbs | Fixed large-airway obstruction |
| Flat **inspiratory** limb only | Variable extrathoracic (e.g. vocal-cord dysfunction) |
| High CO₂, low pH, **normal** HCO₃⁻ | **Acute** type 2 failure |
| High CO₂, normal pH, **high** HCO₃⁻ | **Chronic** type 2 failure (compensated) |
| Rising/normal CO₂ in acute severe asthma | **Exhaustion — life-threatening** |
| Lost right heart border on CXR | Right middle lobe |
| Lost left heart border on CXR | Lingula |
