## Lung Cancer & Mediastinal Disease


### Respiratory Master Notes — Lung Cancer & Mediastinal Disease

#### 9.1 Lung Cancer

Leading cause of cancer death; **smoking** is the dominant risk (also asbestos, radon, occupational carcinogens; note **adenocarcinoma is the commonest type in non-smokers**). **The treatment-defining split is small-cell vs non-small-cell.**

| | **NSCLC (~85%)** | **SCLC (~15%)** |
|---|---|---|
| Subtypes | **Adenocarcinoma** (commonest, peripheral, non-smokers), **squamous** (central, cavitates, **hypercalcaemia via PTHrP**), large cell | Neuroendocrine, **central**, aggressive |
| Behaviour | Slower; **staged surgically if early** | **Very aggressive**, usually **metastatic at diagnosis** |
| Treatment | Surgery/SABR (early) → chemo-radiotherapy, **targeted therapy & immunotherapy** by molecular markers | **Chemotherapy ± radiotherapy** (rarely surgical); often responds then relapses |
| Paraneoplastic | **Squamous → hypercalcaemia (PTHrP)** | **SIADH, ectopic ACTH (Cushing's), LEMS** |

**Presentation:**
- **Local:** cough, haemoptysis, dyspnoea, recurrent/non-resolving pneumonia, wheeze, chest pain.
- **Local invasion syndromes:** **Pancoast tumour** (apex) → **Horner syndrome** (ptosis, miosis, anhidrosis) + T1 wasting/arm pain; **hoarseness** (recurrent laryngeal nerve); **SVC obstruction** (facial/arm swelling, distended veins, Pemberton's sign — **an oncological emergency**); phrenic nerve → diaphragm palsy; dysphagia; malignant effusion.
- **Metastatic:** liver, bone (pain, hypercalcaemia), brain (headache, seizures, focal signs), adrenal.
- **Paraneoplastic:** **SIADH** (SCLC), **ectopic ACTH** (SCLC), **hypercalcaemia** (squamous, PTHrP), **Lambert–Eaton myasthenic syndrome** (SCLC), **clubbing/hypertrophic pulmonary osteoarthropathy**.

⚑ **Staging — TNM 9th edition (in clinical use from 1 January 2025):** the framework is unchanged but nodal and metastatic categories are subdivided for prognostic precision:
- **N2 is now split** into **N2a** (single ipsilateral mediastinal/subcarinal nodal station) and **N2b** (multiple N2 stations) — because single-station N2 has a better prognosis. (N1 and N3 unchanged.)
- **M1c is split** into **M1c1** (multiple extrathoracic metastases in a **single** organ system) and **M1c2** (multiple organ systems). (T categories and M1a/M1b unchanged.)
- Investigations: **contrast CT chest/upper abdomen**, **PET-CT** for staging, **MRI brain**, and **tissue** via bronchoscopy/**EBUS-TBNA** (also samples mediastinal nodes) or image-guided biopsy; **molecular testing (EGFR, ALK, ROS1, PD-L1, etc.)** guides targeted therapy in NSCLC.
- **Modern therapy:** **targeted agents** (EGFR/ALK inhibitors) and **immune checkpoint inhibitors** (anti-PD-1/PD-L1) have substantially changed advanced-NSCLC outcomes — a key contrast with older "chemo only" teaching. ⚑
- **Screening:** **low-dose CT** screening for high-risk (heavy) smokers is now recommended/implemented in several countries. ⚑

#### 9.2 The Solitary Pulmonary Nodule

A single ≤3 cm rounded opacity. Weigh malignancy risk: **larger, spiculated/irregular, upper-lobe, older, smoker, growth** favour cancer; **benign calcification patterns (central, laminated, popcorn = hamartoma), fat, and 2-year stability** favour benign. Manage by risk with **CT surveillance vs PET-CT vs biopsy/resection** (BTS/Fleischner algorithms). *(A nodule >8 mm with intermediate/high risk generally goes to PET-CT ± biopsy.)*

#### 9.3 Mediastinal Masses

Localise by compartment (see Section 2): **anterior = 4 Ts** (**Thymoma** — associated with **myasthenia gravis, red-cell aplasia, hypogammaglobulinaemia**; **Teratoma/germ-cell**; **Lymphoma**; retrosternal **Thyroid**); **middle** = lymphadenopathy, vascular, cysts; **posterior** = **neurogenic tumours**. Metastatic disease to the lung is common (breast, colon, renal, sarcoma → "cannonball" metastases with renal/choriocarcinoma).

**Differentials**

- Lung metastases
- Pulmonary neuroendocrine tumour
- Benign tumours (hamartomas “popcorn” classifications)
- Infectious granulomas (Tb, non-Tb mycobacteria, histoplasmosis)
- Inflammatory conditions (sarcoidosis, Wegener’s)
