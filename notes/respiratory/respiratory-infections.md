## Respiratory Infections


### Respiratory Master Notes — Respiratory Infections

#### 7.1 Pneumonia

**Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP)** — fever, productive cough, pleuritic pain, dyspnoea; signs of **consolidation** (bronchial breathing, dull percussion, increased vocal resonance, crackles); CXR consolidation.

**Severity — CURB-65** (1 point each): **C**onfusion (AMTS ≤8), **U**rea >7 mmol/L, **R**espiratory rate ≥30, **B**P <90 systolic or ≤60 diastolic, age **≥65**.
- **0–1** → home/oral; **2** → hospital (consider); **≥3** → severe, consider ICU. *(CRB-65 omits urea for primary-care use.)*

**Typical vs atypical:**

| | Typical | Atypical |
|---|---|---|
| Organisms | ***Strep pneumoniae*** (commonest overall), *H. influenzae*, *Moraxella*, *Staph aureus* | *Mycoplasma*, *Legionella*, *Chlamydophila*, *Coxiella* |
| Features | Lobar consolidation, rusty sputum | Extra-pulmonary features, dry cough, CXR worse than exam |
| Antibiotic | β-lactam (amoxicillin) | **Macrolide/tetracycline/fluoroquinolone** (atypicals lack a cell wall → β-lactams don't work) |

**Empirical therapy** (per local/NICE guidance): **low severity → amoxicillin** (or doxycycline/macrolide if penicillin-allergic); **moderate → amoxicillin + macrolide**; **high severity → co-amoxiclav (or cephalosporin) + macrolide**, IV. Add **antipseudomonal cover** in bronchiectasis/structural disease. Review at 48–72 h; step down.

**Organism-specific clues (exam gold):**

| Clue | Organism |
|---|---|
| **Hyponatraemia, deranged LFTs, air-con/hotel/water source, diarrhoea** | ***Legionella*** (urinary antigen) |
| Young adult, **erythema multiforme, haemolytic anaemia (cold agglutinins), dry cough** | ***Mycoplasma*** |
| **Cavitation, post-influenza, IVDU** | ***Staph aureus*** |
| Abattoir/farm animal/parturient exposure | ***Coxiella burnetii*** (Q fever) |
| Bird exposure | *Chlamydophila psittaci* (psittacosis) |
| **HIV/immunocompromised, exertional desaturation, dry cough, bilateral perihilar infiltrates, ↑LDH** | ***Pneumocystis jirovecii*** (PCP → high-dose **co-trimoxazole**) |
| Currant-jelly sputum, alcoholic/diabetic, upper-lobe cavitation | *Klebsiella* |

**Complications:** **para-pneumonic effusion → empyema** (see Section 8 — pleural fluid **pH <7.2 needs a chest drain**), **lung abscess** (swinging fever, cavity with air–fluid level; aspiration/anaerobes), sepsis, respiratory failure.

**Hospital-acquired pneumonia (HAP)** (>48 h after admission) and **VAP** — Gram-negatives (incl. *Pseudomonas*), MRSA; broader empirical cover. **Aspiration pneumonia** — dependent segments (right lower/posterior upper lobe), anaerobes; risk in stroke, reduced consciousness, swallowing disorders.

#### 7.2 Tuberculosis

*Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, spread by respiratory droplets. **Primary** (Ghon focus + hilar node = Ghon complex, often asymptomatic) vs **post-primary/reactivation** (apical, cavitating). **Latent TB** = infected, asymptomatic, non-infectious; can reactivate (risk ↑ with **HIV, anti-TNF, immunosuppression, diabetes**).

**Clinical:** chronic cough, **haemoptysis, weight loss, night sweats, fever**, lethargy. **Extra-pulmonary:** lymphadenitis (scrofula), pleural, **miliary** (haematogenous), CNS (meningitis, tuberculoma), spinal (**Pott's disease**), GU, adrenal, pericardial.

**Diagnosis:** **≥3 sputum samples for AFB smear (Ziehl–Neelsen) and culture** (culture is the gold standard but slow), **NAAT/GeneXpert** (rapid, detects rifampicin resistance), CXR (apical cavitation, upper-lobe fibrosis). **Latent TB:** **IGRA** or **tuberculin skin test (Mantoux)** — neither distinguishes latent from active.

**Standard treatment — "RIPE" for 2 months, then RI for 4 (6 months total):** **R**ifampicin, **I**soniazid, **P**yrazinamide, **E**thambutol. Learn the toxicities:

| Drug | Signature toxicity |
|---|---|
| **Rifampicin** | **Orange secretions**, enzyme **inducer** (↓ OCP/warfarin), hepatitis |
| **Isoniazid** | **Peripheral neuropathy** (give **pyridoxine/B6**), hepatitis |
| **Pyrazinamide** | Hepatitis, **hyperuricaemia/gout** |
| **Ethambutol** | **Optic neuritis** (colour vision/acuity — check before starting) |

**MDR-TB** (resistant to rifampicin + isoniazid) needs prolonged specialist regimens. TB is **notifiable**; do **contact tracing**; supervise adherence (DOT) where needed.

#### 7.3 Other Infections

**Influenza** — sudden fever, myalgia, dry cough; **oseltamivir** if early/high-risk; complications include secondary bacterial (especially **Staph**) pneumonia. **COVID-19** — spectrum from mild URTI to viral pneumonitis/ARDS; risk-stratify by hypoxia. **Aspergillus spectrum:** **aspergilloma** (fungus ball in a pre-existing cavity — haemoptysis), **ABPA** (asthmatics — see Section 4), **invasive aspergillosis** (immunocompromised — angioinvasion, halo sign). **Paediatric-relevant:** bronchiolitis (RSV, infants), croup (parainfluenza — barking cough, stridor), whooping cough (*Bordetella pertussis* — paroxysmal cough, whoop). Always consider the **immunocompromised host** (PCP, CMV, fungi, atypical mycobacteria).

**Presentation**

TYPICAL

- High fever and chills; malaise
- Productive cough with purulent sputum
- Pleuritic chest pain (which can project to epigastric region, particularly in children)
- Respiratory exam: ↑HR, ↑RR,  lung percussion dullness, coarse wet inspiratory crackles, bronchial breath sounds, ↑ vocal resonance/fremitus, ↓ chest expansion

ATYPICAL

- Low fever, no chills
- Non-productive, dry cough
- Dyspnoea
- Extrapulmonary: fatigue, headaches, sore throat, myalgias, eryhthema multiforme, auscultation often remarkable

**Investigations**

- BLOOD: ↑ESR, ↑CRP, BUN, leucocytosis, ↑procalcitonin, troponin/CK
- ABG: rule out respiratory failure
- SPUTUM/BLOOD CULTURE, PCR/ANTIBODY (chlamydia)
- URINE: legionella antigen, pneumococcal antigen
- CXR:
- Lobar pneumonia: extensive opacity in one lobe, air bronchogram, unilateral pleural effusion
- Bronchopneumonia: patchy infiltrates, air bronchogram
- Atypical/interstitial: diffuse reticular opacity, absent (or minimal) consolidation)
- Consider thorax CT, pleurocentesis, bronchoscopy

**CURB-65 Criteria for Hospitalisation**

- Confusion: disorientation, impaired consciousness
- Urea ≥ 20 mg/dL
- Respiratory rate ≥ 30/min
- Blood pressure: systolic BP ≤ 90 mm Hg or diastolic BP ≤ 60 mm Hg
- Age ≥ 65 years
- Interpretation
- Each finding is appointed 1 point; max. 5 points possible
- CURB-65 score ≤ 1: Outpatient
- CURB-65 score ≥ 2: Hospitalization
- CURB-65 score ≥ 4: ICU

**Treatment**

- GENERAL

- Rest with regular pulse oximetry monitoring
- Physiotherapy to encourage cough
- Supplemental O2
- IVF to avoid dehydration
- Medication: salbutamol, analgesia, antipyretics, mucolytics, anti-tussive (e.g. codeine)

- EMPIRICAL TREATMENT WITH ANTIBIOTICS

- Macrolide (azithromycin),
- Doxycline
- Fluoroquinolone (lecofloxacin, moxifloxacin)
- Antipneumococcal beta-lactam (amoxicillin ± clavulanate; ceftriaxone; cefotaxime)
- Antipseudomonal beta-lactam (tazobactam)
- Add vancomycin if MRSA suspected

**Complications**

Pleuritis, pleural effusion, empyema, sepsis, respiratory failure, ARDs, lung abscess

**Prevention**

Pneumococcal and influenza vaccine, smoking cessation
