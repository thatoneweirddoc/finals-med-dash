## Pleural Disease & Pneumothorax (acute — high yield)


### Respiratory Master Notes — Pleural Disease & Pneumothorax (acute — high yield)

#### 8.1 Pleural Effusion

Fluid in the pleural space. **Step one is always: transudate or exudate?** — because it splits the differential in half.

⚑ **Light's criteria** — the fluid is an **exudate** if **any one** of:
1. Pleural **protein / serum protein > 0.5**, or
2. Pleural **LDH / serum LDH > 0.6**, or
3. Pleural **LDH > two-thirds the upper limit of normal serum LDH**.

*Light's criteria are sensitive for exudates but mislabel ~25% of transudates as exudates; if the clinical picture says transudate (e.g. heart failure on diuretics) but Light's says exudate, check the **serum–pleural albumin gradient (>12 g/L favours transudate)**.*

| | **Transudate** (protein <25 g/L) | **Exudate** (protein >35 g/L) |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | ↑hydrostatic / ↓oncotic pressure | ↑capillary permeability (inflammation) |
| Causes | **Heart failure** (commonest), cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome, hypoalbuminaemia | **Pneumonia (para-pneumonic), malignancy, TB, PE, pancreatitis, RA/SLE** |

**Other pleural-fluid clues (the lookup table):**

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| **pH <7.2** in a para-pneumonic effusion | **Empyema / complicated effusion → chest drain** |
| **Low glucose** | Empyema, **RA**, TB, malignancy |
| **Very high amylase** | Pancreatitis, oesophageal rupture |
| **Milky (high triglyceride)** | Chylothorax (thoracic duct) |
| **Blood-stained** | Malignancy, PE, trauma, TB |
| **↑Adenosine deaminase / lymphocytes** | **TB** |
| Positive cytology | Malignant effusion |

Investigate an undiagnosed exudate with **pleural aspiration (protein, LDH, pH, glucose, cytology, microbiology, ADA)**, then **contrast CT and image-guided/thoracoscopic pleural biopsy** if malignancy suspected. Management: treat the cause; **therapeutic aspiration/drainage** for symptoms; **indwelling pleural catheter or pleurodesis** for recurrent malignant effusions. **A unilateral undiagnosed effusion in a smoker is cancer until proven otherwise.**

#### 8.2 Pneumothorax

Air in the pleural space → lung collapse.
- **Primary spontaneous** — no underlying lung disease; **tall, thin, young males, smokers** (apical bleb rupture).
- **Secondary spontaneous** — underlying disease (**COPD**, asthma, CF, ILD, PJP) — more dangerous because of poor reserve.
- **Traumatic/iatrogenic** — subclavian line, pleural procedure, ventilation.
- **Tension pneumothorax** — a **one-way valve** progressively traps air → mediastinal shift, obstructed venous return, cardiovascular collapse.

⚑ **Tension pneumothorax is a clinical diagnosis — do NOT wait for a CXR.** Signs: severe respiratory distress, hypotension/tachycardia, **tracheal deviation *away***, absent breath sounds + hyper-resonance, raised JVP. **Immediate treatment: large-bore needle decompression** (traditionally **2nd intercostal space, midclavicular line**; ⚑ many guidelines now favour the **4th/5th ICS anterior axillary line** — the same site as a chest drain — as more reliable in adults), then a chest drain.

⚑ **Updated (BTS 2023) management pathway — a shift from a purely size-based algorithm to a symptom-based one:** the primary question is now **"is the patient symptomatic and stable?"** rather than a fixed size cut-off.
- **Minimal symptoms and stable** → often **conservative (observation)**, even for larger primary pneumothoraces, with review.
- **Symptomatic** → **needle aspiration or drainage**; **ambulatory devices** (e.g. Heimlich-type one-way valves) are now an option in selected patients.
- **Secondary** pneumothorax and any instability → lower threshold to drain and admit.
- Recurrence prevention: **VATS pleurodesis/pleurectomy**; advise **no flying until resolved and no diving ever** (for those with a history).

#### 8.3 Pleural Malignancy and Other

**Mesothelioma** — malignancy of the pleura, strongly linked to **asbestos** (long latency, 20–40 years); presents with **breathlessness, pleuritic pain, unilateral pleural effusion/thickening**; poor prognosis; **industrial compensation** implications — always take an occupational history. **Pleural metastases** (lung, breast, ovary, lymphoma) are the commonest cause of a malignant effusion. **Haemothorax** (blood), **chylothorax** (lymph, thoracic duct injury/lymphoma), and **empyema** (pus) complete the "what's in the space" differential.

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators — Section 8

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| First split for any effusion | Transudate vs exudate (**Light's criteria**) |
| Exudate if… | Pleural/serum protein >0.5, LDH ratio >0.6, or LDH >⅔ ULN |
| Commonest transudate | Heart failure |
| Para-pneumonic effusion pH <7.2 | Empyema → **chest drain** |
| Low pleural glucose | Empyema, RA, TB, malignancy |
| Milky pleural fluid | Chylothorax |
| Very high pleural amylase | Pancreatitis / oesophageal rupture |
| ↑ADA + lymphocytes | TB |
| Tall thin young male smoker, sudden SOB | Primary spontaneous pneumothorax |
| Trachea deviated **away** + shock + hyper-resonance | **Tension** pneumothorax (needle decompress **now**) |
| Pneumothorax management driver (BTS 2023) | **Symptoms/stability**, not size alone ⚑ |
| Asbestos + unilateral pleural effusion/thickening | Mesothelioma |
| Post-central-line sudden desaturation | Iatrogenic pneumothorax |
