## Obstetric Emergencies

### Finals Tutor — Obstetric Emergencies

#### Antepartum haemorrhage

Bleeding from 20 weeks to delivery. The two that matter:

| | Placenta praevia | Placental abruption |
|---|---|---|
| Pain | **Painless** | **Painful** |
| Uterus | Soft, non-tender | Tender, woody-hard |
| Bleeding | Bright red, revealed | May be **concealed** — blood loss underestimated |
| Fetal condition | Usually normal initially | Distress common |
| Shock | Proportional to visible loss | **Out of proportion** to visible loss |

**Never perform a digital vaginal examination in APH until placenta praevia is excluded by ultrasound** — a finger against a praevia can disrupt the placenta and cause catastrophic haemorrhage. Speculum is acceptable. This is examined repeatedly.

Abruption risk factors: hypertension/pre-eclampsia, trauma, smoking, cocaine, previous abruption, polyhydramnios with sudden decompression, thrombophilia. Complications: DIC, acute kidney injury, fetal death, Couvelaire uterus.

#### Postpartum haemorrhage

≥500 mL after vaginal birth, ≥1000 mL after caesarean; or any loss causing haemodynamic compromise. Primary = within 24 hours; secondary = 24 hours to 6 weeks.

**The four Ts:**
- **Tone** — uterine atony, the commonest cause by far
- **Trauma** — genital tract lacerations, uterine rupture, inversion
- **Tissue** — retained placenta or clot
- **Thrombin** — coagulopathy (DIC, anticoagulants, inherited)

**Management** — simultaneous resuscitation and treatment:
1. Call for help, ABC, two large-bore IVs, bloods including group and crossmatch, fluids, activate massive transfusion protocol if needed
2. **Rub up the fundus**, empty bladder (catheter)
3. **Uterotonics in sequence**: oxytocin → ergometrine (avoid in hypertension/pre-eclampsia) → carboprost (avoid in asthma) → misoprostol
4. **Tranexamic acid** — give early, within 3 hours
5. Examine for trauma and repair; check placenta is complete
6. **Mechanical/surgical**: bimanual compression, intrauterine balloon tamponade, B-Lynch suture, uterine artery ligation, internal iliac ligation, **hysterectomy** as last resort

Note the drug contraindications — ergometrine in hypertension, carboprost in asthma. Both are common exam discriminators.

#### Shoulder dystocia

Failure of the shoulders to deliver after the head, requiring additional manoeuvres. Obstetric emergency — risk of hypoxia, brachial plexus injury (Erb's palsy), fracture.

**HELPERR:**
- **H** — call for Help
- **E** — Evaluate for Episiotomy
- **L** — **Legs** — McRoberts manoeuvre (hyperflexed hips) — the single most effective first step
- **P** — suprapubic **Pressure**
- **E** — **Enter** the pelvis for internal rotational manoeuvres (Rubin, Woods screw)
- **R** — **Remove** the posterior arm
- **R** — **Roll** the patient onto all fours

**Do not apply fundal pressure** — it worsens impaction and risks uterine rupture.

#### Cord prolapse

Cord descends past the presenting part after membrane rupture. Risk factors: abnormal lie, polyhydramnios, prematurity, multiple pregnancy, ARM with a high presenting part.

Management: **call for help, do not handle the cord** (vasospasm), elevate the presenting part manually or by filling the bladder, position knee-chest or left lateral with head down, and **immediate delivery — usually category 1 caesarean**.

#### Amniotic fluid embolism

Rare, catastrophic. Sudden cardiovascular collapse, hypoxia, seizures, and **DIC** around delivery. Management is supportive: resuscitation, oxygenation, massive transfusion protocol, correct coagulopathy. Diagnosis is clinical and one of exclusion.

#### Maternal sepsis

A leading cause of direct maternal death. Sources: chorioamnionitis, endometritis, wound, mastitis, UTI, pneumonia. Physiological changes of pregnancy mask deterioration — tachycardia and a normal-looking BP can precede sudden collapse. Apply the sepsis bundle: **antibiotics within 1 hour**, cultures, fluids, lactate, source control.
