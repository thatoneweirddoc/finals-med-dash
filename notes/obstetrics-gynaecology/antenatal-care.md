## Antenatal Care and Screening

### Finals Tutor — Antenatal Care and Screening

#### Visit schedule (uncomplicated primigravida)

Booking by 10 weeks, then ~16, 20, 24, 28, 31, 34, 36, 38, 40, 41 weeks. Multiparous women have fewer visits. Each visit: BP, urinalysis, fundal height (from 24 weeks), fetal movements, presentation from 36 weeks.

#### Booking bloods and screening

| Test | Purpose |
|---|---|
| FBC | Anaemia, platelets baseline |
| Blood group + antibody screen | Rh status, alloimmunisation |
| Rubella IgG | Immunity — vaccinate **postpartum** (live vaccine, contraindicated in pregnancy) |
| Syphilis, HIV, hepatitis B, hepatitis C | Vertical transmission — all treatable/modifiable |
| MSU | Asymptomatic bacteriuria — **treat**, reduces pyelonephritis and preterm birth |
| Ferritin, vitamin D | Common deficiencies |

#### Aneuploidy screening

- **NIPT (cell-free DNA)** — from 10 weeks, screening not diagnostic, very high sensitivity for T21. Not Medicare-funded in Australia.
- **Combined first trimester screening (cFTS)** — 11+0 to 13+6 weeks: nuchal translucency + PAPP-A + free β-hCG + maternal age.
- **Diagnostic**: CVS (11–14 weeks) or amniocentesis (from 15 weeks). Amniocentesis carries ~0.5–1% miscarriage risk; CVS slightly higher.

#### Ultrasound

- Dating scan (first trimester — crown-rump length is the most accurate dating measure)
- **Morphology scan 18–20 weeks**
- Growth scans if clinically indicated (fundal height discrepancy, risk factors)

#### Gestational diabetes

**OGTT at 24–28 weeks** (earlier if risk factors: BMI >30, previous GDM, family history, PCOS, high-risk ethnicity, previous macrosomia). 75g OGTT diagnostic thresholds (ADIPS):

| Timepoint | Threshold (mmol/L) |
|---|---|
| Fasting | ≥5.1 |
| 1 hour | ≥10.0 |
| 2 hour | ≥8.5 |

**Any one** abnormal value is diagnostic. Management: diet and exercise first, then metformin and/or insulin. Insulin remains first-line pharmacological therapy in many Australian units.

#### Group B Streptococcus

Two acceptable approaches in Australia — **universal screening** (vaginal-rectal swab at 35–37 weeks) or **risk-factor based**. Intrapartum IV benzylpenicillin if positive or risk factors present (prematurity <37 weeks, ROM >18 hours, intrapartum fever, previous GBS-affected infant, GBS bacteriuria this pregnancy).

#### Anti-D prophylaxis — an Australian-specific number

**625 IU IM at 28 and 34 weeks** for Rh D negative women with no preformed anti-D antibodies (NBA/RANZCOG). Note the trap: **the UK uses a single 1500 IU dose at 28 weeks** — answer the Australian regimen.

Also give anti-D after any **sensitising event**: miscarriage or termination (after 10 weeks), ectopic, molar pregnancy, CVS/amniocentesis, APH, abdominal trauma, external cephalic version, and **postpartum if the baby is Rh D positive** (with Kleihauer to quantify feto-maternal haemorrhage and guide additional dosing).

#### Supplements

- **Folic acid 500 mcg** daily, from at least 1 month preconception to 12 weeks — reduces neural tube defects. **5 mg** if high risk (previous NTD, diabetes, BMI >30, antiepileptics).
- **Iodine 150 mcg** daily throughout pregnancy and breastfeeding.
- Vitamin D if deficient.

#### Vaccination in pregnancy

- **Pertussis (dTpa)** — every pregnancy, ideally 20–32 weeks
- **Influenza** — any trimester
- **RSV (maternal)** — now recommended in Australia; check current ATAGI advice
- **Avoid live vaccines**: MMR, varicella — give postpartum
