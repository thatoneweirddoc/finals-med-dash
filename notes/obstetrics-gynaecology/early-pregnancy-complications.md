## Early Pregnancy Complications

### Finals Tutor — Early Pregnancy Complications

#### The first principle

Any woman of reproductive age with abdominal pain or PV bleeding gets a **pregnancy test**. Ectopic pregnancy is the diagnosis that kills, and it is missed by not thinking of it.

#### Ectopic pregnancy

Implantation outside the endometrial cavity; ~1 in 100 pregnancies. **Ampulla is the commonest site (~70%)**, then isthmus, fimbrial, ovarian, interstitial, abdominal.

**Aetiology** — roughly half follow tubal cilia damage from PID. Other risks: previous ectopic, tubal or pelvic surgery, IVF, IUD in situ at conception, endometriosis, smoking.

**Presentation** — amenorrhoea with positive hCG, abdominal pain, PV bleeding. PV bleeding may be **disproportionately light** relative to blood loss, because haemorrhage is intra-abdominal. Rupture: shoulder tip pain (diaphragmatic irritation), syncope, shock, peritonism.

**Investigation**
- Serum hCG — in a normal intrauterine pregnancy it roughly **doubles every 48 hours**; a rise <~50% is suspicious of non-viable or ectopic
- Transvaginal ultrasound — absent intrauterine pregnancy with hCG above the discriminatory zone (~1500–2000 IU/L) suggests ectopic
- FBC, group and hold
- **An unstable patient goes to theatre — do not wait for imaging**

**Management**
- *Expectant* — low and falling hCG, asymptomatic, reliable follow-up
- *Medical* — **methotrexate**, requires stable patient, unruptured, mass <3.5 cm, hCG below threshold, no fetal cardiac activity, reliable follow-up. Avoid pregnancy for 3 months after.
- *Surgical* — laparoscopic **salpingectomy** (preferred if contralateral tube healthy) or **salpingostomy** (if fertility concern with damaged contralateral tube; requires hCG follow-up for persistent trophoblast)

Give **anti-D** if Rh negative.

#### Miscarriage

Pregnancy loss before 20 weeks. Occurs in ~15–20% of clinical pregnancies; most are sporadic chromosomal abnormalities.

**Management of missed/incomplete miscarriage — three options** (a favourite exam structure):
1. **Expectant** — await spontaneous passage, appropriate if stable and no infection
2. **Medical** — misoprostol (± mifepristone)
3. **Surgical** — dilatation and curettage / suction curettage. Indicated for haemodynamic instability, sepsis, or patient preference.

**Consenting for D&C** — worth rehearsing, as WSU has asked this: explain the procedure (cervical dilatation and evacuation of retained products under anaesthetic), the benefits, and the risks — bleeding, infection, uterine perforation, cervical trauma, **Asherman syndrome** (intrauterine adhesions), retained products requiring repeat procedure, and anaesthetic risks. Confirm understanding, alternatives (expectant, medical), and voluntariness.

**Prognosis counselling**: a single sporadic miscarriage does **not** meaningfully reduce the chance of a subsequent successful pregnancy — around 80–85% go on to term. Recurrent miscarriage (≥3 consecutive) warrants investigation: antiphospholipid syndrome, parental karyotype, uterine anatomy, thyroid and diabetes.

#### Hyperemesis gravidarum

Beyond normal nausea and vomiting: weight loss (>5%), dehydration, ketonuria, electrolyte disturbance. Associated with high hCG states — **molar pregnancy and multiple pregnancy** — so scan. Management: IV fluids with potassium, **thiamine** (prevents Wernicke's), antiemetics (metoclopramide, ondansetron, doxylamine-pyridoxine), thromboprophylaxis if admitted.

#### Gestational trophoblastic disease

Complete mole (46XX, entirely paternal, no fetal tissue) or partial mole (triploid, some fetal tissue). Presents with bleeding, uterus large for dates, hyperemesis, very high hCG, "snowstorm" on ultrasound, early pre-eclampsia. Management: suction evacuation and **serial hCG follow-up** to detect persistent trophoblastic disease or choriocarcinoma. Avoid pregnancy until hCG normalises.
