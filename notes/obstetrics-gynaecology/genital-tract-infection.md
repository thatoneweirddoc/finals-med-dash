## Genital Tract Infection


### O&G Master Notes — Genital Tract Infection

*Australian practice: STI Guidelines Australia (ASHM), Therapeutic Guidelines, RANZCOG statements, Queensland Clinical Guidelines, KEMH/SA/Safer Care Victoria perinatal guidelines, Australian Immunisation Handbook. Scope: vaginal discharge, PID, STIs with obstetric consequence, GBS, TSS, antenatal infection screening. Where AU diverges from NICE/RCOG it is flagged.*

#### 13.1 Vaginal discharge — the three-way discrimination

The most reliably examined discrimination in gynae. Almost every MCQ hangs on **pH, itch, and odour** — learn the triad as a unit.

Physiological discharge is white/clear, non-offensive, cycle-varying, pH 3.8–4.5, needs no treatment. Anything alkaline (**pH >4.5**) is BV or trichomonas until proven otherwise, because *Lactobacillus*-driven acidity has been lost. **Candida does not raise the pH** — that fact alone separates it from the other two.

| Feature | Bacterial vaginosis | Vulvovaginal candidiasis | Trichomoniasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Organism | Polymicrobial overgrowth — *Gardnerella vaginalis*, *Atopobium*, anaerobes; loss of lactobacilli | *Candida albicans* (~90%); *C. glabrata* in azole-refractory disease | *Trichomonas vaginalis* — flagellated protozoan |
| STI? | **Not classified as an STI** (sexually associated) | **No** | **Yes** — the only true STI of the three |
| Discharge | **Thin, homogeneous, grey/milky**, adherent | **Thick, curdy, white, "cottage cheese"** | **Frothy, yellow-green**, profuse |
| Odour | **Fishy, worse post-coital/after menses** | None | Offensive, may be malodorous |
| Itch | **Characteristically absent** | **Dominant symptom** — itch, soreness, dyspareunia | Irritation, soreness, sometimes itch |
| Vulval signs | Normal vulva | Erythema, fissures, satellite lesions, excoriation | Vulvitis; **"strawberry cervix"** (punctate haemorrhages, ~2% only) |
| pH | **>4.5** | **Normal (≤4.5)** | **>4.5** |
| Microscopy | **Clue cells** (epithelial cells studded with coccobacilli, blurred borders) | Pseudohyphae/budding yeasts on KOH | **Motile flagellated trichomonads** on wet mount |
| Whiff (KOH) test | **Positive** | Negative | May be positive |
| Best test | Amsel criteria / Gram stain (Nugent score) | Microscopy + culture (culture if recurrent, to speciate) | **NAAT** on vaginal swab (far more sensitive than wet mount) |

**Amsel criteria** — 3 of 4 = BV: thin homogeneous discharge; **pH >4.5**; **positive whiff test**; **clue cells**. Nugent score is the Gram-stain research standard (0–3 normal, 4–6 intermediate, 7–10 BV).

Hook: **B**V = **B**lurred cells (clue cells) and **B**ase (alkaline), **no itch**. Candida = **C**urds, **C**omfortable pH. Trich = **T**ravels sexually, **T**witching organisms.

**Treatment (Australian, STI Guidelines Australia)**
- **BV**: metronidazole 400 mg PO bd 7 days with food, **or** metronidazole 0.75% gel intravaginally nightly 5 nights, **or** clindamycin 2% vaginal cream nightly 7 nights. Recurrence is the rule (>50% at 12 months); intravaginal gel twice weekly for 4 months suppresses while taken. Asymptomatic BV in low-risk pregnancy is **not** treated.
  - ⚑ **Male partner treatment is now recommended** in heterosexual couples (StepUp trial, 2025) — oral metronidazole 400 mg bd 7 days **plus** topical clindamycin 2% to penile skin bd 7 days, started simultaneously. Older textbooks still say partner treatment is useless.
- **Candidiasis**: topical imidazole (clotrimazole 1% cream 6 nights, or 500 mg pessary stat) **or** fluconazole 150 mg PO stat. **In pregnancy use topical azoles only** — high-dose oral fluconazole is teratogenic and even the single 150 mg dose carries a small signal. Recurrent (≥4/year): induction then fluconazole 150 mg weekly for 6 months.
- **Trichomoniasis**: metronidazole 400 mg PO bd 7 days (preferred) or 2 g PO stat. **Treat partners.** Metronidazole is **TGA Category B2** and used in pregnancy — ⚑ the old advice to withhold nitroimidazoles in the first trimester has been dropped in Australian guidance.

**The short version**
- **pH >4.5 + fishy + clue cells + no itch = BV**; **itch + curds + normal pH = candida**; **frothy green + motile organisms = trichomonas**.
- Only trichomoniasis is a true STI → contact tracing and partner treatment mandatory; ⚑ BV now also gets partner treatment in heterosexual couples.
- Metronidazole covers BV and trich; azoles cover candida — and topical azoles are the pregnancy-safe choice.

#### 13.2 Pelvic inflammatory disease

Ascending infection: endometritis → salpingitis → tubo-ovarian abscess → pelvic peritonitis. **Polymicrobial** — *Chlamydia trachomatis* and *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* are the classic initiators, but *Mycoplasma genitalium*, anaerobes and BV-associated organisms are commonly involved, and a substantial proportion of PID is non-STI.

Diagnosis is **clinical and deliberately over-inclusive** — no pathognomonic sign exists, and the cost of missing it (infertility) exceeds the cost of over-treating. Treat empirically on **pelvic/lower abdominal pain plus any of: cervical motion tenderness, adnexal tenderness, uterine tenderness** in a sexually active woman; new pelvic pain under 30 is highly predictive. Supportive: fever >38°C, mucopurulent cervicitis, raised CRP/WCC. Always exclude **ectopic pregnancy (βhCG in every case)**, appendicitis, torsion, endometriosis.

**Fitz-Hugh–Curtis syndrome** — perihepatitis from transcoelomic spread. **RUQ pleuritic pain with normal LFTs** in a young woman with pelvic tenderness; laparoscopy shows **"violin-string" adhesions** between liver capsule and anterior abdominal wall. Chlamydia is the commonest cause. Classic distractors: cholecystitis, pneumonia, PE.

**Empiric antibiotics (Australia)**

| Setting | Regimen |
|---|---|
| Mild–moderate, outpatient | **Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM/IV stat** + **doxycycline 100 mg PO bd 14 days** + **metronidazole 400 mg PO bd 14 days** |
| Pregnancy / breastfeeding / doxycycline intolerance | Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM/IV stat + metronidazole 400 mg PO bd 14 days + **azithromycin 1 g PO stat, repeated 1 week later** |
| Severe / inpatient | **Ceftriaxone 2 g IV daily** + **azithromycin 500 mg IV daily** + **metronidazole 500 mg IV bd**, then oral step-down to complete 14 days |
| Suspected/confirmed *M. genitalium* | Resistance-guided therapy — specialist referral; macrolide resistance is very common in Australia |

Admit for: pregnancy, sepsis/haemodynamic instability, suspected **tubo-ovarian abscess** (drainage often required), failed oral therapy, inability to tolerate oral, surgical diagnosis not excluded. **Remove an IUD only if no improvement at 48–72 h** — routine removal is not required.

⚑ Ceftriaxone dose in Australian STI guidelines is now **500 mg** (older sources say 250 mg), reflecting gonococcal resistance drift.

**Sequelae** — the reason PID is examined so hard. Each episode roughly doubles risk. After one episode: infertility ~10–12%, ectopic risk raised **6–7 fold**, chronic pelvic pain ~20%. Hydrosalpinx and Asherman-like tubal damage are irreversible.

**The short version**
- Low threshold: pelvic pain + **cervical motion tenderness** = treat, after excluding pregnancy.
- Australian empiric = **ceftriaxone + doxycycline + metronidazole × 14 days** (azithromycin replaces doxycycline in pregnancy).
- **RUQ pain + normal LFTs + PID** = Fitz-Hugh–Curtis; **violin-string adhesions**.
- Sequelae: infertility, ectopic (×6–7), chronic pelvic pain.

#### 13.3 STIs with obstetric and gynaecological consequence

**Chlamydia trachomatis** — commonest notifiable STI in Australia, peak 15–29 y. ~70–80% asymptomatic in women; otherwise cervicitis, post-coital or intermenstrual bleeding, dysuria. Diagnose by **NAAT** on self-collected vaginal swab or first-pass urine.
⚑ First-line is **doxycycline 100 mg PO bd 7 days** — azithromycin 1 g stat is now only an alternative where adherence is doubtful (doxycycline has superior cure rates, particularly rectal). **In pregnancy: azithromycin 1 g PO stat** (doxycycline is TGA Category D). Retest for reinfection at **3 months**, not test-of-cure. Neonatal effects: **conjunctivitis at 5–14 days** and **afebrile pneumonia at 4–12 weeks** with staccato cough and eosinophilia.

**Neisseria gonorrhoeae** — purulent cervicitis, PID, bartholinitis, disseminated gonococcal infection (**pustular rash, migratory polyarthralgia, tenosynovitis**). Australian treatment is **dual: ceftriaxone 500 mg IM in 2 mL 1% lignocaine + azithromycin 1 g PO stat** — dual therapy is retained explicitly as a resistance barrier (⚑ differs from US CDC, which moved to ceftriaxone monotherapy). **Test of cure by NAAT at 2 weeks** for pharyngeal and cervical infection. In remote communities with documented penicillin-susceptible strains, amoxicillin 3 g + probenecid 1 g + azithromycin 1 g PO is an alternative. Neonatal **ophthalmia neonatorum presents at 2–5 days** with copious purulent discharge and can perforate the cornea — contrast with chlamydial conjunctivitis at 5–14 days.

**Syphilis (*Treponema pallidum*)** — resurgent in Australia including congenital cases, driven by outbreaks in northern and remote Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities.
- Early (primary, secondary, early latent <2 y): **benzathine benzylpenicillin 2.4 million units (1.8 g) IM stat**, given as two 1.2 MU injections.
- Late latent / unknown duration: the same dose **weekly × 3 doses**.
- **Pregnancy: penicillin only.** Nothing else reliably treats the fetus. Documented penicillin allergy requires **desensitisation**, not substitution — doxycycline is contraindicated and macrolides do not cross the placenta reliably. Seek urgent specialist advice; repeat serology to confirm response and detect reinfection.
- **Jarisch–Herxheimer reaction**: fever, headache, myalgia **6–12 h after the first dose**, from endotoxin-like release on spirochaete lysis. In pregnancy it can precipitate contractions and fetal distress — warn and monitor; it is **not** an allergy and does not stop treatment.
- Congenital syphilis: hydrops, hepatosplenomegaly, rhinitis ("snuffles"), desquamating rash of palms and soles, long-bone metaphyseal lucencies; late stigmata — **Hutchinson teeth, interstitial keratitis, eighth-nerve deafness (Hutchinson triad)**, saddle nose, sabre shins.

**Herpes simplex virus in pregnancy** — the discrimination is **primary vs recurrent**, and **when** in gestation.

| Scenario | Neonatal HSV risk | Management |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary infection in third trimester / within 6 weeks of delivery** | **Up to 40–50%** — no time to make and transfer protective maternal IgG | **Elective caesarean recommended**; aciclovir treatment then suppression |
| Primary infection in 1st/2nd trimester | <3% | Treat episode; **suppressive aciclovir from 36 weeks**; vaginal birth acceptable |
| **Recurrent lesions present in labour** | **2–5%** | Caesarean usually offered, though absolute risk is low |
| Recurrent HSV, no lesions at delivery | **~0.02%** | Vaginal birth; no caesarean |

Suppression from **36 weeks: aciclovir 400 mg PO tds** (or valaciclovir 500 mg daily) reduces recurrence and caesarean rate; aciclovir is **TGA Category B3**. ⚑ Australian practice permits vaginal birth when membranes have been ruptured **>6 hours** with recurrent lesions — some UK sources apply no such cut-off. Avoid **fetal scalp electrodes** and fetal blood sampling. Neonatal HSV comes in three forms — skin/eye/mouth, CNS, disseminated — with high mortality in disseminated disease even on IV aciclovir.

**HIV in pregnancy** — vertical transmission without intervention is ~15–30% (higher with breastfeeding); with full intervention it falls to **<1%**.
- Universal opt-out HIV screening at the first antenatal visit.
- **ART for all pregnant women regardless of CD4**, aiming for undetectable viral load.
- **Mode of delivery is driven by viral load**: undetectable (<50 copies/mL, some services <40) → vaginal birth on obstetric grounds. Detectable or unknown viral load → **elective caesarean at 39 weeks** (note: 39 weeks, not the 38–39 weeks quoted for other elective caesareans, and later than the pre-ART era teaching of 38 weeks).
- **Intrapartum IV zidovudine** only where viral load is detectable or adherence is uncertain — ⚑ it is no longer given routinely to virally suppressed women, which older texts still describe.
- Avoid **ARM, fetal scalp electrodes, fetal blood sampling, instrumental delivery** where viral load is detectable.
- Neonatal post-exposure prophylaxis within **4 hours** of birth.
- **In Australia, exclusive formula feeding is recommended** — safe, accessible replacement feeding is available, so the WHO position favouring breastfeeding in resource-limited settings does not apply here. This is a common exam trap.

**The short version**
- Chlamydia = **doxycycline 7 days** (azithromycin in pregnancy); gonorrhoea = **ceftriaxone 500 mg IM + azithromycin 1 g**.
- Syphilis in pregnancy = **benzathine penicillin, desensitise if allergic** — no alternative agent.
- HSV: **primary infection near term → caesarean**; recurrent without lesions → vaginal birth. Suppression from **36 weeks**.
- HIV: viral load decides the mode of birth; **formula feed in Australia**.

#### 13.4 Group B *Streptococcus* (*Streptococcus agalactiae*)

Colonises vagina/rectum in ~15–25% of pregnant women — asymptomatic carriage, not infection. Matters because it is the leading cause of **early-onset neonatal sepsis** (<7 days: pneumonia, septicaemia, meningitis).

⚑ Australia has **no single national approach**: services use **either universal culture-based or risk-factor-based screening** — RANZCOG accepts both, citing insufficient comparative evidence. This differs from the US (universal) and UK (risk-based); if asked the Australian approach, the answer is "either, per local protocol."

**Universal screening**: combined **low vaginal and rectal swab at 35–37 weeks** (rectal sampling matters — vaginal-only swabbing misses a third of carriers).

**Indications for intrapartum antibiotic prophylaxis (IAP)**
- GBS colonisation detected this pregnancy
- **GBS bacteriuria at any colony count** this pregnancy (marker of heavy colonisation — always treat, and never screen-and-ignore)
- **Previous infant with invasive GBS disease**
- **Preterm labour <37 weeks**
- **Rupture of membranes ≥18 hours**
- **Intrapartum fever ≥38°C**

**Regimens** (all IV, continued until birth; aim for **≥4 hours** of exposure before delivery)
- **Benzylpenicillin 3 g IV loading, then 1.8 g IV 4-hourly**
- Non-severe penicillin allergy: **cefazolin 2 g IV loading, then 1 g IV 8-hourly**
- Anaphylaxis history: **clindamycin 900 mg IV 8-hourly**, or **vancomycin 1 g IV 12-hourly** if clindamycin susceptibility unknown or resistant

IAP prevents **early-onset** disease only. Late-onset GBS (7–90 days, typically meningitis) is unaffected — a favourite MCQ discriminator.

#### 13.5 Toxic shock syndrome

Superantigen-mediated: **TSST-1** from *Staphylococcus aureus* (classically menstrual — high-absorbency or retained tampons, vaginal packs, diaphragms/sponges, post-surgical) or group A *Streptococcus* exotoxins (typically non-menstrual, deep invasive focus, higher mortality).

Recognition: **abrupt high fever, hypotension, diffuse macular erythroderma ("sunburn" rash) with mucosal hyperaemia, plus multi-organ involvement** (vomiting/diarrhoea, myalgia with raised CK, renal impairment, thrombocytopenia, deranged LFTs, confusion). **Desquamation of palms and soles at 1–2 weeks is characteristic but late** — retrospective confirmation only. Blood cultures are usually **negative in staphylococcal TSS** (toxin-mediated, not bacteraemic), often positive in streptococcal.

Management: **remove the source**, aggressive fluid resuscitation and vasopressors, and **combination antibiotics — beta-lactam plus clindamycin**. Clindamycin is not there for coverage; it is a **protein-synthesis inhibitor that switches off toxin production**. IVIG in refractory or streptococcal cases.

#### 13.6 Infections screened for or considered in pregnancy

| Infection | Screened? (Australia) | Key fetal/neonatal effect | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rubella** | **Yes — antibody titre every pregnancy** | Congenital rubella syndrome: **cataracts, sensorineural deafness, PDA**; risk >80% if infected <12 weeks, negligible after 20 weeks | Cannot vaccinate in pregnancy (**live vaccine**) — **vaccinate postpartum before discharge**; avoid pregnancy 28 days |
| **Hepatitis B** | **Yes — universal HBsAg** | Vertical transmission; ~90% chronicity if acquired perinatally | Infant **HBIG + birth-dose vaccine within 12 h (ideally 4 h)**, then vaccine at 2/4/6 months. **Maternal tenofovir from 28–32 weeks if HBV DNA >200,000 IU/mL.** **Breastfeeding is safe** |
| **Hepatitis C** | Risk-based or universal depending on jurisdiction | Vertical transmission ~5% (higher with HIV co-infection) | No intervention prevents transmission; avoid invasive fetal monitoring; **breastfeeding permitted** unless nipples cracked/bleeding; treat mother postpartum with DAAs; test infant |
| **Syphilis** | **Yes — universal at booking**, repeat at 28 weeks and delivery in high-prevalence/outbreak areas | Congenital syphilis, stillbirth, hydrops | Benzathine penicillin; desensitise if allergic |
| **HIV** | **Yes — universal opt-out** | Vertical transmission | ART, viral-load-directed delivery, neonatal PEP, **formula feeding** |
| **CMV** | **No routine serology** | **Commonest congenital infection** — sensorineural hearing loss (may be delayed), microcephaly, **periventricular calcification**, chorioretinitis, IUGR | Hygiene advice (toddler saliva/urine is the main source — relevant to childcare workers); diagnose by amniocentesis PCR; valaciclovir under evaluation |
| **Toxoplasma** | **No routine serology** | **Classic triad: chorioretinitis, hydrocephalus, diffuse intracranial calcification** | Avoid undercooked meat, cat litter, unwashed produce, gardening without gloves. If maternal infection confirmed: **spiramycin** to reduce transmission; if fetal infection confirmed, **pyrimethamine + sulfadiazine + folinic acid** |
| **Listeria monocytogenes** | **No** (serology is useless) | 2nd/3rd trimester infection → **fetal loss ~40–50%**, preterm birth, granulomatosis infantiseptica, neonatal meningitis | Mother often only a flu-like illness. Diagnose by **blood culture** (± placental culture). Treat **amoxicillin/ampicillin high-dose IV ± gentamicin, 14 days**. Avoid soft cheeses, pâté, deli meats, pre-prepared salads, cold smoked seafood, unpasteurised dairy |
| **Parvovirus B19** | No | Vertical transmission ~50%; **hydrops fetalis ~12%** of infected fetuses; fetal loss 5–10%; greatest risk **8–20 weeks** | Serology if exposed/rash. If acute: **serial MCA peak systolic velocity from 4 weeks post-infection for 12 weeks**; **MCA PSV >1.5 MoM = fetal anaemia** → cordocentesis + **intrauterine transfusion** (mortality 6% transfused vs 30% not). No long-term neurological sequelae if hydrops resolves |
| **Varicella** | History-based (serology if no history) | Congenital varicella syndrome (**limb hypoplasia, cicatricial skin scarring, chorioretinitis**) if <20 weeks; **severe neonatal varicella if maternal rash 5 days before to 2 days after birth** | VZIG for susceptible exposed women; aciclovir for maternal infection; VZIG for the at-risk neonate |
| **Asymptomatic bacteriuria** | **Yes — MSU culture at 12–16 weeks / first visit** | Pyelonephritis (20–30% if untreated), preterm birth, low birth weight | Treat on culture; **dipstick is not adequate**; repeat culture after treatment |
| **GBS** | Universal **or** risk-based (local protocol) | Early-onset neonatal sepsis | IAP as above |

Congenital infection hooks: **CMV = periventricular calcification + deafness. Toxo = diffuse calcification + hydrocephalus + chorioretinitis. Rubella = cataracts + deafness + PDA. Parvo = hydrops (no malformation).**

**The short version**
- Universal antenatal screening in Australia: **rubella IgG, HBsAg, syphilis, HIV, blood group/antibodies, asymptomatic bacteriuria**; hepatitis C by jurisdiction; **CMV and toxoplasma are not screened**.
- Rubella vaccine is **live** — postpartum, never antenatal.
- Hepatitis B: **HBIG + vaccine within 12 h**; maternal tenofovir if **DNA >200,000 IU/mL**; breastfeeding safe.
- Parvovirus causes **anaemia and hydrops, not malformations** — surveillance is **MCA PSV**.
