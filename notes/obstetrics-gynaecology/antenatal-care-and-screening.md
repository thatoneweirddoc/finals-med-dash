## Antenatal Care and Screening


### O&G Master Notes — Antenatal Care and Screening

*Australian practice. Anchored to the Australian Pregnancy Care Guidelines, RANZCOG statements, RACGP Red Book (first antenatal visit), National Blood Authority anti-D guideline (2024), Australian Immunisation Handbook, ADIPS 2025 consensus and Queensland Clinical Guidelines. Where Australian practice differs from UK/NICE, this is stated. ⚑ marks a change from older textbooks.*

#### 3.1 The booking visit

Ideally **within the first 10 weeks**. Purpose is risk stratification, dating, screening and shared decision-making about model of care.

**Booking bloods (routine):** FBC; **blood group and antibody screen**; rubella IgG; syphilis serology; HBsAg; **hepatitis C antibody**; HIV. **Ferritin** is increasingly routine (risk-based in older guidance, universal in many Australian services). Risk-based additions: haemoglobin electrophoresis/HPLC (ethnicity, low MCV/MCH, family history), TSH (thyroid disease, T1DM, symptoms — **not universal**), vitamin D (dark skin, veiling, low sun exposure, BMI >30).

**Other booking tests:** **urine MCS for asymptomatic bacteriuria** (culture, not dipstick — dipstick is insufficiently sensitive); **chlamydia NAAT if aged <30 or with risk factors** (⚑ this is an Australian point — the UK does not routinely screen for chlamydia in pregnancy); cervical screening test if due (safe in pregnancy).

**Clinical assessment:** height, weight, BMI; BP; **EPDS depression screen (and repeat at least once later in pregnancy)**; domestic/family violence enquiry; smoking, alcohol and other drug use; medication review for teratogens; **pre-eclampsia risk assessment**; **GDM risk assessment**.

Acting on the risk assessment: high pre-eclampsia risk → **low-dose aspirin 100–150 mg nocte, commenced between 12 and 16 weeks** and continued to ~36 weeks, plus calcium supplementation if dietary intake is low.

**Dating.** Use LMP only if cycles are regular and dates certain. Otherwise, or if there is discrepancy: **CRL is the most accurate parameter between 7 and 13+6 weeks (±5 days)**; after 14 weeks use HC/BPD/FL (±7–10 days, degrading with gestation). ⚑ **Ultrasound dating overrides LMP if the discrepancy exceeds ~5–7 days in the first trimester.** Never re-date on a third-trimester scan — a small fetus late in pregnancy is FGR until proven otherwise, not a wrong date.

**The short version**
- Booking by 10 weeks; dating by CRL if <14 weeks.
- Urine **culture** for asymptomatic bacteriuria — untreated it causes pyelonephritis and preterm birth.
- Aspirin decision is made at booking and started 12–16 weeks.

#### 3.2 Visit schedule

RANZCOG accepts **~10 visits for an uncomplicated nulliparous woman and ~7 for a multiparous woman**; exact gestations vary between states and models of care.

| Gestation | Content |
|---|---|
| **<10 weeks (booking)** | history, exam, booking bloods, urine MCS, EPDS, risk assessment, dating scan if needed |
| 11+0–13+6 | **cFTS** (NT + serum) if chosen; NIPT can be done from 10 weeks |
| 16 | review screening results, BP, urine; commence aspirin by now if indicated |
| **18–22 (usually 19–20)** | **morphology scan** ± cervical length, placental site |
| 24 | BP, urine, **SFH from 24 weeks onwards** |
| **24–28** | **75 g OGTT for GDM** |
| **28** | FBC (± ferritin), repeat antibody screen, **anti-D dose 1 (625 IU)** |
| 31 (nullip) | BP, urine, SFH |
| **34** | **anti-D dose 2 (625 IU)** |
| **35–37** | **GBS swab** (if universal screening used); repeat depression screen |
| 36 | **confirm presentation** (palpation ± USS); discuss ECV if breech |
| 38, 40, 41 | BP/urine/SFH; **discuss IOL at 41 weeks**, membrane sweep |

BP, urinalysis and SFH at every visit from 24 weeks; fetal heart auscultation from ~24 weeks (of reassurance value only, not diagnostic).

#### 3.3 RhD status and anti-D prophylaxis

Alloimmunisation occurs when an RhD-negative woman is exposed to RhD-positive fetal cells; the resulting IgG anti-D crosses the placenta in *subsequent* pregnancies and causes haemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn.

**Australian regimen (National Blood Authority, 2024):**

| Situation | Dose |
|---|---|
| **Routine antenatal prophylaxis** | **625 IU IM at 28 weeks AND 625 IU IM at 34 weeks** |
| **Sensitising event ≤12+6 weeks** | **250 IU IM** |
| **Sensitising event ≥13+0 weeks** | **625 IU IM** |
| **Postnatal (baby confirmed RhD positive)** | **625 IU IM** |
| Large FMH ≥6 mL fetal red cells | 625 IU **plus 100 IU per additional mL** of fetal red cells beyond 6 mL |

Timing: **give within 72 hours**; if missed, still give up to **10 days** (partial protection). Routine doses may be given within ~2 weeks of the scheduled gestation if logistics require.

**Sensitising events:** miscarriage or threatened miscarriage (with heavy/repeated bleeding or pain), termination, ectopic, molar pregnancy, CVS/amniocentesis/fetal blood sampling, **abdominal trauma**, **antepartum haemorrhage**, **external cephalic version**, intrauterine death, and delivery itself.

**Postnatal FMH quantitation (Kleihauer or flow cytometry) is required for every RhD-negative woman delivering an RhD-positive baby**, to identify the large bleeds needing supplementary dosing. Repeat testing 48 h after IV or 72 h after IM administration when a large FMH has been treated.

⚑ **RhD NIPT (cell-free fetal DNA fetal blood group genotyping)** is now available in Australia through Lifeblood **from 15 weeks**, **MBS-funded/bulk-billed** for non-alloimmunised RhD-negative women (not validated in multiple pregnancy). **If the fetus is predicted RhD negative, antenatal anti-D is not indicated.** This is a genuine change from the universal-prophylaxis model in older texts.

⚑ **Anti-D does not treat established alloimmunisation.** If the antibody screen already shows anti-D, giving more is pointless — refer to MFM for antibody titres/quantitation and **MCA peak systolic velocity Dopplers (>1.5 MoM = significant fetal anaemia → consider intrauterine transfusion)**.

**Australia vs UK:** Australia uses **two doses of 625 IU at 28 and 34 weeks**. The UK (NICE) offers either **a single 1500 IU dose at 28 weeks** or **two 500 IU doses at 28 and 34 weeks**. Quoting the UK figures in an Australian paper is a marked error.

#### 3.4 Aneuploidy and structural screening

Screening is **opt-in after counselling**, not automatic. All screening results are risks, not diagnoses.

| Test | Timing | Components | Performance for T21 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **cFTS** | **NT at 11+0–13+6 (CRL 45–84 mm)**; serum from 9–13+6 | NT + free β-hCG + PAPP-A + maternal age | DR **~85–90%**, FPR ~5% |
| **NIPT (cfDNA)** | **from 10 weeks** | placental cfDNA fraction | DR **>99%**, FPR **<0.1%** |
| Second trimester serum (quad) | 15–20 weeks | AFP, hCG, uE3, inhibin A | DR ~75–80% |
| **Morphology scan** | **18–22 weeks** (commonly 19–20 in Australia) | structural survey, placental site, ± cervical length | structural anomalies |

**The short version**
- **NIPT is a screening test, not diagnostic** — every high-risk result needs confirmation by CVS or amniocentesis. Its **PPV depends on prior risk**: in a 25-year-old, PPV for T21 may be ~50–70% and for rare trisomies/microdeletions considerably lower.
- ⚑ **NIPT is not MBS-funded in Australia** (typically $400–500 out of pocket), unlike the separately funded RhD NIPT. cFTS is partly rebatable. This cost difference drives real inequity in access and is worth stating in a short-answer response.
- **Failed/"no-call" NIPT (1–5%)** is usually **low fetal fraction**: high maternal BMI, too-early gestation, twin/vanishing twin, some anticoagulants, and — importantly — **aneuploid placentas (T13, T18, triploidy)**. A no-call is itself a mild risk marker; repeat or offer diagnostic testing rather than reassure.

**Diagnostic testing:** **CVS from 11–14 weeks** (never before 10 weeks — earlier sampling is linked to limb-reduction defects), **amniocentesis from 15 weeks**. ⚑ **Procedure-related miscarriage risk in contemporary series is ~0.1–0.2% above background, not the 1% (amniocentesis) or 1–2% (CVS) quoted in older textbooks.** CVS carries a 1–2% chance of **confined placental mosaicism**, which amniocentesis does not.

**Increased NT ≥3.5 mm** with a normal karyotype/NIPT still warrants **detailed anatomy scan, fetal echocardiography and chromosomal microarray** — the association is with cardiac defects and genetic syndromes, not only aneuploidy. ⚑ **Chromosomal microarray has replaced karyotype as first-line** when a structural anomaly is present, because it detects copy-number variants that karyotype misses.

Rh-negative women undergoing CVS or amniocentesis require anti-D (dose by gestation as above).

#### 3.5 Gestational diabetes screening ⚑

⚑ **The ADIPS 2025 consensus raised all three diagnostic thresholds.** Australian laboratories transitioned during 2025 (Victoria state-wide from 4 August 2025). Older notes quoting 5.1/10.0/8.5 are now out of date.

| 75 g OGTT at 24–28 weeks | **ADIPS 2025** | ADIPS 2014 (superseded) |
|---|---|---|
| Fasting | **≥5.3 mmol/L** | ≥5.1 |
| 1 hour | **≥10.6 mmol/L** | ≥10.0 |
| 2 hour | **≥9.0 mmol/L** | ≥8.5 |

One abnormal value is sufficient. Values above ~7.0 fasting / 11.1 at 2 h suggest **overt diabetes in pregnancy**, not GDM.

Early testing: risk factors (prior GDM, BMI ≥30, age ≥30, family history, PCOS, high-risk ethnicity, previous macrosomia) → **first-trimester HbA1c**; **prior GDM or HbA1c 6.0–6.4% → OGTT before 20 weeks, ideally 10–14 weeks**. Treatment targets: **fasting <5.3, 1 h post-prandial <7.8, or 2 h post-prandial <6.7 mmol/L**.

**Do not screen with urinalysis** — glycosuria is physiological (Section 1.7).

#### 3.6 Group B streptococcus

**Australia has no national consensus.** RANZCOG accepts **either** universal culture-based screening **or** a risk-factor-based approach, and services follow local policy. ⚑ Contrast: **the US (CDC/ACOG) mandates universal screening; the UK (RCOG) and New Zealand use risk-based.** Saying "universal screening is standard" is only half-right in an Australian answer.

- **Swab at 35–37 weeks**: a single swab taken **low vaginal then anorectal** (introitus first, then anus), into standard transport medium.
- **Indications for intrapartum antibiotic prophylaxis (IAP):** positive GBS swab; **GBS bacteriuria at any point in this pregnancy**; **previous infant with invasive GBS disease** (both of the latter override swab results); preterm labour <37 weeks; **ROM ≥18 hours**; intrapartum fever ≥38 °C.
- **Regimen:** **benzylpenicillin 3 g IV loading dose, then 1.8 g IV 4-hourly** until birth. Non-severe penicillin allergy: **cephazolin 2 g then 1 g 8-hourly**. Anaphylaxis: **clindamycin 900 mg 8-hourly** (or vancomycin if resistance unknown).
- **"Adequate" prophylaxis = commenced ≥4 hours before birth** — the figure that determines neonatal observation and workup.
- **Elective caesarean with intact membranes and not in labour does not require GBS IAP** (surgical prophylaxis is a separate indication).

#### 3.7 Vaccination in pregnancy

| Vaccine | Timing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Influenza (inactivated)** | **any trimester, every pregnancy** | NIP-funded; reduces stillbirth, preterm birth and severe maternal illness |
| **dTpa** | **20–32 weeks, every pregnancy** | ⚑ Australian window is **20–32 weeks** (UK: 16–32). Timing optimises transplacental IgG transfer to protect the infant until their 6-week dose |
| **RSV (RSVpreF, Abrysvo)** | ⚑ **28–36 weeks**, single dose **each pregnancy** | ⚑ **New — NIP-funded from 2025.** Registered from 24 weeks but **not routinely recommended before 28 weeks**. ~70% reduction in severe infant RSV disease. Alternative infant pathway: nirsevimab |
| **COVID-19 (mRNA)** | any trimester | |
| Others as indicated | any trimester | hepatitis A/B, pneumococcal, meningococcal, tetanus, rabies post-exposure — all inactivated, all safe |

**Contraindicated — live attenuated vaccines:** **MMR, varicella, zoster (live formulation), BCG, live oral typhoid, yellow fever** (relative — may be given if travel is unavoidable and risk is high).

- Advise avoiding pregnancy for **28 days** after MMR or varicella.
- **Inadvertent administration of a live vaccine in pregnancy is NOT an indication for termination** — actual fetal harm has not been demonstrated. This is a favourite exam point.
- **Non-immune to rubella or varicella → vaccinate postpartum before discharge.** Live vaccines are safe while breastfeeding.

#### 3.8 Nutrition and supplementation

| Nutrient | Dose | Timing / notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Folic acid** | **400–500 µg/day** | from **≥1 month preconception to 12 weeks** |
| **High-dose folic acid** | **5 mg/day** | previous NTD-affected pregnancy, family history of NTD, **pre-existing diabetes**, **BMI >30**, antiepileptics (valproate, carbamazepine), other folate antagonists (methotrexate, trimethoprim, sulfasalazine), malabsorption/bariatric surgery |
| **Iodine** | **150 µg/day** | preconception, pregnancy and lactation. Australia has mandatory iodine fortification of bread, but supplementation is still recommended. Seek advice first in known thyroid disease |
| **Vitamin B12** | **2.6 µg/day** (2.8 lactation) | ⚑ **essential in vegans/strict vegetarians** |
| Vitamin D | 1000 IU if 25(OH)D 30–49 nmol/L; **2000 IU if <30 nmol/L** | risk-based testing; recheck at 28 weeks |
| Calcium | 1000 mg/day (1300 mg if ≤18 y) | supplement reduces pre-eclampsia risk where intake is low |
| Iron | **not routine** | treat if Hb below threshold or **ferritin <30 µg/L**; ~100 mg elemental daily. ⚑ **alternate-day dosing improves absorption** (hepcidin kinetics). IV iron generally avoided in the first trimester |

**Clue → answer:** an exclusively breastfed infant of a **vegan mother** with developmental regression, hypotonia and macrocytic anaemia = **maternal/infant B12 deficiency**. This is the classic reason B12 is asked about, and why B12 status must be checked and supplemented in vegan pregnancies rather than assumed adequate.

**Food and lifestyle:** avoid listeria risks (soft/unpasteurised cheese, pre-prepared salads, deli meats, pâté, cold smoked seafood); avoid high-mercury fish (**shark/flake, swordfish, broadbill, marlin — no more than one serve per fortnight**, FSANZ); **no safe level of alcohol (NHMRC — abstinence)**; caffeine **≤200 mg/day**. Recommended gestational weight gain (IOM ranges): underweight 12.5–18 kg; normal BMI 11.5–16 kg; overweight 7–11.5 kg; obese 5–9 kg.

#### 3.9 Fetal growth surveillance

**Symphysis-fundal height** from **24 weeks** at every visit, measured in cm with the tape reversed to blind the measurer, and **plotted on a customised (e.g. GROW) chart** rather than judged against "cm ≈ weeks ± 2–3". Sensitivity for SGA is poor (~30% alone) — it is a trigger for imaging, not a test.

**Serial growth ultrasound** (typically 4-weekly from 26–28 weeks) is indicated for: previous SGA baby or stillbirth, chronic hypertension or pre-eclampsia, **pre-gestational diabetes**, smoking, maternal age >40, BMI extremes, **low PAPP-A (<0.4 MoM)**, abnormal uterine artery Dopplers, APH, multiple pregnancy, renal disease, SLE/APS. Also whenever SFH plots off-centile or palpation is unreliable (obesity, fibroids, polyhydramnios).

Definitions and surveillance:
- **SGA** = EFW or AC **<10th customised centile**. **FGR** = **<3rd centile**, or <10th centile **with abnormal Dopplers or poor interval growth**.
- ⚑ **Umbilical artery Doppler is the only surveillance tool proven to reduce perinatal mortality in high-risk pregnancies** — but it is **useless as a screening test in low-risk women.** Deterioration sequence: raised PI → **absent end-diastolic flow → reversed end-diastolic flow**.
- **MCA Doppler / cerebroplacental ratio** detects "brain sparing" and is more useful in **late-onset** FGR, where umbilical artery Dopplers often remain normal.
- **Ductus venosus a-wave loss or reversal** is a late sign of cardiac decompensation in **early-onset** FGR and, with the TRUFFLE approach, guides timing of delivery before 32 weeks.
- **Reduced fetal movements** → same-day assessment: CTG plus ultrasound for growth and liquor. ⚑ **Formal kick-counting charts are no longer recommended** — the AFFIRM trial showed no reduction in stillbirth and increased obstetric intervention. Advise awareness of *change*, not counting.
- **Routine third-trimester ultrasound in low-risk pregnancy is not recommended** by RANZCOG; some Australian units nevertheless offer a ~36-week scan for presentation and growth, and practice varies.

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Australian routine antenatal anti-D regimen | **625 IU IM at 28 AND 34 weeks** (UK: 1500 IU once at 28, or 500 IU ×2) |
| Anti-D dose for a sensitising event at 9 weeks vs 20 weeks | **250 IU** vs **625 IU** |
| Anti-D already-formed anti-D on antibody screen | **Do not give anti-D** — refer MFM, titres + **MCA PSV >1.5 MoM = fetal anaemia** |
| RhD-negative woman, fetal RhD NIPT negative at 16 weeks | **No antenatal anti-D required** ⚑ (Lifeblood, MBS-funded, from 15 weeks) |
| Postnatal test required in every RhD-neg woman with RhD-pos baby | **Kleihauer/flow cytometry for FMH volume** |
| Window for NT measurement and valid CRL range | **11+0–13+6 weeks, CRL 45–84 mm** |
| "No-call" NIPT in a woman with BMI 42 — and why it matters | **Low fetal fraction**; also seen with **T13/T18/triploidy** — repeat or offer diagnostic testing, do not reassure |
| NIPT funding in Australia | ⚑ **Not MBS-funded** (~$400–500 out of pocket) |
| Procedure-related loss rate for amniocentesis | ⚑ **~0.1–0.2% above background**, not the 1% of older texts |
| First-line genetic test when a structural anomaly is seen | ⚑ **Chromosomal microarray**, not karyotype |
| Current Australian GDM thresholds (75 g OGTT) | ⚑ **ADIPS 2025: 5.3 / 10.6 / 9.0 mmol/L** (was 5.1 / 10.0 / 8.5) |
| GBS swab timing and site | **35–37 weeks, low vaginal + anorectal, one swab** |
| Is universal GBS screening standard in Australia? | ⚑ **No** — RANZCOG accepts universal **or** risk-based. US = universal, UK/NZ = risk-based |
| GBS IAP regimen and "adequate" duration | **Benzylpenicillin 3 g then 1.8 g q4h**; **≥4 h before birth** |
| GBS bacteriuria this pregnancy, negative 36-week swab | **Still give IAP** — bacteriuria and previous invasive GBS override the swab |
| dTpa timing in Australia | **20–32 weeks, every pregnancy** (UK: 16–32) |
| RSV vaccine in pregnancy | ⚑ **28–36 weeks, single dose each pregnancy, NIP-funded** |

| Indications for 5 mg folate | Previous NTD, family history, **pre-existing diabetes**, **BMI >30**, antiepileptics/folate antagonists, malabsorption |

| Reduced fetal movements — recommend kick charts? | ⚑ **No** (AFFIRM). Assess same day with CTG + USS |
