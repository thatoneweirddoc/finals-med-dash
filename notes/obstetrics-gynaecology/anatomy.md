## Anatomy


### O&G Master Notes — Anatomy

*Written as prose and tables — deliberately image-independent. Aligned to RANZCOG and the ACSQHC Third and Fourth Degree Perineal Tears Clinical Care Standard for tear classification; SA Health PPG for repair detail. Scope: what is examinable in a written paper — relations, supply, drainage, diameters and the clinical consequences of each.*

#### 2.1 The bony pelvis

The **true pelvis** (below the pelvic brim) is the birth canal. Its three planes have different shapes, and this is the whole explanation for the mechanism of labour.

| Plane | AP diameter | Transverse diameter | Widest axis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inlet (brim) | true conjugate ~11 cm; **obstetric conjugate ~10.5 cm** (sacral promontory → nearest/posterior point of symphysis — the true limiting diameter); diagonal conjugate ~12.5 cm (**the only one clinically measurable**, promontory → inferior symphysis) | ~13 cm | **Transverse** |
| Mid-cavity | ~12 cm | ~12 cm (interspinous ~10.5 cm — **narrowest pelvic diameter overall**) | round |
| Outlet | ~12.5–13 cm | intertuberous ~11 cm | **AP** |

**Memory hook:** widest **transverse at the inlet**, widest **AP at the outlet**. The fetal head therefore **engages in the transverse, then rotates to occipito-anterior** to deliver — the rotation exists because the pelvis changes shape, not because the head "prefers" it.

The **ischial spines** are the reference point for everything: station 0 by definition, the narrowest interspinous diameter, the landmark for pudendal block, and the attachment of the sacrospinous ligament (used in sacrospinous fixation for vault prolapse).

| Pelvis type | Inlet shape | Obstetric relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **Gynaecoid** (~50%) | round/slightly oval transversely | most favourable |
| Android | heart-shaped, narrow forepelvis, prominent spines | deep transverse arrest, OP position, arrest of descent |
| Anthropoid | oval AP > transverse | favours **direct OA or OP** delivery |
| Platypelloid | flat, wide transverse, short AP | head engages transverse, **fails to rotate** |

**The short version**
- Obstetric conjugate is the true limiting inlet diameter; diagonal conjugate is the one your finger can reach.
- Interspinous distance is the narrowest diameter in the entire pelvis.
- Inlet transverse → outlet AP explains internal rotation.

#### 2.2 The pelvic floor

The **pelvic diaphragm** = **levator ani** (pubococcygeus, puborectalis, iliococcygeus) + **coccygeus**, slung from the tendinous arch of the obturator fascia. Innervation: direct branches from **S3–S5 (nerve to levator ani)** with a variable contribution from the pudendal nerve.

**Puborectalis** forms the sling around the anorectal junction and maintains the anorectal angle — the principal mechanism of faecal continence, more so than the external anal sphincter at rest. Avulsion of the puborectalis from the pubic ramus occurs in ~10–15% of first vaginal births (higher with forceps) and is a major driver of later prolapse.

Below the levators, the **perineal membrane** separates the **deep perineal pouch** (external urethral sphincter, compressor urethrae, deep transverse perineal muscle) from the **superficial perineal pouch** (bulbospongiosus, ischiocavernosus, superficial transverse perineal muscle, vestibular bulbs, **Bartholin's [greater vestibular] glands** — which drain at 4 and 8 o'clock in the vestibule, hence the classic posterolateral Bartholin's cyst).

The **perineal body** is the fibromuscular central tendon between vagina and anus, into which bulbospongiosus, superficial and deep transverse perineal muscles, the external anal sphincter and the levators all insert. **It is the structure destroyed in a second-degree tear or episiotomy, and its integrity underpins both posterior compartment support and anal continence.**

**Uterine support** — the discriminator that matters:

| Ligament | Composition | Does it actually support? |
|---|---|---|
| **Cardinal (transverse cervical, Mackenrodt's)** | fibromuscular, cervix/upper vagina → lateral pelvic wall; **carries the uterine vessels** | **Yes — principal lateral support** |
| **Uterosacral** | cervix → sacrum | **Yes — principal posterior support**; the structure plicated in prolapse surgery |
| Pubocervical | cervix → pubis | Yes, anterior (weak) |
| Round | cornu → inguinal canal → labium majus (contains artery of Sampson) | **No** — maintains anteversion only; source of round ligament pain |
| Broad | peritoneal fold | **No** — a mesentery, not a ligament |
| Suspensory (infundibulopelvic) | ovary → pelvic sidewall; **contains ovarian vessels** | supports ovary; **the pedicle clamped in oophorectomy — the ureter runs close beneath it** |
| Ovarian ligament | ovary → cornu (gubernaculum remnant) | no |

#### 2.3 The genital tract

Uterus: fundus, body, isthmus (becomes the lower segment in pregnancy — the site of LSCS incision), cervix. Layers: endometrium (functionalis shed, basalis regenerates), myometrium, perimetrium. Normally **anteverted and anteflexed** (~80%); retroversion is a normal variant but relevant to incarceration of a retroverted gravid uterus at 12–16 weeks.

Cervix: endocervix lined by columnar epithelium, ectocervix by stratified squamous. The **squamocolumnar junction** everts onto the ectocervix under oestrogen (puberty, pregnancy, COCP) and the exposed columnar epithelium undergoes squamous metaplasia — the **transformation zone**. This is where HPV-driven dysplasia arises and therefore what a cervical screening sample and colposcopy must sample. It regresses into the canal after menopause, which is why colposcopy is more often unsatisfactory in older women.

Fallopian tube: interstitial (intramural) → isthmus → **ampulla** (fertilisation site; **commonest site of ectopic, ~70%**) → infundibulum/fimbriae. **Interstitial (cornual) ectopics present later (~8–12 weeks) and bleed catastrophically** because of the surrounding myometrial vascularity.

Vagina: stratified squamous, no glands; lubricated by transudate and cervical mucus. Upper third from Müllerian duct, lower part from urogenital sinus — the embryological seam that explains the **watershed at the hymen** in both lymphatic drainage and nerve supply.

**Key surgical relation — "water under the bridge":** the **ureter passes beneath the uterine artery** approximately **1.5–2 cm lateral to the cervix at the level of the internal os**, within the cardinal ligament. This is the site of ureteric injury at hysterectomy and at uterine artery ligation for PPH. The ureter is also at risk at the pelvic brim (crossing the bifurcation of the common iliac) and where it runs beneath the infundibulopelvic ligament.

#### 2.4 Blood supply

| Structure | Arterial supply | Origin |
|---|---|---|
| Ovary (and lateral tube) | ovarian artery | **abdominal aorta, ~L2** (below the renal arteries) |
| Uterus, medial tube, upper vagina | uterine artery | **anterior division of internal iliac** |
| Vagina (mid) | vaginal artery | internal iliac (or uterine) |
| Vulva, perineum, lower vagina | internal pudendal artery | anterior division of internal iliac |
| Lower vagina/perineum (accessory) | inferior rectal, posterior labial | internal pudendal |

The uterine and ovarian arteries **anastomose freely within the broad ligament** — which is why unilateral internal iliac ligation rarely produces uterine necrosis, and why uterine artery embolisation for fibroids can still fail via ovarian collaterals.

Venous drainage is the classic asymmetry: **right ovarian vein → IVC; left ovarian vein → left renal vein.** This underlies the left-sided predominance of pelvic congestion syndrome and the right-sided predominance of postpartum ovarian vein thrombosis (right vein is longer, crosses the ureter, and drains directly to IVC).

#### 2.5 Lymphatic drainage

Lymphatics follow arteries, so **ovarian drainage is high (para-aortic), not pelvic** — which is why ovarian cancer staging requires para-aortic nodal assessment and why an ovarian cancer can have para-aortic nodes with clean pelvic nodes.

| Structure | Primary nodes |
|---|---|
| **Ovary, fallopian tube (lateral)** | **para-aortic** (follow ovarian vessels to L2) |
| Uterine fundus | para-aortic, plus a minor route **along the round ligament to superficial inguinal** |
| Uterine body | external iliac |
| **Cervix** | **obturator, internal and external iliac, then common iliac and para-aortic**; sacral |
| Upper ⅔ vagina | internal and external iliac |
| **Lower ⅓ vagina (below hymen), vulva, perineum** | **superficial inguinal → deep inguinal (node of Cloquet) → external iliac** |

**Clue → answer:** an inguinal node metastasis from a gynaecological primary means the tumour is **at or below the hymen** — vulva, lower vagina, or (rarely, via the round ligament) fundal.

#### 2.6 Nerve supply and the anatomy of labour analgesia

| Structure | Segmental supply | Pain in labour |
|---|---|---|
| Uterine body, cervix (stretch) | **T10–L1** sympathetic via inferior hypogastric plexus | **first stage** — visceral, poorly localised, referred to back/lower abdomen |
| Cervix, upper vagina | S2–S4 parasympathetic (pelvic splanchnic) | |
| Lower vagina, vulva, perineum | **pudendal nerve, S2–S4** | **second stage** — somatic, sharp, well localised |
| Anterior vulva/mons | ilioinguinal, genitofemoral (L1) | not covered by pudendal block |

Consequences: an **epidural must cover T10–L1 for the first stage and extend to S2–S4 for the second stage**; a **pudendal block (needle guided to the ischial spine, injecting medial and inferior to it, targeting the nerve as it re-enters through the lesser sciatic foramen) covers only the perineum** — adequate for outlet forceps or perineal repair, useless for uterine pain, and it will **miss the anterior vulva** (L1 territory), which needs local infiltration.

#### 2.7 The fetal skull

Bones: 2 frontal, 2 parietal, 2 temporal, 1 occipital. Sutures: sagittal, coronal, lambdoid, frontal (metopic). Fontanelles: **anterior (bregma)** — diamond-shaped, 4 sutures, closes 18–24 months; **posterior (lambda)** — triangular, 3 sutures, closes ~6–8 weeks. **On vaginal examination, feeling a triangular fontanelle in the anterior pelvis = occiput anterior; feeling a diamond fontanelle anteriorly = occiput posterior or deflexed.**

Landmarks: occiput, vertex (between fontanelles, bounded by parietal eminences), sinciput/brow, bregma, mentum (chin), glabella.

| Diameter | Measurement | Attitude / presentation |
|---|---|---|
| **Suboccipito-bregmatic (SOB)** | **9.5 cm** | **fully flexed vertex — the optimal diameter** |
| Suboccipito-frontal | 10.0–10.5 cm | partially deflexed vertex |
| Occipito-frontal (OF) | **11.5 cm** | deflexed / "military" — the OP diameter |
| **Mento-vertical (MV)** | **13.5 cm** | **brow — largest, will not deliver vaginally at term** |
| Submento-vertical | 11.5 cm | partially extended face |
| **Submento-bregmatic (SMB)** | **9.5 cm** | **fully extended face — mento-anterior can deliver vaginally** |
| Biparietal (BPD) | 9.5 cm | transverse, all vertex presentations |
| Bitemporal | 8.0–8.2 cm | transverse, smallest |

**Memory hook:** the head presents its smallest diameter when it is either **fully flexed (SOB 9.5)** or **fully extended (SMB 9.5)**. Everything in between is worse, and the halfway point — **brow, MV 13.5 cm — is the worst possible attitude**. Hence: **face mento-anterior may deliver vaginally; face mento-posterior and brow may not** (mento-posterior requires the neck to extend further than anatomically possible).

**Moulding** (overlapping at sutures) and **caput succedaneum** (diffuse subcutaneous oedema, **crosses suture lines**, present at birth, resolves in days) are physiological. Discriminate from **cephalhaematoma** (subperiosteal, **does not cross sutures**, appears hours after birth, jaundice risk) and **subgaleal haemorrhage** (boggy, crosses sutures, shifts with position, **can exsanguinate — associated with ventouse**).

#### 2.8 The perineum and classification of tears

The perineum is the diamond between the pubic symphysis, ischial tuberosities and coccyx, divided by an intertuberous line into the **urogenital triangle** (anterior) and **anal triangle** (posterior, containing the ischiorectal fossae and the pudendal canal of Alcock on the lateral wall of each).

**Classification (ACSQHC Clinical Care Standard / RANZCOG, adopting the RCOG–Sultan system):**

| Degree | Definition |
|---|---|
| **First** | perineal or vaginal **skin/mucosa only** |
| **Second** | into the **perineal muscles**, anal sphincter intact (**an episiotomy is by definition a second-degree injury**) |
| **3a** | **<50%** of external anal sphincter (EAS) thickness torn |
| **3b** | **>50%** of EAS thickness torn |
| **3c** | **EAS and internal anal sphincter (IAS)** both torn |
| **Fourth** | EAS + IAS + **anal epithelium** breached |

⚑ Older texts describe "third degree = any sphincter involvement" without subclassification; **the 3a/3b/3c split is now standard and must be documented**, because it determines repair technique and prognosis. Third- and fourth-degree tears together are termed **OASIS** (obstetric anal sphincter injuries).

**Every woman after vaginal birth requires a systematic perineal and digital rectal examination before repair** — the standard exists because 3a and IAS injuries are otherwise missed, and missed OASIS is the leading preventable cause of later faecal incontinence.

Risk factors: **first vaginal birth, instrumental delivery (forceps > ventouse), midline episiotomy, birthweight >4 kg, prolonged second stage, OP position, shoulder dystocia, induction, epidural.** A **mediolateral episiotomy cut at 60° from the midline at crowning** reduces OASIS risk; **midline episiotomy increases it** by directing extension into the sphincter.

Repair principles: theatre, regional or general anaesthesia, good lighting and assistance. **IAS — end-to-end with interrupted 3-0 PDS; EAS — overlapping repair preferred for full-thickness (3b/3c/4th), end-to-end mandatory for 3a (there is insufficient torn tissue to overlap), using 2-0 PDS with buried knots.** Post-repair: broad-spectrum antibiotics (IV cephazolin + metronidazole, then oral amoxicillin–clavulanate for 5 days; clindamycin ± gentamicin if penicillin-anaphylactic), **laxatives and bulking agents for ~2 weeks** (constipation disrupts the repair), physiotherapy, and follow-up with consideration of endoanal ultrasound and manometry before a subsequent birth.

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Narrowest diameter in the whole pelvis | **Interspinous (~10.5 cm)** |
| Only inlet conjugate you can measure clinically | **Diagonal conjugate (~12.5 cm)**; obstetric conjugate (~10.5) is the true limit |
| Why the head engages transverse and delivers OA | **Inlet is widest transverse, outlet is widest AP** |
| Ovarian cancer with para-aortic nodes and clean pelvic nodes | **Expected** — ovary drains to para-aortic via ovarian vessels |
| Inguinal node metastasis from a gynae primary | Tumour is **at or below the hymen** (vulva/lower vagina) |
| Ureter injured at hysterectomy — where? | **~1.5–2 cm lateral to cervix at internal os, under the uterine artery** ("water under the bridge") |
| Structure clamped in oophorectomy, with ureter beneath | **Infundibulopelvic (suspensory) ligament** |
| Ligament providing principal uterine support | **Cardinal** (lateral) and **uterosacral** (posterior); **round and broad do NOT support** |
| Diamond fontanelle felt anteriorly on VE | **Deflexed / occipito-posterior** (triangular anteriorly = OA) |
| Smallest presenting diameter | **SOB 9.5 cm** (flexed vertex) — equalled by SMB 9.5 cm (full face) |
| Largest, undeliverable diameter | **Mento-vertical 13.5 cm — brow presentation** |
| Face presentation that can deliver vaginally | **Mento-anterior** only |
| Swelling crossing suture lines, present at birth | **Caput** (cephalhaematoma does not cross; subgaleal crosses and can exsanguinate) |
| Pudendal block landmark, and what it misses | **Ischial spine**; misses the **anterior vulva (L1 — ilioinguinal/genitofemoral)** and all uterine pain |
| 40% of EAS torn | **3a — end-to-end repair** (not enough tissue to overlap) |
| EAS + IAS torn, anal mucosa intact | **3c** (4th degree requires breach of anal epithelium) |
| Episiotomy angle and type to reduce OASIS | **Mediolateral, 60° from midline at crowning**; midline increases OASIS |
