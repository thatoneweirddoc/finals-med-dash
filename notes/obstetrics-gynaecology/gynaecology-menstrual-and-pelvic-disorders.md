## Menstrual and Pelvic Disorders

### Finals Tutor — Menstrual and Pelvic Disorders

#### Abnormal uterine bleeding — PALM-COEIN

Structural causes (**PALM**): **P**olyp, **A**denomyosis, **L**eiomyoma (fibroid), **M**alignancy and hyperplasia.
Non-structural (**COEIN**): **C**oagulopathy, **O**vulatory dysfunction, **E**ndometrial, **I**atrogenic, **N**ot otherwise classified.

**Red flag**: postmenopausal bleeding is endometrial cancer until proven otherwise → **transvaginal ultrasound** (endometrial thickness >4 mm warrants sampling) **and endometrial biopsy** (Pipelle or hysteroscopy).

Management of heavy menstrual bleeding without structural cause: **levonorgestrel IUD is first-line**, then tranexamic acid or NSAIDs during menses, combined oral contraceptive, progestogens; surgical options are endometrial ablation or hysterectomy.

#### Endometriosis

Endometrial-like tissue outside the uterine cavity — commonly ovaries (endometrioma, "chocolate cyst"), pouch of Douglas, uterosacral ligaments, bladder, bowel.

**Classic triad**: cyclical **dysmenorrhoea**, **dyspareunia** (deep), and **subfertility**. Also dyschezia, dysuria, chronic pelvic pain. Symptom severity correlates poorly with disease extent.

**Diagnosis** — **laparoscopy with histology is the gold standard**. Transvaginal ultrasound and MRI detect endometriomas and deep infiltrating disease but a normal scan does not exclude it. Note the pattern of the WSU question: "best investigation for diagnosis" → laparoscopy.

**Management of pain**: NSAIDs, **combined oral contraceptive** (continuous), progestogens, levonorgestrel IUD, GnRH agonists with add-back therapy; surgical excision/ablation of deposits.

**Management of infertility** — different answer from pain: **surgical excision/ablation of endometriosis improves fertility**, and assisted reproduction (IVF) is used where surgery does not achieve conception or where disease is severe. Hormonal suppression does **not** improve fertility (it prevents pregnancy while used) — a classic distractor.

#### Polycystic ovary syndrome

**Rotterdam criteria — two of three**: oligo/anovulation, clinical or biochemical hyperandrogenism, polycystic ovaries on ultrasound (≥20 follicles per ovary or ovarian volume ≥10 mL), **after exclusion of other causes** (thyroid disease, hyperprolactinaemia, congenital adrenal hyperplasia, Cushing's).

Associated with insulin resistance, obesity, type 2 diabetes, dyslipidaemia, obstructive sleep apnoea, and — importantly — **endometrial hyperplasia and carcinoma** from unopposed oestrogen with chronic anovulation.

Management: **weight loss and lifestyle first-line** (even 5–10% improves ovulation); COC for cycle regulation and hyperandrogenism; metformin for metabolic features; **letrozole is first-line for ovulation induction** (superior to clomiphene); antiandrogens for hirsutism; ensure endometrial protection.

#### Fibroids (leiomyomas)

Benign smooth muscle tumours, oestrogen-dependent, commoner in women of African ancestry. Classified by location: submucosal (most likely to cause bleeding and subfertility), intramural, subserosal.

Present with heavy menstrual bleeding, pressure symptoms, subfertility, or pain if degenerating (red degeneration in pregnancy). Management: medical as for HMB, **GnRH agonists** to shrink preoperatively, uterine artery embolisation, myomectomy (fertility-preserving), hysterectomy.

#### Pelvic inflammatory disease

Ascending infection — commonly *Chlamydia trachomatis* and *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*, often polymicrobial. Presents with lower abdominal pain, deep dyspareunia, abnormal discharge, intermenstrual or postcoital bleeding, **cervical motion tenderness**, fever.

**Have a low threshold to treat empirically** — the sequelae (infertility, ectopic pregnancy, chronic pelvic pain) are severe and diagnosis is imprecise. Treat with broad cover for chlamydia, gonorrhoea and anaerobes; contact tracing and screening for other STIs. **Fitz-Hugh–Curtis syndrome** is perihepatitis causing RUQ pain.

#### Menopause

12 months of amenorrhoea. Vasomotor symptoms, urogenital atrophy, mood and sleep disturbance, and accelerated bone loss.

**MHT (menopausal hormone therapy)** is the most effective treatment for vasomotor symptoms. **Oestrogen alone only if hysterectomised** — a woman with a uterus needs a progestogen to prevent endometrial hyperplasia and carcinoma. Contraindications include oestrogen-dependent cancer, active VTE, active liver disease, undiagnosed PV bleeding.
