## Antepartum haemorrhage, placental problems and preterm birth


### O&G Master Notes — Antepartum haemorrhage, placental problems and preterm birth

*Anchored to Queensland Clinical Guidelines (PPROM, preterm labour, antenatal corticosteroids, PPH), RANZCOG (Vasa Praevia C-Obs 47, Placenta Accreta Spectrum C-Obs 20), WA KEMH and SA Perinatal Practice Guidelines, and the National Blood Authority Rh D guideline. Scope: bleeding after 20 weeks, abnormal placentation, and the preterm birth package. AU practice throughout; divergence from NICE/RCOG flagged inline.*

---

#### 6.1 Antepartum haemorrhage — the framework

APH = genital tract bleeding from **20+0 weeks** to birth. ⚑ The 20-week floor is the AU convention (matching the stillbirth definition); UK texts often say 24 weeks.

| Grade (Safer Care Victoria) | Loss |
|---|---|
| Spotting | Staining/streaking on pad |
| Minor | <50 mL |
| Major | 50–1000 mL, **no shock** |
| Massive | >1000 mL **and/or shock** |

Two traps. **Visible loss underestimates true loss in abruption** — much can be concealed retroplacentally, so a woman can be shocked with a dry pad. And **the fetus decompensates before the mother**: uteroplacental flow is sacrificed to defend maternal BP, so an abnormal CTG may be the first sign while obs still look reassuring.

Causes: praevia, abruption, vasa praevia, uterine rupture, marginal sinus bleed, and local causes (ectropion, cervicitis, polyp, cervical cancer, trauma, "show"). About half is unexplained — but unexplained APH still predicts SGA and preterm birth, so it earns growth surveillance, not reassurance.

**The rule that gets examined most: no digital vaginal examination until the placental site is known.** Speculum is permitted and useful (visualises cervix, quantifies loss, excludes local causes, checks dilatation and membranes). A digit through a praevia can provoke catastrophic haemorrhage. Both WA KEMH and Safer Care Victoria state this explicitly.

**Investigations:** FBC, group and hold/crossmatch, coagulation profile and **fibrinogen** (falls early in abruption-related DIC — the single most useful marker), U&Es, **Kleihauer if Rh D negative**, CTG once viable, ultrasound for placental site.

**Anti-D — Australian doses (National Blood Authority 2024):** **250 IU** for a sensitising event ≤12 weeks; **625 IU** from 13 weeks (including APH, trauma, ECV); **625 IU routine prophylaxis at 28 and 34 weeks**; **625 IU postnatally** if the baby is Rh D positive. Give within **72 h** (up to 10 days, reduced efficacy). After 20 weeks quantify FMH and add **100 IU per mL of fetal red cells above 6 mL**. ⚑ AU doses are in IU (625 IU ≈ 125 µg), not the UK's 500/1500 IU.

**The short version**
- APH = bleeding ≥20+0 weeks; visible loss underestimates abruption.
- **No digital VE until placental site known** — speculum is fine.
- Fibrinogen is the early DIC marker; Kleihauer if Rh D negative.
- AU anti-D: **250 IU ≤12 weeks, 625 IU thereafter and at 28/34 weeks.**

#### 6.2 Praevia vs abruption vs vasa praevia — the discriminators

| Feature | **Placenta praevia** | **Placental abruption** | **Vasa praevia** | Uterine rupture |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pain | **Painless** | **Painful** — constant, severe | Painless | Sudden severe pain, may ease after rupture |
| Uterus | **Soft, non-tender** | **Woody-hard, tender, irritable**, hypertonic | Soft | Contractions cease; **fetal parts palpable abdominally** |
| Bleeding | Bright red, **revealed**, recurrent, escalating | Dark, **may be concealed** — loss ≠ severity | Modest volume, **at membrane rupture** | Variable, may be intra-abdominal |
| Mother | Shock **proportional** to visible loss | Shock **out of proportion**; DIC | **Mother is well** — the blood is fetal | Shock, haematuria, shoulder tip pain |
| Fetus | Usually well | Distressed or dead | **Catastrophic** — bradycardia/sinusoidal then death | Acute bradycardia |
| Presentation | **High head, malpresentation** (placenta occupies lower segment) | Engaged, normal lie | Normal | Presenting part recedes |
| Discriminator | **Painless + soft uterus + abnormal lie** | **Painful + woody uterus + fetal compromise** | **Fetal compromise, well mother, triggered by ARM/SROM** | **Prior scar + loss of contractions + station regression** |

Hook: **praevia bleeds the mother, vasa praevia bleeds the baby, abruption bleeds into the uterus.**

**Placenta praevia.** ⚑ **Grades I–IV (and marginal/partial/complete) are abandoned.** Current terminology is binary, defined on **transvaginal ultrasound** (safe in praevia, far more accurate than transabdominal): **placenta praevia** = covers the internal os; **low-lying placenta** = edge **<20 mm** from the os but not covering. Most low-lying placentas at the 18–20 week morphology scan resolve as the lower segment forms — **rescan ~32 weeks**, again at ~36 weeks if still low. **An edge <20 mm from the os in the third trimester generally means caesarean**; ≥20 mm can usually attempt vaginal birth.
Risks: **previous caesarean** (dose-dependent), previous praevia, prior uterine surgery/curettage, multiparity, multiple pregnancy, advanced maternal age, smoking, ART.
Management: avoid intercourse and digital VE; anti-D; corticosteroids if preterm birth anticipated; inpatient care for recurrent or major bleeds; correct anaemia antenatally. Planned caesarean **around 36–38 weeks, individualised** (earlier for recurrent bleeding; Safer Care Victoria advises against elective birth before 38 weeks if truly asymptomatic). Praevia is a major PPH risk — the lower segment contracts poorly, so the "living ligature" fails.

**Placental abruption.** Incidence ~0.4–1%; recurrence 4–12%. Strongest risk factor is **previous abruption**; then **hypertension/pre-eclampsia**, abdominal trauma (**consider intimate partner violence**), **smoking, cocaine/methamphetamine**, PPROM, chorioamnionitis, **sudden uterine decompression** (polyhydramnios, twins), thrombophilia, advanced maternal age, FGR.
⚑ **Abruption is a clinical diagnosis; ultrasound is insensitive** — fresh retroplacental clot is isoechoic with placenta. A visible haematoma rules it *in*; a normal scan excludes nothing. Favourite MCQ distractor.
Complications: **DIC**, shock, AKI, **Couvelaire uterus** (blood tracking into myometrium — bruised, boggy, poorly contractile), **PPH**, fetal hypoxia, stillbirth.
Management: resuscitate, continuous CTG, correct coagulopathy, anti-D. **Fetus alive and compromised → immediate caesarean. Fetus dead → aim for vaginal birth** (amniotomy + oxytocin), because operating into established DIC is dangerous. Expectant management only if preterm, stable, minor and non-progressive.

**Vasa praevia.** Unprotected fetal vessels in the membranes **within 2 cm of the internal os**. Types (RANZCOG C-Obs 47): **1** velamentous cord insertion; **2** vessels between lobes of a bilobed/succenturiate placenta; **3** aberrant marginal vessels, often after a "resolved" praevia. Risks: velamentous insertion, bilobed/succenturiate placenta, low-lying placenta or resolved praevia, **IVF/ART**, multiple pregnancy.
Vignette: **SROM/ARM → modest fresh bleeding → immediate profound fetal bradycardia or sinusoidal CTG, mother entirely well.** Undiagnosed perinatal mortality **44–70%** — a fetus has only ~80–100 mL/kg blood volume, so a trivial-looking loss is exsanguinating.
Detection: TVUS with colour Doppler; RANZCOG suggests assessing cord insertion at the morphology scan, with **TVUS + Doppler at 28–32 weeks** if risk features. Management: corticosteroids, consider admission from ~32 weeks, **planned caesarean from 36+0 weeks** (**34+0** with additional preterm risk factors), before labour and before membranes rupture. Any bleeding or labour = emergency caesarean. ⚑ Apt test/Kleihauer on vaginal blood is obsolete — there is no time.

**The short version**
- **Painless + soft uterus + malpresentation** = praevia. **Painful + woody uterus + fetal distress** = abruption. **Bleeding at SROM + fetal collapse + well mother** = vasa praevia.
- ⚑ Praevia now defined on TVUS: covers os = praevia, <20 mm = low-lying; grades I–IV gone.
- **A normal ultrasound does not exclude abruption.**
- Vasa praevia: planned caesarean **36+0** weeks (34+0 with risk factors).

#### 6.3 Placenta accreta spectrum (PAS)

Abnormally adherent/invasive placentation from deficient decidua basalis, usually over a caesarean scar.

| Term | Depth |
|---|---|
| **Accreta** | Villi adherent to myometrium; will not separate cleanly |
| **Increta** | Villi **invade into** myometrium |
| **Percreta** | Villi **penetrate through** serosa ± adjacent organs (bladder) |

Independent risks in AU/NZ data (C-Obs 20): **previous caesarean**, **placenta praevia**, older maternal age, multiparity — risk climbs steeply with each additional caesarean. **Praevia + previous caesarean is the accreta stem until proven otherwise.** Also prior myomectomy, curettage, endometrial ablation, Asherman, ART.

Diagnosis: **ultrasound first-line** — loss of the retroplacental clear zone, myometrial thinning, placental lacunae ("moth-eaten"/"Swiss cheese"), bridging vessels on Doppler, bladder wall interruption. **MRI is adjunctive only** (uncertain ultrasound, posterior placenta); it does not outperform ultrasound.

Management: **MDT in a tertiary centre** with massive transfusion, critical care and complex pelvic surgical capability. Planned **caesarean, usually with hysterectomy, leaving the placenta in situ** — RANZCOG explicitly discourages manual removal, and balloon/compression sutures have a role only after spontaneous separation. Uterus-conserving approaches require intensive surveillance and the ability to convert to emergency hysterectomy. Timing is individualised against fetal maturity — most units plan **34–36 weeks** with steroid cover, before labour supervenes; RANZCOG declines to specify a week.

**The short version**
- **Praevia + previous caesarean = suspect accreta.** Ultrasound first-line, MRI only if uncertain.
- Planned caesarean hysterectomy, tertiary MDT; **leave the placenta in situ, do not pull.**

#### 6.4 Preterm labour

Preterm = birth 20+0 to <37+0 weeks. Subcategories: extremely preterm <28, very preterm 28+0–31+6, moderately preterm 32+0–33+6, late preterm 34+0–36+6. Two-thirds are spontaneous; the rest provider-initiated.

Risks: **previous spontaneous preterm birth** (strongest), short cervix, multiple pregnancy, uterine anomaly, prior cervical surgery (LLETZ/cone), PPROM, infection (BV, UTI, chorioamnionitis, periodontal), APH, polyhydramnios, smoking, low BMI, short inter-pregnancy interval, socioeconomic disadvantage.

**Prediction (QCG).** **Transvaginal cervical length** — screen 16–24 weeks; **<25 mm = short**, with risk rising steeply as length falls. **Fetal fibronectin / Actim Partus** — valid in symptomatic women with intact membranes ~22–36 weeks; **its value is the negative predictive value, 95–98% for ruling out birth within 7 days.** A negative avoids admission, steroids and transfer; a positive is only weakly predictive. Frame it as a **rule-out** test, never rule-in.

**Prevention:** **vaginal progesterone 200 mg nocte, started 16–24 weeks, continued to 36+0** for previous spontaneous preterm birth and/or short cervix. **Cervical cerclage** where length ≤25 mm before 24 weeks with supporting history; history-indicated cerclage for recurrent mid-trimester loss.

##### Tocolysis
**Tocolysis buys ~48 hours — enough to complete antenatal corticosteroids and transfer to a centre with neonatal facilities.** It does not improve perinatal mortality on its own; do not tocolyse simply to prolong pregnancy.
- **Agent of choice in Australia: nifedipine (immediate-release).** QCG: 20 mg oral stat, repeatable every 30 min up to three doses, then 20 mg every 3–8 h for up to 48 h (max 160 mg/day). ⚑ A shift from older texts teaching **betamimetics (salbutamol/terbutaline) first-line** — now second-line at best (maternal tachycardia, hyperglycaemia, pulmonary oedema). Atosiban is used in some units; NICE prefers nifedipine with atosiban as alternative.
- **Indomethacin** restricted to **<28–32 weeks**, short courses (ductal constriction, oligohydramnios).
- Contraindications: chorioamnionitis/sepsis, significant APH or abruption, severe pre-eclampsia, non-reassuring fetal status, fetal death, lethal anomaly, gestation beyond ~34 weeks. Nifedipine specifically: maternal hypotension, cardiac disease. **Avoid casually combining nifedipine with MgSO4** — additive hypotension and neuromuscular blockade.

##### Antenatal corticosteroids
Reduce **RDS, IVH, NEC and neonatal death** — the highest-impact intervention in preterm birth.
- **Betamethasone 11.4 mg IM × 2 doses, 24 h apart** (may shorten to 12 h if birth likely within 24 h), **or dexamethasone 6 mg IM × 4 doses, 12 h apart.**
- **Window: 22+0 to 34+6 weeks** (QCG) when birth is anticipated within 7 days; at 22+0–23+6 only where active neonatal care is planned. ⚑ The lower bound has crept down — older texts say 24–34 weeks.
- **Maximal benefit if birth occurs 24 h to 7 days after the second dose**, but give even if birth is imminent — a single dose helps.
- **Repeat/rescue:** single further 11.4 mg dose if >7 days since the last with ongoing risk (most effective ≤32+6); QCG caps at three repeat doses.
- **Late preterm:** consider a course 48 h before **planned caesarean >34+6 weeks** with suspected lung immaturity or other risk factors — though deferring elective caesarean to ≥39+0 weeks is usually the better answer.
- Cautions, not contraindications: **diabetes** (expect BGL destabilisation — monitor, often insulin infusion); FGR (repeat doses → lower birth weight); chorioamnionitis. Steroids do **not** increase maternal infection risk.

##### Magnesium sulfate for fetal neuroprotection
Same drug as eclampsia prophylaxis, different indication.
- **Anticipated preterm birth before 30+0 weeks, within the next 24 hours** (QCG; SA specifies 24+0 to <30+0).
- **4 g IV load over 20 min, then 1 g/hour until birth or 24 h**, whichever first. Aim to give **as close to 4 h before birth as possible**; never delay an indicated urgent birth for it.
- Reduces **cerebral palsy and gross motor dysfunction** (~63 women treated to prevent one case).
- Monitor RR, deep tendon reflexes, urine output, conscious state. **Loss of patellar reflexes is the earliest toxicity sign**, then respiratory depression, then cardiac arrest. Antidote **calcium gluconate 1 g IV**. Caution in renal impairment (renally cleared).
- ⚑ **AU/UK difference:** Australia uses **<30 weeks**. NICE offers at **24+0–29+6** and *considers* at **30+0–33+6**. In an Australian paper, answer **30 weeks**.

**The short version**
- fFN/Actim Partus is a **rule-out** test (NPV 95–98% for birth within 7 days); cervix <25 mm = short.
- ⚑ **Nifedipine** is the AU tocolytic; tocolysis buys **48 h for steroids and transfer**, nothing more.
- **Betamethasone 11.4 mg IM × 2, 24 h apart, 22+0–34+6 weeks.**
- **MgSO4 4 g then 1 g/h, before 30+0 weeks, birth expected within 24 h** — prevents cerebral palsy.

#### 6.5 PPROM

Rupture of membranes before 37+0 weeks and before labour. Complicates 2–3% of pregnancies but causes a third of preterm births.

**Diagnosis: sterile speculum** — pooling in the posterior fornix or leakage on Valsalva/cough is the gold standard. If equivocal: **IGFBP-1 (Actim PROM) or PAMG-1 (AmniSure)**; oligohydramnios on ultrasound is supportive, not diagnostic. **Nitrazine/pH paper and ferning are unreliable** (false positives from blood, semen, urine, vaginitis). Take a low vaginal/perianal GBS swab at the same speculum.

⚑ **Avoid digital vaginal examination in PPROM** — QCG states it plainly: digital VE increases ascending infection and shortens latency.

**Antibiotics — the highest-yield fact here.**
- **Erythromycin 250 mg orally 6-hourly for up to 7 days or until birth** (QCG; azithromycin 1 g single dose is an accepted alternative).
- ⚑ **Amoxicillin–clavulanate (co-amoxiclav) is specifically avoided because of increased neonatal necrotising enterocolitis** (ORACLE I). If a stem offers co-amoxiclav for PPROM, it is the wrong answer.
- ⚑ **AU vs UK:** Queensland *adds* **ampicillin/amoxicillin 2 g IV 6-hourly for 48 h, then amoxicillin 250 mg orally 8-hourly** to complete 7 days (Mercer regimen). **NICE uses erythromycin monotherapy 250 mg qds for 10 days or until established labour.** Two differences: the added beta-lactam, and **7 days (AU) vs 10 days (UK)**. If only one drug is offered, **erythromycin**.
- Antibiotics prolong latency and reduce chorioamnionitis, neonatal infection and surfactant use.

**Also give:** antenatal corticosteroids per the window above (PPROM is not a contraindication), **MgSO4 if <30 weeks with birth expected within 24 h**, and **GBS intrapartum prophylaxis** once labour establishes (QCG: **benzylpenicillin 3 g IV load then 1.8 g every 4 h**, ideally ≥4 h before birth; Queensland uses a **risk-based**, not universal-screening, approach — preterm labour is itself a trigger).

| Gestation | Approach |
|---|---|
| **<34+0** | **Expectant** — prematurity risk exceeds the risk of waiting. Steroids, erythromycin, MgSO4 if <30 weeks, close surveillance. |
| **34+0–37+0** | Evidence "limited and inconsistent" (QCG); individualised. Many AU units await spontaneous labour to ~36–37 weeks, earlier if GBS positive. ⚑ A softening from the older "deliver everyone at 34 weeks." |
| Any | **Deliver regardless** if chorioamnionitis, abruption, cord prolapse, established labour or non-reassuring fetal status. |

**Surveillance:** 4-hourly maternal HR, temperature, vaginal loss, uterine tenderness/activity, plus fetal movements and FHR. **Chorioamnionitis is clinical** — fever plus ≥2 of: maternal tachycardia, **fetal tachycardia** (often earliest), uterine tenderness, offensive discharge, WCC >15 × 10⁹/L, CRP >40 (SA PPG). Treat with **broad-spectrum IV antibiotics and expedited birth at any gestation** — not an indication to tocolyse or wait out a steroid course.

**Prolonged PPROM at early gestation:** pulmonary hypoplasia and limb contractures from prolonged oligohydramnios (especially rupture before 22–24 weeks), cord prolapse, abruption, neonatal sepsis.

**The short version**
- Diagnose on **sterile speculum**; **no digital VE**; pH/ferning unreliable.
- ⚑ **Erythromycin, not co-amoxiclav — co-amoxiclav causes NEC.**
- **<34 weeks → expectant + steroids + erythromycin (+ MgSO4 if <30 weeks).**
- Chorioamnionitis at any gestation → **IV antibiotics and deliver.**

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators — Section 6

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Painless bright red bleed, **soft non-tender uterus**, transverse lie at 32 weeks | Placenta praevia |
| Constant pain, **woody-hard tender uterus**, shock out of proportion to visible loss | Placental abruption |
| **ARM → fresh bleeding → immediate fetal bradycardia/sinusoidal CTG, mother well** | Vasa praevia — emergency caesarean |
| APH with placental site not documented — what must you NOT do? | **Digital vaginal examination** (speculum only) |
| Suspected abruption, ultrasound normal | **Ultrasound is insensitive — does not exclude it**; manage clinically |
| Earliest lab marker of abruption-related DIC | **Falling fibrinogen** |
| Placental edge <20 mm from internal os, not covering | **Low-lying placenta** (⚑ grades I–IV abandoned) |
| Placenta praevia + two previous caesareans | **Placenta accreta spectrum** — tertiary MDT, caesarean hysterectomy |
| First-line imaging for PAS | **Ultrasound** (MRI adjunct only) |
| At caesarean for accreta the placenta will not separate | **Leave it in situ — do not attempt manual removal** |
| Planned birth for asymptomatic vasa praevia | **36+0 weeks** (34+0 with preterm risk factors) |
| Antibiotic of choice in PPROM | **Erythromycin 250 mg qid** |
| Antibiotic specifically avoided in PPROM, and why | ⚑ **Amoxicillin–clavulanate — neonatal necrotising enterocolitis** |
| Antenatal corticosteroid regimen and window (AU) | **Betamethasone 11.4 mg IM × 2, 24 h apart; 22+0–34+6 weeks** |
| MgSO4 neuroprotection — gestation, dose, purpose | **<30+0 weeks; 4 g IV over 20 min then 1 g/h; reduces cerebral palsy** |
| Earliest sign of magnesium toxicity / antidote | **Loss of patellar reflexes / calcium gluconate 1 g IV** |
| AU tocolytic of choice, and its only real purpose | ⚑ **Nifedipine; 48 h to complete steroids and transfer** |
