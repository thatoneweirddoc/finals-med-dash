## Gynaecological Oncology and Screening

### Finals Tutor — Gynaecological Oncology and Screening

#### Cervical screening in Australia — know the current program

The National Cervical Screening Program changed from 2-yearly Pap smears to **5-yearly HPV testing**:

- **Start at 25 years**, every **5 years**, until **74**
- Primary test is **HPV PCR** with reflex liquid-based cytology if HPV positive
- **Self-collection** is now available to all screened participants
- HPV 16/18 positive → direct referral for **colposcopy**
- Other oncogenic HPV positive → reflex cytology guides management

Any woman with **abnormal bleeding** (postcoital, intermenstrual, postmenopausal) needs assessment regardless of screening history — screening is for asymptomatic women.

**HPV vaccination**: Gardasil 9, school-based at ~12–13 years, single dose now sufficient for immunocompetent adolescents in the Australian program.

#### Cervical cancer

Squamous cell carcinoma (most) or adenocarcinoma. Driven by persistent oncogenic HPV (16 and 18 account for ~70%). Risk factors: HPV, smoking, immunosuppression, non-participation in screening, early coitarche, multiple partners.

Presents with postcoital or intermenstrual bleeding, offensive discharge, pelvic pain; advanced disease with renal failure from ureteric obstruction. Staged FIGO. Treatment: fertility-sparing options for very early disease, radical hysterectomy with lymphadenectomy, chemoradiation for locally advanced.

#### Endometrial cancer

**The commonest gynaecological malignancy in Australia.** Typically postmenopausal, presenting with **postmenopausal bleeding** — this symptom is investigated in all cases.

Type 1 (endometrioid, oestrogen-driven, better prognosis) — risks are all sources of **unopposed oestrogen**: obesity, nulliparity, early menarche/late menopause, PCOS, tamoxifen, unopposed oestrogen therapy, Lynch syndrome. Type 2 (serous, clear cell) is oestrogen-independent and more aggressive.

Investigation: TVUS then **endometrial sampling**. Treatment: total hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy ± lymphadenectomy, adjuvant radiotherapy/chemotherapy by stage.

#### Ovarian cancer

Presents late and vaguely — bloating, early satiety, abdominal or pelvic pain, urinary urgency, weight loss — which is why mortality is high. There is **no effective population screening program**.

Epithelial (high-grade serous commonest) — risks: age, nulliparity, early menarche/late menopause, endometriosis, **BRCA1/2 and Lynch syndrome**. Protective: pregnancy, breastfeeding, COC, tubal ligation.

Investigation: pelvic ultrasound, **CA-125** (raised in many benign conditions too — endometriosis, fibroids, PID, pregnancy — so poor specificity), CT for staging. In younger women consider germ cell tumours (AFP, hCG, LDH).

Management: surgical staging and cytoreduction plus platinum-based chemotherapy.

#### Vulval conditions

**Lichen sclerosus** — white atrophic plaques, intense pruritus, "figure of eight" distribution, architectural loss. Treat with **potent topical corticosteroid**. Small but real risk of vulval SCC, so biopsy anything that fails to respond or looks atypical.

Vulval cancer — usually SCC, in older women or HPV-related in younger. Any persistent vulval lump, ulcer or pruritus needs examination and biopsy.
