## Labour and Delivery

### Finals Tutor — Labour and Delivery

#### The four Ps

**Power** (uterine contractions), **Passenger** (fetal size, lie, presentation, position, attitude), **Passage** (bony pelvis and soft tissues), **Psyche** (maternal state and support). Failure to progress is analysed by working through these four in turn — that structure is the expected answer.

#### Stages

| Stage | Definition |
|---|---|
| First — latent | Onset of contractions to ~4–6 cm, irregular progress |
| First — active | ~4–6 cm to full dilatation; expect ~1 cm/hour (nullip) |
| Second | Full dilatation to delivery of baby; passive then active pushing |
| Third | Delivery of baby to delivery of placenta |

**Active management of the third stage** reduces PPH: prophylactic uterotonic (**oxytocin 10 IU IM**), controlled cord traction, uterine massage. Signs of placental separation: cord lengthening, gush of blood, uterus rising and becoming globular.

#### Mechanism of labour

Engagement → descent → **flexion** → internal rotation → extension (crowning) → restitution/external rotation → expulsion. The occiput-anterior position is the most favourable; occipito-posterior gives longer labour, more backache, and higher instrumental delivery rates.

#### CTG interpretation — DR C BRAVADO

- **DR** — Define Risk (maternal and fetal risk factors)
- **C** — Contractions (frequency in 10 minutes, duration)
- **BRa** — Baseline Rate (normal **110–160 bpm**)
- **V** — Variability (normal **5–25 bpm**; reduced <5 for >50 min is concerning; sinusoidal is ominous — think fetal anaemia)
- **A** — Accelerations (reassuring)
- **D** — Decelerations (early = head compression, physiological; **variable** = cord compression; **late** = uteroplacental insufficiency, the worrying one; prolonged >3 min needs urgent action)
- **O** — Overall impression

Causes of fetal bradycardia to act on immediately: cord prolapse, uterine rupture, placental abruption, maternal hypotension (e.g. post-epidural), uterine hyperstimulation.

#### Induction of labour

**Indications**: post-dates (usually offered 41–42 weeks), pre-eclampsia, diabetes, IUGR, PROM, obstetric cholestasis, reduced fetal movements, fetal death.

**Bishop score** assesses cervical favourability — dilatation, effacement, station, consistency, position. A low score favours cervical ripening first.

**Methods**: prostaglandin (dinoprostone) gel/pessary, Foley balloon catheter (mechanical), artificial rupture of membranes, oxytocin infusion.

**Complications**: uterine hyperstimulation with fetal compromise, failed induction, cord prolapse at ARM, uterine rupture (especially with previous caesarean — prostaglandins used cautiously).

#### Preterm labour

Before 37 weeks. Management aims:
- **Corticosteroids** (betamethasone) if <34–35 weeks — reduces RDS, IVH, necrotising enterocolitis, and neonatal death
- **Magnesium sulfate** if <30–32 weeks — **fetal neuroprotection**, reduces cerebral palsy
- **Tocolysis** (nifedipine) — buys ~48 hours for steroids and transfer, does not improve outcomes on its own
- **Antibiotics** if GBS positive or PPROM
- Transfer to a unit with appropriate neonatal facilities

#### PROM and PPROM

Rupture of membranes before labour. **Preterm** PROM (<37 weeks) risks chorioamnionitis, cord prolapse, pulmonary hypoplasia. Confirm with sterile speculum (pooling); **avoid digital examination** — it introduces infection. Manage with steroids, antibiotics (erythromycin), monitoring for infection, and planned delivery timing.
