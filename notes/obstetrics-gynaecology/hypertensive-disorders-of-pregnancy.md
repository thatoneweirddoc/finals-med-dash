## Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy

### Finals Tutor — Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy

#### Classification (SOMANZ)

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| Chronic hypertension | Pre-existing, or before 20 weeks |
| Gestational hypertension | New hypertension **after 20 weeks**, no proteinuria or organ dysfunction |
| Pre-eclampsia | Hypertension after 20 weeks **plus** end-organ involvement |
| Pre-eclampsia superimposed on chronic HTN | New organ dysfunction in a chronic hypertensive |

#### Pre-eclampsia — the diagnostic shift worth knowing

**Proteinuria is no longer required.** Diagnosis is hypertension (≥140/90) after 20 weeks **plus any one** of:

- **Renal** — proteinuria (PCR ≥30 mg/mmol), creatinine rise, oliguria
- **Haematological** — thrombocytopenia, haemolysis, DIC
- **Hepatic** — raised transaminases, severe RUQ/epigastric pain
- **Neurological** — headache, visual disturbance, hyperreflexia with clonus, seizure (eclampsia), stroke
- **Pulmonary oedema**
- **Uteroplacental** — fetal growth restriction, abnormal umbilical artery Dopplers

#### Pathophysiology in one line

Failure of trophoblastic invasion of maternal spiral arteries → high-resistance, low-flow placental perfusion → placental ischaemia → release of antiangiogenic factors (sFlt-1, soluble endoglin) → **systemic maternal endothelial dysfunction**. That endothelial injury explains every organ manifestation.

#### Risk factors

Nulliparity, previous pre-eclampsia, chronic hypertension, diabetes, renal disease, autoimmune disease (SLE, antiphospholipid), multiple pregnancy, BMI >30, maternal age >40, family history, new partner, long interpregnancy interval.

**Prevention**: aspirin 100–150 mg nocte from 12 weeks (before 16 weeks) in high-risk women; calcium supplementation if low dietary intake.

#### Management

**Antihypertensives** — treat sustained BP ≥140/90. Safe in pregnancy: **labetalol, nifedipine, methyldopa, hydralazine**. **Contraindicated: ACE inhibitors and ARBs** (fetal renal dysgenesis, oligohydramnios, skull defects) — a reliable exam point.

**Magnesium sulfate** — for seizure prophylaxis in severe pre-eclampsia and for **treatment of eclampsia** (it is the anticonvulsant of choice, not diazepam or phenytoin). Monitor for toxicity: loss of deep tendon reflexes first, then respiratory depression, then cardiac arrest. **Antidote: calcium gluconate.**

**Corticosteroids** — betamethasone if <34 weeks for fetal lung maturity.

**Definitive treatment is delivery of the placenta.** Timing balances maternal risk against prematurity: deliver at term, earlier for severe or deteriorating disease.

#### HELLP syndrome

**H**aemolysis, **E**levated **L**iver enzymes, **L**ow **P**latelets. May occur without marked hypertension. Presents with RUQ/epigastric pain, nausea, malaise. Risks hepatic haematoma and rupture. Management: stabilise, magnesium, deliver.

#### Postpartum

Pre-eclampsia can present or worsen **up to 6 weeks postpartum** — eclampsia occurs postpartum in a substantial minority. Continue monitoring. Long-term: markedly increased lifetime cardiovascular risk, so counsel and arrange follow-up.
