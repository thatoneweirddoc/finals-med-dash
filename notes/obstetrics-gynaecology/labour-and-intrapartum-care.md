## Labour and intrapartum care


### O&G Master Notes — Labour and intrapartum care

*Anchored to Queensland Clinical Guidelines (Normal birth, Prolonged/obstructed labour, Intrapartum fetal surveillance, Induction of labour, Instrumental vaginal birth, Primary PPH), RANZCOG (Intrapartum Fetal Surveillance C-Obs 1, Management of Breech Presentation), the ACSQHC Third and Fourth Degree Perineal Tears Clinical Care Standard, and WA KEMH labour guidelines. CTG is described in words only — no figure required.*

---

#### 7.1 Stages of labour and normal progress

**First stage** — onset of labour to full dilatation.
- **Latent phase:** irregular painful contractions with effacement and dilatation **up to 4–6 cm**; may last days. This is not "failure to progress." ⚑ The old teaching that active labour starts at 3–4 cm has shifted — contemporary data (Zhang; WHO Labour Care Guide) show the curve does not steepen until ~5–6 cm, and the **Friedman "1 cm/hour" alert line is obsolete**.
- **Established/active phase:** regular painful contractions with progressive dilatation from at least 4 cm. **Normal progress ≥0.5 cm/hour (2 cm in 4 hours)** — below this with adequate contractions, escalate.

**Second stage** — full dilatation to birth. **Passive** = full dilatation without expulsive effort (allows descent and rotation; valuable with an epidural or OP position). **Active** = expulsive pushing or head visible.

| | Nulliparous | Multiparous |
|---|---|---|
| Birth expected within | **3 hours** | **2 hours** |
| Suspect delay after (active) | **1 hour** | **30 minutes** |
| Escalate as duration approaches | **2 hours** | **1 hour** |

Epidural prolongs the second stage — allow a longer passive phase, add roughly an hour.

**Third stage** — birth of baby to birth of placenta. Prolonged if **>30 min (active management)** or **>60 min (physiological)**.

**Routine care (QCG Normal birth):** VE offered **4-hourly** in active labour; intermittent auscultation **every 15–30 min in the first stage** and **at least every 5 min in the active second stage** (immediately after a contraction, for a full minute); **deferred cord clamping ≥1 minute** or until pulsation ceases.

**The three Ps of poor progress** — Power (inadequate contractions; commonest in nulliparas, correctable with amniotomy and oxytocin), Passenger (malposition, malpresentation, macrosomia, deflexion, caput and moulding), Passage. ⚑ **Beware oxytocin in the multipara with poor progress** — in a parous uterus, failure to progress far more often means obstruction, and augmenting obstruction risks rupture. QCG: use oxytocin cautiously in multiparas, never with signs of obstruction.

**Signs of obstructed labour:** maternal and fetal tachycardia, pyrexia, oedematous vulva/cervix, haematuria, vaginal bleeding, constant severe pain, hypertonic contractions, and **Bandl's ring** (palpable retraction ring between thick upper and thinned lower segment — imminent rupture).

Oxytocin target: **3–4 contractions per 10 min, each 40–60 s, ≥60 s rest between.** More than 5 in 10 = tachysystole.

**The short version**
- Active first stage from ~4–6 cm; **normal progress ≥0.5 cm/h**. ⚑ Friedman's 1 cm/h is obsolete.
- Second stage: birth within **3 h nullip / 2 h multip**; suspect delay after **1 h / 30 min** of pushing.
- Poor progress in a **multipara** = obstruction until proven otherwise.

#### 7.2 Fetal monitoring and CTG interpretation

**Who gets what.** Intermittent auscultation suits low-risk women in established labour. **Continuous CTG** where risk factors exist: antenatal (pre-eclampsia, diabetes, FGR/SGA, oligo/polyhydramnios, ≥41 weeks, multiple pregnancy, breech, APH, reduced fetal movements, previous caesarean) or intrapartum (oxytocin, epidural, **meconium**, abnormal auscultation, PV bleeding, **maternal pyrexia ≥38 °C**, suspected chorioamnionitis, excessive uterine activity, prolonged labour).

Context first: **CTG is sensitive but poorly specific for fetal acidaemia.** Continuous monitoring in low-risk labour raises caesarean and instrumental rates without reducing cerebral palsy — which is why RANZCOG restricts it to risk-flagged labours. Never interpret a trace without gestation, stage, meconium, temperature, contraction frequency and progress.

##### The four features — in words

**Baseline rate** — mean FHR over 5–10 min, excluding accelerations and decelerations. **Normal 110–160 bpm.**
- **Tachycardia >160**: maternal pyrexia/chorioamnionitis (commonest), dehydration, anxiety, beta-agonists, prematurity, fetal anaemia or tachyarrhythmia, and — late and ominously — chronic hypoxia. **A rising baseline through labour is a red flag even while still "normal."**
- **Bradycardia <110**: may be benign at 100–110 post-dates with normal variability; otherwise cord compression, maternal hypotension (post-epidural), rapid descent, hyperstimulation, abruption, cord prolapse, uterine rupture, maternal beta-blockade. **A sustained fall >5 minutes is a bradycardia, not a deceleration — an emergency.**

**Baseline variability** — the bandwidth of beat-to-beat fluctuation; visually, how "fuzzy" versus how "flat" the line is. **The single most important indicator of wellbeing**, because it reflects an intact, well-oxygenated autonomic nervous system. **Normal 6–25 bpm.**
- **Reduced 3–5 bpm** — usually **fetal sleep** (should not exceed ~40 min), also opioids/sedatives, magnesium sulfate, prematurity, fetal tachycardia. Beyond 40 min, or with any other abnormality, it becomes concerning.
- **Absent <3 bpm** — significant; suggests established hypoxia/acidaemia or neurological injury.
- **Increased/saltatory >25 bpm** — a swinging exaggerated pattern associated with acute hypoxia and rapidly evolving compromise. **Do not read it as reassuring "good variability."**
- **Sinusoidal** — a smooth, regular, undulating sine wave, amplitude ~5–15 bpm, **2–5 cycles per minute, with absent beat-to-beat variability and no accelerations**, persisting ≥10–20 min. Pathognomonic of **severe fetal anaemia** — fetomaternal haemorrhage, ruptured vasa praevia, rhesus disease, parvovirus B19. Distinguish from *pseudosinusoidal* (jagged, short-lived, retains variability, often post-opioid), which is benign.

**Accelerations** — rise **≥15 bpm sustained ≥15 seconds** (≥10 bpm/10 s before 32 weeks). Presence effectively excludes acidaemia at that moment; **absence alone with an otherwise normal trace is not pathological**.

**Decelerations** — timing relative to the contraction is everything.

| Type | Shape and timing | Mechanism | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Early** | Shallow, **uniform, symmetrical**, gradual on and off; **nadir coincides with the contraction peak**; mirrors the contraction | **Head compression** → vagal reflex | **Physiological.** Late first/second stage. No action |
| **Variable** | **Abrupt onset and abrupt recovery** ("V"/"W" shaped), varying shape, depth and timing | **Cord compression** | Usually tolerated. Reassuring if <60 s, <60 bpm deep, quick recovery, normal baseline, retained variability, with "shouldering". **Concerning if:** >60 s, >60 bpm, slow return, **loss of shouldering, overshoot, reduced variability within the deceleration, rising baseline** |
| **Late** | Uniform, gradual, shallow but **repetitive**; begins after the contraction starts, **nadir >20 s after the peak**; recovers after the contraction ends | **Uteroplacental insufficiency** | **Always pathological when repetitive** — the most sinister routine pattern. Escalate |
| **Prolonged** | Fall lasting **>90 s up to 5 min** | Acute event | Urgent assessment; **beyond 5 min it is a bradycardia** — prepare for immediate birth |

Hooks: **VEAL CHOP** (Variable→Cord, Early→Head, Acceleration→OK, Late→Placental). For shape: **early decelerations mirror the contraction; late decelerations lag it; variable decelerations ignore it.**

**Classification.** QCG uses colour-coded escalation (green normal, blue borderline, yellow concerning, red abnormal) rather than the older normal/suspicious/pathological triad. A **normal CTG** = baseline 110–160, variability 6–25, accelerations present, **no decelerations**.

**Intrauterine resuscitation, in order:**
1. **Call for help; stop or halve the oxytocin; remove any prostaglandin pessary.**
2. **Reposition** — left lateral (relieves aortocaval compression); knee–chest if cord compression suspected.
3. **IV fluid bolus**; correct maternal hypotension (ephedrine/metaraminol post-epidural).
4. **Vaginal examination** — exclude **cord prolapse**, assess progress (rapid descent explains many decelerations).
5. **Acute tocolysis** for excessive uterine activity — **terbutaline 250 µg subcutaneously** (or GTN).
6. Treat maternal pyrexia ≥38 °C; screen and treat sepsis.
7. Not resolving → **fetal blood sampling** (if non-urgent and feasible) or **expedite birth**.

⚑ **Routine maternal facemask oxygen is no longer recommended** in a normoxic woman — no demonstrated benefit, possible harm. Older textbooks list it first; it has been dropped.

**Fetal blood sampling (RANZCOG and QCG concur):**

| | pH | Lactate (mmol/L) | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | **≥7.25** | **≤4.0** | Continue; repeat in 40–60 min if concerns persist |
| Borderline | **7.21–7.24** | **4.1–4.7** | Repeat in **20–30 min** |
| Abnormal | **≤7.20** | **≥4.8** | **Acidaemia — expedite birth** |

Lactate has largely displaced pH in AU practice (smaller sample, higher success rate). Contraindicated with maternal HIV/hepatitis, suspected fetal bleeding disorder, <34 weeks, and acute compromise where the answer is to deliver. **Fetal scalp stimulation producing an acceleration** is a quicker surrogate for a non-acidaemic fetus.

**The short version**
- **Baseline 110–160; variability 6–25; accelerations ≥15 bpm for ≥15 s.**
- **Early = head = mirrors the contraction = benign. Late = placental = lags the peak by >20 s = pathological. Variable = cord = abrupt V.**
- **Sinusoidal = fetal anaemia; fall >5 min = bradycardia = emergency.**
- Resuscitate: stop oxytocin, left lateral, fluids, exclude cord prolapse, tocolyse. ⚑ **Not routine oxygen.**
- **Lactate ≥4.8 or pH ≤7.20 → deliver.**

#### 7.3 Malpresentation and malposition

**Malposition** = cephalic but occiput not anterior. **OP** gives severe continuous backache, early urge to push before full dilatation, long latent phase, slow progress, and a deflexed head presenting a larger diameter (occipitofrontal ~11.5 cm vs suboccipitobregmatic 9.5 cm). Most rotate spontaneously — manage with patience, hydration, good analgesia, a longer passive second stage, and oxytocin for inadequate contractions. Persistent OP or **deep transverse arrest** may need rotational instrumental birth (Kielland forceps or rotational vacuum) by an experienced operator, or caesarean.

**Breech** — 3–4% of singletons at term.
- **Frank** (extended legs — commonest, most favourable); **complete** (hips and knees flexed); **footling** (**unsuitable for vaginal birth**, highest cord prolapse risk).
- **ECV (RANZCOG):** offer from **37+0 weeks**, consider from **36+0 in nulliparas** (less spontaneous version after this). Success ~**40% nulliparous, 60% multiparous**. Serious complication needing emergency caesarean ~**1 in 200**; transient FHR changes ~4%. Tocolysis improves success. **Give anti-D 625 IU to Rh D negative women whether ECV succeeds or fails.**
- ECV contraindications: any existing indication for caesarean, **placenta praevia**, APH within 7 days, abnormal CTG, ruptured membranes, multiple pregnancy (except second twin), rhesus isoimmunisation. Relative: FGR with abnormal Dopplers, pre-eclampsia, oligohydramnios, major anomaly, uterine anomaly. **Previous caesarean is not an absolute contraindication.**
- Mode of birth: the **Term Breech Trial (2000)** showed lower perinatal death (0.3% vs 1.3%) and serious neonatal morbidity with planned caesarean, no difference in serious maternal morbidity. ⚑ Later data soften this — a large Dutch cohort suggested ~175 caesareans to avoid one fetal death, and meta-analysis puts mortality ~1/333 vaginal vs 1/2000 caesarean: statistically real, absolutely small. Planned vaginal breech birth is reasonable **with strict selection and a skilled attendant**: frank or complete breech, EFW ~2.5–3.8 kg, **no fetal head hyperextension**, no fetal compromise, continuous CTG, immediate theatre access.

**Transverse/oblique and unstable lie** — think placenta praevia, polyhydramnios, uterine anomaly, high parity, fetal anomaly. **High cord prolapse risk**; admit from ~37 weeks for unstable lie; never labour a persistent transverse lie.

**Brow** presents the mentovertical diameter (~13 cm) — **undeliverable at term, caesarean.** **Face** is deliverable only if **mento-anterior**; **mento-posterior is undeliverable** (the neck cannot extend further).

**Cord prolapse** — risks: malpresentation, unstable lie, polyhydramnios, prematurity, multiple pregnancy, high presenting part, **ARM with an unengaged head** (an iatrogenic cause worth remembering). Management: **call for help; elevate the presenting part manually or by filling the bladder with 500 mL saline; knee–chest or exaggerated Sims; minimal cord handling** (handling provokes vasospasm — keep it warm, do not replace it); tocolysis if needed; **immediate category 1 caesarean** unless vaginal birth is genuinely imminent.

**The short version**
- OP: backache, slow progress, deflexed head — most rotate; be patient.
- ECV from **37+0** (36+0 if nulliparous); **anti-D 625 IU**; success 40/60%.
- **Footling breech, brow and mento-posterior face = caesarean.**
- Cord prolapse: **elevate presenting part, don't handle the cord, category 1 caesarean.**

#### 7.4 Shoulder dystocia

Birth requiring **additional manoeuvres beyond routine axial traction** after the head delivers — the anterior shoulder impacted behind the symphysis. Clue: the **"turtle sign"** — head delivers then retracts tightly against the perineum, restitution fails.

Risks: macrosomia >4.5 kg, **maternal diabetes** (disproportionate shoulder/abdominal fat), obesity or excessive gestational weight gain, **previous shoulder dystocia** (recurrence 10–15%), post-dates, prolonged second stage, induction/augmentation, **instrumental birth**. ⚑ **Largely unpredictable — around half of cases have none of these risk factors**, and most macrosomic babies deliver uneventfully. Prophylactic induction or caesarean for suspected macrosomia is not routinely justified in a non-diabetic woman.

**HELPERR** (the AU caveat: the order of *internal* manoeuvres matters less than performing them competently and fast):
1. **H — Help.** Obstetrician, senior midwife, anaesthetist, neonatal team. **Note the time the head delivered.**
2. **E — Evaluate for Episiotomy.** Does not relieve a bony impaction, but creates room for the operator's hand.
3. **L — Legs: McRoberts.** Hyperflex and abduct the hips onto the abdomen — flattens the lumbosacral lordosis, rotates the symphysis cephalad. **Resolves ~40–50% alone; always first.**
4. **P — suprapubic Pressure.** From the side of the fetal back, downward and laterally, to adduct the anterior shoulder into the oblique. **McRoberts + suprapubic pressure resolves the large majority.**
5. **E — Enter: internal rotation.** **Rubin II** (pressure on the posterior aspect of the anterior shoulder), **Woods screw** (pressure on the anterior aspect of the posterior shoulder, rotating 180°), reverse Woods screw.
6. **R — Remove the posterior arm.** Sweep it across the chest and deliver — reduces the shoulder-to-shoulder diameter by 2–3 cm. Highly effective; humeral fracture is an acceptable trade.
7. **R — Roll** onto all fours (Gaskin), then reattempt.
8. **Last resort:** cleidotomy, symphysiotomy, **Zavanelli** (replace the head, proceed to caesarean).

**Never:** **fundal pressure** (worsens impaction, risks rupture), excessive downward traction (brachial plexus injury), or twisting/lateral flexion of the neck.

**Complications.** Fetal: **brachial plexus injury — Erb palsy (C5–C6, "waiter's tip")** is classic and >90% resolve (some are pre-existing and unrelated to traction); clavicular/humeral fracture; **HIE** and death if prolonged. Maternal: **PPH (commonest)**, **OASI**, symphyseal separation, psychological sequelae.

**Documentation is examinable:** time of head and body delivery, which shoulder was anterior, sequence and timing of each manoeuvre, who attended and when, cord gases, neonatal condition. Debrief the woman.

**The short version**
- Turtle sign + failed restitution = shoulder dystocia; **half have no risk factors.**
- **McRoberts + suprapubic pressure first.** **Never fundal pressure.**
- Commonest fetal injury **Erb palsy**; commonest maternal complication **PPH**.

#### 7.5 Induction of labour

**Indications (QCG):** prolonged pregnancy from **41+0 weeks**, PPROM/term PROM, hypertensive disease and pre-eclampsia, diabetes, **intrahepatic cholestasis** (timed by bile acids), FGR/abnormal Dopplers, oligohydramnios, reduced fetal movements, twins from **37+0**, suspected macrosomia from 38+0 with ultrasound, **maternal age ≥40 at 39–40 weeks**, chorioamnionitis, fetal death.

**Contraindications:** anything contraindicating vaginal birth — praevia, vasa praevia, transverse lie, cord prolapse, **active primary genital herpes**, prior **classical** uterine incision or previous rupture, invasive cervical cancer, acute fetal compromise (caesarean instead).

**Modified Bishop score:** cervical **dilatation, length/effacement, consistency, position**, plus **fetal station**. **≥7 = favourable** (ARM ± oxytocin); **≤6 = unfavourable** → ripen first.

| Method | When | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Membrane sweep** | Offer from **39+0 weeks** | Reduces need for formal IOL; best in multiparas; safe in GBS carriers |
| **Balloon catheter** | MBS ≤6 | **Mechanical → lower hyperstimulation risk; preferred with a previous caesarean** |
| **Dinoprostone (PGE2)** gel or 10 mg pessary | MBS ≤6 | Effective; **main risk hyperstimulation** — the pessary can be removed, the gel cannot |
| **ARM** | MBS ≥7 or post-ripening | Needs an engaged head — **ARM with a high head risks cord prolapse** |
| **Oxytocin** | After ARM, or ruptured membranes | **Continuous CTG required**; titrate to 3–4 per 10 min |
| **Misoprostol** | ⚑ Not recommended by QCG for routine live-fetus IOL | Higher odds of hyperstimulation with FHR changes (widely used internationally — know the AU position) |

**Post-term:** offer IOL at **41+0 weeks**; by **42+0** birth is clearly indicated. ⚑ Older UK teaching of "between 41 and 42 weeks" has converged on 41 in AU practice.

**Risks:** **tachystole** = >5 contractions in 10 min averaged over 30 min; **hyperstimulation** = tachysystole or hypertonus **with FHR changes** → **cease/reduce oxytocin, remove the prostaglandin, left lateral, IV fluids, terbutaline or GTN.** Also failed induction, **cord prolapse at ARM**, **uterine rupture** (especially prostaglandins after previous caesarean), more instrumental birth, chorioamnionitis with prolonged rupture.

**VBAC:** balloon preferred for ripening; prostaglandins increase rupture risk (background ~0.5% with spontaneous labour after one lower-segment caesarean, higher with induction/augmentation). **The cardinal sign of scar rupture is acute fetal bradycardia**, often with loss of contractions, station regression, maternal pain and haematuria.

**The short version**
- **Bishop ≥7 → ARM ± oxytocin; ≤6 → ripen** (balloon or dinoprostone).
- ⚑ **Balloon preferred after previous caesarean**; misoprostol not routine in AU.
- **Offer IOL at 41+0 weeks.**
- Hyperstimulation → stop oxytocin, remove pessary, left lateral, fluids, **terbutaline**.

#### 7.6 Instrumental birth and caesarean section

##### Instrumental vaginal birth
**Indications:** delay in the second stage despite adequate contractions; suspected fetal compromise in the second stage; maternal exhaustion; or shortening the second stage where maternal effort is contraindicated (severe cardiac disease, hypertensive crisis, cerebral aneurysm, myasthenia).

**Prerequisites (QCG):** **fully dilated cervix; ruptured membranes; vertex presentation with position known (OA/OT/OP); head ≤1/5 palpable abdominally and at or below the ischial spines; adequate pelvis and EFW; caput and moulding assessed; adequate analgesia; empty bladder; consent; skilled operator; facilities for immediate caesarean if it fails.**

**Station classification:** **outlet** (scalp visible without parting the labia, skull at the pelvic floor, rotation ≤45°); **low** (at/below +2 cm, not at the pelvic floor); **mid-cavity** (head ≤1/5 palpable, at/below the spines but above +2 cm — do these and rotational births in theatre as a **trial**); **high** (head ≥2/5 palpable) — **not appropriate**, except a second twin.

| | **Vacuum** | **Forceps** |
|---|---|---|
| Success | **Higher failure rate** | **More likely to achieve birth** |
| Maternal trauma / OASI | **Lower** (~82/1000) | **Higher** (~150/1000) |
| Cephalohaematoma, retinal haemorrhage, **subgaleal** | **More common** (subgaleal 3–7.6/1000) | Less (subgaleal 1.6/1000) |
| Facial nerve palsy, facial marking, skull fracture | Rare | **More common** |
| Contraindicated | **<34 weeks** (subgaleal/IVH), face presentation, suspected fetal bleeding disorder | Face only if mento-anterior with specific instrument |

Hook: **vacuum spares the mother and risks the baby's scalp; forceps spare the scalp and risk the mother's sphincter.**

**Abandon and convert to caesarean:** vacuum — after **two detachments** (one if inexperienced), no descent on the first three pulls, or **20 minutes**; forceps — blades will not apply without undue force, or no descent after three pulls. **Sequential instrument use increases maternal and neonatal morbidity** — go to caesarean rather than try a second instrument.

**Two things after instrumental birth:** **mediolateral episiotomy** (strongly consider, especially at a first vaginal birth) cut at **≥60° from the fourchette at crowning**; and ⚑ **amoxicillin–clavulanate 1 g + 200 mg IV, single dose within 6 hours** (the ANODE trial) — newer than most textbooks and now in QCG.

##### Caesarean section

| Category | Definition | Decision-to-delivery target |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Immediate threat to life of woman or fetus** (cord prolapse, prolonged bradycardia, abruption with compromise, scar rupture) | **30 minutes** |
| **2** | Compromise, **not immediately life-threatening** | **60 minutes** |
| **3** | Needs early birth, **no current compromise** | No fixed target |
| **4** | Elective — timing to suit woman and team | ≥39+0 weeks |

⚑ The 30 minutes is a **service audit standard, not a threshold at which harm begins** — deliver as fast as *safely* possible; sacrificing safe anaesthesia for speed is never the right answer.

**Elective caesarean at ≥39+0 weeks** without other indication — earlier birth increases neonatal respiratory morbidity (TTN, RDS). If unavoidable earlier, consider a steroid course 48 h beforehand.

**Peri-operative package:** ⚑ **prophylactic cefazolin before skin incision** (beats post-cord-clamp for maternal infection with no neonatal downside — a change from older teaching); **antacid/aspiration prophylaxis** (famotidine ± metoclopramide, sodium citrate before GA); **regional anaesthesia preferred**; **VTE prophylaxis** (mechanical ± LMWH by risk); **prophylactic oxytocin** (below). Complications: haemorrhage, endometritis and wound infection, VTE, bladder/ureteric/bowel injury; and for future pregnancies **praevia, accreta spectrum, scar rupture, adhesions** — which is why the number of previous caesareans matters.

**The short version**
- **Fully dilated, membranes ruptured, head ≤1/5 palpable and at/below spines, position known, bladder empty, analgesia, consent, theatre available.**
- **Vacuum = more failures and more neonatal scalp injury; forceps = more success and more OASI.**
- **Category 1 = 30 min; category 2 = 60 min; elective ≥39 weeks.**
- ⚑ **Co-amoxiclav single dose after instrumental birth; cefazolin before skin incision at caesarean.**

#### 7.7 Third stage, perineal trauma and analgesia

##### Active management of the third stage
Reduces PPH by 50–60%; recommended for all women with informed choice preserved. Three components: **prophylactic uterotonic, controlled cord traction with counter-traction guarding the uterus, and deferred cord clamping (≥1 min or until pulsation ceases)**. ⚑ **Early cord clamping is no longer part of active management** — deferring improves neonatal haemoglobin and iron stores without increasing PPH; older definitions bundled it in.

| Setting | Prophylactic uterotonic (QCG) |
|---|---|
| Vaginal birth, IV access | **Oxytocin 10 IU IV slowly over 3–5 min** (preferred) |
| Vaginal birth, no IV access | **Oxytocin 10 IU IM** |
| **Caesarean under regional** | **Oxytocin 3–5 IU IV over 1–2 min** with haemodynamic monitoring |

⚑ Note the **lower caesarean dose** — a rapid 10 IU IV bolus causes vasodilatation, hypotension and tachycardia, unsafe with a spinal sympathectomy or cardiac disease. **Carbetocin** is an alternative (heat-stable, long-acting); **misoprostol** only where injectables are unavailable. ⚑ AU first-line prophylaxis is **oxytocin alone**; **Syntometrine** (oxytocin + ergometrine) is reserved for higher-risk women, **contraindicated in hypertension/pre-eclampsia and cardiac disease**, and causes far more nausea and vomiting.

Physiological third stage is a legitimate choice for a low-risk woman — convert to active management for excessive bleeding or if undelivered at one hour.

**Retained placenta** (>30 min active / >60 min physiological): IV access, empty bladder, oxytocin, then **manual removal in theatre under regional anaesthesia** with antibiotic cover. **Do not pull hard on a cord with a fundal placenta** — risk of **uterine inversion** (shock out of proportion to loss, fundal dimple or mass at the introitus; replace immediately, Johnson manoeuvre, and give the uterotonic **only after** replacement).

**PPH (AU definitions):** primary PPH **≥500 mL in 24 h**; **≥1000 mL severe**; **≥2500 mL major haemorrhage**. **Tranexamic acid 1 g IV over 10 min as soon as possible, ideally within 3 hours** (WOMAN trial), repeatable once after 30 min.

##### Perineal trauma

| Degree | Definition |
|---|---|
| **First** | Perineal skin and/or vaginal mucosa only |
| **Second** | **Perineal muscles**, sphincter intact (includes episiotomy) |
| **3a** | **<50% of external anal sphincter (EAS)** torn |
| **3b** | **>50% of EAS** torn |
| **3c** | **EAS and internal anal sphincter (IAS)** torn |
| **Fourth** | EAS + IAS + **anorectal mucosa** |

Third and fourth degree together = **OASI**.

**Risks:** first vaginal birth, **South Asian ethnicity**, birth weight >3.5–4 kg, **OP position**, shoulder dystocia, prolonged second stage, **instrumental birth (forceps > vacuum)**, **midline episiotomy**, epidural.

**Prevention:** **warm perineal compresses**, slowing the head at crowning with manual perineal protection, and **mediolateral (not midline) episiotomy at ≥60°** when indicated — midline extends straight into the sphincter, which is why it is not used in Australia.

**Repair:** every woman needs a **systematic perineal and digital rectal examination after birth** — missed OASI is the commonest reason for later incontinence. OASI repair by an **obstetrician, GP obstetrician or colorectal surgeon**, **in theatre** with adequate lighting, anaesthesia and instruments; **repeat the rectal examination after repair** to confirm no suture has penetrated the anorectal mucosa. **Broad-spectrum antibiotics and a stool softener (lactulose, not a bulking agent)**, pelvic floor physiotherapy, structured follow-up, and counselling about mode of birth next pregnancy.

##### Analgesia in labour
Non-pharmacological: **continuous one-to-one support has the best evidence base of anything on this list**; also water immersion, TENS, massage, position and mobility.

**Nitrous oxide (Entonox) 50:50** — self-administered, onset in seconds, offset within a minute, no effect on labour progress or the neonate; nausea and light-headedness common.

**Parenteral opioids** — morphine/fentanyl IM or IV, remifentanil PCA in some units. Modest analgesia; **maternal sedation and nausea, reduced CTG variability (a benign cause — know this), delayed gastric emptying, neonatal respiratory depression if given close to birth** (naloxone available).

**Epidural** (low-dose bupivacaine/ropivacaine + fentanyl, catheter at L3/4, infusion or PCEA) — the most effective analgesia in labour.
- **Hypotension** from sympathetic blockade — preload/co-load, vasopressors, left lateral tilt. **Maternal hypotension causes fetal bradycardia** — a common, reversible cause of an abnormal CTG shortly after siting.
- **Maternal fever** — confuses the picture with chorioamnionitis, increasing antibiotics and neonatal septic screens.
- **Urinary retention** — needs catheterisation and bladder care.
- **Longer second stage** and **increased instrumental birth**.
- ⚑ **Epidural does NOT increase the caesarean rate** (Cochrane 2018) — older textbooks and much folk teaching say it does. A very common MCQ discriminator.
- **Post-dural puncture headache** (~1%, from inadvertent dural puncture) — postural, worse sitting up, relieved lying flat; conservative measures then **epidural blood patch**.
- Rare: high/total spinal, LA toxicity, epidural haematoma or abscess, permanent neurological injury (~1 in 100,000).
- **Contraindications:** refusal, coagulopathy or therapeutic anticoagulation, **thrombocytopenia** (generally <70–80 × 10⁹/L), local or systemic sepsis, raised ICP, uncorrected hypovolaemia.
- **Positively indicated** in severe pre-eclampsia (blunts the hypertensive response, if platelets and coagulation permit), cardiac disease, and where operative birth is likely (top-up rather than GA).

**The short version**
- Active third stage = **uterotonic + controlled cord traction + deferred cord clamping ≥1 min**. ⚑ Early clamping is out.
- **Oxytocin 10 IU at vaginal birth; only 3–5 IU IV at caesarean.**
- **3a <50% EAS, 3b >50% EAS, 3c EAS+IAS, 4th adds anorectal mucosa** — always do a rectal exam after birth.
- ⚑ **Epidural increases instrumental birth and maternal fever, but NOT caesarean rate.**

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators — Section 7

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Normal progress in established first stage | **≥0.5 cm/hour (2 cm in 4 hours)** |
| Second stage limits, nullip vs multip | Birth within **3 h / 2 h**; suspect delay after **1 h / 30 min** of pushing |
| Multipara with poor progress — the trap | **Suspect obstruction**; be cautious with oxytocin (rupture risk) |
| Palpable ridge between upper and lower segments | **Bandl's ring** — imminent uterine rupture |
| Normal baseline / variability / acceleration | **110–160 bpm / 6–25 bpm / ≥15 bpm for ≥15 s** |
| Deceleration whose nadir coincides with the contraction peak | **Early** — head compression — benign |
| Repetitive deceleration with nadir **>20 s after** the peak | **Late** — uteroplacental insufficiency — pathological |
| Abrupt V-shaped decelerations of varying shape | **Variable** — cord compression |
| Smooth undulating 2–5 cycles/min, absent variability | **Sinusoidal — severe fetal anaemia** (FMH, ruptured vasa praevia, rhesus) |
| FHR fall lasting more than 5 minutes | **Bradycardia — emergency**, prepare for immediate birth |
| Reduced variability shortly after IM morphine | **Opioid effect (or fetal sleep)** — benign if <40 min, otherwise normal |
| Intervention no longer recommended for an abnormal CTG | ⚑ **Routine maternal facemask oxygen** |
| Scalp lactate / pH mandating birth | **Lactate ≥4.8 mmol/L or pH ≤7.20** |
| Head delivers then retracts against the perineum | **Shoulder dystocia** (turtle sign) |
| First manoeuvre in shoulder dystocia | **McRoberts**, then suprapubic pressure |
| Forbidden manoeuvre in shoulder dystocia | **Fundal pressure** |
| Commonest fetal / maternal complication of shoulder dystocia | **Erb palsy (C5–6) / PPH** |
| Bishop score cut-off for a favourable cervix | **≥7** |
| Ripening agent of choice with a previous caesarean | **Balloon catheter** (avoid prostaglandins) |
| Gestation to offer IOL for prolonged pregnancy (AU) | **41+0 weeks** |
| Tachysystole with FHR changes on oxytocin — first actions | **Stop oxytocin, remove pessary, left lateral, fluids, terbutaline 250 µg SC** |
| Acute bradycardia + loss of contractions + station regression in VBAC | **Uterine rupture** |
| Instrument with more OASI vs more neonatal scalp injury | **Forceps / vacuum** |
| Maximum vacuum attempt before abandoning | **3 pulls, 2 detachments, or 20 minutes** |
| ⚑ Drug given routinely after instrumental birth | **Amoxicillin–clavulanate 1 g + 200 mg IV, single dose** |
| Decision-to-delivery targets, category 1 and 2 | **30 minutes / 60 minutes** |
| Earliest gestation for elective caesarean without other indication | **39+0 weeks** |
| Uterotonic dose, vaginal birth vs caesarean | **10 IU IM/slow IV vs 3–5 IU IV** |
| Uterotonic contraindicated in pre-eclampsia | **Ergometrine / Syntometrine** |
| Tear involving >50% of the external anal sphincter | **3b** |
| Episiotomy type and angle used in Australia | **Mediolateral, ≥60° from the fourchette at crowning** |
| ⚑ Outcome epidural does NOT increase | **Caesarean rate** (it does increase instrumental birth and maternal fever) |
| Postural headache after epidural, worse sitting up | **Post-dural puncture headache** → conservative, then **blood patch** |
| PPH definition and TXA dose/window | **≥500 mL in 24 h; TXA 1 g IV over 10 min within 3 hours** |
