## Contraception and Fertility

### Finals Tutor — Contraception and Fertility

#### Methods and effectiveness

Effectiveness is best framed as **typical use** versus perfect use — the gap is what actually matters clinically.

| Method | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Levonorgestrel IUD** | LARC. Also treats heavy menstrual bleeding; endometrial protection |
| **Copper IUD** | LARC, non-hormonal, lasts 5–10 years. Can make periods heavier. Also the **most effective emergency contraception** |
| **Etonogestrel implant** | LARC, 3 years. Irregular bleeding is the commonest reason for removal |
| **DMPA injection** | 12-weekly. Delayed return to fertility; reduces bone mineral density with prolonged use |
| **COC** | Cycle control, reduces ovarian and endometrial cancer risk; VTE risk |
| **POP** | Suitable when oestrogen contraindicated, including breastfeeding |
| Barrier | Only method protecting against STIs |

**LARCs are the most effective** because they remove user error — the typical-use failure rate approaches the perfect-use rate. That reasoning is the point of the question when asked "which is most effective".

#### Combined oral contraceptive — contraindications

Absolute or near-absolute (UKMEC 4): **migraine with aura**, history of VTE or known thrombophilia, current breast cancer, active liver disease, uncontrolled hypertension, **smoking ≥15/day at age ≥35**, <6 weeks postpartum and breastfeeding, major surgery with immobilisation, complicated diabetes, SLE with antiphospholipid antibodies.

**Migraine with aura is the one most often tested** — the stroke risk makes combined oestrogen unsuitable; a progestogen-only method is appropriate instead.

#### Emergency contraception

| Option | Window | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Copper IUD** | Up to 5 days | **Most effective**; also provides ongoing contraception |
| Ulipristal acetate | Up to 120 hours | More effective than levonorgestrel; delays ovulation |
| Levonorgestrel | Up to 72 hours (best early) | Less effective as BMI rises |

The exam answer to "most effective emergency contraception" is the **copper IUD** — oral options are the common but incorrect reflex.

#### Infertility

Failure to conceive after 12 months of regular unprotected intercourse (or 6 months if the woman is over 35). Investigate **both partners together** from the start.

**Causes** — roughly: male factor ~30%, ovulatory ~25%, tubal ~20%, unexplained ~25%, with overlap.

**Investigations**
- **Semen analysis** — the single most important male investigation, and often forgotten
- **Mid-luteal progesterone** (day 21 of a 28-day cycle) to confirm ovulation
- FSH/LH, TSH, prolactin, AMH for ovarian reserve
- Rubella immunity, chlamydia screening
- **Tubal patency** — hysterosalpingogram, HyCoSy, or laparoscopy with dye if pathology suspected
- Pelvic ultrasound

**Management**: optimise modifiable factors (weight, smoking, alcohol, folate), **letrozole or clomiphene** for ovulation induction in PCOS, surgery for tubal disease or endometriosis, IUI, and **IVF/ICSI** for severe male factor, tubal disease, or failed simpler treatment.

#### Termination of pregnancy

Legal in NSW up to 22 weeks on request from a medical practitioner; beyond that requires two practitioners. Options are **medical** (mifepristone + misoprostol, up to 9 weeks in the community setting) or **surgical** (suction curettage). Provide contraception counselling at the same visit — this is the moment of highest engagement. Give anti-D if Rh negative.
