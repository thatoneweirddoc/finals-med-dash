## Mood Disorders

### Finals Tutor — Mood Disorders

#### Major depression

Core features are persistently low mood and/or **anhedonia** (loss of interest or pleasure) present most of the day, nearly every day, for at least two weeks, with associated features: disturbed sleep, appetite or weight change, fatigue, poor concentration, psychomotor change, worthlessness or inappropriate guilt, and thoughts of death or suicide. Diagnosis requires functional impairment.

**Biological ("somatic") features** — early morning waking, diurnal mood variation worse in the morning, marked appetite and weight loss, psychomotor retardation — suggest a more severe, melancholic pattern that responds well to biological treatment.

**Always screen for**: past hypomania/mania (changes the treatment entirely), psychotic features, substance use, and **organic causes** — hypothyroidism, anaemia, B12 deficiency, Cushing's, Parkinson's, medications (corticosteroids, interferon, isotretinoin).

**Management**
- Mild: psychological therapy first — CBT, behavioural activation, IPT
- Moderate–severe: antidepressant **plus** psychological therapy
- **SSRIs are first-line** in Australia (sertraline, escitalopram). Allow **2–4 weeks** for onset, continue at least **6–12 months after remission** to prevent relapse
- Consider **ECT** for severe depression with psychosis, catatonia, food refusal, or high suicide risk — it is the most rapidly effective treatment, not a last resort
- Risk assessment at every review

**SSRI adverse effects**: GI upset, headache, sexual dysfunction, **hyponatraemia (SIADH — particularly in the elderly)**, increased bleeding risk with NSAIDs, and an early increase in agitation/suicidal thoughts in young people — review within 1–2 weeks.

**Serotonin syndrome** — from serotonergic combinations (SSRI + tramadol, MAOI, triptan, St John's wort). Triad: **neuromuscular excitation (clonus, hyperreflexia, rigidity), autonomic instability, altered mental state**. Clonus is the discriminating sign. Management is cessation, supportive care, benzodiazepines, cyproheptadine in severe cases.

**Discontinuation syndrome** — flu-like symptoms, dizziness, "electric shock" sensations, insomnia. Commonest with short half-life agents (paroxetine, venlafaxine); taper rather than stop abruptly.

#### Bipolar affective disorder

**Mania** — abnormally elevated, expansive or irritable mood for at least a week (or any duration if hospitalised), with increased activity or energy, plus features such as grandiosity, reduced need for sleep, pressured speech, flight of ideas, distractibility, and risk-taking behaviour with painful consequences (spending, sexual, driving). **Psychotic features may be present.**

**Hypomania** — the same quality but less severe, at least 4 days, **no psychosis and no marked functional impairment or hospitalisation**. That threshold is the discriminator.

**Bipolar I** requires a manic episode. **Bipolar II** requires hypomania plus major depression, never mania.

**Management**
- Acute mania: **antipsychotic** (olanzapine, risperidone, quetiapine) ± benzodiazepine for sedation; **stop antidepressants**
- Maintenance: **lithium** (best evidence for relapse prevention and for reducing suicide), sodium valproate, or an atypical antipsychotic
- **Never give an antidepressant alone in bipolar depression** — risk of switching to mania. Use a mood stabiliser, and quetiapine or lamotrigine for bipolar depression

**Lithium — the exam favourite**
- **Narrow therapeutic index**: target 0.6–0.8 mmol/L (up to 1.0 in acute mania); levels taken **12 hours post-dose**
- Monitoring: levels, **U&E and eGFR, TFTs, calcium** — baseline and periodically
- Adverse effects: fine tremor, polyuria/polydipsia (**nephrogenic diabetes insipidus**), hypothyroidism, hyperparathyroidism, weight gain, chronic kidney disease
- **Toxicity**: coarse tremor, ataxia, dysarthria, vomiting, confusion, seizures, arrhythmia. Precipitated by **dehydration, NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, thiazides**, and renal impairment. Management: stop lithium, IV fluids, haemodialysis if severe
- **Teratogenic** — Ebstein's anomaly; valproate is worse (neural tube defects, neurodevelopmental harm) and should be avoided in people who can become pregnant

#### Perinatal mood disorders

- **Baby blues** — days 3–10, transient tearfulness and lability, self-limiting, reassurance only
- **Postnatal depression** — up to a year postpartum; screen with EPDS; treat with psychological therapy ± SSRI (sertraline is well established in breastfeeding)
- **Postpartum psychosis** — a **psychiatric emergency**, onset typically within 2 weeks, with confusion, mood lability, delusions often concerning the infant. High risk of suicide and infanticide. Requires urgent admission, ideally to a mother-and-baby unit
