## Personality, Eating and Neurocognitive Disorders

### Finals Tutor — Personality, Eating and Neurocognitive Disorders

*Descriptions in plain language rather than reproduced diagnostic-manual criteria.*

#### Personality disorders

An enduring, pervasive and inflexible pattern of inner experience and behaviour that departs markedly from cultural expectation, is evident by adolescence or early adulthood, is stable over time, and causes distress or impairment. Traits become a **disorder** only when inflexible, maladaptive and impairing.

Grouped into three clusters:

| Cluster | Character | Disorders |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Odd or eccentric | Paranoid, schizoid, schizotypal |
| **B** | Dramatic, emotional, erratic | Antisocial, borderline, histrionic, narcissistic |
| **C** | Anxious or fearful | Avoidant, dependent, obsessive-compulsive |

**Borderline personality disorder** is the one most often examined: pervasive instability of relationships, self-image and affect with marked impulsivity — frantic efforts to avoid abandonment, alternating idealisation and devaluation, identity disturbance, self-damaging impulsivity, recurrent self-harm or suicidal behaviour, affective lability lasting hours, chronic emptiness, intense anger, and transient stress-related paranoid or dissociative symptoms.

Management is **psychological therapy** — dialectical behaviour therapy has the best evidence, also mentalisation-based therapy. Medication treats comorbidity, not the disorder itself; **avoid long-term benzodiazepines and polypharmacy**. Structured, consistent boundaries and a clear crisis plan matter more than any drug.

Useful distinctions: borderline vs bipolar — borderline mood shifts last **hours** and are reactive to interpersonal events; bipolar episodes last **days to weeks** and are more autonomous. Antisocial vs borderline manipulation — antisocial for material gain or power, borderline to secure care and avoid abandonment.

#### Eating disorders

**Anorexia nervosa** — restriction of intake leading to significantly low body weight, intense fear of weight gain or persistent behaviour preventing it, and disturbance in how body weight or shape is experienced.

Medical complications: bradycardia, hypotension, **prolonged QT and arrhythmia**, osteoporosis, amenorrhoea, lanugo hair, hypothermia, marrow suppression, deranged electrolytes, raised cholesterol, transaminitis.

**Refeeding syndrome** is the critical management point: reintroducing carbohydrate drives an insulin surge, shifting phosphate, potassium and magnesium intracellularly. **Hypophosphataemia** is the hallmark, causing cardiac failure, arrhythmia, rhabdomyolysis, seizures, and death. Prevent by refeeding **slowly** with close electrolyte monitoring and **thiamine supplementation** before and during.

**Bulimia nervosa** — recurrent binge eating with compensatory behaviours (vomiting, laxatives, fasting, excessive exercise), typically at normal or above-normal weight. Signs: **Russell's sign** (knuckle calluses), dental erosion, parotid swelling, **hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis** from vomiting.

Treatment: specialist-led psychological therapy (CBT-E, family-based therapy in adolescents); fluoxetine has evidence in bulimia. Refer early to the National Eating Disorders Collaboration pathways; medical instability requires admission.

#### Delirium

An **acute, fluctuating** disturbance of attention and awareness with cognitive change, caused by an underlying medical condition — the defining feature is **inattention**.

Hypoactive delirium is more common than hyperactive and is frequently missed because the patient is quiet rather than agitated. Assess with **4AT** or **CAM/CAM-ICU**.

**Causes — think systematically**: infection, drugs (anticholinergics, opioids, benzodiazepines, steroids) and **withdrawal**, metabolic (sodium, glucose, calcium, uraemia, hepatic), hypoxia, pain, constipation, urinary retention, stroke or intracranial event, post-operative.

**Management is treating the cause plus non-pharmacological measures**: reorientation, familiar people, daylight and sleep hygiene, hearing aids and glasses, mobilisation, hydration, avoid restraints and unnecessary lines. **Antipsychotics only for severe distress or risk**, lowest dose, shortest duration — and **benzodiazepines are avoided except in alcohol/benzodiazepine withdrawal**, where they are the treatment.

#### Dementia

Progressive, acquired decline across cognitive domains with functional impairment, in **clear consciousness** — that last point is the discriminator from delirium.

| Type | Distinguishing features |
|---|---|
| **Alzheimer's** | Insidious, early episodic memory loss, temporoparietal atrophy |
| **Vascular** | Stepwise decline, vascular risk factors, focal signs |
| **Lewy body** | **Fluctuating** cognition, **visual hallucinations**, **spontaneous parkinsonism**, REM sleep behaviour disorder; **severe antipsychotic sensitivity** |
| **Frontotemporal** | Younger onset, personality and behavioural change or language decline, memory relatively preserved early |

**Delirium and dementia coexist frequently** — delirium is superimposed on dementia in a large proportion of hospitalised older people, and dementia is the single biggest risk factor for delirium.

Screen for reversible contributors: B12, folate, TFTs, calcium, glucose, syphilis/HIV where indicated, depression ("pseudodementia"), medication review, and neuroimaging.

Management: cholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine) in Alzheimer's and Lewy body; memantine in moderate–severe. Non-pharmacological management of behavioural and psychological symptoms comes first; **avoid antipsychotics in Lewy body dementia** — they can cause severe, sometimes fatal, sensitivity reactions.
