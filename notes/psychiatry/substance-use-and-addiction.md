## Substance Use and Addiction

### Finals Tutor — Substance Use and Addiction

#### Alcohol

**Australian guidelines**: healthy adults should drink no more than **10 standard drinks per week and no more than 4 on any one day**. One standard drink = 10 g alcohol.

Screening: **AUDIT-C** or full AUDIT; CAGE is older and less sensitive.

**Alcohol withdrawal — the timeline is the exam point:**

| Time from last drink | Features |
|---|---|
| **6–12 hours** | Tremor, anxiety, sweating, nausea, tachycardia, hypertension |
| **12–24 hours** | **Alcoholic hallucinosis** — usually visual, *with intact orientation* |
| **24–48 hours** | **Withdrawal seizures** — generalised tonic-clonic |
| **48–72+ hours** | **Delirium tremens** — confusion, disorientation, marked autonomic instability, vivid hallucinations. Mortality if untreated |

Delirium tremens is distinguished from alcoholic hallucinosis by **clouded consciousness and disorientation** — hallucinosis occurs in a clear sensorium.

**Management of withdrawal**
- **Benzodiazepine (diazepam) — symptom-triggered regimen guided by a withdrawal scale (CIWA-Ar)**. Use shorter-acting lorazepam or oxazepam in significant liver disease
- **Thiamine before any glucose** — giving glucose first can precipitate **Wernicke's encephalopathy**
- Fluids, electrolytes (correct magnesium and potassium), monitor
- Treat the precipitant and screen for injuries, infection, GI bleed

**Wernicke's encephalopathy** — the triad is **confusion, ophthalmoplegia, ataxia**, though all three are present in a minority. Treat with high-dose parenteral thiamine. Untreated it progresses to **Korsakoff syndrome** — irreversible anterograde amnesia with confabulation.

**Relapse prevention**: psychosocial support plus **naltrexone** (reduces craving/reward; avoid with opioids), **acamprosate** (reduces craving; safe in liver disease), or **disulfiram** (aversive; needs supervision and motivation).

#### Opioids

**Overdose** — the triad is **pinpoint pupils, respiratory depression, reduced consciousness**. Treat with **naloxone**, titrated to respiratory rate not to full consciousness (precipitating full withdrawal in a dependent person is distressing and counterproductive). Naloxone has a shorter half-life than most opioids — **observe for re-sedation**, and consider an infusion for long-acting opioids like methadone.

**Withdrawal** — very unpleasant but **not life-threatening** (in contrast to alcohol and benzodiazepines): dilated pupils, lacrimation, rhinorrhoea, yawning, piloerection, myalgia, abdominal cramps, diarrhoea, agitation.

**Opioid substitution therapy** — methadone (full agonist, long half-life, QT prolongation, risk in induction) or **buprenorphine/naloxone** (partial agonist, ceiling effect on respiratory depression, safer in overdose; must wait for withdrawal to begin before induction or it precipitates withdrawal).

**Red flags for drug-seeking/aberrant behaviour**: requesting a specific drug by name, claiming allergy to alternatives, lost or stolen scripts, early refill requests, multiple prescribers ("doctor shopping"), travelling from out of area, escalating doses, injecting oral formulations.

In NSW, prescribing Schedule 8 drugs to a **drug-dependent person requires authority** under the *Poisons and Therapeutic Goods Act*; check **SafeScript NSW** (real-time prescription monitoring) before prescribing. The safe approach with an unknown patient requesting opioids is to provide a limited supply if clinically justified, contact the usual prescriber, verify via the monitoring system, and arrange proper follow-up — not to refuse care outright, and not to simply issue the requested script.

#### Benzodiazepines

Withdrawal **can be life-threatening** (seizures, delirium) — like alcohol, unlike opioids. Taper slowly, converting to a long-acting agent such as diazepam.

#### Methamphetamine

Acute intoxication: agitation, psychosis, hyperthermia, hypertension, tachycardia, dilated pupils, risk of intracranial haemorrhage, seizures, rhabdomyolysis. Management: de-escalation, **benzodiazepines first-line for agitation**, antipsychotic if psychotic, cooling, fluids, monitor CK.

#### Stages of change and motivational interviewing

Precontemplation → contemplation → preparation → action → maintenance (± relapse). Motivational interviewing works with ambivalence rather than confronting it: express empathy, develop discrepancy between behaviour and goals, roll with resistance, support self-efficacy.
