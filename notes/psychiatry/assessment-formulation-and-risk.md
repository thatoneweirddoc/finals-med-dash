## Assessment, Formulation and Risk


### Psychiatry Master Notes — Assessment, Formulation and Risk

*Sources: RANZCP CPGs (deliberate self-harm; schizophrenia GRADE 2026; initial physical health assessment for psychosis ANZ 2026), Therapeutic Guidelines: Psychotropic, AMH, RACGP, AIHW, ACSQHC NSQHS Standards 2nd ed, National SEWB Framework 2017–2023. Scope: taking, examining, formulating and risk-assessing a psychiatric presentation, and excluding organic mimics before committing to a psychiatric diagnosis.*

#### 1.1 The psychiatric history

**The short version**
- Same skeleton as a medical history plus five domains medicine omits: **risk, forensic, developmental/personal, premorbid personality, substance use**.
- Presenting complaint goes in **the patient's own words, in quotes** — the idiom carries diagnostic information ("my thoughts are being taken out") that paraphrase destroys.
- **Collateral is not optional in psychosis, mania or eating disorders.**
- Always ask what has changed *from this person's baseline* — psychiatry grades deviation from premorbid function.

**Structure.** HOPC (onset, course, precipitants, prodrome, functional impact); past psychiatric history (admissions **voluntary vs involuntary**, treatment response, prior attempts, treatment orders); medications, adherence, agents ceased and why; medical and family history (psychiatric *and* completed suicide); substance use; forensic; personal/developmental; premorbid personality; social circumstances; risk.

**Substance use** — alcohol, tobacco, cannabis, methamphetamine, opioids, benzodiazepines: quantity, route, frequency, duration, **last use**, withdrawal history (seizures, DTs), dependence. Route matters (injecting → BBV screening); last use matters because separating substance-induced from primary psychosis rests on temporal relationship and persistence beyond the expected intoxication/withdrawal window.

**Forensic history** — charges, convictions, custodial time, bail/parole/AVOs, violence irrespective of charge. Not voyeurism: it changes risk formulation, disposition and legal framework (forensic vs civil), and pending charges are a live stressor.

**Developmental and personal history** — birth complications, milestones, schooling (attainment, bullying, disruption), abuse and neglect, relationships, sexual history, occupation, supports, finances. The predisposing factors for the formulation (§1.3) live here.

**Premorbid personality** — how the person was when well, ideally from an informant. Distinguishes a personality disorder (lifelong, pervasive) from the state change of an episodic illness. Do not diagnose personality from one presentation in crisis.

⚑ **Why collateral is mandatory in psychosis, mania and eating disorders:** all three feature *impaired insight into the abnormality itself*. The psychotic patient does not experience the delusion as a symptom; the manic patient experiences elevated mood, disinhibition and reduced sleep need as wellness; the eating-disordered patient minimises restriction, purging and exercise as ego-syntonic. Self-report systematically *under*states severity, so an assessment without collateral is not shorter — it is unreliable. It also establishes rate of change and premorbid baseline. Sources: family, GP, case manager, ambulance/police, old records. Consent is preferred, but where the patient lacks capacity or refuses and there is risk, jurisdictions permit *receiving* third-party information.

#### 1.2 The Mental State Examination

**The short version**
- MSE = cross-sectional observation *now*; the history is the longitudinal narrative. Do not import history into the MSE.
- Order: appearance/behaviour → speech → mood & affect → thought form → thought content → perception → cognition → insight → judgement.
- **Mood is subjective and sustained; affect is observed and momentary** — mood is climate, affect is weather.
- **Form** = how thought is organised; **content** = what it is about.

**Appearance and behaviour** — self-care, grooming, weight, stigmata (track marks, self-harm scars, jaundice, tremor), psychomotor retardation vs agitation, abnormal movements (tardive dyskinesia; akathisia — inner restlessness, distinguish from agitation), eye contact, rapport. Catatonic signs: stupor, mutism, negativism, posturing, waxy flexibility. **Speech** — rate, volume, quantity, tone, latency: pressured (rapid, hard to interrupt) vs poverty of speech (alogia); dysarthria/dysphasia signal neurology.

**Mood vs affect** — *Mood*: the patient's own sustained, pervasive emotional state, quoted, with a severity rating. *Affect*: observed moment-to-moment expression on four axes — **quality**, **range** (full, restricted, blunted, flat), **reactivity**, **congruity** with stated mood and content. Incongruent affect (laughing while describing a bereavement) is a schizophrenia-spectrum flag.

**Thought form** — organisation of thought, inferred from speech:

| Term | Definition and association |
|---|---|
| Circumstantiality | Over-inclusive, digressive, but **eventually reaches the point** — obsessionality, epilepsy, normal |
| Tangentiality | Veers off and **never returns** — psychosis, mania, organic |
| Flight of ideas | Rapid ideas with **discernible links** (puns, clang, distraction) — mania |
| Loosening of associations (derailment, "knight's move") | Links between ideas **absent or opaque to the listener** — schizophrenia |
| Word salad | **No coherent syntax or meaning** — extreme derailment |

Also thought blocking (abrupt cessation mid-stream), neologisms, perseveration (organic), echolalia.

**Thought content** — preoccupations, ruminations, obsessions (intrusive, ego-dystonic, resisted, **recognised as own** — contrast thought insertion), compulsions, overvalued ideas, delusions, suicidal/homicidal ideation.

- **Delusion**: a fixed, false belief held with total conviction, out of keeping with the person's cultural and educational background, unshakeable by argument or contrary evidence.
- **Overvalued idea**: a comprehensible, non-bizarre belief pursued beyond reasonable proportion and dominating the person's life, but **held with less than delusional conviction and conceded as possibly wrong**. The distinction is conviction, not content — anorexia, dysmorphophobia, morbid jealousy.

**Named delusional themes**

| Theme | Content |
|---|---|
| Persecutory | Being harmed, followed, poisoned, conspired against (commonest) |
| Grandiose | Inflated worth, power, identity, special mission — mania, neurosyphilis |
| Nihilistic | Self, a body part or the world does not exist / is destroyed / rotting |
| Reference | Neutral events, media or remarks carry personal significance for the patient |
| Capgras | Familiar person replaced by an identical impostor (cf. Fregoli — strangers in disguise) |
| Cotard | Being dead, having no organs, damned — psychotic depression, elderly |
| de Clérambault (erotomania) | A higher-status person is secretly in love with the patient |

**Passivity and thought alienation** — the self/world boundary is breached. **Thought insertion**: thoughts placed in the mind by an external agency, experienced as *not the patient's own*. **Thought withdrawal**: thoughts removed. **Thought broadcasting**: thoughts escape and become accessible to others unspoken. **Passivity (made phenomena)**: actions, impulses, emotions or bodily sensations controlled from outside ("made volition", "somatic passivity"). ⚑ These Schneiderian first-rank symptoms are **no longer diagnostically specific for schizophrenia** — DSM-5 dropped their special weighting; they occur in mania, psychotic depression and organic psychoses.

**Perception** — **illusion**: misperception of a *real* external stimulus (curtain shadow seen as a figure); normal and common in delirium. **Hallucination**: percept *without* external stimulus, in external space, with the full force of a true percept, not under voluntary control. **Pseudohallucination**: in *inner subjective space* and/or recognised as unreal, insight retained (contested term — describe the phenomenon). Specify modality. Auditory: elementary vs second-person ("you are worthless") vs third-person running commentary or voices conversing. **Visual, olfactory, gustatory and tactile hallucinations point to an organic or substance cause** until proven otherwise (visual → delirium, Lewy body dementia, withdrawal; olfactory/gustatory → temporal lobe epilepsy aura; tactile formication → stimulants, alcohol withdrawal). Also record depersonalisation/derealisation; **hypnagogic** and **hypnopompic** hallucinations are normal.

**Cognition** — orientation, attention, registration and recall, language, praxis, executive function. Screen with MMSE, MoCA (better for executive deficits) or RUDAS (built for CALD populations). Acute fluctuating inattention = delirium (§1.5).

**Insight** — not binary; three axes: does the patient recognise the experiences as abnormal, attribute them to mental illness, and accept treatment? Treatment can be accepted while illness is denied, and vice versa. **Judgement** — reasoning through consequences and acting on it, tested on the **actual current situation** ("what will you do tonight if the voices tell you to leave the ward?"), not hypotheticals about stamped letters; it is not legal capacity, which is decision-specific and statutory.

#### 1.3 Case formulation

**The short version**
- **Diagnosis** classifies the disorder (what); **formulation** explains *this* person's presentation *now* (why this person, why this problem, why now) and drives the plan.
- The syllabus wants an **aetiological statement** integrating **biological, psychological and cultural/social** contributors.
- Tool: the **4 P grid** — predisposing, precipitating, perpetuating, protective — crossed with biopsychosocial.
- **Never leave the protective column empty** — it is where the plan gets its leverage, and the column students forget.

| | Biological | Psychological | Social / cultural |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Predisposing** (remote) | Family history, birth complications, chronic illness, ABI | Early trauma, attachment, cognitive style | Poverty, migration, dislocation from Country, disrupted schooling |
| **Precipitating** (trigger) | Substance use/withdrawal, non-adherence, new illness | Loss, humiliation, anniversary reaction | Relationship breakdown, job loss, eviction |
| **Perpetuating** | Ongoing substance use, side effects, pain, poor sleep | Avoidance, rumination, hopelessness | Homelessness, unemployment, isolation, high expressed emotion |
| **Protective** | Treatment response, physical health, abstinence | Insight, help-seeking, coping skills | Engaged family, housing, employment, cultural connection |

**Worked example.** 24-year-old man, second admission with persecutory delusions and third-person hallucinations, three weeks after ceasing aripiprazole and escalating cannabis, following a relationship breakdown. **Predisposing:** paternal uncle with schizophrenia; isolated adolescence; cannabis-using peer group since 15. **Precipitating:** medication cessation, escalating cannabis, humiliating relationship breakdown, loss of housing. **Perpetuating:** ongoing cannabis; sedation driving non-adherence; belief his life is over; unstable housing, no income, disengaged. **Protective:** prior response to aripiprazole; physically well; mother willing to be involved; presented voluntarily. Management maps off the grid — reinstate the agent that worked while fixing the side effect driving non-adherence (consider LAI), cannabis intervention, housing referral, psychoeducation with the mother, assertive follow-up. **Aetiological statement:** *"An acute psychotic relapse in a young man with familial vulnerability and long-standing cannabis use, precipitated by antipsychotic cessation and interpersonal loss, maintained by ongoing substance use and housing instability, with good prior treatment response and family support."*

**Why formulation ≠ diagnosis.** A diagnosis is a category label with defined criteria (ICD-11/DSM-5-TR): reliable, communicable, linked to evidence-based treatment — but it says nothing about *this* individual. A formulation is individual, explanatory, testable, revisable and immediately actionable. Diagnosis says *schizophrenia*; formulation says *why this man relapsed in March and what to change.*

#### 1.4 Risk assessment

**The short version**
- Three domains: **risk to self** (suicide, self-harm, misadventure), **risk to others** (violence; risk to dependent children), **risk from self-neglect** (nutrition, hydration, untreated illness, exposure, exploitation).
- Factors split **static** (unmodifiable, set the baseline), **dynamic** (modifiable — the treatment targets) and **protective** (built upon).
- ⚑ **Risk-stratification tools do not predict individual suicide and must not allocate care.** RANZCP (2016, deliberate self-harm CPG) recommends verbatim: *"Do not use risk assessment scales or tools to determine the need for clinical services or follow-up in people treated in hospital after deliberate self-harm."* No empirical study has shown that low-/high-risk categorisation reduces overall rates of self-harm; "high risk" has poor positive predictive value and most suicides occur in the far larger "low-risk" group.
- ⚑ **Asking about suicide does not plant the idea** (Dazzi 2014; DeCou & Schumann 2018). Ask directly and unapologetically.
- Risk is **not a number** — it is a *narrative* linking the formulation to a specific, time-limited, contingency-based plan.

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Static | Male sex, older age, past attempt (**strongest single predictor**), family history of suicide, chronic illness, childhood abuse, prior violence |
| Dynamic | Ideation/intent/plan, hopelessness, depression severity, psychosis (esp. command hallucinations), agitation/insomnia, intoxication, recent discharge, access to means |
| Protective | Dependent children, engaged supports, treatment alliance, help-seeking, religious/cultural prohibition, future orientation |

**Assessing suicidality.** Progress ideation → intent → plan → preparation → rehearsal → access to means, asking about reasons for living in the same breath. Record what you asked and what was answered, not just your conclusion.

**Features after an attempt that raise concern:**
- **Planning** — premeditated over days/weeks vs impulsive.
- **Lethality** — objective lethality *and* the patient's belief about it; belief matters more (a believed-lethal but benign overdose signals high intent).
- **Precautions against discovery** — acting alone, locked door, cancelled visitors.
- **Final acts** — will, giving away possessions, notes, settling debts, care of pets.
- **Regret at survival** — persisting wish to be dead, disappointment at being found. The corollary (relief, future orientation) is reassuring but not alone a discharge criterion.
- Also: no help-seeking afterwards, continuing access to means, ongoing intent.

**Risk to others** — nature, target, imminence and means of any threat; command hallucinations with intent to comply; persecutory delusions with an identified persecutor; morbid jealousy (**high risk of serious violence to the partner**); history of violence (the strongest predictor); intoxication; weapons. Consider duty to warn an identifiable third party — jurisdiction-specific.

**Risk to dependent children** — mandatory in every assessment of an adult with mental illness. Who is caring for them now? Are they safe, fed, at school? Do they see or hear the symptoms, or carry a caring role? Is a child incorporated into a parent's delusional system? Australian clinicians are **mandatory reporters** of reasonable suspicion of abuse or neglect; thresholds vary by jurisdiction.

**Absconding risk** — previous absconding, involuntary status, denial of illness or refusal of treatment, intent to leave, agitation, recent admission, withdrawal. The period after unauthorised leave, and the first weeks post-discharge, carry markedly elevated suicide risk.

⚑ **Governance.** The NSQHS Comprehensive Care Standard requires systems for *predicting, preventing and managing self-harm and suicide*, *aggression and violence*, *minimising restrictive practices* and *preventing delirium* — a system and a response, not a score.

#### 1.5 Organic causes before psychiatric diagnosis

**The short version**
- Psychiatric diagnoses are made **after exclusion** — a new presentation with atypical features is organic until proven otherwise.
- Red flags: first presentation aged <14 or >40, abrupt onset, fluctuating course, **impaired consciousness or inattention**, focal neurology, seizures, catatonia, cognitive deficits, non-auditory hallucinations, abnormal vitals, no psychiatric history.
- **Delirium is the most important mimic** — see Geriatrics notes §1 for diagnosis, causes, 4AT/CAM and management; do not re-derive it here.
- Baseline bloods are cheap and justified at first presentation; **imaging is not a screening test**.

**The mimics:** delirium; thyroid disease (hypothyroidism → depression, cognitive slowing, psychosis; hyperthyroidism → anxiety, agitation, mania-like state); B12/folate deficiency (neuropsychiatric features can precede anaemia); substance intoxication and withdrawal; autoimmune encephalitis (esp. anti-NMDAR — young, often female, viral-like prodrome, psychosis with **catatonia, seizures, movement disorder, autonomic instability, cognitive decline**; ovarian teratoma); medication-induced (corticosteroids, dopamine agonists, isotretinoin, interferon, anticholinergics); intracranial pathology (frontal/temporal tumour, subdural haematoma, stroke, MS, epilepsy, neurosyphilis, HIV, NPH).

| Routine at first presentation | Rationale |
|---|---|
| FBC | Infection, anaemia, macrocytosis (alcohol, B12); baseline before clozapine/carbamazepine |
| UEC ± calcium | Renal function (lithium!), hyponatraemia, hypercalcaemia → depression/confusion |
| LFT | Alcohol, hepatic dysfunction; baseline before hepatically metabolised agents |
| BSL or HbA1c, lipids | Dysglycaemia as a cause; cardiometabolic baseline (§1.7) |
| ECG (QTc) | Baseline before QT-prolonging psychotropics; arrhythmia, electrolytes |
| Urine drug screen | Shows recent use, not causation; misses many novel agents |
| β-hCG | Anyone who could be pregnant — changes every prescribing decision |

| Targeted, not blanket | When |
|---|---|
| TFT | ⚑ In **first-episode psychosis specifically**, the 2026 ANZ recommendations (Warren et al., ANZJP 2026) move TFT out of the universal baseline panel to **testing where history/examination raise concern** (prominent mood/anxiety, weight or heat/cold intolerance, goitre, tachycardia, sleep, menstrual or hair/skin change). This scoping does **not** extend to other presentations: **TSH stays in the routine baseline panel in depression (§2.2) and anxiety (§3.1)**, where hypothyroidism is the classic mimic. Keep a low threshold; **mandatory before and during lithium** |
| B12/folate | Older adults, malnutrition, alcohol, restrictive diet, macrocytosis, cognitive impairment |
| Vitamin D | ⚑ Not an organic screen, but part of Australian **cardiometabolic monitoring** on antipsychotics |
| CRP/ESR, ANA, encephalitis antibodies (serum ± CSF) | Atypical psychosis: catatonia, rapid onset, fluctuating course, seizures, movement disorder |
| Syphilis, HIV, hepatitis B/C | Sexually active with risk factors, injecting or methamphetamine use, cognitive decline |
| CT/MRI brain (MRI preferred) | **On indication only**: focal neurology, seizures, head injury, headache/vomiting, catatonia, cognitive deficits, onset <14 or >40, treatment resistance. Routine imaging in uncomplicated first-episode psychosis has low yield |
| EEG / LP | Seizure-like activity, suspected encephalitis or CNS infection, unexplained fluctuating conscious state |

⚑ QT: **QTc >500 ms**, or a rise **>60 ms** from baseline, is the usual threshold for concern and review of QT-prolonging agents; correct K⁺ and Mg²⁺ and check interacting drugs first. ⚑ Australian caveat: Australian Prescriber argues fixed QTc cut-offs are unreliable (500 ms is "problematic for patients with tachycardia", 440 ms "too sensitive") and prefers the **QT nomogram** (QT plotted against heart rate) for risk assessment.

#### 1.6 Cultural and social dimensions

**The short version**
- **Idiom of distress** varies: distress is expressed somatically, spiritually or relationally in many cultures — not a lesser or "masked" disorder.
- A belief is only delusional if **out of keeping with the person's own cultural and religious background** — the reference group is the patient's community, not yours.
- For Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander patients, work within the **social and emotional wellbeing (SEWB)** framework.
- Migration, trauma and disaster shape what is presented and whether the person presents at all.

**Presentation.** Somatic presentation of depression and anxiety (pain, fatigue, dizziness, GI symptoms) is common globally; explanatory models (spirit involvement, sorcery, punishment) shape help-seeking and adherence. Ask: what do you think is causing this, what do you call it, what treatment do you expect? Stigma shapes disclosure, especially of suicidality and substance use.

**Interpreters.** Use a qualified interpreter whenever language proficiency is uncertain, not only when there is no English — psychiatric assessment demands nuance far above transactional English. Family members introduce distortion, censorship (psychotic content, sexual history, family violence, suicidality) and confidentiality breach; **children must never interpret**. Brief the interpreter first, speak to the patient in the second person, allow double the time, and check whether dialect, gender or community matching is needed. ⚑ **TIS National 131 450**; **Doctors Priority Line 1300 131 450**, free to eligible private practitioners.

**Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander patients.** SEWB is holistic and collectivist: wellbeing arises from **seven overlapping domains of connection — body; mind and emotions; family and kinship; community; culture; Country; spirituality and ancestors** — the self embedded in family and community. ⚑ SEWB problems and mental illness overlap but are not the same: strong SEWB can coexist with mental illness, and poor SEWB with no diagnosable disorder. The framework treats **social, historical and political determinants** — colonisation, dispossession, the Stolen Generations, intergenerational and racial trauma, incarceration, racism — as causal, not contextual. Practically: offer an Aboriginal Health Worker or SEWB worker; ask who should be involved in decisions, about connection to Country, family and community, and about grief and loss (sorry business, often cumulative); do not read culturally normative experiences (seeing a deceased relative, spiritual explanations) as psychosis; weigh distance from Country when considering admission.

**Migration, trauma and disaster.** Refugees and asylum seekers carry pre-migration trauma (war, torture, persecution) and post-migration stressors — visa insecurity, detention, family separation, unemployment, discrimination — the last often the strongest *current* driver and the most modifiable. Screen sensitively and only where there is capacity to respond; work trauma-informed (safety, choice, collaboration, trustworthiness, empowerment); avoid detailed re-narration at first assessment. After disaster most acute distress is normal and resolves — watch for the minority with persisting PTSD, depression, alcohol misuse and prolonged grief; effects peak well after the event.

#### 1.7 Physical health of people with severe mental illness

**The short version**
- ⚑ People with severe mental illness die **roughly 10–20 years earlier** than the general population; the WA population analysis found **15.9 years for men, 12.0 for women**, and the gap **widened** to 2005.
- ⚑ **~80% of that excess mortality is preventable physical illness** — chiefly cardiovascular and metabolic — **not** suicide.
- Drivers: antipsychotic-induced weight gain, dyslipidaemia and dysglycaemia, very high smoking rates, inactivity, poor diet, plus **diagnostic overshadowing** and lower rates of screening, statin prescribing and revascularisation.
- Examinable as a *principle*, not a platitude: physical complaints in a patient with a psychiatric diagnosis get attributed to that diagnosis far too readily.

**Diagnostic overshadowing** is the mechanism to name: symptoms in a person with known mental illness are misattributed to that illness (chest pain called anxiety, delirium called relapse), delaying diagnosis. Australian general practice data show higher dyslipidaemia (25% vs 18%), hypertension (27% vs 22%) and obesity (29% vs 19%) in severe mental illness; under 50, coronary heart disease mortality risk is roughly threefold.

| Parameter | Schedule |
|---|---|
| Weight/BMI | Weekly for the first 6–8 weeks after starting/changing an antipsychotic, then 3, 6, 9, 12 months, then 6-monthly |
| Waist circumference | Weekly for the first 6–8 weeks after starting/changing an antipsychotic, then baseline, 3, 6, 9, 12 months, then 6-monthly |
| BP, fasting/random glucose or HbA1c, lipids | Baseline, **3, 6, 9, 12 months**, then 6-monthly |

Thresholds prompting action: weight gain **>5 kg (or waist +5 cm) in 3 months**, BMI >25 kg/m² (>23 in higher-risk populations), waist **>94 cm men / >80 cm women**, BP **>140 mmHg systolic and/or >90 mmHg diastolic**, **HbA1c ≥6.0% (42 mmol/mol)** or fasting glucose **≥5.6 mmol/L**, LDL ≥2.0, HDL <1.0, triglycerides ≥1.7 mmol/L. Respond by reviewing the antipsychotic (metabolic burden highest with olanzapine and clozapine, lower with aripiprazole and lurasidone), starting lifestyle intervention *before* the weight is gained, considering metformin, treating lipids and BP to the same standard as anyone else, and offering smoking cessation — it does not worsen psychiatric outcomes, but ⚑ **stopping smoking raises clozapine and olanzapine levels** via CYP1A2 de-induction and may need dose reduction.

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators

| Cue | Answer |
|---|---|
| Digressive but eventually gets to the point | Circumstantiality (tangentiality never arrives) |
| Links between consecutive ideas absent or incomprehensible | Loosening of associations — schizophrenia (cf. flight of ideas: links audible, mania) |
| Sustained subjective state vs observed momentary expression | Mood vs affect (climate vs weather) |
| Misperception of a real stimulus vs percept with no stimulus | Illusion vs hallucination |
| Less than full conviction, conceded as possibly wrong | Overvalued idea, not a delusion |
| Familiar person replaced by an identical impostor | Capgras (cf. Fregoli) |
| Higher-status person secretly in love with the patient | de Clérambault; belief one is dead/has no organs = Cotard |
| Visual, olfactory or tactile hallucinations at first presentation | Organic or substance cause until excluded |
| Predisposing / precipitating / perpetuating / protective × biopsychosocial | The 4 P grid — formulation, not diagnosis |
| Strongest single predictor of completed suicide | Previous attempt |
| Does asking about suicide plant the idea? | No — meta-analyses show no increase in ideation |
| Stratifying "low/high risk" to decide follow-up | Not supported — RANZCP advises against risk scales allocating care |
| Life expectancy gap in severe mental illness | ~10–20 years (WA: 15.9 men, 12.0 women); ~80% preventable physical illness |
| Weight gain prompting action on an antipsychotic | >5 kg, or waist +5 cm, in 3 months |
| Body; mind and emotions; family and kinship; community; culture; Country; spirituality | The seven SEWB domains of connection |
| Catatonia, seizures, autonomic instability, rapid cognitive decline in a young woman | Anti-NMDAR encephalitis — seek ovarian teratoma |
