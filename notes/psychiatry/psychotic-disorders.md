## Psychotic Disorders

### Finals Tutor — Psychotic Disorders

#### Schizophrenia

Characterised by **positive symptoms** (delusions, hallucinations — classically auditory, third-person or running commentary, thought disorder, passivity phenomena), **negative symptoms** (blunted affect, avolition, alogia, anhedonia, social withdrawal), and **cognitive impairment**. Symptoms persist for at least 6 months including at least 1 month of active symptoms, with functional decline.

Negative and cognitive symptoms drive long-term disability and respond least well to medication — worth knowing, because the instinctive answer is always the positive symptoms.

**Always exclude organic causes and substances first**: delirium, temporal lobe epilepsy, encephalitis (including autoimmune), space-occupying lesion, thyroid disease, B12 deficiency, HIV, syphilis, and drug-induced psychosis (methamphetamine, cannabis, cocaine, corticosteroids). Investigations: bloods, urine drug screen, and neuroimaging if atypical, first episode with neurological signs, or late onset.

**Differentials by duration**: brief psychotic disorder (<1 month), schizophreniform (1–6 months), schizophrenia (≥6 months), schizoaffective (psychosis with prominent mood episodes, but psychosis present for ≥2 weeks without mood symptoms), delusional disorder (non-bizarre delusions, otherwise intact functioning).

#### Antipsychotics

**Typical (first-generation)** — haloperidol, chlorpromazine. Strong D2 blockade → higher extrapyramidal risk.
**Atypical (second-generation)** — olanzapine, risperidone, quetiapine, aripiprazole. Lower EPSE, higher **metabolic** burden.

**Extrapyramidal side effects — know the time course, it is regularly examined:**

| Effect | Onset | Features | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acute dystonia** | Hours–days | Oculogyric crisis, torticollis, laryngospasm | **Benztropine** |
| **Akathisia** | Days–weeks | Inner restlessness, inability to sit still | Reduce dose; propranolol |
| **Parkinsonism** | Weeks–months | Bradykinesia, rigidity, tremor | Reduce dose, switch, anticholinergic |
| **Tardive dyskinesia** | Months–years | Orofacial choreoathetoid movements | Often irreversible; stop/switch |

Akathisia is frequently mistaken for agitation or worsening psychosis — and increasing the dose makes it worse. That error is a favourite stem.

**Metabolic monitoring** — weight, waist, BP, fasting glucose/HbA1c, lipids at baseline and regularly. Olanzapine and clozapine are the worst offenders.

**Hyperprolactinaemia** — especially risperidone and typicals: galactorrhoea, amenorrhoea, sexual dysfunction, reduced bone density.

**QT prolongation** — check ECG, particularly with haloperidol and higher doses.

#### Neuroleptic malignant syndrome

Rare but life-threatening. **Fever, muscle rigidity ("lead-pipe"), autonomic instability, altered consciousness**, with **raised creatine kinase** and leucocytosis; risks rhabdomyolysis and acute kidney injury.

Management: **stop the antipsychotic**, aggressive supportive care and cooling, IV fluids, benzodiazepines; dantrolene or bromocriptine in severe cases.

**Distinguish from serotonin syndrome**: NMS has rigidity with **hyporeflexia** and evolves over days; serotonin syndrome has **clonus and hyperreflexia** and evolves over hours. That pairing is the discriminator.

#### Clozapine

Reserved for **treatment-resistant schizophrenia** — failure of two adequate trials of different antipsychotics, at least one atypical. It is the most effective antipsychotic and also reduces suicidality.

Requires **mandatory FBC monitoring** for **agranulocytosis** (weekly initially, then reducing). Other serious effects: **myocarditis** (especially in the first 4–8 weeks — check troponin/CRP/echo if symptoms), seizures (dose-related), **severe constipation progressing to ileus and bowel obstruction — a genuine cause of death and frequently underestimated**, hypersalivation, weight gain, sedation.

Any clozapine patient with fever and sore throat needs an urgent FBC.
