## Recognition Summary


### Psychiatry Master Notes — Recognition Summary

*Sources: consolidated from sections 1–7. Scope: one-page lookup layer for the written paper.*

#### 8.1 Syndrome lookup — stem to diagnosis

| Stem cue | Think | Why it's not the near-miss |
|---|---|---|
| Digressive, over-inclusive speech that eventually reaches the point | Circumstantiality (§1.2) | Tangentiality veers off and never returns |
| Links between consecutive ideas absent or opaque to the listener | Loosening of associations — schizophrenia (§1.2) | Flight of ideas keeps discernible links (puns, clang, distraction) — mania |
| Belief pursued beyond all proportion but conceded as possibly wrong | Overvalued idea (§1.2) | A delusion is held with total conviction; the discriminator is conviction, not content |
| Familiar person replaced by an identical impostor | Capgras (§1.2) | Fregoli is strangers in disguise, not a substituted familiar |
| Believes he is dead, has no organs, is damned; elderly and depressed | Cotard, psychotic depression (§1.2, §2.1) | Nihilistic and mood-congruent — not persecutory schizophrenia |
| Higher-status stranger is secretly in love with the patient | de Clérambault / erotomania (§1.2) | Not grandiosity — no inflated self-worth or special mission is claimed |
| Thoughts placed in the mind by an external agency | Thought insertion / passivity (§1.2) | Obsessions are recognised as the patient's own, ego-dystonic and resisted |
| Visual, olfactory or tactile hallucinations at first presentation | Organic or substance cause until excluded (§1.2, §4.1) | Primary psychotic illness is auditory-dominant |
| Young woman, viral prodrome, psychosis then seizures, orofacial dyskinesias, autonomic instability | Anti-NMDAR encephalitis; CSF antibodies, hunt ovarian teratoma (§1.5, §4.4) | Not schizophrenia — serum antibodies can be falsely negative, so send CSF |
| Stereotyped brief episodes, olfactory/gustatory aura, déjà vu, automatisms, post-ictal confusion | Temporal lobe epilepsy (§4.4) | Psychosis is not stereotyped, aura-heralded or post-ictal |
| Psychosis under 40 with tremor, dysarthria, deranged LFTs, Kayser–Fleischer rings | Wilson disease → caeruloplasmin, 24-h urinary copper (§4.4) | Onset <40 with neurology and hepatic signs forces a metabolic cause, not first-episode schizophrenia |
| Non-reactive mood, early-morning wakening, diurnal variation worse in the morning | Melancholic depression (§2.1) | Atypical depression keeps mood reactivity |
| Hypersomnia, hyperphagia, leaden paralysis, longstanding rejection sensitivity | Atypical depression — suspect bipolar II (§2.1) | Often misread as personality disorder; mood still reacts |
| Depression with guilt, poverty or nihilistic delusions | Psychotic depression (§2.1) | Antidepressant alone is inadequate — add an antipsychotic, or ECT |
| Chronic low-grade depression ≥2 years, no symptom-free interval >2 months | Persistent depressive disorder (dysthymia) (§2.1) | MDE is episodic; an MDE on top is "double depression" |
| Elevated or irritable mood ≥1 week with marked impairment, admission or psychosis | Mania (§2.5) | Hypomania is ≥4 days, function preserved or enhanced, never psychotic |
| Depressed with racing thoughts, agitation and distractibility | Mixed features — highest suicide risk of any bipolar state (§2.5) | Antidepressants worsen it; this is not agitated unipolar depression |
| Coarse tremor, ataxia, dysarthria and confusion after gastroenteritis on lithium | Lithium toxicity (§2.6) | Therapeutic lithium gives a *fine* tremor; precipitants are dehydration, D&V, NSAIDs, ACEi/ARB, thiazides |
| Tearful, labile, poorly slept on day 4 postpartum, function preserved | Baby blues (§2.7) | Self-limiting by day 10–14 and mood is reactive |
| Day 5 postpartum: fluctuating confusion with delusions about the infant | Puerperal psychosis — emergency (§2.7) | Rapid onset with confusion and marked fluctuation separates it from postnatal depression |
| Disabling yearning and preoccupation with the deceased beyond 6 months | Prolonged grief disorder (§2.8) | Focus stays on the deceased, not on the worthless self |
| Free-floating, topic-mobile, uncontrollable worry ≥6 months with muscle tension | GAD (§3.1) | Panic is discrete attacks; social anxiety is confined to scrutiny |
| Discrete surge of fear peaking in minutes, recurrent and unexpected, plus ≥1 month of anticipatory fear | Panic disorder (§3.2) | A panic attack alone is a descriptor, not a diagnosis |
| Episodic headache, sweating and palpitations with paroxysmal hypertension, pallor not flushing | Phaeochromocytoma → plasma free metanephrines (§3.2) | Rare — do not screen every panic patient |
| Anxiety only where evaluation is possible; fine when alone | Social anxiety disorder (§3.3) | Agoraphobia fears the body and inability to escape, not the audience |
| Syncope at venepuncture | Blood-injection-injury phobia — applied tension (§3.3) | Biphasic vasovagal response; relaxation is the wrong treatment |
| Intrusive taboo thoughts, repugnant and resisted, with mental rituals | OCD (§3.4) | Ego-dystonic with no intent — the opposite of a violent or paraphilic drive |
| Perfectionism and rigidity seen as correct and virtuous; the distress is in others | OCPD (§3.4) | Ego-syntonic pervasive trait, not discrete obsessions with linked compulsions |
| Preoccupation with a slight defect, mirror-checking, seeking cosmetic procedures | Body dysmorphic disorder (§3.4) | Procedures typically worsen it — CBT + SSRI |
| Intrusions, avoidance, negative cognitions and hyperarousal beyond 1 month post-trauma | PTSD (§3.5) | ASD occupies days 3–30; adjustment disorder needs no criterion A trauma |
| PTSD features plus affect dysregulation, negative self-concept and interpersonal disturbance after prolonged inescapable trauma | Complex PTSD (§3.5) | Commonly mislabelled BPD — negative self-concept is stable and re-experiencing is required |
| Weak hip extension that normalises when the contralateral hip is flexed against resistance | FND — Hoover's sign (§3.7) | A rule-in sign, not diagnosis by exclusion; FND coexists with real neurological disease |
| Event >2 min, waxing–waning, eyes closed with resistance, no post-ictal confusion, normal prolactin/lactate | Dissociative seizures (§3.7) | Epileptic seizures are shorter, stereotyped and post-ictally confused |
| Genuine symptoms — even explained ones — with disproportionate health thoughts and time, >6 months | Somatic symptom disorder (§3.7) | The symptom does **not** have to be medically unexplained |
| 22-year-old, a year of withdrawal and decline, then voices commenting and passivity, ≥6 months | Schizophrenia (§4.2) | Schizophreniform is 1–6 months; brief psychotic disorder <1 month with full recovery |
| ≥2 weeks of delusions or hallucinations with mood symptoms absent | Schizoaffective disorder (§4.2) | In psychotic depression or mania the psychosis sits **only inside** mood episodes |
| ≥1 month of delusions, no hallucinations, disorganisation or negatives, function preserved outside the theme | Delusional disorder (§4.2) | Schizophrenia brings negative symptoms and global functional decline |
| Inner restlessness with pacing, unable to sit, weeks into an antipsychotic | Akathisia (§4.5) | Anticholinergics do not help it; it is linked to suicidality, not to psychotic agitation |
| Choreoathetoid lip-smacking and tongue protrusion after years of antipsychotic | Tardive dyskinesia (§4.5) | Stop the anticholinergic — the opposite of acute dystonia management |
| Lead-pipe rigidity, fever, autonomic instability and CK in the thousands over 3 days | NMS (§4.7) | Serotonin toxicity is hours, clonic and hyperreflexic |
| Clonus and hyperreflexia legs > arms, mydriasis, diarrhoea, hours after adding tramadol | Serotonin toxicity (§4.7, §7.2) | Reflexes are the best single discriminator from NMS |
| Week 3 of clozapine: tachycardia out of proportion, dyspnoea, unexplained CRP rise | Clozapine myocarditis (§4.6) | Peaks in the first 3–4 weeks — not benign clozapine tachycardia |
| Hallucinations at 24 h with intact orientation and a clear sensorium | Alcoholic hallucinosis — escalate benzodiazepines (§5.3) | DT clouds consciousness and disorients; hallucinosis does not |
| Dense anterograde amnesia with confabulation, immediate recall preserved | Korsakoff syndrome (§5.3) | The chronic, largely irreversible sequel of missed Wernicke |
| Mydriasis, lacrimation, yawning, piloerection, cramps, diarrhoea, craving | Opioid withdrawal (§5.5) | Miserable but rarely lethal — unlike alcohol, benzodiazepine or GHB withdrawal |
| Agitated, mydriatic, hyperthermic and drenched in sweat after a binge | Sympathomimetic toxidrome — benzodiazepines first (§5.7) | Wet skin separates it from anticholinergic toxicity |
| Paranoid with formication, clearing over 10 days of abstinence | Stimulant psychosis (§5.7) | Third-person auditory hallucinations and negative symptoms point to schizophrenia |
| Years of daily cannabis, cyclical vomiting relieved only by hot showers | Cannabinoid hyperemesis (§5.8) | Antiemetics fail; cessation is the cure |
| Festival collapse with seizure and Na⁺ 118 | MDMA hyponatraemia (§5.8) | SIADH plus excess free water — not simple heat stroke |
| Abrupt coma with rapid spontaneous recovery, bradycardia, myoclonus | GHB/GBL (§5.8) | No antidote; withdrawal in dependent users is severe and benzodiazepine-refractory |
| Photophobia, hyperacusis, metallic taste and paraesthesiae days after stopping a hypnotic | Benzodiazepine withdrawal (§5.6) | Perceptual hypersensitivity is characteristic and it can progress to seizures and delirium |
| BMI 19 but 15 kg lost in 3 months, HR 44, postural SBP drop 25 mmHg | Medically unstable atypical AN/OSFED — admit (§6.1) | Normal BMI does not exclude instability; BMI must not gate admission |
| Knuckle callus, painless parotid swelling, erosion of palatal incisor surfaces | Self-induced vomiting (§6.3) | Russell's sign, sialadenosis with raised *salivary* amylase, perimylolysis |
| Mood shifts over minutes to hours, abandonment sensitivity, self-harm to end dissociation | BPD (§6.5) | Bipolar mood episodes run days to weeks with sleep, energy and goal-directed change |
| Onset before 12, impairment in more than one setting, more than one informant | ADHD (§6.7) | Never diagnosed on rating scales or observation alone |
| Morning abdominal pain on school days only; child at home, parents aware, distressed | School refusal (§6.7) | Truancy is concealed, undistressed and elsewhere with peers |
| Movements preceded by a premonitory urge and temporarily suppressible | Tic disorder / Tourette (§6.7) | The urge and suppressibility separate tics from other hyperkinetic movements |
| 10-year-old with irritability, somatic complaints and school decline | Childhood depression (§6.7) | Presents as irritability and behaviour change, not classical low mood |

#### 8.2 Signature findings

- **Thought alienation** — insertion, withdrawal, broadcasting, made acts/feelings/impulses; ⚑ no longer diagnostically specific for schizophrenia (§1.2).
- **Incongruent affect** (laughing while describing a bereavement) — schizophrenia-spectrum flag (§1.2).
- **Lead-pipe rigidity with CK in the thousands** — NMS. **Inducible or ocular clonus with hyperreflexia** — serotonin toxicity (§4.7).
- **Oculogyric crisis, trismus, opisthotonus; laryngeal dystonia = airway emergency** — acute dystonia (§4.5).
- **Post-injection delirium/sedation syndrome** up to 3 h after olanzapine pamoate LAI (§4.5).
- **Waxy flexibility, catalepsy, negativism, echolalia/echopraxia, automatic obedience, ambitendency** — catatonia (§4.8).
- **Ebstein anomaly** — first-trimester lithium; **neural tube defects** — valproate and carbamazepine (§2.6).
- **"Electric shock" brain zaps** with dizziness and flu-like malaise days after a dose cut — antidepressant discontinuation (§2.3, §7.2).
- **HLA-B*15:02** and carbamazepine SJS/TEN — testing *considered* in Han-Chinese patients in Australia (§2.6).
- **Macrocytosis without anaemia, GGT rise, AST:ALT >2:1** — alcohol (§5.2).
- **Gelatinous marrow transformation**, with infection occurring without fever or leucocytosis — anorexia (§6.1).
- **Formication ("bugs under the skin"), bruxism, "meth mouth", mydriasis, skin excoriation** — methamphetamine (§4.4, §5.7).
- **Russell's sign; bilateral painless parotid sialadenosis with raised salivary amylase; perimylolysis of the palatal surfaces of the upper incisors; palatal petechiae** — purging (§6.3).
- **Pseudo-Bartter rebound oedema** on stopping purging; **refeeding oedema** is expected — pre-warn (§6.1, §6.3).
- **Lanugo, carotenoderma, hypothermia, SMA syndrome, sick euthyroid (↓T3)** — starvation; all reverse with nutrition **except bone density**, and the OCP does not restore it (§6.1).
- **Hypophosphataemia at 24–72 h of feeding** — the hallmark of refeeding syndrome (§6.2).
- **Hypokalaemic hypochloraemic metabolic alkalosis** (vomiting) versus **normal anion gap metabolic acidosis with hypokalaemia** (laxatives) (§6.3).
- **Kayser–Fleischer rings** — Wilson disease; **ovarian teratoma** — anti-NMDAR encephalitis (§4.4).
- **Tubular (non-expanding) visual field; give-way ratchety weakness; tremor entrainment** — FND rule-in signs (§3.7).
- **Splitting and team splitting** — BPD; the antidote is structural (one shared care plan, one message) (§6.5).
- **High expressed emotion** — criticism, hostility, emotional over-involvement; predicts relapse in schizophrenia (§4.9).
- **Nocturnal sialorrhoea in 30–80% on clozapine**; tachycardia is benign only once myocarditis is excluded (§4.6).
- **Subacute combined degeneration** in a nitrous oxide user (B12 inactivation) (§5.8).
- **Hoover's sign, tremor entrainment and Hoover-type inconsistency are positive findings** — la belle indifférence and secondary gain are unreliable and must not be used (§3.7).

#### 8.3 Confusable pairs

| Pair | The discriminator |
|---|---|
| Delirium vs dementia vs depression | Delirium — acute, **fluctuating attention and consciousness**, disorientation, reversed sleep–wake, visual hallucinations (§1.5, §4.4). Dementia — chronic, clear consciousness. Depression — "memory problems" are impaired concentration on a background of pervasive low mood and anhedonia (§2.1). ⚑ Delirium detail is deferred to Geriatrics §1 |
| NMS vs serotonin syndrome vs malignant catatonia vs anticholinergic toxicity | NMS — dopamine blocker started/increased (or agonist withdrawn), **days (1–3)**, generalised lead-pipe rigidity, normal-to-reduced reflexes, **CK very high**. Serotonin — serotonergic added, **hours (<24 h)**, **hyperreflexia and clonus, legs > arms**, mydriasis, hyperactive bowel sounds and diarrhoea, CK normal/mild. Malignant catatonia — catatonic background, waxy flexibility, posturing, negativism; **lorazepam then ECT**, antipsychotics may worsen it. Anticholinergic — **dry, flushed, hot, absent bowel sounds, retention**, no rigidity (§4.7, §7.2) |
| Akathisia vs agitation vs anxiety | Akathisia — **subjective inner restlessness** with pacing and inability to sit, timed to antipsychotic start or dose rise (usually <4 weeks); **anticholinergics do not help** (reduce dose/switch, propranolol, mirtazapine). Psychotic agitation responds to the antipsychotic; anxiety has cognitive content and no drug-timing relationship (§4.5) |
| Bipolar vs unipolar depression | Any past **mania or hypomania** — ask directly, take collateral. Bipolar depression: earlier onset, more episodes, atypical/psychotic/postpartum features, prior antidepressant switch. Consequence: antidepressant monotherapy risks a manic switch and rapid cycling (§2.1, §2.5) |
| Schizophrenia vs schizoaffective vs mood disorder with psychotic features | Schizoaffective needs **≥2 weeks of psychosis with mood symptoms absent**, with mood episodes present for most of total illness duration. In psychotic depression or mania the psychosis occurs **only within** mood episodes and is usually mood-congruent. Schizophrenia: ≥6 months, negatives and functional decline dominate (§4.2) |
| BPD vs bipolar II | **Minutes to hours**, interpersonally reactive, baseline emptiness, self-harm to regulate affect (BPD) vs **days to weeks** of sustained change with reduced sleep need and increased goal-directed activity (bipolar II) (§6.5) |
| Postnatal blues vs postnatal depression vs puerperal psychosis | Blues — day 3–5, gone by day 10–14, mood reactive, function preserved, ~50–80%. PND — anhedonia, guilt, impaired bonding, months if untreated, ~1 in 7. Psychosis — **first 2 weeks**, rapid onset with marked fluctuation and confusion, infant-focused delusions, 1–2/1000, emergency (§2.7) |
| Alcohol withdrawal vs delirium tremens vs Wernicke's | Uncomplicated withdrawal 6–24 h with a **clear sensorium**; hallucinosis 12–48 h with **intact orientation**; DT 48–72 h with **clouded consciousness and disorientation**; Wernicke is **confusion + ophthalmoplegia + ataxia** from thiamine deficiency and needs parenteral thiamine, not more benzodiazepine (§5.3) |
| Panic vs GAD vs social anxiety | Panic — **discrete crescendo attacks peaking in minutes**, fear of the attack and the body. GAD — **continuous topic-mobile worry ≥6 months** with muscle tension and initial insomnia. Social anxiety — symptoms **only where evaluation is possible**, absent when alone (§3.1–3.3) |
| PTSD vs acute stress disorder vs adjustment disorder | **ASD 3 days–1 month; PTSD beyond 1 month** (delayed expression if full criteria first met ≥6 months out); adjustment disorder needs **no criterion A trauma**, begins within 1 month (ICD-11) / 3 months (DSM-5-TR) of an identifiable stressor and resolves within ~6 months of it ending (§2.8, §3.5) |
| Anorexia vs bulimia vs ARFID | **Weight decides between AN and BN** — low weight with binge–purge is AN, BP subtype. BN sits at normal or higher weight with regular compensation and electrolyte-dominant risk. ARFID has **no body-image disturbance or drive for thinness** (§6.3) |
| FND vs factitious vs malingering | FND — **positive rule-in signs** of internal inconsistency or incompatibility; intent is deliberately **not assessed**. Factitious — conscious falsification with **no obvious external incentive**, and motive need not be proven. Malingering — conscious with an **obvious external incentive**, and not a psychiatric disorder (§3.7) |
| The toxidromes | Sympathomimetic — **wet**, mydriatic, hyperthermic, agitated. Anticholinergic — **dry**, flushed, absent bowel sounds, retention. Opioid — **pinpoint pupils, bradypnoea, sedation**. Serotonergic — clonus, hyperreflexia, diarrhoea. NMS — rigid, hot, slow onset, very high CK (§5.7, §7.2) |
| ADHD vs autism vs conduct disorder | ADHD — inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity from before 12, impairing in >1 setting, corroborated by >1 reporter. Autism — **social communication and reciprocity difficulty plus restricted repetitive behaviours**; a social *skill* deficit, not fear or inattention. Conduct disorder — repetitive **violation of others' rights** across aggression, property destruction, deceit/theft and serious rule-breaking; ODD is defiance towards authority without rights violation (§3.3, §6.7) |
| Catatonia vs stupor | Catatonia is a **psychomotor syndrome diagnosed on ≥3 signs** — mutism, negativism, catalepsy, waxy flexibility, posturing, echophenomena — with response to a **lorazepam challenge**; undifferentiated stupor demands organic exclusion (EEG separates catatonia from non-convulsive status) (§4.8) |
| OCD vs OCPD | **Ego-dystonic**, resisted and distressing, with discrete obsessions and linked compulsions vs **ego-syntonic** pervasive lifelong perfectionism, orderliness and rigidity where the distress is in those around them (§3.4) |
| Illusion vs hallucination vs pseudohallucination | Misperception of a **real** stimulus vs a percept with **no** stimulus in external space with the full force of a true percept vs an experience in inner subjective space and/or recognised as unreal (§1.2) |
| Discontinuation syndrome vs relapse | **Onset within 1–4 days of the dose change**, physical/neurological, settling within hours of reinstatement — relapse takes weeks and reproduces the illness. ⚑ Duration is **not** the discriminator (§2.3, §7.2) |
| Complex PTSD vs BPD | CPTSD — **stable** negative self-concept, less prominent frantic abandonment-avoidance, re-experiencing required, following prolonged inescapable interpersonal trauma (§3.5) |
| Stimulant psychosis vs schizophrenia | Abrupt onset locked to a binge, **tactile and visual** hallucinations, negative symptoms uncommon, resolving in days to ~1 month with abstinence — the diagnosis is retrospective (§5.7) |
| Physiological dependence vs addiction | Tolerance and withdrawal occur with prescribed opioids and benzodiazepines; a use disorder needs **impaired control, salience and use despite harm** (§5.1) |
| Boundary crossing vs boundary violation | A crossing is a minor, context-dependent deviation that should be conscious, justifiable and documented; a **violation serves the clinician and harms the patient** — any sexual contact, financial dealings, dual relationships (§6.6) |

#### 8.4 Must-not-miss

| Emergency | Immediate action as stated in the sections |
|---|---|
| **NMS** | Stop all antipsychotics and other dopamine blockers (including metoclopramide, prochlorperazine); medical/ICU transfer; aggressive IV fluids against rhabdomyolysis-induced AKI, active cooling, electrolyte correction, VTE prophylaxis, serial renal function and CK; **benzodiazepine (lorazepam) first pharmacologically**; dantrolene and/or bromocriptine if severe or refractory; ECT if refractory or if malignant catatonia cannot be excluded (§4.7) |
| **Serotonin toxicity** | Cease the drug, supportive care, **benzodiazepines**, cooling and fluids; **cyproheptadine** if moderate–severe (§4.7, §7.2) |
| **Malignant catatonia** | **Lorazepam then ECT — first-line without delay**; withhold or minimise antipsychotics, which can precipitate or worsen it; VTE prophylaxis, hydration, pressure-area and nutritional care from day one (§4.7, §4.8) |
| **Acute laryngeal dystonia** | **Benztropine 1–2 mg IM/IV** (or promethazine), repeat if needed — relief within minutes is diagnostic; **secure the airway**; then reduce dose or switch (§4.5, §4.7) |
| **Clozapine agranulocytosis** | Red result (ANC <1.5 or WCC <3.0) → **cease immediately**, daily FBC, haematology, watch for infection; **ANC <0.5 = agranulocytosis** — treat as febrile neutropenia with cultures, broad-spectrum antibiotics, consider G-CSF (§4.6) |
| **Fever or sore throat on clozapine** | **Urgent FBC, whenever the last one was taken** (§4.6) |
| **Clozapine myocarditis** | Rising **troponin >2× ULN and/or CRP >100 mg/L** → **stop clozapine, echocardiogram, cardiology** (§4.6) |
| **Clozapine ileus/toxic megacolon** | Treat abdominal pain, distension, vomiting or absent bowel sounds as **ileus/toxic megacolon until excluded — imaging and surgical review** (§4.6) |
| **Lithium toxicity** | Stop lithium, **IV normal saline**, levels 4–6 hourly with UEC and ECG; **haemodialysis if >4 mmol/L**, or >2.5 with significant symptoms or renal failure; levels rebound after dialysis; ⚑ activated charcoal does not bind lithium (§2.6) |
| **Lamotrigine rash** | **Stop immediately**, especially with mucosal involvement, purpura or fever (§2.6) |
| **Severe alcohol withdrawal / DT** | **Diazepam loading 10–20 mg orally every 1–2 h until 60–80 mg given or lightly sedated**; review if still agitated after 80 mg or 4 h; **>120 mg only after specialist review**; refractory DT → ICU with parenteral benzodiazepine ± phenobarbitone; rehydrate, replace Mg/K/PO₄, hunt occult infection (§5.3) |
| **Wernicke encephalopathy** | **Treat empirically on suspicion** — ≥500 mg/day thiamine parenterally for ≥3–5 days; **give thiamine before (or with) any glucose load**, and never withhold glucose from a hypoglycaemic patient (§5.3) |
| **Opioid overdose** | Naloxone **titrated to respiration, not arousal** — 100 µg IV increments (community 400 µg IM/SC or 1.8 mg intranasal); remove any fentanyl or buprenorphine patch; observe **≥2 h** after the last dose in short-acting overdose and **admit for prolonged observation (a day or more) after methadone or controlled-release opioids** (§5.5) |
| **Refeeding syndrome** | **Thiamine before or with the first carbohydrate**, prophylactic phosphate if high risk, daily bloods, cautious escalation with cardiac and fluid monitoring; if levels fall, **replace and hold the feed rate steady — do not stop feeding**; correct **magnesium first** if K⁺ is refractory (§6.2) |
| **Medically unstable anorexia nervosa** | Any single admission criterion generally warrants **medical (not psychiatric) admission** — BMI must not gate it; involuntary admission and NG feeding where refusal of life-saving nutrition is itself a symptom (§6.1, §6.4) |
| **Puerperal psychosis** | **Same-day assessment and almost always admission, ideally to a mother–baby unit**; exclude organic causes (sepsis, eclampsia, thyroiditis) first; antipsychotic ± mood stabiliser, benzodiazepine, **ECT if severe, catatonic or refusing food and fluid**; assess risk to mother **and** infant (§2.7) |
| **Anti-NMDAR encephalitis** | Confirm on **CSF antibodies — serum can be falsely negative**; seek an ovarian teratoma; MRI, EEG and LP where the picture points that way (§1.5, §4.4) |
| **Suicidality after an attempt** | Collaborative **written safety plan** (never a no-suicide contract), means restriction, limited quantities dispensed, SSRIs over TCAs where overdose risk is real; **contact within 7 days of discharge as an actual appointment**, same-day discharge summary to the GP, assertive follow-up (§2.9) |
| **Risk to dependent children** | Mandatory in every adult assessment; Australian clinicians are **mandatory reporters on reasonable suspicion, not proof**; a parent's illness triggers consideration of the children's safety **regardless of the parent's consent** (§1.4, §7.7) |
| **Stimulant hyperthermia** | **Cool aggressively if >39 °C — antipyretics do not work**; benzodiazepines first-line and before antihypertensives; ⚑ avoid unopposed beta-blockade in cocaine toxicity (§5.7) |
| **QTc >500 ms or a rise >60 ms** | Stop or switch the QT-prolonging agent; **correct K⁺ and Mg²⁺** and check interacting drugs first (§1.5, §4.5, §7.2) |
| **Over-sedation after acute behavioural disturbance** | Escalating observation — difficult to rouse: **5–10-minutely**; unresponsive: **call the MET**; record RR, continuous SpO₂, BP, HR, temperature and conscious level until ambulant (§7.6) |

#### Gaps

What a written finals paper could reasonably test that sections 1–7 do not cover:

- **Delirium and dementia proper.** §1.5 names delirium as the most important mimic but explicitly defers diagnosis, causes, 4AT/CAM and management to Geriatrics §1. No BPSD pathway beyond "antipsychotics are not first-line", and no cut-off scores for MMSE, MoCA or RUDAS despite all three being named.
- **The four-part capacity test.** Capacity is load-bearing throughout (ECT consent, Gillick, guardianship) but understand / retain / weigh / communicate is never set out.
- **Mental health legislation outside NSW and Victoria.** Qld, WA, SA, Tas, ACT and NT thresholds, orders and time limits are absent (only the second-opinion right is mentioned for other jurisdictions).
- **Forensic psychiatry.** No fitness to stand trial, mental impairment defence, forensic orders or prison mental health pathway — only the ASPD/custodial overlap in §6.5.
- **Overdose management detail.** TCA overdose is named as bicarbonate-responsive with broad QRS, but there is no paracetamol, mixed-overdose or lithium-specific decontamination content and no ECG criteria for TCA toxicity.
- **Sleep disorders.** CBT-I is named as first-line for insomnia; nothing on melatonin, Z-drug dosing, OSA workup or circadian disorders.
- **Older-persons psychiatry.** Late-onset psychosis, late-life depression specifics, elder abuse, and prescribing in frailty beyond "lowest dose, no anticholinergics".
- **Intellectual disability psychiatry**, including diagnostic overshadowing in that population.
- **Gambling and other behavioural addictions.**
- **Premenstrual dysphoric disorder, menopause-related mood change**, and psychiatric aspects of specific physical illness (e.g. post-stroke depression).
- **Gender dysphoria and LGBTIQ+ specific care** beyond LGBTIQ+ appearing as a suicide risk demographic.
- **Adult ADHD pharmacology** — agents are named but with no doses, titration schedules or formulation differences.
- **Antipsychotic dose equivalence and oral-to-LAI conversion** — chlorpromazine equivalents appear only in the TRS definition.
- **Fitness to drive** — appears only as a confidentiality/duty exception, with no standards or reporting thresholds.
- **Perinatal disorders beyond mood** — no perinatal anxiety, OCD or tokophobia; antipsychotic choice in breastfeeding is only "olanzapine and quetiapine have the most safety data".
- **Childhood bereavement and developmental trauma**, and paediatric acute sedation dosing (all ABD doses given are adult).
- **Neurosyphilis, HIV-associated neurocognitive disorder and CNS infection management** once identified as the cause of a psychiatric presentation.
