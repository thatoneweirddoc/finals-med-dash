## Anxiety, Obsessive-Compulsive and Trauma-Related Disorders

### Finals Tutor — Anxiety, OCD and Trauma-Related Disorders

#### The shared principles

All are characterised by excessive fear or anxiety with avoidance, causing functional impairment. Two rules apply throughout:

1. **Exclude organic mimics** — hyperthyroidism, phaeochromocytoma, arrhythmia, hypoglycaemia, asthma, caffeine, stimulants, alcohol and benzodiazepine **withdrawal**
2. **Psychological therapy (CBT with exposure) is first-line**; SSRIs are the first-line pharmacological option

**Benzodiazepines** may be used very short-term in a crisis but are avoided because of tolerance, dependence, falls in the elderly, and cognitive impairment. If a stem describes ongoing benzodiazepine use for anxiety, that is usually the problem being tested.

#### Generalised anxiety disorder

Excessive, difficult-to-control worry about multiple domains, most days for **at least 6 months**, with physical features — restlessness, fatigue, poor concentration, irritability, muscle tension, disturbed sleep.

Treatment: CBT; SSRI or SNRI (venlafaxine, duloxetine). Buspirone or pregabalin as alternatives.

#### Panic disorder

Recurrent **unexpected** panic attacks — abrupt surges of intense fear peaking within minutes, with palpitations, sweating, trembling, dyspnoea, chest pain, nausea, dizziness, paraesthesia, derealisation, and fear of dying or losing control — **plus persistent worry about further attacks or maladaptive behaviour change**. That anticipatory anxiety is what makes it a disorder rather than isolated attacks.

Frequently presents to ED as chest pain; exclude ACS, PE, arrhythmia, thyrotoxicosis before settling on it. Treatment: CBT with interoceptive exposure; SSRI.

**Agoraphobia** — fear of situations where escape is difficult (public transport, open or enclosed spaces, crowds, being outside home alone), leading to avoidance. May become housebound.

#### Social anxiety disorder

Marked fear of social or performance situations with scrutiny by others, and fear of humiliation. Distinguish from **avoidant personality disorder** (pervasive, lifelong pattern) — though the two overlap heavily and may be the same construct at different severity.

#### Specific phobia

Marked fear of a specific object or situation, out of proportion to actual danger, with avoidance. **Graded exposure therapy** is the treatment; medication has little role.

#### Obsessive-compulsive disorder

**Obsessions** — intrusive, unwanted, distressing thoughts, images or urges recognised as one's own (ego-dystonic). **Compulsions** — repetitive behaviours or mental acts performed to neutralise the obsession or prevent a feared outcome, not realistically connected or clearly excessive.

Common themes: contamination/washing, checking, symmetry/ordering, taboo intrusive thoughts (aggressive, sexual, religious), hoarding.

**Treatment — note the two differences from depression, both examinable:**
- **CBT with exposure and response prevention (ERP)** is the specific psychological treatment
- **SSRI at higher doses than for depression** (e.g. fluoxetine 40–60 mg, up to 80 mg) and a **longer trial before judging response — 8–12 weeks** rather than 4–6

Clomipramine (a TCA) is effective second-line. Antipsychotic augmentation for refractory cases.

**Distinguish OCD from obsessive-compulsive personality disorder** — OCPD is a pervasive, ego-**syntonic** pattern of perfectionism, orderliness and control, without true obsessions and compulsions.

#### Post-traumatic stress disorder

Follows exposure to actual or threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence — directly, as a witness, learning of it happening to a close other, or repeated exposure to aversive details (e.g. first responders).

Four symptom clusters:
1. **Intrusion** — flashbacks, nightmares, intrusive memories
2. **Avoidance** — of reminders, thoughts, feelings
3. **Negative alterations in cognition and mood** — numbing, detachment, negative beliefs, amnesia for aspects of the event
4. **Hyperarousal** — hypervigilance, exaggerated startle, irritability, sleep and concentration disturbance

Symptoms >1 month (under 1 month with similar features = **acute stress disorder**).

**Treatment**: trauma-focused psychological therapy first-line — **trauma-focused CBT or EMDR**. SSRI/SNRI second-line. **Avoid benzodiazepines** — they worsen outcomes in PTSD, which is a common wrong answer. Prazosin may help nightmares.

**Adjustment disorder** — emotional or behavioural symptoms out of proportion to an identifiable stressor, within 3 months, resolving within 6 months of the stressor ending; does not meet criteria for another disorder. Treatment is supportive and problem-solving focused.
