## The Red Eye

### Finals Tutor — The Red Eye

The task is separating benign causes from sight-threatening ones. Three questions do most of the work: **Is there pain? Is vision affected? Is the pupil abnormal?**

| | Conjunctivitis | Keratitis | Anterior uveitis | Acute angle closure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pain | Gritty, mild | **Significant** | **Aching, deep** | **Severe** + headache, **nausea/vomiting** |
| Vision | Normal | Reduced | Reduced, blurred | **Markedly reduced**, haloes |
| Redness | **Diffuse** | Diffuse/circumciliary | **Circumciliary (ciliary flush)** | Diffuse, congested |
| Pupil | Normal | Normal/small | **Small, irregular** (posterior synechiae) | **Mid-dilated, fixed, oval** |
| Discharge | **Yes** | Watery ± | Watery | None |
| Cornea | Clear | **Opacity/ulcer, stains** | Clear ± keratic precipitates | **Hazy/oedematous** |
| Photophobia | Mild | Yes | **Marked** | Yes |

**Pattern of redness** is the workshop's emphasis: *diffuse* suggests conjunctivitis; *circumciliary (limbal flush)* suggests uveitis or keratitis; *sectoral* suggests episcleritis or scleritis.

#### Conjunctivitis

- **Bacterial** — purulent discharge, lids stuck together in the morning. Usually self-limiting; topical chloramphenicol if severe or persistent
- **Viral** (adenovirus) — watery discharge, **follicles**, preauricular lymphadenopathy, often bilateral sequentially, very contagious. Supportive; hygiene advice
- **Allergic** — **itch is the dominant symptom**, bilateral, **papillae**, stringy mucoid discharge, associated atopy. Antihistamine or mast cell stabiliser drops

**Gonococcal conjunctivitis** — hyperacute, copious purulent discharge, an emergency because it can perforate the cornea. **Neonatal conjunctivitis (ophthalmia neonatorum)** within the first days of life needs urgent assessment for gonococcus and chlamydia.

#### Keratitis and corneal ulcer

**A contact lens wearer with a red painful eye has microbial keratitis until proven otherwise** — including *Pseudomonas* and *Acanthamoeba* (severe pain out of proportion, associated with water exposure). Same-day referral; do **not** patch; do not prescribe steroid drops.

**Herpes simplex keratitis** — **dendritic ulcer** staining with fluorescein, reduced corneal sensation. Treat with topical aciclovir. **Topical corticosteroids are contraindicated** — they cause geographic ulceration and corneal melt. This is one of the most reliably examined points in ophthalmology.

#### Anterior uveitis (iritis)

Deep aching pain, photophobia (including **consensual** photophobia), circumciliary flush, small irregular pupil from synechiae, cells and flare in the anterior chamber, hypopyon if severe.

**Systemic associations** — this is why it appears in medicine papers: **HLA-B27** conditions (ankylosing spondylitis, reactive arthritis, IBD, psoriatic arthritis), sarcoidosis, TB, syphilis, herpes, juvenile idiopathic arthritis. Ask about back pain, bowel symptoms, rashes, joint disease.

Treatment: topical corticosteroid **plus cycloplegic** (relieves ciliary spasm and prevents synechiae) — under ophthalmology.

#### Acute angle-closure glaucoma

Sudden severe pain, **headache, nausea and vomiting** (patients are sometimes worked up as an abdominal or neurological problem — a classic trap), haloes around lights, markedly reduced vision, **fixed mid-dilated oval pupil**, hazy cornea, **hard eye** on palpation, very high intraocular pressure.

Precipitants: dim light, pupil-dilating drugs (**anticholinergics, sympathomimetics, some antidepressants**), prone position. Risks: hypermetropia, older age, female, Asian ethnicity.

**Emergency management**: urgent ophthalmology; lie supine; topical agents to lower pressure (beta-blocker, alpha-agonist, pilocarpine to constrict the pupil), **IV/oral acetazolamide**, analgesia and antiemetics; definitive treatment is **laser peripheral iridotomy** — and to **both** eyes, since the fellow eye is anatomically at risk.

#### Episcleritis vs scleritis

**Episcleritis** — sectoral redness, mild or no pain, vision normal, **blanches with topical phenylephrine**, self-limiting.
**Scleritis** — **severe boring pain, often waking the patient**, deep violaceous hue, does **not** blanch, vision may be affected, and is strongly associated with systemic autoimmune disease (**rheumatoid arthritis, granulomatosis with polyangiitis**). Sight-threatening; needs systemic immunosuppression.

#### Subconjunctival haemorrhage

Painless, flat, uniformly red patch with normal vision. Benign; check BP and ask about anticoagulants and Valsalva. Reassure; resolves in 1–2 weeks. If it follows trauma with no visible posterior margin, consider globe rupture or orbital fracture.
