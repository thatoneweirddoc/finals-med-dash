## Recognition Summary and Confusable Pairs


### Geriatrics Master Notes — Recognition Summary and Confusable Pairs

*A lookup layer over Sections 1–5. Nothing new is taught here — this is for the last week before the paper.*

#### 6.1 Syndrome recognition — one line in, one answer out

| Presentation | Answer |
|---|---|
| Fluctuating confusion with inattention 2 days after admission | Delirium — find and treat the cause; non-pharmacological management first |
| Quiet, drowsy, withdrawn post-op patient, "settled", poor intake | Hypoactive delirium — commonest, most missed, worst prognosis |
| Confusion day 2–4 of admission, tremulous, sweaty, hallucinating | Alcohol/benzodiazepine withdrawal delirium — the one indication for a benzo |
| Delirium with no cause found, on apixaban, fall last week | CT brain — subdural haematoma |
| Agitated confusion, distended bladder / loaded rectum | Retention or faecal impaction — bladder scan and PR |
| Insidious 3-year episodic memory loss, repeats questions, hides deficits | Alzheimer's disease |
| Stepwise decline, HTN/AF, focal signs, gait apraxia, early urinary symptoms | Vascular dementia |
| Fluctuating cognition, formed visual hallucinations, parkinsonism, acts out dreams | Dementia with Lewy bodies |
| 58-year-old, disinhibition, apathy, hyperorality, memory relatively intact | Behavioural-variant frontotemporal dementia — ChEIs not indicated |
| Magnetic wide-based gait, incontinence, cognitive slowing, ventriculomegaly | Normal pressure hydrocephalus — CSF tap test, then VP shunt |
| Objective memory decline but fully independent | Mild cognitive impairment — review 6–12 months |
| Cognitive testing needed via interpreter, 4 years' schooling | RUDAS (≤22/30) |
| Cognitive screening in an Aboriginal patient aged 50 | KICA-Cog (≤33 remote, ≤35 urban/regional); screen from 45 |
| Newly diagnosed dementia, still driving | Conditional licence only, with review; patient must notify; consider on-road OT assessment |
| Nursing home resident newly agitated at showering time | BPSD — look for the unmet need (pain, retention, constipation, fear) before any drug |
| Regular antipsychotic started for behaviour in an RACF | Chemical restraint — last resort, consent, documented behaviour support plan, review |
| Persistent aggression in moderate–severe AD, non-drug measures failed | Risperidone — only PBS-listed agent, up to 12 weeks with review |
| Somatic complaints, weight loss, "I don't know" answers, mood preceded memory | Depression (pseudodementia) — treat, then reassess cognition |
| Older man, recent bereavement, hopeless, hoarding tablets | High suicide risk — ask directly, low threshold for admission |
| Confusion + hyponatraemia 3 weeks after starting sertraline | SSRI-induced SIADH — stop/change the drug |
| Found on floor at home, no memory of falling | Treat as syncope until proven otherwise — ECG, postural BP |
| Collapse while standing at a queue, prodromal nausea and greying vision | Vasovagal syncope |
| Exertional syncope, slow-rising pulse, ESM to carotids | Aortic stenosis — echocardiogram |
| Abrupt collapse, no prodrome, immediate full recovery | Arrhythmic syncope — Holter, patch, then implantable loop recorder |
| Falls on head turning or while shaving | Carotid sinus hypersensitivity — massage with ECG/BP monitoring |
| Lateral tongue bite, post-event confusion for an hour | Seizure |
| Dizzy on standing, systolic drop 25 mmHg at 3 min | Orthostatic hypotension — review antihypertensives, sedatives, volume |
| Wants a plan to prevent further falls at home | Balance-challenging exercise ≥3 h/week, ongoing, plus multifactorial assessment |
| Multifocal wearer falling on kerbs and stairs | Single-vision distance glasses outdoors; expedite first-eye cataract surgery |
| Colles fracture after a stand-height fall, woman aged 62 | Minimal-trauma fracture = osteoporosis; treat, don't wait for DXA |
| Height loss 4 cm, new thoracic kyphosis | Occult vertebral fracture — lateral thoracolumbar imaging/VFA |
| New thigh/groin pain, 6 years of alendronate | Atypical femoral fracture — image both femurs |
| Shortened, externally rotated, abducted leg, cannot weight-bear | Hip fracture — analgesia/nerve block, surgery within 48 h, orthogeriatric care |
| Cannot weight-bear after fall, X-ray normal | Occult #NOF — MRI (or CT) |
| Displaced intracapsular #NOF in an independent, mobile patient | Total hip replacement (hemiarthroplasty if less fit) |
| Grip weak, gait 0.6 m/s, 6 kg unintentional weight loss | Sarcopenia/frailty — resistance exercise plus protein 1.0–1.5 g/kg/day |
| Ankle oedema 6 weeks after amlodipine, JVP normal, chest clear | CCB-induced oedema — reduce/switch, do not add frusemide |
| Symmetrical parkinsonism weeks after an antiemetic | Drug-induced parkinsonism — cease metoclopramide/prochlorperazine |
| New urge incontinence after starting donepezil | Cascade — do not add oxybutynin; review the ChEI |
| AKI on ramipril + indapamide + newly added ibuprofen | Triple whammy — stop the NSAID |
| Frail 91-year-old on a statin for primary prevention, prognosis <2 years | Deprescribe — time to benefit 2–5 years |
| Confusion, myoclonus and seizures on pethidine, eGFR 25 | Norpethidine accumulation — never use pethidine in older people |
| Hypoglycaemia, falls and confusion on glibenclamide | Long-acting sulfonylurea — cease; relax HbA1c to ~8% |
| Nursing home "diarrhoea", intermittent, patient constipated | Overflow from faecal impaction — PR and disimpaction, not loperamide |
| Leaks on coughing, small volumes, no urge | Stress incontinence — supervised pelvic floor muscle training |
| Sudden undeferrable urge, nocturia, large volumes | Urge incontinence — bladder retraining; check PVR before any antimuscarinic |
| Dribbling, poor stream, palpable bladder, high PVR | Overflow — relieve obstruction; stop anticholinergics |
| Continent in hospital, wet at home where the toilet is upstairs | Functional incontinence — OT, commode, clothing, lighting |
| Non-blanchable erythema over the sacrum, skin intact | Stage 1 pressure injury — offload, reposition, support surface |
| Heel wound with stable dry adherent eschar | Leave it alone; do not debride |
| Nursing home resident refusing food for 2 days | Acute change — hunt delirium, pain, mouth, drugs, impaction before anything else |
| Cachectic patient, phosphate falls on day 2 of feeding | Refeeding syndrome — thiamine, slow rate, replace electrolytes daily |
| Fearful, defers to a son who answers for her and blocks private review | Elder abuse — see alone, interpreter not family, 1800 ELDERHelp |
| Delirious patient refusing an operation | Treat the delirium first, then reassess — capacity is time-specific |
| Capacitous patient refusing life-saving amputation | Refusal binds you — document capacity, do not override |
| Son with financial power of attorney asked to consent to surgery | No authority — find the health substitute decision-maker |
| Frail patient wants to go home to a house you consider unsafe | Dignity of risk — minimise, don't eliminate; document capacity |
| End-stage frailty, family ask "do everything" | Recommend, don't offer a menu; CPR non-beneficial, other treatment continues |
| Breathless, dying, SpO₂ 96% on air | Fan, positioning, low-dose opioid — oxygen only helps the hypoxic |
| Noisy respiratory secretions in the last hours | Hyoscine butylbromide/glycopyrrolate early, reposition, no routine suction |
| Death 3 weeks after a hip fracture from a fall | Injury-related — generally reportable to the coroner |

#### 6.2 Signature findings — the single fact that clinches it

| Finding | What it means |
|---|---|
| Inattention (fails months backwards) | Delirium — the cardinal feature |
| Visual hallucinations + fluctuating cognition + parkinsonism | Dementia with Lewy bodies |
| REM sleep behaviour disorder years before cognitive change | Lewy body disease |
| Dementia >1 year after established motor Parkinson's | Parkinson's disease dementia (one-year rule) |
| Stepwise decline with focal neurological signs | Vascular dementia |
| Personality change in a 58-year-old with preserved memory | Behavioural-variant FTD |
| Gait + urinary + cognitive triad, gait first | Normal pressure hydrocephalus |
| Patient complains loudly of memory; family unconcerned | Depression, not dementia |
| Recall improves with cueing | Depression (pseudodementia) — encoding intact |
| Shortened, externally rotated, abducted leg | Fractured neck of femur |
| Facial injury with no protective hand injuries | Syncope, not a mechanical trip |
| Flat pulse during a postural BP drop | Autonomic failure or beta-blockade |
| Asystole ≥3 s on carotid sinus massage | Cardioinhibitory carotid sinus hypersensitivity — pacing |
| Hyponatraemia weeks after starting an SSRI | Drug-induced SIADH |
| Ankle oedema after a calcium-channel blocker | Vasodilator oedema — not heart failure |
| New parkinsonism after metoclopramide | Drug-induced parkinsonism |
| Visual halos, nausea and bradyarrhythmia | Digoxin toxicity (check K⁺, Mg²⁺, renal function) |
| Rebound vertebral fractures after stopping a drug | Denosumab cessation without a follow-on agent |
| Prodromal thigh pain on long-term antiresorptive | Atypical femoral fracture |
| Normal creatinine in a frail 55 kg 88-year-old | Concealed low CrCl — calculate Cockcroft–Gault |
| Normal albumin in a visibly wasted patient | Albumin measures inflammation, not nutrition |
| IADL loss before ADL loss | The earliest, most sensitive functional marker — screen cognition |
| Intact purple/maroon discolouration over the sacrum | Deep tissue injury |
| Bruising of inner arms, trunk and ears of different ages | Physical elder abuse |

#### 6.3 Confusable pairs

| Pair | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|
| **Delirium vs dementia** | Hours–days and fluctuating with impaired attention and altered consciousness, vs months–years, progressive, attention and consciousness preserved until late |
| **Delirium vs depression** | Inattention and clouded consciousness vs intact attention if engaged, clear sensorium, mood-congruent content; delirium worse at night, depression worse in the morning |
| **Dementia vs depression** | Attitude to deficits: dementia conceals, confabulates, tries hard; depression complains, gives "I don't know", poor effort but benefits from cueing |
| **Hypoactive delirium vs depression** | Both quiet and withdrawn — but delirium has acute onset, fluctuation and *inattention*; depression has weeks–months of anhedonia with testable attention |
| **Alzheimer's vs vascular dementia** | Smooth insidious episodic-memory-first decline vs stepwise course, vascular risk factors, focal signs, executive/processing loss with early gait and urinary change |
| **DLB vs Parkinson's disease dementia** | The one-year rule — dementia before or within 1 year of parkinsonism = DLB; dementia >1 year after established motor PD = PDD. Same pathology, different label |
| **DLB vs Alzheimer's** | DLB fluctuates markedly, has formed visual hallucinations, spontaneous parkinsonism, RBD and neuroleptic sensitivity; AD has steady progression with early amnesia and no early motor signs |
| **Frontotemporal dementia vs psychiatric illness** | FTD is progressive with loss of empathy, hyperorality, ritualistic behaviour and frontal atrophy in a 50s–60s patient; primary psychiatric illness is episodic, with insight and treatment response, and does not progress on imaging |
| **MCI vs early dementia** | Function. MCI = objective decline with independence preserved; once IADLs fail, it is dementia |
| **NPH vs Parkinson's disease** | NPH gait is wide-based, magnetic and shuffling **with** early urinary incontinence and ventriculomegaly, and arms are spared; PD is asymmetrical with rest tremor, rigidity, small-stepped festinant gait and levodopa response |
| **Pseudodementia vs dementia** | Short datable history, mood change precedes cognition, poor effort, cueing helps, cognition recovers with antidepressant — versus years of decline, confabulation and no cueing benefit |
| **Fall vs syncope vs seizure** | Mechanical fall: trip trigger, no LOC, hands out, immediate recovery. Syncope: seconds of LOC, pale/limp, facial injury, oriented within seconds. Seizure: aura, 1–3 min, lateral tongue bite, post-ictal confusion for minutes–hours |
| **Orthostatic vs vasovagal vs cardiac syncope** | Orthostatic: on standing, drop ≥20/10 within 3 min, drug-related. Vasovagal: emotional/pain/micturition trigger with long autonomic prodrome. Cardiac: exertional or supine, **no prodrome**, palpitations, family history of sudden death, abnormal ECG |
| **Osteoporosis vs osteomalacia vs Paget's** | Osteoporosis: normal calcium/phosphate/ALP, low T-score, fragility fracture. Osteomalacia: low vitamin D, low calcium/phosphate, **high ALP**, bone pain, proximal myopathy. Paget's: isolated markedly high ALP with normal calcium, focal bone pain/deformity |
| **Osteopenia vs osteoporosis on DXA** | T −1.0 to −2.5 vs T ≤ −2.5 — but a minimal-trauma fracture over 50 makes the diagnosis osteoporosis whatever the T-score; in the osteopenic range use FRAX/Garvan to decide treatment |
| **Urge vs stress vs overflow vs functional incontinence** | Urge: sudden undeferrable urge, large volumes, nocturia. Stress: leak on cough/lift, small volumes, no urge. Overflow: hesitancy, dribbling, palpable bladder, high PVR. Functional: normal tract, can't get there — mobility, cognition, environment |
| **Constipation with overflow vs infective diarrhoea** | Overflow: liquid stool past a hard plug, loaded rectum on PR, no fever, opioids/immobility. Infective: acute, febrile, other cases on the ward, empty rectum. Loperamide is the wrong answer in the first |
| **Frailty vs comorbidity vs disability** | Frailty = loss of reserve, so a small stressor causes disproportionate decline (CFS/Fried). Comorbidity = a count of diseases. Disability = established functional dependence. They overlap, but CFS is not for stable long-term disability |
| **Advance care directive vs statement of preferences** | An instructional directive is a specific consent to or refusal of nominated treatment — **legally binding** when valid and applicable; a values/preferences statement guides interpretation but does not of itself bind |
| **Capacity vs competence vs guardianship** | Capacity is the clinical, decision- and time-specific functional assessment (understand/retain/weigh/communicate); competence is the legal conclusion a court reaches; guardianship is a tribunal *appointment* of a substitute for a defined class of decisions |
| **EPOA (financial) vs medical/personal decision maker** | Financial power covers money, bills and property only; treatment consent needs the personal/health instrument (enduring guardian, EPOA personal/health, medical treatment decision maker). Read the actual document |
| **Not-for-CPR vs not-for-active-treatment** | NFR limits **only** attempted resuscitation at arrest — antibiotics, fluids, transfusion, NIV and surgery all continue unless separately limited. Conflating them causes real harm |
| **Double effect vs voluntary assisted dying** | Double effect: intention is symptom relief, dose proportionate and titrated, death foreseen but not the means — lawful everywhere. VAD: a distinct patient-initiated statutory process with eligibility criteria and independent assessments; not palliative care |
| **Delirium tremens vs delirium from other causes** | DT is withdrawal at day 2–4 with prominent tremor, sweating, tachycardia, fever and vivid hallucinations — the one delirium where a benzodiazepine (oxazepam/lorazepam if frail or hepatic) is the treatment rather than the cause |

#### 6.4 The must-not-miss list

| Trap / time-critical item | Correct action |
|---|---|
| Benzodiazepine given for undifferentiated delirium | Wrong — treat the cause plus non-pharmacological measures; benzos only for alcohol/benzo withdrawal |
| Antipsychotic in Lewy body dementia or PD | Contraindicated — severe neuroleptic sensitivity; low-dose short-acting benzodiazepine if sedation is unavoidable |
| "Unexplained mechanical fall" with amnesia for the event | Treat as syncope — ECG in everyone, postural BP, echo if murmur, loop recorder if recurrent |
| Denosumab stopped, delayed or lapsed | Rebound vertebral fractures — keep the 6-monthly interval; if stopping, transition immediately to a bisphosphonate |
| Unwitnessed long lie after a fall | Rhabdomyolysis/AKI, hypothermia, pressure injury, aspiration — CK, fluids, skin check, and change the discharge plan |
| Hip fracture waiting on surgery | Nerve block and analgesia now, surgery within 48 h, mobilise day 1 unrestricted, delirium prevention, bone protection before discharge |
| Treating a patient as lacking capacity because the decision is unwise | Not a capacity test — presume capacity, assess the four functional elements, document, respect dignity of risk |
| PEG feeding requested in advanced dementia | No survival, aspiration, pressure injury or functional benefit — careful hand feeding and a goals-of-care conversation |
| Elder abuse missed behind a controlling companion | See the patient alone, professional interpreter, assess capacity and undue influence, 1800 ELDERHelp, police/tribunal/SIRS as indicated |
| Refeeding a chronically underfed patient at full rate | Thiamine before and during feeding, start low, daily phosphate/potassium/magnesium |
| New hyponatraemia in a patient started on an SSRI, thiazide or tramadol | Drug-induced — stop or change the culprit; check sodium at baseline and ~2 weeks |
| Withholding anticoagulation in AF because of falls | Not justified — manage the falls; treat the bleeding risk factors, not the indication |
| Valid advance care directive overridden by distressed family | The directive prevails — family have no authority to override it |

#### 6.5 Numbers, tools and cut-offs worth memorising

| Figure | Value |
|---|---|
| 4AT | ≥4 possible delirium; 1–3 possible cognitive impairment |
| CAM positive | Acute onset/fluctuation **and** inattention, **plus** disorganised thinking **or** altered consciousness |
| Cognitive screening on presentation | Within 24 h, age ≥65 (≥45 for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people) |
| MMSE / MoCA | <24 abnormal / <26 abnormal (+1 if ≤12 years' education) |
| RUDAS / KICA-Cog | ≤22 of 30 / ≤33 remote, ≤35 urban–regional of 39 (screen from 45) |
| GDS-15 | 0–4 normal, 5–9 mild, ≥10 moderate–severe |
| Delirium first-line drug doses (if unavoidable) | Haloperidol 0.5 mg; risperidone 0.25–0.5 mg; olanzapine 2.5 mg — single dose, reassess |
| Cognitive reassessment after delirium | 4–6 weeks |
| Risperidone for BPSD (PBS) | Up to 12 weeks with review |
| Citalopram over 65 | Max 20 mg/day (QT) |
| SSRI review points | Response 4–6 weeks; continue ≥12 months after remission; sodium at baseline and ~2 weeks |
| Falls incidence | ~30% of >65s, ~50% of >80s per year; ~5% of falls fracture |
| Long lie | >1 hour on the floor |
| Hip fracture mortality | ~25% within 12 months |
| Orthostatic hypotension | Systolic ≥20 or diastolic ≥10 mmHg within 3 min (≥30 systolic if supine hypertension); measure at 1 and 3 min after ≥5 min supine |
| Timed Up and Go / gait speed | ≥12 s flags impaired mobility; <0.8 m/s = slowness |
| Carotid sinus hypersensitivity | Asystole ≥3 s and/or systolic fall ≥50 mmHg; no massage within 3 months of stroke/TIA/MI or with a bruit |
| Falls-prevention exercise | Balance-challenging, ~3 h/week, ongoing — rate ↓~23% (balance ~24%, combined ~34%, Tai Chi ~19%) |
| DXA T-score | Normal ≥ −1.0; osteopenia −1.0 to −2.5; osteoporosis ≤ −2.5. Z ≤ −2.0 = below expected for age |
| Screening DXA | Medicare-funded from age 70; rescreen ~5-yearly if T ≥ −1.5, ~2-yearly if −1.5 to −2.5 |
| Treat in the osteopenic range | T −1.5 to −2.5 **plus** FRAX major ≥20% or hip ≥3% |
| Steroid risk threshold | Prednisolone ≥7.5 mg/day for ≥3 months |
| Calcium / vitamin D | 1000 mg/day (1300 mg women >50, men >70); supplement only 500–600 mg. 25(OH)D >50 nmol/L; 800–1000 IU/day |
| Bisphosphonate dosing | Alendronate 70 mg weekly; risedronate 35 mg weekly; zoledronic acid 5 mg IV over ≥15 min 12-monthly; upright, fasting, nil by mouth ≥30 min |
| Bisphosphonate renal limits | Zoledronic acid eGFR <35; avoid orals below ~30 |
| MRONJ / atypical femoral fracture | ~1 in 10,000–100,000 patient-years / ~1 in 10,000 patient-years, rising after 5 years |
| Bisphosphonate drug holiday | **Reassess after 5 years oral / 3 years IV**; §3.5’s "3–6 years" is the trial-evidence range for cessation not altering fracture risk — same idea, quote the 5/3 |
| Denosumab | 60 mg SC 6-monthly; a missed dose within 4–6 weeks; rebound fractures <3%, mainly if treated >3 years |
| Teriparatide / romosozumab | 20 microgram SC daily, max 18 months lifetime (PBS T ≤ −3.0) / 210 mg SC monthly for 12 months; avoid within 12 months of MI or stroke |
| Hip fracture targets | Surgery within 48 h; mobilise day 1 with no weight-bearing restriction |
| Sarcopenia/frailty nutrition | Protein 1.0–1.5 g/kg/day with progressive resistance exercise |
| Polypharmacy | ≥5 regular medicines (hyperpolypharmacy ≥10); ~36% of Australians ≥70; ~90% in RACFs |
| Anticholinergic Cognitive Burden | Total ≥3 is the usual concern threshold |
| PPI review | Beyond 8 weeks without indication |
| Digoxin | Avoid >125 microgram/day |
| Frail HbA1c target | ~8% (64 mmol/mol) rather than 7% |
| Time to benefit | Statin ~2–5 years; bisphosphonate 3–6 years |
| Falls vs anticoagulation | ~295 falls/year needed to offset warfarin's stroke reduction |
| Benzodiazepine taper / steroid taper threshold | 10–25% every 1–2 weeks, slower at the end / any course >3 weeks |
| Fried phenotype | Weight loss >5%/yr, exhaustion, weak grip, slow gait, low activity: 1–2 pre-frail, ≥3 frail |
| Clinical Frailty Scale | 9 levels, scored at baseline ~2 weeks pre-illness; ≥5 commonly triggers geriatrics referral |
| Inpatient CGA | RR ~1.06 alive and in own home at 3–12 months; RR ~0.80 residential care admission |
| Support at Home | Replaced Home Care Packages from 1 Nov 2025; 8 ongoing levels |
| Malnutrition screen | MST ≥2 = at risk → dietitian |
| Elder abuse prevalence | ~15% past year; psychological commonest ~12%, financial ~2%. 1800 ELDERHelp 1800 353 374 |
| Anticipatory subcut PRN (opioid-naive) | Morphine 2.5 mg 1-hourly (pain) or 1.5–2.5 mg 2-hourly (dyspnoea); metoclopramide 10 mg 6–8-hourly; haloperidol 0.5–1 mg; midazolam 2.5 mg 2-hourly; hyoscine butylbromide 20 mg 4-hourly or glycopyrronium 200 microgram 4-hourly |
| Opioid conversions | Oral:subcut morphine ≈3:1; reduce 25–50% when rotating; breakthrough = 1/6 of the 24 h dose |
| Morphine and renal function | Avoid below eGFR 30 — fentanyl or hydromorphone |
| Voluntary assisted dying | Operating in all six states and the ACT (from 3 Nov 2025); NT the exception |

#### ⚠ Inconsistencies found in Sections 1–5

1. **Commonest form of elder abuse.** Section 4.9 (citing AIFS 2021) says **psychological** is commonest (~12% vs ~2% financial) and warns that "financial commonest" reflects helpline caseloads, not prevalence. Section 5.5 states "financial abuse is commonest, often by the EPOA holder". These cannot both stand; 4.9 is the better-sourced claim, but the conflict is left visible rather than silently resolved.

2. **Bisphosphonate drug-holiday timing.** Section 2.7 says reassess after **5 years oral / 3 years IV**; Section 3.5 says cessation after **3–6 years** does not alter fracture risk. Compatible in spirit, different figures — quote 2.7's if asked for one number.
