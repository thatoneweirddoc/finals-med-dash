## Skin Infections and Infestations

### Finals Tutor — Skin Infections and Infestations

#### Bacterial

**Impetigo** — *Staph aureus* ± *Strep pyogenes*. **Golden/honey-coloured crusts**, highly contagious, common in children. Localised: topical mupirocin. Widespread: oral flucloxacillin. Exclude from school until crusts resolve or 24 hours of treatment.

**Cellulitis** — spreading infection of deep dermis and subcutis. Hot, tender, erythematous, poorly demarcated, often unilateral lower leg with a portal of entry (tinea pedis, ulcer, wound). *Strep pyogenes* and *Staph aureus*. Treat with **flucloxacillin**.

**Erysipelas** — more superficial, **sharply demarcated raised border**, usually *Strep pyogenes*, often facial.

**The most useful discriminator**: bilateral "cellulitis" is almost never cellulitis — consider **venous eczema/stasis dermatitis**, which is the commonest misdiagnosis and does not improve with antibiotics.

**Necrotising fasciitis** — surgical emergency. **Pain out of proportion to appearance**, rapid progression, systemic toxicity, crepitus, skin necrosis, bullae, anaesthesia over the area. Management is **urgent surgical debridement** plus broad-spectrum antibiotics and resuscitation — imaging must not delay theatre.

**Staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome** — toxin-mediated, in young children: fever, widespread erythema, superficial peeling, **positive Nikolsky sign**, mucosa spared (distinguishing it from SJS/TEN).

#### Viral

**Herpes zoster (shingles)** — reactivation of varicella-zoster in a **dermatomal** distribution, unilateral, not crossing the midline, with prodromal pain then grouped vesicles on erythematous base. Start **antivirals within 72 hours** to reduce severity and post-herpetic neuralgia.

Two important variants:
- **Herpes zoster ophthalmicus** — V1 involvement; **Hutchinson sign** (vesicles on the nose tip, nasociliary branch) predicts ocular involvement → urgent ophthalmology
- **Ramsay Hunt syndrome** — geniculate ganglion: facial palsy with vesicles in the ear canal, ± hearing loss and vertigo

**Herpes simplex** — grouped vesicles on an erythematous base, recurrent at the same site, prodromal tingling. **Eczema herpeticum** as above is the emergency form.

**Molluscum contagiosum** — poxvirus; dome-shaped **umbilicated** pearly papules in children; self-limiting.

**Viral warts** — HPV; verrucous papules with thrombosed capillaries (black dots) on paring — distinguishes from a corn.

**Hand, foot and mouth disease** — coxsackievirus; oral ulcers with vesicles on palms, soles and buttocks.

#### Fungal

**Tinea** — dermatophyte. **Annular, scaly plaque with an active raised advancing edge and central clearing**; asymmetric. Diagnose with skin scrapings for microscopy and culture.

**Tinea incognito** — the classic trap: tinea treated with a topical corticosteroid loses its scale and defined edge, becomes more extensive and atypical, and appears to "improve then worsen". If a "rash" worsens on steroids, scrape it.

Treat: topical antifungal for localised skin; **oral terbinafine** for scalp (tinea capitis — always oral), nails, or extensive disease.

**Pityriasis versicolor** — *Malassezia*. Hypo- or hyperpigmented finely scaly macules on trunk, more obvious after sun exposure. Topical antifungal shampoo.

**Candida** — moist flexures, beefy red with **satellite lesions**; consider diabetes and immunosuppression.

#### Infestations

**Scabies** — *Sarcoptes scabiei*. **Intense itch, worse at night**, with **burrows** in finger webs, wrists, axillae, periumbilical, genitals; **spares the head in adults** (but not in infants). Itch is a delayed hypersensitivity response, so it **persists for 2–4 weeks after successful treatment** — patients must be told this or they return thinking treatment failed.

Treat with **permethrin 5% cream**, applied neck-down (whole body including head in infants and elderly), left 8–14 hours, **repeated in 7 days**. **Treat all household and close contacts simultaneously**, and wash linen in hot water. Oral ivermectin for crusted scabies or outbreaks.

**Crusted (Norwegian) scabies** — massive mite burden in immunosuppressed or elderly patients, thick hyperkeratotic crusts, highly contagious, may have little itch. This is a significant issue in remote Aboriginal communities in Australia and warrants combined topical plus oral ivermectin.

**Head lice** — live lice or viable nits; treat with topical insecticide or physical (dimeticone) agents, repeat at 7 days.
