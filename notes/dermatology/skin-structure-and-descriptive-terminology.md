## Skin Structure and Descriptive Terminology

### Dermatology Lecture (Dr John Schibeci) — Skin Structure

About **70% of rashes are caused by 10 conditions** — pattern recognition of a small set carries most of the clinical load.

**Epidermis**
- Turnover from basal membrane to stratum corneum takes about **60 days**. In **psoriasis** this is markedly accelerated, and the keratin produced is structurally poor
- Keratin functions as the **barrier**
- **Melanocytes** sit near the basal layer, arise from neuroectoderm, and inject melanosomes into keratinocytes via dendrites. Clumps of melanocytes form a **mole** — babies are not born with moles, and very few moles become melanoma. Melanin provides UV protection
- **Stratum lucidum** lies between corneum and granulosum, present only in thick (palmar/plantar) skin
- **Merkel cells** — mechanoreceptors; malignant transformation gives **Merkel cell carcinoma**, typically a pink lesion
- **Langerhans cells** — antigen-presenting cells derived from monocytes, near the basal layer, ~800 per mm². Malignancy gives **mycosis fungoides**, a cutaneous T-cell lymphoma that mimics dermatitis despite the "fungal" name

Antibodies against the basement membrane separate dermis from epidermis → **bullous pemphigoid**.

**Dermis**
- Provides strength — mainly **collagen**, with **elastin**
- Contains sweat glands, hair follicles, erector pili (smooth muscle), nerves
- Two vascular plexuses with anastomoses — blood is shunted superficially or deeply for thermoregulation

### Finals Tutor — Descriptive Terminology

Describing a lesion accurately is what earns marks; the diagnosis often follows from the description.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Macule** | Flat, <1 cm, colour change only |
| **Patch** | Flat, >1 cm |
| **Papule** | Raised, <1 cm |
| **Plaque** | Raised, flat-topped, >1 cm |
| **Nodule** | Raised, >1 cm, deeper |
| **Vesicle** | Fluid-filled, <1 cm |
| **Bulla** | Fluid-filled, >1 cm |
| **Pustule** | Pus-filled |
| **Wheal** | Transient oedematous papule/plaque (urticaria) |
| **Scale** | Visible flakes of stratum corneum |
| **Crust** | Dried exudate |
| **Erosion** | Loss of epidermis only — heals without scar |
| **Ulcer** | Loss through to dermis or deeper — scars |
| **Lichenification** | Thickened skin with accentuated markings, from chronic rubbing |
| **Excoriation** | Scratch marks |
| **Purpura** | Non-blanching, from extravasated blood |

**Systematic description**: Site and distribution → Size → Shape → Surface (scale, crust) → Colour → Border → Consistency → Secondary change. Then examine nails, scalp, mucosa, and hair.

**Distribution matters diagnostically**: extensor surfaces (psoriasis), flexural (atopic dermatitis in older children/adults), photodistributed (drug or lupus), dermatomal (shingles), symmetric (endogenous), asymmetric/bizarre (exogenous — contact or infection).
