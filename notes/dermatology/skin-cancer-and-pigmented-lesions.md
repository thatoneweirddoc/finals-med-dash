## Skin Cancer and Pigmented Lesions

### Finals Tutor — Skin Cancer and Pigmented Lesions

Australia has among the highest skin cancer incidence in the world — this is high-yield for Australian finals, and a genuine clinical priority.

#### Basal cell carcinoma

The **commonest** skin cancer. Locally invasive, essentially never metastasises, but destroys local tissue if neglected.

Appearance: **pearly, translucent papule or nodule with rolled edges, arborising (branching) telangiectasia**, often with central ulceration ("rodent ulcer"). Sun-exposed sites — head and neck especially. Superficial BCC on the trunk appears as a scaly erythematous patch mimicking eczema or psoriasis, but with a fine "thread-like" pearly rim.

Treatment: surgical excision with margin; **Mohs micrographic surgery** for high-risk sites (central face, periocular, nose, ear) or recurrent disease; imiquimod or 5-FU for superficial lesions; curettage; radiotherapy where surgery is unsuitable.

#### Squamous cell carcinoma

Arises from keratinocytes; **can metastasise** (unlike BCC), especially lip, ear, and immunosuppressed patients — a key discriminator.

Appearance: firm, hyperkeratotic or crusted nodule or plaque, often tender, sometimes ulcerated, on chronically sun-exposed skin. May arise in chronic wounds, scars or sinuses (**Marjolin ulcer**).

**Precursors**: **actinic (solar) keratosis** — rough, scaly, "sandpaper" macules on sun-damaged skin, small individual risk of transformation; **Bowen disease** — SCC in situ, a well-demarcated scaly erythematous plaque.

Treatment: excision with margin; consider imaging and node assessment in high-risk lesions.

**Keratoacanthoma** — rapidly growing dome-shaped nodule with a central keratin plug that may involute spontaneously; treated as a well-differentiated SCC because they cannot be reliably distinguished.

#### Melanoma

The one that kills. **Prognosis depends primarily on Breslow thickness** — depth of invasion in millimetres — which is why early diagnosis and *excisional* biopsy matter.

**ABCDE**: **A**symmetry, **B**order irregularity, **C**olour variation, **D**iameter >6 mm, **E**volution (change). Evolution/change is the most sensitive single feature.

The **"ugly duckling" sign** — the lesion that looks different from the patient's other naevi — is often more useful than ABCDE in someone with many moles.

**Subtypes**: superficial spreading (commonest), nodular (rapid vertical growth, worse prognosis, may be amelanotic), lentigo maligna melanoma (chronically sun-damaged skin in the elderly), acral lentiginous (palms, soles, subungual — the type least related to sun exposure and most easily missed).

**Diagnosis**: **excisional biopsy with a 2 mm margin** for a suspicious pigmented lesion — do **not** do a shave or punch biopsy of suspected melanoma, since it prevents accurate Breslow measurement. Then wide local excision with margins determined by Breslow thickness, with sentinel node biopsy considered for staging in intermediate-thickness lesions.

**Risk factors**: fair skin (Fitzpatrick I–II), high naevus count, atypical/dysplastic naevi, personal or family history, intermittent intense sun exposure and sunburn especially in childhood, solarium use, immunosuppression.

**Nail sign**: **Hutchinson sign** — pigment extending onto the proximal nail fold from a longitudinal melanonychia — suggests subungual melanoma.

#### Dermoscopy in brief

Dermoscopy improves diagnostic accuracy over naked-eye examination in trained hands.

Reassuring: symmetric pigment network, regular globules, uniform structure.
Concerning: **atypical network, blue-white veil, irregular streaks/pseudopods, regression structures, atypical vascular pattern, multiple colours**.

BCC-specific: **arborising vessels**, blue-grey ovoid nests, leaf-like areas, absent pigment network.

Seborrhoeic keratosis: **milia-like cysts, comedo-like openings**, "stuck-on" appearance, cerebriform surface — benign and extremely common, but a frequent source of anxiety and misdiagnosis.

#### Prevention

Sun protection (SunSmart: slip, slop, slap, seek, slide), avoid solaria, skin self-examination, and targeted surveillance for high-risk individuals. Australia has no population-based screening program, but high-risk patients warrant structured surveillance, often with dermoscopy and total-body photography.
