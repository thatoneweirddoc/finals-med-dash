## Metabolic Bone Disease


### MSK & Rheumatology Notes — Metabolic Bone Disease

*(MSK/fragility-fracture angle — the calcium-axis biochemistry lives in the endocrine notes.)*

#### 7.1 Osteoporosis

**Reduced bone mass and microarchitectural deterioration → fragility fractures.** Bone **quantity** falls but **quality (mineralisation) is normal** — contrast with osteomalacia below.

**A fragility fracture = a fracture from a fall from standing height or less** (classic sites: **vertebrae, hip/NOF, distal radius/wrist, proximal humerus**). ⚑ *Any fragility fracture in an older adult should trigger osteoporosis assessment, whatever the DEXA.*

**Risk factors — "SHATTERED FAMILY":** **S**teroids, **H**yperthyroid/hyperparathyroid, **A**lcohol/smoking, **T**hin (low BMI), **T**estosterone low, **E**arly menopause, **R**enal/liver failure, **E**rosive/inflammatory disease (RA), **D**iabetes/**D**ietary calcium low; plus age, female, family history, prior fracture.

**Diagnosis:** **DEXA** scan → **T-score** (SD vs young adult):

| T-score | Category |
|---|---|
| ≥ −1.0 | Normal |
| −1.0 to −2.5 | **Osteopenia** |
| **≤ −2.5** | **Osteoporosis** |
| ≤ −2.5 + fragility fracture | Established (severe) osteoporosis |

**FRAX** (or QFracture) estimates **10-year fracture probability** to guide who to treat/scan.
**Bloods are typically normal** (Ca, PO₄, ALP all normal) — abnormal bone biochemistry points elsewhere (osteomalacia, Paget's, myeloma, hyperparathyroidism).

**Management:** lifestyle (weight-bearing exercise, stop smoking, reduce alcohol, calcium/vitamin D repletion, falls prevention). **First-line pharmacological: oral bisphosphonate (alendronate/risedronate)**. ⚑ Anabolic options for severe/very-high-risk disease: **romosozumab** (anti-sclerostin, builds bone) and **teriparatide** (PTH analogue); **denosumab** (anti-RANKL) as an alternative — ⚑ **do not stop denosumab abruptly** (rebound vertebral fractures — transition to a bisphosphonate). HRT/raloxifene in selected women. (Drug detail in Section 8.3.)

#### 7.2 Osteomalacia & rickets

**Defective mineralisation of osteoid** (soft bones), usually from **vitamin D deficiency** (or hypophosphataemia). **Rickets** is the same process in the growing child (before physeal fusion).
- **Clinical:** **bone pain and tenderness, proximal myopathy (waddling gait), fragility fractures**; children → bowed legs, costochondral "rickety rosary", growth failure.
- **Biochemistry (contrast to osteoporosis):** **low vitamin D, low/normal calcium, low phosphate, HIGH ALP, high PTH (secondary hyperparathyroidism)**.
- **Radiology:** **Looser's zones (pseudofractures)** — the buzz finding.
- **Management:** vitamin D (± calcium) replacement; treat the cause.

#### 7.3 Paget's disease of bone

**Disordered, accelerated bone turnover** (overactive osteoclasts then chaotic osteoblasts) → structurally weak, enlarged, deformed bone. Older patients; often **asymptomatic, found via isolated raised ALP**.
- **Clinical:** **bone pain, deformity (bowed tibia, skull enlargement — "hat no longer fits"), warmth over bone, fractures, deafness** (VIII compression), **secondary OA**; rarely **high-output cardiac failure** and **osteosarcoma** (sudden pain increase).
- **Biochemistry:** ⚑ **isolated markedly raised ALP with NORMAL calcium and phosphate** (the classic pattern). X-ray/bone scan: lytic + sclerotic, cortical thickening.
- **Management:** **bisphosphonates** (esp. IV zoledronate) for pain/active disease; analgesia.

#### 7.4 Osteonecrosis (avascular necrosis, AVN)

**Death of bone from interrupted blood supply.** Sites/causes to know:
- **Femoral head** (after **displaced intracapsular NOF fracture**, steroids, alcohol, **sickle cell disease**, SLE, the "bends"), **scaphoid proximal pole**, **talus**, humeral head, femoral condyles.
- **Investigation:** X-ray may be normal early → **MRI is the most sensitive test**; later X-ray shows sclerosis, subchondral collapse ("crescent sign").
- ⚑ **Bisphosphonate/denosumab-related osteonecrosis of the jaw** and atypical femoral fractures — a drug-related form to be aware of when counselling.

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators — Metabolic Bone

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Fragility fracture + T-score ≤ −2.5, **normal Ca/PO₄/ALP** | **Osteoporosis** |
| Bone pain + proximal myopathy + **low Ca/PO₄, high ALP/PTH**, Looser's zones | **Osteomalacia** |
| **Isolated raised ALP, normal Ca/PO₄**, bowed tibia, deafness | **Paget's disease** |
| Hip/scaphoid/talus pain, X-ray normal, MRI shows necrosis | **Avascular necrosis** |
| First-line osteoporosis drug / anabolic options | **Bisphosphonate** / romosozumab, teriparatide ⚑ |
| Drug you must not stop abruptly (rebound fractures) | **Denosumab** ⚑ |
