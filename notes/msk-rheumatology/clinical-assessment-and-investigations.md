### MSK & Rheumatology Notes — Clinical Assessment & Investigations

*Sources: Kumar & Clark, Oxford Handbook of Clinical Medicine, StatPearls, Arthritis Research UK / Versus Arthritis REMS-GALS, BSR guidance. Autoantibody associations cross-checked against Robbins and UpToDate-style summaries. Discriminators **bolded**.*

#### 3.1 History and the screening examination

##### Inflammatory vs mechanical — the first fork
Almost every rheumatology history hinges on separating **inflammatory** from **mechanical/degenerative** pain. Get this and the differential collapses.

| Feature | **Inflammatory** (RA, spondyloarthropathy, crystal) | **Mechanical** (OA, injury) |
|---|---|---|
| Morning stiffness | **Prolonged, >30–60 min** | Brief (<30 min) |
| Effect of activity | **Better with use** ("warms up") | **Worse with use**, better with rest |
| Effect of rest | Worse (stiffens up) | Better |
| Night pain / waking | Common | Uncommon (except severe OA) |
| Swelling | **Soft-tissue, warm** (synovitis) | Bony (osteophyte) |
| Systemic features | Fatigue, weight loss, fever, rash | Absent |
| Response to NSAIDs | Often marked | Partial |

**Memory hook:** *inflammatory joints hate rest and love movement; mechanical joints are the opposite.*

##### Pattern-spotting: distribution
- **Symmetrical small-joint polyarthritis** → **RA** (also SLE, viral).
- **Asymmetrical large-joint / lower-limb oligoarthritis** → **spondyloarthropathy** (reactive, psoriatic, AS).
- **Axial (spine/sacroiliac) inflammatory back pain in a young adult** → **axial spondyloarthritis**.
- **Monoarthritis, acutely hot** → **septic until proven otherwise**, then crystal (gout/pseudogout).
- **Migratory** → rheumatic fever, gonococcal, some viral.

##### GALS and REMS
- **GALS screen** (Gait, Arms, Legs, Spine) — the rapid MSK screening exam: *"Any pain or stiffness in your muscles, joints or back? Can you dress/wash yourself fully? Can you walk up and down stairs?"* + inspection of gait, arms, legs and spine.
- **REMS** (Regional Examination of the MSK System) — the structured **Look – Feel – Move – (Special tests / function)** approach applied to a region. Always compare sides; assess active then passive movement; screen the joint above and below.

##### Systemic and extra-articular clues (the "it's more than a joint" flags)
Uveitis/red eye (spondyloarthropathy, sarcoid), psoriasis/nail pitting (PsA), oral/genital ulcers (Behçet's, reactive), Raynaud's (connective tissue disease), rash (SLE, dermatomyositis, vasculitis), sicca (Sjögren's), ENT/sinus disease + haemoptysis (GPA), recent GI/GU infection (reactive arthritis).

#### 3.2 Blood tests and the acute-phase response

**ESR vs CRP** — both rise in inflammation but they are not interchangeable:

| | **CRP** | **ESR** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Acute-phase protein (hepatic, IL-6 driven) | Rate red cells settle (surrogate for fibrinogen/immunoglobulins) |
| Speed | **Rises and falls fast** (hours–days) | **Slow** to rise and fall (days–weeks) |
| Confounders | Fairly specific to inflammation/infection | Raised by **age, anaemia, pregnancy, paraproteins**; can be very high in myeloma |
| Classic use | Infection, most inflammation | **GCA/PMR, SLE activity** |

⚑ *Useful discordance:* in **active SLE**, **ESR is high but CRP is often normal/only mildly raised** — a rising CRP in a lupus patient should prompt a search for **infection** or serositis rather than being assumed to be a flare.

**Other core bloods:** FBC (anaemia of chronic disease, cytopenias in SLE, eosinophilia in EGPA), U&E (renal involvement — lupus nephritis, vasculitis), LFTs (baseline for hepatotoxic DMARDs), **urate** (gout — but may be normal during an acute attack), **bone profile** (Ca/PO₄/ALP), **CK** (grossly raised in inflammatory myopathy/rhabdomyolysis), vitamin D, urinalysis (an essential, cheap screen for renal involvement — **blood + protein/casts = glomerulonephritis until proven otherwise**).

#### 3.3 Autoantibodies and immunology *(the highest-yield table in rheumatology)*

**How to use antibodies:** they are **specific but not always sensitive**, and several are for **classification not diagnosis**. Order them to confirm a clinical suspicion, not to screen a well patient.

| Antibody | Strongest association | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **RF (rheumatoid factor)** | RA (sensitive, **not specific** — also Sjögren's, cryoglobulinaemia, chronic infection, healthy elderly) | Positive RF ≠ RA |
| **Anti-CCP (anti-citrullinated peptide)** | **RA — specific**, predicts erosive disease | The RA-defining antibody |
| **ANA (antinuclear antibody)** | **SLE screen** (sensitive, not specific); also many CTDs, some healthy people | **Entry criterion** for SLE classification |
| **Anti-dsDNA** | **SLE — specific**; tracks activity/nephritis | Rises in flare |
| **Anti-Sm (Smith)** | **SLE — specific** (but insensitive) | Confirmatory |
| **Anti-Ro (SSA) / Anti-La (SSB)** | **Sjögren's**; SLE; **neonatal lupus / congenital heart block** in pregnancy | Ro also in subacute cutaneous lupus |
| **Anti-Scl-70 (topoisomerase)** | **Diffuse** systemic sclerosis | ILD risk |
| **Anti-centromere** | **Limited** systemic sclerosis (CREST) | Pulmonary hypertension risk |
| **Anti-Jo-1 (anti-synthetase)** | **Polymyositis/dermatomyositis** | Antisynthetase syndrome (ILD, mechanic's hands) |
| **Anti-U1-RNP** | **Mixed connective tissue disease** (high titre) | Overlap features |
| **c-ANCA / anti-PR3** | **Granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA)** | ENT + lung + kidney |
| **p-ANCA / anti-MPO** | **MPA and EGPA** | Pulmonary–renal, eosinophilic |
| **Antiphospholipid** (lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin, anti-β2GPI) | **Antiphospholipid syndrome** | Thrombosis + pregnancy loss; **paradoxically prolonged APTT** |

**Complement (C3/C4):** **consumed (low) in active SLE** and in immune-complex disease → a **low complement + high anti-dsDNA** picture = active lupus (especially nephritis). **Memory hook:** *in lupus, complement goes down as the disease goes up.*

#### 3.4 Synovial fluid, imaging and biopsy

##### Joint aspiration (arthrocentesis) — the reflex investigation of the acute hot joint
**Any acutely hot, swollen, painful joint must be aspirated before antibiotics** (unless it's a prosthetic joint → refer to orthopaedics; don't aspirate a prosthesis in ED). Send fluid for:

1. **Cell count + differential** — the septic-vs-inflammatory gradient (there is overlap, so it's supportive not absolute):

| Appearance / WCC | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Clear, WCC <2,000/mm³, low PMN | **Non-inflammatory** (OA, trauma) |
| Cloudy/yellow, WCC ~2,000–50,000 | **Inflammatory** (RA, crystal, spondyloarthropathy) |
| Turbid/purulent, **WCC >50,000 (often >100,000), PMN >90%** | **Septic arthritis** (but lower counts don't exclude it) |
| Blood-stained (haemarthrosis) | Trauma/ACL, haemophilia, PVNS |

2. **Gram stain + culture** — the definitive septic-arthritis test (may be negative even when infected).
3. **Polarised-light microscopy for crystals:**

| Crystal | Shape | Birefringence | Disease |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Monosodium urate** | **Needle-shaped** | **Negatively** birefringent (yellow when parallel) | **Gout** |
| **Calcium pyrophosphate** | **Rhomboid** | **Positively** birefringent | **Pseudogout (CPPD)** |

**Memory hook:** *"goutt" needles are **N**egative; **P**seudogout is **P**ositive and **P**laty/rhomboid.* ⚑ *Crystals and infection can coexist — finding crystals does not exclude sepsis.*

##### Imaging

| Modality | Best for | Signature findings |
|---|---|---|
| **Plain X-ray** | OA vs RA, fractures | **OA = LOSS** (Loss of joint space, Osteophytes, Subchondral Sclerosis, Subchondral cysts); **RA = symmetrical joint-space loss, periarticular osteopenia, marginal erosions, soft-tissue swelling** (LESS mnemonic) |
| **Ultrasound** | Synovitis, effusion, guided aspiration, **GCA** | **"Halo sign"** around temporal artery in GCA; power-Doppler synovitis |
| **MRI** | **Cord/cauda equina compression**, **sacroiliitis** (early axSpA), osteomyelitis, marrow oedema | Sacroiliac bone-marrow oedema; the emergency spine scan |
| **DEXA** | Bone density (osteoporosis) | **T-score ≤ −2.5 = osteoporosis** (see Section 6/endocrine) |
| **CT** | Complex fractures, staging | — |
| **Temporal artery biopsy** | GCA confirmation | **Granulomatous inflammation, giant cells; skip lesions → don't let a negative biopsy overrule strong clinical GCA** ⚑ |

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators — Assessment

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Morning stiffness >60 min, better with use | **Inflammatory** arthritis |
| Symmetrical small joints (MCP/PIP), spares DIP | **RA** |
| Asymmetrical lower-limb + enthesitis + HLA-B27 | **Spondyloarthropathy** |
| High ESR but normal CRP in a young woman | **Active SLE** (and a rising CRP → think infection) |
| First and only test in the acute hot joint | **Joint aspiration** (before antibiotics) |
| Synovial WCC >50,000, >90% PMN | **Septic arthritis** (but lower doesn't exclude) |
| Needle-shaped, negatively birefringent crystals | **Gout** (MSU) |
| Rhomboid, positively birefringent crystals | **Pseudogout** (CPPD) |
| Radiographic LOSS | **Osteoarthritis** |
| Antibody specific for RA / for SLE | **Anti-CCP** / **anti-dsDNA or anti-Sm** |
| c-ANCA/PR3 vs p-ANCA/MPO | **GPA** vs **MPA/EGPA** |
| Low C3/C4 + high anti-dsDNA | Active **lupus** (nephritis) |
| Negative temporal artery biopsy but classic GCA | **Still treat** — skip lesions; biopsy is not 100% sensitive |

### Orthopaedics (SNEHA) — Examination of radiographs — Clinical Assessment & Investigations

AP and lateral films 🡪 look at each radiograph from a wide to medium to close up look.

- Bone – normal shape, size, contour or are they thicker or thinner than normal, shorter/longer or abnormally curved
- Joints – bony components in correct alignment or displaced / angled
- Bone texture – paget’s disease, avascular necrosis, osteoporosis, new bone formation.
- Contours of bone – contours of bine, noting any abnormality en route. Make a list on an anatomical base: assess ligamentous attachments, joint margins, the joint space and the cortical and cancellous bone elements.
- Comparison films
- Oblique projections
- Localized views
- Stress films - radiograph of the joint in use.

#### FURTHER INVESTIGATIONS

Additional screening tests include:

- ESR, CRP
- FBC with differential
- Rheumatoid factor and anti-CCP
- Serum calcium, phosphate and alp
- Serum uric acid
- Chest x-ray
- Synovial fluid aspiration for MCS, gram stain, micrscopy for monosodium urate crystals and pyrophosphate crystals.

Additional imaging techniques

- CT scans
- AP and lateral tomography
- MRI scans
- Ultrasound - fluid? Haemarthrosis, pyarthrosis.

Functional imaging techniques

- Technetium bone scans – 99mtc-methylene diphosphonate) 🡪 sensitive to areas of unusual bone rebuilding activity since it is taken up by osteoblast cells. 🡪 sensitive to fractures and bone tumors, including metastases
- SPECT (single-photon electron 🡪 better localization and assessment of an active area discovered by a technetium bone scan
- PET (flurodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography)

### Orthopaedics (SNEHA) — inflammatory joint diseases — Clinical Assessment & Investigations

Hyaline cartilage is composed of type 2 collagen, water, proteoglycans and chondrocytes.

- Collagen: resist tensile stresses and transmit force
- Water & proteoglycans: turgor and elasticity
- Chondrocytes: synthesis and digest matrix; secrete degrading enzymes in an inactive form and enrich matrix with enzyme inhibitors

Diseases that destroy articulate cartilage do so by activating catabolic enzyme, decreasing inhibitors 🡪 accelerated rate of matrix breakdown. Cytokines such as il-1 and TNF trigger the degradative process; their sources include chondrocytes, synoviocytes, fibroblasts, and inflammatory cells. Destruction of articular cartilage by indigenous cells is an important mechanism in many joint diseases.

### Rheumatology & Immunology (ANU) — PLASMA PROTEINS ASSOCIATED WITH INFLAMMATORY REACTIONS (play a role in innate immunity) — Clinical Assessment & Investigations

- ACUTE PHASE PROTEINS: liver releases 30 acute-phase reactants, including CRP, ferritin, fibrinogen, α1-antitrypsin, haptoglobin, procalcitonin, IL-6
- NOTABLE NEGATIVE ACUTE PHASE REACTANTS: albumin, transferrin, anti-thrombin
- COMPLEMENT SYSTEM: after stimulation from Ab-complexes by pathogens → compliment activation →enhance functions of antibodies and phagocytesClassical pathway: IgM or IgG binds to pathogen (via C1)
- Alternative pathway: direct activation by pathogen rather than Ab-Ag complexes (via C3)
- Causes: opsonisation (C3), bacteria lysis (C5-9) and activation of mast cells/granulocytes (C3/C4/C5)

- PRO-INFLAMMATORY CYTOKINESIFN-α: ↑MHC I expression
- IFN-β: stimulates NK cells and cytotoxic
- IFN-γ: activated macrophages
- TNF-α: pyrogenic, cytotoxic, mediates septic shock
- Interleukins: IL-1, 2, 4, 6
- Arachidonic acid derivatives: PGE2/PGI2 increases pain sensation, vasodilation, and inhibits platelet aggregation and  leukotrienes which cause chemotaxis, exudation and bronchoconstriction

### Rheumatology & Immunology (ANU) — systemic-onset juvenile idiopathic arthritis (still’s disease) — Clinical Assessment & Investigations

- Commonly affects children between the ages of 2–4 years
- Arthritis involving ≥ 1 joint
- Intermittent fever that lasts for at least 2 weeks with fever spikes occurring on at least 3 consecutive days
- ≥ 1 extra-articular manifestation (e.g., salmon-pink rash, lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly/hepatomegaly, serositis)
