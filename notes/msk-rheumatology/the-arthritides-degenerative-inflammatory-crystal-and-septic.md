### MSK & Rheumatology Notes — The Arthritides — Degenerative, Inflammatory, Crystal & Septic

---

#### 5.1 Osteoarthritis (OA)

The commonest joint disease — a **degenerative "wear-and-repair" disorder of cartilage** with secondary bone response, not purely "wear and tear" (there is an active, if failed, repair process).

**Risk factors:** age, female, obesity (knees), previous joint injury/surgery, occupation, genetics, malalignment.

**Distribution (a diagnosis in itself):** **DIP (Heberden's nodes), PIP (Bouchard's nodes), first carpometacarpal (thumb-base squaring), hips, knees, cervical/lumbar spine.** Typically **asymmetrical**, **mechanical** pain (worse with use, better with rest), **short-lived** morning stiffness (<30 min), bony (not soft) swelling, crepitus, reduced range.

**Radiograph — "LOSS":** **L**oss of joint space (asymmetric), **O**steophytes, **S**ubchondral **S**clerosis, subchondral cysts. (Contrast with RA below.)

**Management (ladder):** education, weight loss, exercise/physiotherapy → topical NSAIDs (esp. knee/hand) and paracetamol (limited efficacy) → oral NSAIDs (lowest dose, gastroprotection) → intra-articular steroid for flares → **joint replacement** for end-stage disease refractory to conservative measures. ⚑ *Newer guidance de-emphasises paracetamol monotherapy (small effect) and prioritises exercise and topical NSAIDs.*

#### 5.2 Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) ⚑

A **chronic, symmetrical, inflammatory synovitis** driven by autoimmunity; the synovium proliferates into an erosive **pannus**.

**Presentation:** insidious, **symmetrical polyarthritis of the small joints (MCP, PIP, wrists), sparing the DIPs**, with **prolonged early-morning stiffness (>30–60 min)**, soft-tissue (boggy) swelling, fatigue. Later **deformities:** ulnar deviation, **swan-neck**, **boutonnière**, Z-thumb, subluxation.

**Investigations:** **anti-CCP (specific, erosive-predictive)** and **RF**; raised ESR/CRP; anaemia of chronic disease; X-rays show **periarticular osteopenia, soft-tissue swelling, marginal erosions, symmetrical joint-space loss** (mnemonic contrast to OA). Ultrasound/MRI detect early synovitis/erosions.

**Classification — 2010 ACR/EULAR** (score ≥6/10 → definite RA): points from **joint involvement** (number/size of joints), **serology** (RF/anti-CCP), **acute-phase reactants** (CRP/ESR), and **symptom duration ≥6 weeks**. (Classification, not a bedside diagnostic rule — but the components are examinable.)

**Extra-articular / systemic RA (higher-yield than students expect):** rheumatoid nodules, **ILD** and pleural effusion (low glucose exudate), pericarditis, **anaemia**, secondary Sjögren's, scleritis/episcleritis, **Felty's syndrome** (RA + splenomegaly + neutropenia), **AA amyloidosis**, accelerated cardiovascular disease, carpal tunnel. ⚑ **Atlantoaxial subluxation** — cervical instability from erosion of the transverse ligament; **flag before intubation/anaesthesia** (risk of cord compression on neck extension).

**Management — treat-to-target (2021 ACR):**
- **Start a DMARD early** ("window of opportunity"); target **remission or low disease activity**, escalating until met.
- ⚑ **Methotrexate is the anchor DMARD** — first-line monotherapy for most (weekly, with **folic acid**; monitor FBC/LFTs; teratogenic, hepatotoxic, pneumonitis risk). Alternatives/additions: sulfasalazine, hydroxychloroquine, leflunomide.
- **Short-term glucocorticoids** as a bridge only.
- If target not met → **biologic (TNF inhibitor first-line class; also IL-6/tocilizumab, rituximab, abatacept)** or **targeted synthetic (JAK inhibitor)**.
- ⚑ **JAK-inhibitor safety signal (ORAL Surveillance trial):** increased **major adverse cardiovascular events, venous thromboembolism, serious infection (incl. herpes zoster) and malignancy** vs TNF inhibitors, especially in **older patients (≥65) and those with cardiovascular risk factors** — reserve accordingly. (Full pharmacology in Section 7.)

#### 5.3 Seronegative spondyloarthropathies

A family sharing: **RF-negative**, **HLA-B27** association, **enthesitis** (not synovitis) and **dactylitis** ("sausage digit"), **axial ± asymmetrical lower-limb** involvement, and extra-articular overlap — the **"SPINE-ACHE"** features: **anterior uveitis, psoriasis/skin, IBD/gut, aortic regurgitation, apical lung fibrosis, achilles enthesitis**. **Memory hook — the shared "A"s:** *Axial, Asymmetrical, Anterior uveitis, Aortic regurgitation, Apical fibrosis, Achilles enthesitis.*

##### Ankylosing spondylitis / axial spondyloarthritis
- **Young man, inflammatory back pain** (insidious, <45 yrs, morning stiffness, **better with exercise/worse with rest, night pain, buttock pain**), progressive spinal stiffness, **reduced chest expansion**, **reduced lumbar flexion (Schober's test)**.
- ⚑ **Nomenclature (2022 ASAS-EULAR):** **radiographic axial SpA (r-axSpA = classic ankylosing spondylitis)** when sacroiliitis is visible on X-ray, vs **non-radiographic axial SpA (nr-axSpA)** where MRI shows sacroiliitis but the X-ray is normal — same disease spectrum, MRI catches it earlier.
- **X-ray:** **sacroiliitis**, **"bamboo spine"** (syndesmophytes, squaring of vertebrae), enthesophytes.
- **Complications:** anterior uveitis (commonest extra-articular), aortic regurgitation, apical pulmonary fibrosis, atlantoaxial subluxation, osteoporosis/fracture.
- **Management (2022 ASAS-EULAR):** exercise/physiotherapy + **NSAIDs first-line**; if inadequate → **biologics: TNF inhibitor or IL-17 inhibitor**; **JAK inhibitor** an option. (No role for conventional DMARDs in purely axial disease.)

##### Psoriatic arthritis (PsA)
- Occurs in ~10–30% of psoriasis patients; **5 patterns:** asymmetrical oligoarthritis, symmetrical (RA-like), **DIP-predominant**, **arthritis mutilans** ("telescoping"/pencil-in-cup), and axial/spondylitic.
- **Nail disease (pitting, onycholysis)**, **dactylitis**, enthesitis; skin psoriasis may be subtle (scalp, umbilicus, natal cleft). X-ray: **"pencil-in-cup"** deformity, DIP erosions with new bone.
- Management: NSAIDs → csDMARDs (methotrexate, esp. with skin disease) → biologics (TNF, **IL-17, IL-23/IL-12-23**).

##### Reactive arthritis (formerly Reiter's)
- **Sterile** arthritis **1–4 weeks after** a **GI (Campylobacter, Salmonella, Shigella, Yersinia)** or **GU (Chlamydia)** infection.
- Classic triad: **"can't see, can't pee, can't climb a tree"** — **conjunctivitis/uveitis + urethritis + arthritis**; also keratoderma blennorrhagicum, circinate balanitis. Usually self-limiting; NSAIDs, treat underlying infection.

##### Enteropathic arthritis
- Peripheral (parallels IBD activity) or axial (independent) arthritis in **inflammatory bowel disease**.

#### 5.4 Crystal arthropathies

##### Gout ⚑
Deposition of **monosodium urate (MSU)** crystals from **hyperuricaemia**.
- **Acute:** sudden, exquisitely painful **monoarthritis**, classically **first MTP joint = podagra**; also mid-foot, ankle, knee. Triggers: alcohol, purine-rich food, dehydration, diuretics, starting/stopping urate-lowering therapy, trauma, illness.
- **Diagnosis:** **needle-shaped, negatively birefringent MSU crystals** on polarised microscopy of aspirate. Serum urate may be **normal during an acute attack** — recheck after it settles. Chronic → **tophi** (ear, joints, tendons) and erosions with **overhanging edges/"rat-bite"**.
- **Acute management:** **NSAID, colchicine, or corticosteroid** (choose by comorbidity — e.g. steroid or colchicine if renal impairment/PUD). ⚑ **Colchicine now dosed low** (e.g. 1 mg then 0.5 mg an hour later, then 0.5 mg 2–4×/day) — the old high-dose-to-diarrhoea regimen is abandoned. **Don't stop existing urate-lowering therapy during a flare.**
- **Urate-lowering therapy (ULT) — treat-to-target (2020 ACR):** ⚑ **indications: ≥2 flares/year, tophi, radiographic damage, or urate nephrolithiasis.** First-line **allopurinol** (xanthine oxidase inhibitor), **start low (100 mg/day; 50 mg if CKD) and titrate up**, with **NSAID/colchicine prophylaxis for the first 3–6 months** to prevent flare on initiation. **Target serum urate <0.36 mmol/L (<6 mg/dL)**, or **<0.30 mmol/L (<5 mg/dL) in tophaceous/severe disease**. ⚑ Consider **HLA-B*5801 testing before allopurinol** in high-risk groups (Han Chinese, Thai, Korean with CKD) — risk of severe cutaneous reaction (SJS/TEN). Second-line **febuxostat**.

##### Pseudogout (calcium pyrophosphate deposition, CPPD)
- **Calcium pyrophosphate** crystals → **rhomboid, positively birefringent**.
- Older patients; **knee and wrist** commonest; **chondrocalcinosis** (linear cartilage calcification) on X-ray. Associations: ⚑ **haemochromatosis, hyperparathyroidism, hypomagnesaemia, hypophosphatasia** (screen young/recurrent cases).
- Management: NSAIDs/colchicine/steroid for the acute attack; treat the underlying metabolic cause; no urate-lowering role.

**Gout vs pseudogout, side by side:**

| | **Gout** | **Pseudogout** |
|---|---|---|
| Crystal | Monosodium urate | Calcium pyrophosphate |
| Shape | **Needle** | **Rhomboid** |
| Birefringence | **Negative** | **Positive** |
| Classic joint | **1st MTP (podagra)** | **Knee, wrist** |
| X-ray | Erosions, overhanging edges, tophi | **Chondrocalcinosis** |
| Underlying | Hyperuricaemia, diuretics, alcohol | Age, **haemochromatosis, hyperparathyroidism, low Mg** |

#### 5.5 Septic arthritis & bone/joint infection

##### Septic arthritis *(emergency — the acute hot joint)* ⚑
Infection within a joint → cartilage destroyed within **days** if untreated.
- **Presentation:** acutely **hot, swollen, red, exquisitely painful joint the patient won't move**, ± fever; usually **monoarticular (knee > hip)**. Any acute hot joint is septic arthritis until proven otherwise — **including in a patient with known gout or RA** (crystal and infection coexist; an RA flare and joint sepsis look alike).
- **Organisms:** **Staphylococcus aureus** commonest overall; **Neisseria gonorrhoeae** in young sexually active adults (migratory arthritis, tenosynovitis, pustular rash); Gram-negatives in elderly/immunocompromised/IVDU; **Streptococci**.
- **Investigation:** ⚑ **urgent joint aspiration BEFORE antibiotics** — Gram stain, culture, WCC (>50,000 supports, but no count excludes), crystals; blood cultures; FBC/CRP/ESR/urate.
- **Management:** **IV antibiotics** (empirical anti-staph, e.g. flucloxacillin; per local policy and Gram stain) for ~2 weeks IV then oral (total ~4–6 weeks), plus **joint drainage/washout** (arthroscopic or open); analgesia; do not delay for imaging. **Prosthetic joint → refer to orthopaedics, do not aspirate in ED.**

##### Osteomyelitis
Infection of **bone**. **Acute** (haematogenous in children — metaphysis of long bones; *S. aureus*) or **chronic** (contiguous spread, diabetic foot, post-traumatic; may form a **sequestrum** [dead bone] and **involucrum** [new bone shell]).
- **Diabetic foot osteomyelitis:** suspect if **ulcer probes to bone** or is chronic/large; **MRI** is the imaging of choice; bone biopsy/culture guides therapy.
- **Vertebral osteomyelitis / discitis:** back pain, fever, raised inflammatory markers; **MRI**; think **IVDU, bacteraemia, TB (Pott's disease)**.
- **Management:** prolonged targeted antibiotics ± surgical debridement.

##### Prosthetic joint infection
Early (perioperative) vs late (haematogenous); pain, loosening, sinus; needs specialist debridement/revision + prolonged antibiotics.

**A condition that occurs when a joint begins to dislocate, the joint surfaces only partially lose contact**

CAUSE: trauma, acute injury, due to loose joints, or in patients with generalized joint laxity

PRESENTATION: may present with pain, change in mobility or may be asymptomatic.
