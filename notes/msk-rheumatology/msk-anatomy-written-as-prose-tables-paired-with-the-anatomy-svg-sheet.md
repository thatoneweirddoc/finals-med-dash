### MSK & Rheumatology Notes — MSK Anatomy (written as prose + tables; paired with the anatomy SVG sheet)

##### 2.1 The spine and the neuro-emergency landmarks

The vertebral column: **7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, sacrum (5 fused), coccyx**. The **spinal cord terminates at ~L1/L2** (conus medullaris); below that the lumbosacral nerve roots run as the **cauda equina** ("horse's tail"). **This single fact underlies two emergencies:**
- A lesion **above L1/L2** compresses the **cord** → **spinal cord compression** (UMN signs below the level, a sensory level).
- A lesion **below L1/L2** compresses the **cauda equina** (roots) → **cauda equina syndrome** (LMN signs, saddle anaesthesia, bladder/bowel).

**Dermatomes/myotomes worth memorising for OSCEs:**

| Level | Sensory landmark | Motor / reflex |
|---|---|---|
| C5 | Lateral upper arm | Shoulder abduction; biceps reflex |
| C6 | Thumb | Wrist extension / elbow flexion; **supinator reflex** |
| C7 | Middle finger | Elbow extension / finger extension; **triceps reflex** |
| C8/T1 | Little finger / medial forearm | Finger flexion / intrinsics |
| T4 / T10 | Nipple / umbilicus | (sensory level landmarks) |
| L3/L4 | Medial knee / medial shin | Knee extension; **knee jerk (L3/4)** |
| L5 | Dorsum of foot, big toe | **Big-toe dorsiflexion (EHL)**, foot drop |
| S1 | Lateral foot / sole | Plantarflexion; **ankle jerk (S1)** |
| S2–S4 | Perineum / **saddle** | Anal tone, bladder — **the cauda equina territory** |

**Nerve root (radiculopathy) vs peripheral nerve** — root lesions follow a dermatome/myotome and often a reflex; peripheral nerve lesions follow that nerve's cutaneous + motor territory.

##### 2.2 Shoulder and upper limb

**Rotator cuff (SITS)** — the dynamic stabilisers of the glenohumeral joint:

| Muscle | Action | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **S**upraspinatus | Initiates **abduction** (0–15°) | Empty-can test; **most commonly torn/impinged** |
| **I**nfraspinatus | External rotation | Resisted ER |
| **T**eres minor | External rotation | — |
| **S**ubscapularis | Internal rotation | Gerber's lift-off |

The **subacromial space** (between the acromion above and the cuff/humeral head below) is where **impingement** and subacromial bursitis occur.

**The signature peripheral nerve palsies** (very high yield):

| Nerve | Roots | Common injury | Deficit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Axillary** | C5–6 | Shoulder dislocation, surgical neck of humerus | Deltoid weakness, **regimental-badge** sensory loss |
| **Radial** | C5–T1 | **Mid-shaft humerus** fracture, "Saturday night palsy" | **Wrist drop**, sensory loss dorsal hand |
| **Median** | C6–T1 | **Carpal tunnel** (wrist), supracondylar # | Thenar wasting, LOAF muscles, **thumb/index/middle** sensory loss |
| **Ulnar** | C8–T1 | Medial epicondyle, elbow | **Claw hand**, wasting of interossei/hypothenar, medial 1½ digits |

##### 2.3 Hip, knee and lower limb

**Hip** — a ball-and-socket joint with a clinically critical blood supply: the femoral head is fed largely by the **medial and lateral circumflex femoral arteries running retrograde up the femoral neck**. **A displaced intracapsular neck-of-femur fracture tears these vessels → avascular necrosis** → why displaced intracapsular fractures in the elderly are usually replaced (arthroplasty) rather than fixed (Section 4).

**Knee** — stabilised by four ligaments and two menisci:

| Structure | Injury clue |
|---|---|
| **ACL** | Non-contact pivot, "pop", immediate **haemarthrosis**, positive Lachman |
| **PCL** | Dashboard injury (posterior force) |
| **MCL** | Valgus stress |
| **Menisci** | Twisting on a loaded knee, locking, joint-line tenderness |

**Common peroneal (fibular) nerve** winds around the **fibular neck** — superficial there → damaged by fibular neck fracture, plaster casts, or crossing legs → **foot drop** + sensory loss dorsum of foot (mimics an L5 radiculopathy — the discriminator is the distribution and the mechanism).

##### 2.4 Hand, wrist and the small joints

The hand is where rheumatology and orthopaedics both live. **Joint distribution is a diagnosis in itself:**

| Joints affected | Suggests |
|---|---|
| **DIP** (+ nodes) | **OA** (Heberden's nodes) or **psoriatic arthritis** |
| **PIP + MCP**, symmetrical, **spares DIP** | **Rheumatoid arthritis** |
| **PIP** nodes | **OA** (Bouchard's nodes) |
| **First carpometacarpal (thumb base)** | **OA** (squaring of the thumb base) |
| **First MTP (big toe)** | **Gout** (podagra) |

**The scaphoid** — the wrist's version of the femoral neck: **retrograde blood supply enters distally**, so a **proximal-pole fracture threatens avascular necrosis and non-union**. Suspect it with **anatomical snuffbox tenderness** after a fall on the outstretched hand (FOOSH), even if the initial X-ray is normal — treat and re-image (Section 4).

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators — Anatomy

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Cord ends at which level; what's below | **L1/L2**; cauda equina (roots) |
| Saddle anaesthesia + bladder dysfunction localises to | **Cauda equina** (S2–S4 roots) |
| Rotator cuff muscle usually torn / initiates abduction | **Supraspinatus** |
| Wrist drop after mid-shaft humerus fracture | **Radial nerve** |
| Foot drop after fibular neck injury | **Common peroneal nerve** (mimics L5) |
| RA joint distribution vs OA distribution | RA: **MCP/PIP, spares DIP, symmetrical**; OA: **DIP/PIP/1st CMC** |
| Snuffbox tenderness after FOOSH, normal X-ray | Treat as **scaphoid fracture** (AVN/non-union risk) |
| Displaced intracapsular NOF fracture risk | **AVN** (retrograde femoral head supply) |
