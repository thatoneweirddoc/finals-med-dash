## Endocrine Anatomy


### Endocrine Master Notes — Endocrine Anatomy

*Written out as prose + tables (not image-only) — pair with `endocrine_anatomy_sheet.svg`.*

#### 2.1 Hypothalamus and Pituitary

The **pituitary (hypophysis)** sits in the **sella turcica** of the sphenoid bone, connected to the hypothalamus by the **infundibulum (stalk)**. Two critical neighbours make pituitary pathology a clinical emergency of *location*:
- **Optic chiasm** sits directly **above** the gland → an expanding pituitary mass compresses the crossing nasal fibres → **bitemporal hemianopia** (a superior-quadrant "upper temporal" defect first, as the chiasm is pushed from below).
- **Cavernous sinuses** flank it, carrying the **internal carotid** and cranial nerves **III, IV, V1, V2, VI** → lateral extension or apoplexy causes **ophthalmoplegia** (CN III palsy classic).

Two functionally and embryologically distinct lobes:

| Lobe | Origin | Nature | Blood supply | Hormones |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Anterior (adenohypophysis)** | Rathke's pouch (oral ectoderm) | Glandular | **Hypophyseal portal veins** (not direct arterial) | GH, TSH, ACTH, LH, FSH, prolactin |
| **Posterior (neurohypophysis)** | Neuroectoderm (downgrowth of hypothalamus) | Neural | Direct arterial | **Stores** ADH, oxytocin (made in hypothalamus) |

**Discriminator:** the anterior pituitary's **portal (not arterial) blood supply** is why it is vulnerable to ischaemia after post-partum haemorrhage → **Sheehan syndrome** (the gland enlarges in pregnancy, then infarcts when perfusion drops).

#### 2.2 Thyroid and Parathyroids

The **thyroid** has two lobes joined by an **isthmus** (over the 2nd–4th tracheal rings), invested in pretracheal fascia so it **moves up with swallowing** — the exam sign that a neck lump is thyroid. Key surgical relations:
- **Recurrent laryngeal nerves** run in the tracheo-oesophageal groove behind the gland → damage in thyroidectomy → **hoarse voice** (unilateral) or airway compromise (bilateral).
- **External branch of the superior laryngeal nerve** (to cricothyroid) runs with the superior thyroid artery → injury changes voice pitch (the "opera-singer" nerve).

**Embryology:** the thyroid descends from the **foramen caecum** at the tongue base down the **thyroglossal duct** → a persistent duct gives a midline **thyroglossal cyst** (moves up on tongue protrusion) and failed descent gives a **lingual thyroid**. *Memory: thyroglossal cyst rises with tongue protrusion; thyroid lump rises with swallowing.*

**Parathyroids** — usually **four** (two superior, two inferior), tiny, on the posterior thyroid. Embryology explains their variable position and a common exam trap:
- **Superior** parathyroids ← **4th pharyngeal pouch** (more constant position).
- **Inferior** parathyroids ← **3rd pharyngeal pouch**, migrating **with the thymus** → can end up ectopic in the mediastinum/thymus (relevant when a missing adenoma is hunted at surgery).

#### 2.3 Adrenal Glands and Endocrine Pancreas

The **adrenal (suprarenal) glands** cap the kidneys. Cortex and medulla have separate origins (cortex = mesoderm; medulla = neural crest) and separate jobs:

| Layer | Zone | Product | Regulator | Memory hook |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cortex** (outer) | **Glomerulosa** | **Aldosterone** (mineralocorticoid) | RAAS / K⁺ | **"Salt"** |
| | **Fasciculata** | **Cortisol** (glucocorticoid) | ACTH | **"Sugar"** |
| | **Reticularis** | **Androgens** (DHEA) | ACTH | **"Sex"** |
| **Medulla** (inner) | — | **Catecholamines** (adrenaline > noradrenaline) | Sympathetic (splanchnic) | **"The deeper you go, the sweeter it gets"** |

*Memory: **G-F-R = Salt, Sugar, Sex; the deeper you go, the sweeter (more valued) it gets.*** The medulla is effectively a modified sympathetic ganglion — hence phaeochromocytoma behaves like sympathetic overdrive. Adrenal **venous drainage is asymmetric**: right adrenal vein → IVC directly (short); left → left renal vein — relevant to **adrenal vein sampling**.

**Endocrine pancreas — islets of Langerhans** (~1–2% of pancreatic mass):

| Cell | Product | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **β (beta)** — central, most numerous | **Insulin** (+ C-peptide, amylin) | Lowers glucose |
| **α (alpha)** — peripheral | **Glucagon** | Raises glucose |
| **δ (delta)** | **Somatostatin** | Inhibits insulin, glucagon, GH, gut hormones |
| **PP/F cells** | Pancreatic polypeptide | Regulates pancreatic secretion |

**Discriminator:** **C-peptide** is co-secreted 1:1 with endogenous insulin — so it separates endogenous hyperinsulinism (insulinoma → **high** C-peptide) from **exogenous insulin** administration (suppressed C-peptide). Sulfonylurea abuse raises both (measure the drug screen).

#### 2.4 Gonads and Diffuse Endocrine Tissue

The **ovaries** (theca/granulosa cells → oestrogen, progesterone; regulated by LH/FSH) and **testes** (Leydig cells → testosterone under LH; Sertoli cells → inhibin/sperm under FSH) are endocrine as well as reproductive organs. The **pineal gland** secretes **melatonin** (circadian rhythm).

**Diffuse neuroendocrine system:** scattered **enterochromaffin/neuroendocrine cells** in the gut and bronchi are the origin of **carcinoid/neuroendocrine tumours** (serotonin and other peptides) and are the conceptual link to **MEN syndromes** (Section 10) — multiple neuroendocrine tissues sharing a genetic driver.

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators — Section 2

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Visual defect from a pituitary mass | **Bitemporal hemianopia** (chiasm compression) |
| Cranial nerve palsy in pituitary apoplexy | **CN III** (cavernous sinus) |
| Anterior pituitary blood supply | **Hypophyseal portal veins** (why Sheehan's occurs) |
| Neck lump that moves on **swallowing** | Thyroid |
| Neck lump that moves on **tongue protrusion** | Thyroglossal cyst |
| Nerve at risk in thyroidectomy → hoarse voice | **Recurrent laryngeal** nerve |
| Adrenal cortex zones & products | **G-F-R = aldosterone, cortisol, androgens** ("Salt, Sugar, Sex") |
| Adrenal medulla product & origin | Catecholamines; **neural crest** |
| Superior vs inferior parathyroid origin | Superior = **4th** pouch; inferior = **3rd** pouch (migrates with thymus) |
| Distinguishes endogenous from injected insulin | **C-peptide** (high vs suppressed) |
| Islet cell secreting glucagon | **α cells** |
| Right adrenal vein drains into | **IVC directly** |
