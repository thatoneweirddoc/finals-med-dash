## Endocrine Pharmacology & Prescribing


### Endocrine Master Notes — Endocrine Pharmacology & Prescribing

*Consolidated drug section — the guaranteed pharmacology part of the spine. Mechanisms + the exam-critical adverse effects and prescribing rules.*

#### 11.1 Glucose-Lowering Therapies

| Drug / class | Mechanism | Key benefits | Key adverse effects / rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Metformin** (biguanide) | ↓hepatic gluconeogenesis, ↑insulin sensitivity | First-line, weight-neutral, **no hypoglycaemia**, CV-safe | GI upset, B12 deficiency; **lactic acidosis** — stop if eGFR <30 and during acute illness/contrast |
| **SGLT2 inhibitors** (-gliflozin) | Block renal glucose reabsorption (glycosuria) | **Cardiorenal protection**, weight loss, BP↓, no hypos | **Euglycaemic DKA**, genital/urinary infection, volume depletion, (rare) Fournier's; **sick-day: withhold when acutely unwell** ⚑ |
| **GLP-1 receptor agonists** (-tide: semaglutide, dulaglutide) | Incretin — ↑glucose-dependent insulin, ↓glucagon, ↓gastric emptying, satiety | **Weight loss**, CV benefit | Nausea/vomiting, (rare) pancreatitis; injectable (oral semaglutide exists); withhold peri-op per guidance |
| **Tirzepatide** | **Dual GIP/GLP-1** agonist | Greatest HbA1c + weight reduction ⚑ | As GLP-1 RA |
| **DPP-4 inhibitors** (-gliptin) | Prolong endogenous incretins | Oral, weight-neutral, no hypos | Modest; (rare) pancreatitis; avoid combining with GLP-1 RA |
| **Sulfonylureas** (gliclazide) | Close β-cell K-ATP channel → insulin release | Cheap, rapid, effective | **Hypoglycaemia** (prolonged), **weight gain** |
| **Pioglitazone** (TZD) | PPAR-γ → ↑insulin sensitivity | No hypos, durable | **Fluid retention/heart failure**, weight gain, fractures, bladder-cancer caution |
| **Insulin** | Replaces/augments insulin | Universally effective | **Hypoglycaemia**, weight gain; never omit basal in T1DM |

**Insulin types:** rapid (aspart/lispro), short (soluble/regular), intermediate (NPH/isophane), long-acting analogues (glargine, detemir, **degludec** ultra-long); pre-mixed. Regimens: basal–bolus, twice-daily mixed, basal-only (often added to orals in T2DM).

#### 11.2 Glucocorticoids and Thyroid/Pituitary Drugs

**Glucocorticoids** — relative potency (learn the ladder):

| Steroid | Approx. equivalent dose | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Hydrocortisone | 20 mg | Physiological replacement; used IV in crises |
| Prednisolone | 5 mg | Standard oral anti-inflammatory |
| Dexamethasone | 0.75 mg | Long-acting, minimal mineralocorticoid; used in tests/cerebral oedema |

*Adverse effects (Cushingoid): hyperglycaemia, hypertension, osteoporosis, weight gain, thin skin/bruising, cataracts/glaucoma, proximal myopathy, mood change, immunosuppression, peptic ulcer, adrenal suppression.* **Never stop long-term steroids abruptly** (risk of adrenal crisis) — taper; **sick-day rules** (double the dose in illness).

**Thyroid drugs:**
- **Carbimazole** (first-line antithyroid) — blocks thyroid peroxidase; **agranulocytosis** warning; teratogenic (aplasia cutis) → **use PTU in first trimester**. ⚑
- **Propylthiouracil (PTU)** — also blocks peripheral T4→T3; first-trimester and thyroid-storm agent; rare **hepatotoxicity**.
- **Levothyroxine** — titrate to TSH; interactions (iron/calcium/PPIs reduce absorption — separate dosing); dose **rises in pregnancy**.

**Pituitary/posterior drugs:** **dopamine agonists** (cabergoline/bromocriptine — prolactinoma; adverse: nausea, postural hypotension, impulse-control disorders, fibrosis); **somatostatin analogues** (octreotide/lanreotide — acromegaly, NETs; adverse: gallstones, GI upset); **pegvisomant** (GH-receptor antagonist); **desmopressin** (cranial DI — watch hyponatraemia); **GH** replacement.

#### 11.3 Bone, Adrenal and Other Agents

- **Bisphosphonates** (alendronate/risedronate/zoledronate) — inhibit osteoclasts; take upright/fasting; **oesophagitis, osteonecrosis of the jaw, atypical femoral fractures, acute-phase reaction (IV)**; correct vitamin D/calcium first.
- **Denosumab** (RANKL monoclonal) — 6-monthly SC; **do not stop abruptly** (rebound vertebral fractures). ⚑
- **Romosozumab** (sclerostin antibody), **teriparatide/abaloparatide** (PTH analogues, anabolic) — very-high-risk osteoporosis; teriparatide contraindicated in Paget's/prior skeletal radiotherapy.
- **Mineralocorticoid-receptor antagonists**: **spironolactone** (Conn's, also causes gynaecomastia) and the more selective **eplerenone**.
- **Phaeochromocytoma blockade**: **α-blocker first** (phenoxybenzamine/doxazosin) **then β-blocker** — never the reverse.
- **Cortisol-synthesis inhibitors**: **metyrapone, ketoconazole** (medical control of Cushing's).
- **Fludrocortisone** — mineralocorticoid replacement in Addison's.

#### 11.4 Prescribing and Safety

- **Steroid sick-day rules & withdrawal:** double glucocorticoid in intercurrent illness, parenteral if vomiting, never stop long-term steroids suddenly, steroid emergency card.
- **Drug-induced endocrine disease (common exam links):** steroids → Cushing's/diabetes; **amiodarone/lithium** → thyroid dysfunction; **thiazides** → hyperglycaemia/hyponatraemia/hypercalcaemia; antipsychotics/metoclopramide → hyperprolactinaemia; **checkpoint inhibitors** → autoimmune endocrinopathies (hypophysitis, thyroiditis, adrenalitis, type 1 diabetes). ⚑
- **Peri-operative endocrine management:** stress-dose steroids for adrenal insufficiency/long-term steroid users; euglycaemia and a variable-rate insulin infusion for insulin-dependent diabetics; α-blockade optimisation before phaeochromocytoma surgery; euthyroid before elective thyroid surgery.
