## Diabetes & Endocrine Emergencies (acute — very high yield)


### Endocrine Master Notes — Diabetes & Endocrine Emergencies (acute — very high yield)

#### 9.1 Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) ⚑

**Who:** usually **type 1** (new presentation or insulin omission/illness); can occur in type 2, and **euglycaemic DKA** with SGLT2 inhibitors. Precipitants: infection, missed insulin, MI, new diagnosis.

**Pathophysiology:** absolute insulin deficiency + high counter-regulatory hormones → lipolysis → **ketone (β-hydroxybutyrate) overproduction** → **high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis**, osmotic diuresis → dehydration and total-body **potassium depletion** (despite a normal/high initial serum K⁺).

**Diagnostic criteria — note the guideline shift:** ⚑

| | UK JBDS (traditional) | 2024 international consensus |
|---|---|---|
| Glucose | >11 mmol/L (or known diabetes) | **≥11.1 mmol/L (≥200 mg/dL)** or known diabetes |
| Ketones | **capillary ketones ≥3.0** mmol/L or urine ≥2+ | **β-hydroxybutyrate ≥3.0 mmol/L** (or urine ≥2+) |
| Acidosis | **bicarbonate <15** mmol/L and/or pH <7.3 | **bicarbonate <18** and/or **pH <7.3** |
| Anion gap | — | **removed** from criteria |

*Key change: β-hydroxybutyrate (not urine ketones) is the preferred measure; the anion gap has been dropped; severity is now graded by ketone/pH level. Quote the JBDS numbers for a UK/Australian exam but recognise the 2024 update.*

**Management (JBDS "fix the fluids, insulin, potassium"):**
1. **IV 0.9% saline** — restore circulating volume first (a litre stat if hypotensive; then a structured rehydration regimen).
2. **Fixed-rate IV insulin infusion at 0.1 units/kg/hour.** Continue the patient's **long-acting basal insulin**. ⚑
3. **Potassium** — add to fluids once K⁺ is known and urine is flowing; insulin drives K⁺ into cells (monitor closely — hypokalaemia is the dangerous iatrogenic complication).
4. **Add glucose (e.g. 10% dextrose)** when glucose falls **<14 mmol/L** so insulin can continue clearing ketones without hypoglycaemia.
5. Monitor ketones/bicarbonate/pH to confirm **resolution** (ketones <0.6, pH >7.3, bicarbonate >15/18); convert to subcutaneous insulin only when eating and ketones cleared. Find and treat the precipitant.

**Complications:** **hypokalaemia** (commonest iatrogenic, potentially fatal), **cerebral oedema** (especially children — avoid over-rapid fluids), aspiration, VTE, ARDS.

#### 9.2 Hyperosmolar Hyperglycaemic State (HHS)

**Who:** older **type 2** patients, often with intercurrent illness; develops over days.

**Features/criteria:** **marked hyperglycaemia (often >30 mmol/L)**, **high osmolality (>320 mosmol/kg)**, **profound dehydration**, and **NO significant ketosis/acidosis** (pH >7.3, bicarbonate >15, ketones <3) — because residual insulin suppresses ketogenesis. Altered consciousness correlates with osmolality.

**Management — gentler than DKA:**
- **Fluid (0.9% saline) is the mainstay**; replace slowly to avoid rapid osmotic shifts (cerebral oedema, central pontine myelinolysis).
- **Insulin only after fluids** (often lower/later than in DKA; start ~**0.05 units/kg/h** if ketones present or glucose not falling with fluid alone). ⚑
- **High VTE risk → prophylactic (sometimes therapeutic) anticoagulation.** Watch potassium. Higher mortality than DKA.

**Discriminator:** **DKA = acidosis + ketones, younger/type 1, hours; HHS = hyperosmolar, minimal ketones, older/type 2, days, higher mortality, more cautious insulin/fluids.**

#### 9.3 Hypoglycaemia

**Definition:** **Whipple's triad** — low plasma glucose (**<3.0 mmol/L** clinically significant; <4.0 "treat"), consistent symptoms, and relief on correction.
- **Autonomic** (early, glucose ~3.3): sweating, tremor, palpitations, hunger, anxiety.
- **Neuroglycopenic** (lower glucose): confusion, drowsiness, seizures, focal deficits, coma.
- **Causes:** insulin/**sulfonylurea** excess, missed meals, alcohol, exercise, renal/liver failure, Addison's, **insulinoma** (endogenous — high insulin **and** high C-peptide).

**Treatment ladder:**
- **Conscious, able to swallow:** 15–20 g fast-acting carbohydrate (glucose tablets/juice), recheck in 15 min, repeat, then a long-acting carbohydrate.
- **Impaired/unable to swallow:** **IM glucagon** (won't work in alcohol/liver failure or sulfonylurea-depleted glycogen) or **IV glucose (e.g. 100 mL 20% dextrose)**.
- **Sulfonylurea overdose:** prolonged — admit, may need **octreotide**. Recheck and treat the cause.

#### 9.4 Other Endocrine Emergencies (consolidated cross-reference)

| Emergency | One-line recognition | Immediate action |
|---|---|---|
| **Addisonian crisis** (§7) | Shock + ↓Na⁺/↑K⁺ + pigmentation | **IV hydrocortisone + saline** |
| **Thyroid storm** (§5) | Fever, tachyarrhythmia, delirium, thyrotoxic | β-blocker + PTU + iodine (after PTU) + hydrocortisone |
| **Myxoedema coma** (§5) | Hypothermia, bradycardia, ↓GCS, hypoventilation | IV levothyroxine + hydrocortisone + rewarm |
| **Severe hypercalcaemia** (§6) | Confusion, dehydration, short QT | **IV saline** then bisphosphonate |
| **Phaeochromocytoma crisis** (§7) | Severe paroxysmal hypertension, headache/sweating | α-blockade (**never β first**) |
| **Pituitary apoplexy** (§4) | Sudden headache, visual loss, ophthalmoplegia | **Urgent hydrocortisone** + neurosurgery |
| **Severe symptomatic hyponatraemia** (§4) | Seizures/↓GCS, Na⁺ very low | Hypertonic (3%) saline, correct slowly |

### Endocrinology (ANU) — insulin — Diabetes & Endocrine Emergencies (acute — very high yield)

Definition: anabolic peptide hormone produced and secreted from pancreatic β cells located to counteract hyperglycaemia

Synthesis: Preproinsulin (synthesised in RER) → cleavage of “pre-signal” → proinsulin (stored in secretory granules) → cleavage of proinsulin → exocytosis of insulin and C-peptide equally

Function: primarily contributing to the generation of energy reserves and glycaemic control

- Carbohydrate metabolism: Insulin is the only hormone in the body that lowers the blood glucose level.
- Protein metabolism: stimulates protein synthesis
- Lipid metabolism: maintains a fat depot and has an antiketogenic effect
- Electrolyte regulation: stimulates intracellular potassium accumulation

Receptors

- Insulin-dependent glucose transporters:
- GLUT4: adipose tissue, striated muscle (exercise can also increase GLUT4 expression)
- Insulin-independent transporters (GLUT 1, 2, 3,5) :
- Mnemonic BRICK L: Brain, RBCs, Intestine, Cornea, Kidney, Liver
- Brain utilizes glucose for metabolism normally and ketone bodies during starvation
- RBCs utilize glucose because they lack mitochondria for aerobic metabolism.
- Glucose enters β cells → ↑ ATP generated from glucose metabolism → closes K+ channels (target of sulfonylureas) and depolarizes β cell membrane → voltage-gated Ca2+ channels open → Ca2+ infux and stimulation of insulin exocytosis
