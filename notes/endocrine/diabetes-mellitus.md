## Diabetes Mellitus


### Endocrine Master Notes — Diabetes Mellitus

#### 8.1 Classification and Diagnosis

| Type | Mechanism | Clues |
|---|---|---|
| **Type 1** | Autoimmune β-cell destruction → **absolute insulin deficiency** | Younger/lean, rapid onset, ketosis-prone, **GAD/IA-2/ZnT8 antibodies**, low C-peptide |
| **Type 2** | Insulin **resistance** + relative deficiency | Older/overweight, metabolic syndrome, gradual, family history |
| **LADA** | Slow autoimmune (type 1 in adults) | "Type 2" that fails oral agents fast; antibody-positive |
| **MODY** | Monogenic, autosomal dominant | Young, non-obese, strong AD family history, antibody-negative, C-peptide preserved |
| **Secondary** | Pancreatic (CF, pancreatitis, haemochromatosis), endocrine (Cushing's, acromegaly, phaeo), drug (steroids, thiazides), gestational | Context-dependent |

**Diagnostic thresholds** (WHO/NICE — confirm with a repeat unless symptomatic):

| Test | Diabetes | "Pre-diabetes" |
|---|---|---|
| **HbA1c** | **≥48 mmol/mol (6.5%)** | 42–47 mmol/mol (6.0–6.4%) |
| **Fasting glucose** | **≥7.0 mmol/L** | Impaired fasting glucose 6.1–6.9 |
| **2 h OGTT / random** | **≥11.1 mmol/L** | Impaired glucose tolerance 7.8–11.0 (OGTT) |

**Rules:** in an **asymptomatic** person you need **two abnormal results** (same or different tests); one abnormal result **plus symptoms** (osmotic symptoms, or a hyperglycaemic crisis) is enough. **HbA1c is unreliable** and should not be used when red-cell turnover is abnormal: **pregnancy, recent blood loss/transfusion, haemolysis, haemoglobinopathies, iron/B12 deficiency, CKD, recent-onset (rapid) type 1**. ⚑

#### 8.2 Chronic Management of Type 2 Diabetes ⚑

**Structure:** lifestyle (diet, weight loss, activity, smoking cessation) + **cardiorenal risk management** (BP, statin, address albuminuria) run alongside glucose-lowering. Modern therapy is chosen for **organ protection**, not glucose alone.

**The updated drug pathway (NICE NG28, revised):** ⚑
- **First-line is now dual therapy — metformin + an SGLT2 inhibitor** — offered from diagnosis (metformin titrated first, then the SGLT2i added), even without established cardiovascular disease. SGLT2i monotherapy if metformin is not tolerated.
- **If atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, heart failure, or high CV risk / CKD** → ensure an **SGLT2 inhibitor** is included (proven cardiorenal benefit); a **GLP-1 receptor agonist** is added/preferred where CVD risk or weight is the priority. NICE now supports early **triple therapy (metformin + SGLT2i + semaglutide)** in ASCVD.
- **GLP-1 RA** (e.g. semaglutide) or the dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist **tirzepatide** — strong HbA1c and weight benefit; consider where BMI is high or weight is a driver.
- **Insulin** when other measures fail to control (e.g. HbA1c persistently high, or symptomatic hyperglycaemia).

*This is the single biggest change from older texts: the old "metformin alone, then add" ladder has been replaced by **early combination and organ-protective drug choice**. Glucose target is **individualised** (a common default is 48 mmol/mol on lifestyle/metformin, 53 mmol/mol if on a drug that can cause hypoglycaemia).*

**Drug one-liners (full detail in Section 11):**

| Class | Benefit | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| **Metformin** | Weight-neutral, no hypos, first-line | GI upset; **lactic acidosis** risk in renal impairment; stop if eGFR <30 |
| **SGLT2 inhibitor** (-gliflozin) | **Cardiorenal protection**, weight loss, no hypos | **Euglycaemic DKA**, genital thrush/UTI, volume depletion; **sick-day: stop when acutely unwell** |
| **GLP-1 RA / tirzepatide** | Weight loss, CV benefit | Nausea, pancreatitis (rare); injectable (oral semaglutide exists) |
| **DPP-4 inhibitor** (-gliptin) | Weight-neutral, oral, no hypos | Modest effect; pancreatitis (rare) |
| **Sulfonylurea** (gliclazide) | Cheap, effective, rapid | **Hypoglycaemia, weight gain** |
| **Pioglitazone** | No hypos | Weight gain, **fluid retention/HF**, fractures, bladder-cancer caution |

#### 8.3 Type 1 Diabetes and Insulin

- **Basal–bolus (multiple daily injections):** long-acting basal (e.g. glargine/degludec) + rapid-acting bolus with meals (carbohydrate counting), or an **insulin pump (CSII)**; increasingly **hybrid closed-loop ("artificial pancreas")** systems. ⚑
- **Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM/flash)** is now standard of care in type 1. Aim for **"time in range"** as well as HbA1c.
- Never stop basal insulin in type 1, even when not eating (fasting/illness) — omission precipitates **DKA**.

#### 8.4 Chronic Complications

**Microvascular:**
- **Retinopathy** — background → pre-proliferative → **proliferative** (neovascularisation → vitreous haemorrhage/retinal detachment); **maculopathy** is the main cause of visual loss. Annual retinal screening; laser/anti-VEGF for proliferative/maculopathy.
- **Nephropathy** — **albuminuria** (screen with urine ACR) → declining eGFR. **ACE inhibitor/ARB** for albuminuria + BP; **SGLT2 inhibitors** slow progression. ⚑
- **Neuropathy** — symmetrical **glove-and-stocking** sensory loss (the basis of foot ulceration), autonomic (gastroparesis, postural hypotension, erectile dysfunction), mononeuropathy.

**Macrovascular:** accelerated atherosclerosis → **IHD, stroke, peripheral arterial disease** — the leading cause of death; manage BP, lipids (statin), antiplatelet where indicated, smoking.

**Diabetic foot:** the interaction of **neuropathy + ischaemia + infection**; annual foot risk assessment; multidisciplinary foot team for ulcers/**Charcot** arthropathy.
