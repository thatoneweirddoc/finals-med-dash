## Thyroid Disorders


### Endocrine Master Notes — Thyroid Disorders

#### 5.1 Hyperthyroidism (Thyrotoxicosis)

**Common features:** weight loss despite good appetite, heat intolerance, sweating, tremor, palpitations/AF, anxiety, loose stools, oligomenorrhoea, proximal myopathy, lid lag/stare. **Biochemistry: ↑free T4/T3 with ↓TSH** (primary).

**Separate the causes — the key is the uptake scan + eye signs + antibodies:**

| Cause | Uptake scan | Distinctive features |
|---|---|---|
| **Graves' disease** (autoimmune, commonest) | **Diffuse ↑uptake** | **TSH-receptor antibodies (TRAb)**; **eye disease** (proptosis, ophthalmoplegia), **pretibial myxoedema**, thyroid acropachy; diffuse goitre with bruit |
| **Toxic multinodular goitre** | **Patchy/multifocal uptake** | Older patient, nodular gland, often AF |
| **Toxic adenoma** | **Single hot nodule** | Solitary autonomous nodule |
| **Thyroiditis** (de Quervain's, postpartum, silent) | **Absent/low uptake** | Transient, painful (de Quervain's) or painless; raised ESR (de Quervain's) |
| **Exogenous thyroxine** | **Absent uptake, low thyroglobulin** | Factitious/over-replacement |

**Discriminator:** **eye signs (Graves' orbitopathy) and pretibial myxoedema occur only in Graves'** — they are extrathyroidal autoimmune features, so their presence makes the diagnosis clinically. **Absent uptake = thyroiditis or exogenous hormone**, not a hyperfunctioning gland.

**Management of Graves'/thyrotoxicosis:**
- **Symptom control:** **β-blocker (propranolol)** for tremor/palpitations.
- **Antithyroid drugs — carbimazole** (or **propylthiouracil, PTU**): either **titration** or **"block-and-replace"** (block with carbimazole + add levothyroxine). Key adverse effect: **agranulocytosis** — warn the patient to seek an urgent FBC if sore throat/fever. **PTU preferred in the first trimester of pregnancy** (carbimazole is teratogenic — aplasia cutis); switch back to carbimazole later. ⚑
- **Definitive:** **radioiodine** (avoid in pregnancy/active eye disease; can worsen orbitopathy) or **surgery** (for large goitre, compressive symptoms, suspected malignancy, pregnancy where drugs fail).
- **Graves' ophthalmopathy:** smoking cessation (strongly worsens it), selenium in mild disease, glucocorticoids/orbital radiotherapy/decompression in severe; the anti-IGF-1R antibody **teprotumumab** is a newer option for active disease. ⚑

**Thyroid storm** *(emergency)* — decompensated thyrotoxicosis with fever, tachyarrhythmia, agitation/delirium, vomiting: treat with **β-blocker + high-dose antithyroid drug (PTU) + iodine (given ≥1 h after the antithyroid drug — Wolff–Chaikoff) + hydrocortisone**, plus supportive cooling and treat the precipitant.

#### 5.2 Hypothyroidism

**Common features:** fatigue, cold intolerance, weight gain, constipation, dry skin/hair, hoarseness, bradycardia, delayed-relaxing reflexes, menorrhagia, low mood; goitre (Hashimoto's) or none.

- **Primary (95%)**: gland failure. **↑TSH with ↓free T4.** Causes: **Hashimoto's (autoimmune, anti-TPO antibodies)** — commonest in iodine-replete regions; iodine deficiency (commonest worldwide); iatrogenic (post-radioiodine/surgery, amiodarone, lithium); transient after thyroiditis.
- **Central/secondary**: pituitary/hypothalamic. **↓free T4 with low/inappropriately normal TSH.**
- **Subclinical hypothyroidism**: **↑TSH with normal free T4.** Treat if TSH persistently **>10 mU/L**, or if symptomatic/antibody-positive/pregnant/planning pregnancy at lower levels. ⚑

**Levothyroxine replacement:** titrate to **normalise TSH** (in primary disease); recheck ~6–8 weeks after a dose change. Start **low and slow in the elderly/ischaemic heart disease**. In pregnancy, requirements **rise (~25–50%)** — increase the dose promptly and aim for trimester-specific TSH targets.

**Myxoedema coma** *(emergency)* — severe decompensated hypothyroidism: hypothermia, bradycardia, hypoventilation (hypercapnia), hyponatraemia, reduced consciousness. Treat with **IV levothyroxine (± T3), IV hydrocortisone (cover possible coexisting adrenal insufficiency), and supportive rewarming/ventilation.**

#### 5.3 Thyroiditis and Thyroid in Pregnancy

- **De Quervain's (subacute granulomatous) thyroiditis:** **painful** tender goitre after a viral illness, **raised ESR/CRP**, **absent uptake**; classically **biphasic** — an early thyrotoxic phase (release of stored hormone) → hypothyroid phase → recovery. Treat with NSAIDs/steroids; β-blocker for symptoms. Self-limiting.
- **Postpartum and silent (painless) thyroiditis:** autoimmune, painless, biphasic; usually resolve but may leave permanent hypothyroidism.
- **Pregnancy:** hCG (a weak TSH agonist) can cause mild **gestational thyrotoxicosis**; requirements for levothyroxine **rise** in treated hypothyroidism; PTU in the first trimester, carbimazole later, for hyperthyroidism.

#### 5.4 Thyroid Nodules and Cancer

**Solitary nodule pathway:** check **TSH first** — a suppressed TSH suggests an autonomous ("hot", usually benign) nodule → uptake scan; a **normal/high TSH** → **ultrasound risk stratification → FNA** of suspicious nodules. Features raising concern: rapid growth, hard/fixed nodule, cervical lymphadenopathy, hoarseness, microcalcification/irregular margins on US, prior neck irradiation.

**Thyroid cancers:**

| Type | Frequency/behaviour | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Papillary** | Commonest (~80%), young, good prognosis | **Lymphatic** spread; psammoma bodies, Orphan-Annie nuclei; associated with **radiation** |
| **Follicular** | ~10%, older | **Haematogenous** spread (lung/bone); diagnosis needs histology (capsular invasion) — **FNA can't distinguish** follicular adenoma from carcinoma |
| **Medullary** | Parafollicular **C-cells** → **calcitonin** | Sporadic or **MEN 2** (RET); screen with calcitonin/CEA; not iodine-avid |
| **Anaplastic** | Elderly, aggressive | Rapidly enlarging fixed mass, poor prognosis, airway compromise |
| **Lymphoma** | Rare | Arises in a background of **Hashimoto's** |

**Discriminator:** **medullary carcinoma secretes calcitonin and is part of MEN 2** — think of it when there's a thyroid mass plus phaeochromocytoma/hyperparathyroidism (MEN 2A) or a marfanoid/mucosal-neuroma habitus (MEN 2B). Differentiated (papillary/follicular) cancers are treated with **thyroidectomy ± radioiodine**, and monitored with **thyroglobulin** as a tumour marker.

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators — Section 5

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| ↑T4, ↓TSH, diffuse uptake, eye signs | **Graves' disease** |
| Thyrotoxicosis with **absent** uptake | **Thyroiditis** or exogenous thyroxine |
| Antibody in Graves' | **TSH-receptor antibody (TRAb)** |
| Antibody in Hashimoto's | **Anti-TPO** (anti-thyroid peroxidase) |
| Antithyroid drug in first-trimester pregnancy | **PTU** (carbimazole teratogenic) |
| Antithyroid drug adverse effect to warn about | **Agranulocytosis** (sore throat → urgent FBC) |
| ↑TSH, normal T4 | **Subclinical hypothyroidism** (treat if TSH >10 / symptomatic / pregnant) |
| Painful goitre + ↑ESR post-viral, biphasic | **De Quervain's thyroiditis** |
| Commonest thyroid cancer, lymphatic spread | **Papillary** |
| Thyroid cancer spreading haematogenously | **Follicular** |
| Thyroid cancer secreting calcitonin / MEN 2 | **Medullary** |
| Tumour marker after differentiated-cancer surgery | **Thyroglobulin** |
| Severe decompensated hypothyroidism | **Myxoedema coma** (IV T4 + hydrocortisone) |

### Thyroid & Parathyroid Summary — Iodine uptake “ trapping” — Thyroid Disorders

Iodine is found in food as NaI . I- uptake by follicle cells is an energy-dependent process occurring against a gradient of follicular cell iodine: normal serum iodine ~ 25:1.

Iodide is captured from the blood and trapped in the colloid by two transporters:

**Na+/I- symporter (NIS) **in basolateral surface 🡪 moves I- from blood to the cytosol.

It is driven by the Na+ gradient formed by the 3Na/2K ATP exchange.

Normally, TSH acts to increase uptake by stimulating the transcription of NIS..so!

- when there is LOW TSH eg from a hypophysectomy, this ratio is low
- when there is HIGH TSH, there is more uptake 🡪 HIGHER ratio 🡪up to 250:1

***Pendrin*1 (*PDS*), an I-/Cl- porter** in the apical membranes 🡪 moves I- from the cytosol of the cell into the colloid. It is driven by I- gradient.

### Thyroid & Parathyroid Summary — ![](notes/images/thyroid-and-parathyroid-summary/img018.jpg) — Thyroid Disorders

Transport

In circulation 99.98% of T4 and 99.5% of T3 are bound to plasma proteins:

- 70% of T4 and T3  is bound to  TBG (thyroid hormone binding  globulin)
- 10% of T4 and very little of T3 Transthyretin (TTR)
- 15% of T4 and 25% of T3 bound to albumin

Advantages:

- –  Large buffer that minimises minute-to-minute variations
- –  Prolongs the half-lives of both T4 (8 days) and T3 (24 hours) they diffuse off slowly

–  T4 acts as a large pool of reserve prohormone available for conversion to T3.

T3/4 are carried to target tissues. Changes in binding protein levels can affect the total levels. The unbound fraction is active / free hormone. It is this free T4 and T3 that is measured in assays 🡪 reflect thyroid status

**Subclinical hypothyroidism: **if TSH is raised but T4/T3 is normal range

#### Difficulties with thyroid function testing

**Serious acute chronic illness**

Systemically ill patients can therefore have an apparently low total and free T4 and T3 with a normal or low basal TSH (the ‘sick euthyroid’ syndrome). Levels are usually only mildly below normal and are thought to be mediated by interleukins IL-1 and IL-6; the tests should be repeated after resolution of the underlying illness.

**Pregnancy and oral contraceptives**

These lead to greatly increased TBG levels and thus to high or high-normal total T4. Free T4 is usually normal. Normal ranges for free T4 and TSH alter with the normal physiological changes during pregnancy and TSH is often slightly suppressed in the first trimester, but this rarely causes clinical problems.

**Drugs**

Amiodarone decreases T4 to T3 conversion and free T4 levels may therefore be above normal in a euthyroid patient; conversely amiodarone may induce both hyper- and hypo- thyroidism – the TSH level is usually reliable.  Many drugs affect thyroid function tests by interfering with protein binding but this now rarely causes a problem with free T4 assays.
