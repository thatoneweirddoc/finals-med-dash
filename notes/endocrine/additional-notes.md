## Additional Notes


### Endocrinology (ANU) — properties — Additional Notes

- Thyroxine (T4) and tri-iodothyronine (T3)
- Formed by iodine binding to tyrosine residues
- T3 is much more biologically active (3-8 x more potent) than T4­ (longer half-life)
- Hence, plasma concentration of free T4 much higher than T3
- Circulate while being bound by plasma proteins thyroid-binding globulin, albumin and transthyretin
- Unbound fraction is the active component  (‘free hormone’). Usually free T4 (0.03%) and free T3 (0.3%)
- 85% of T4 is converted into T3 or reverse T3 (RT3) in the peripheral tissue by tissue-specific deiodinases
- Type I: converts T4 into T3 for circulation in plasma; found in liver, kidney, thyroid and muscle
- Type II: produce high proportion of receptor-bound T3 themselves; found in pituitary, thyroid, cardiac and skeletal muscle, brown fat, and cerebral cortex
- Type III: converts T4 to rT3 and T3 to T2, hence inactivating hormones; found in placenta, developing brain and skin. RT3 is metabolically inactive but produced in times of stress to ↓ metabolic activity

### Endocrinology (ANU) — regulation — Additional Notes

![](notes/images/endocrinology-anu/img009.jpg)

- Stimulation: TRH (hypothalamus) stimulates TSH (pituitary), which stimulates follicular cells. May also be stimulated by thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulin (TSI) in Graves disease
- Extrathyroid Regulation:
- Stimulation of thyroid by TSH, epinephrine, prostaglandins (cAMP stimulators)
- Negative feedback by free T3/T4 on the anterior pituitary to inhibit TSH and on hypothalamus to inhibit TRH
- Intrathyroid Autoregulation:
- There is varying thyroid sensitivity to TSH in response to iodide availability
- Wolff-Chaikoff: excess iodine temporarily inhibits thyroid peroxidase → ↑ iodine organification → ↑ T3/T4
- Jod-Basedow effect (opposite to Wolff-Chaikoff): thyrotoxicosis if a patient with iodine deficiency and partially autonomous thyroid tissue (e.g. autonomous nodule) is made iodine replete
- Increased activity of peripheral 5’deiodinase in hypothyroidism increases T3 production despite low T4 levels

### Adrenal Summary — Explanation — Additional Notes

The adrenal tumour cells produce large amounts of cortisol and other hormones that caused her weight gain, back pain, skin bruising, acne and facial hair. Also cause disturbance of the regulation of protein, carbohydrates and lipid metabolism. Consequently, her blood sugar is raised, the muscles of the shoulder region and thighs lost their protein and became weak and the vertebral bones lost their minerals and became rarefied and liable for fractures. In addition, the small blood vessels in the skin lost their normal vascular responses and became vulnerable to minor trauma and bruising.

### Thyroid & Parathyroid Summary — Histology — Additional Notes

The functional unit of the thyroid gland are the 50 - 500 µm diameter FOLLICLES.

Each follicle is a single layer of follicular epithelial cells which secrete T3 and T4 into a colloid filled lumen. The colloid contains thyroglobulin.

![](notes/images/thyroid-and-parathyroid-summary/img012.jpg)

**Thyroglobulin **is a large glycoprotein (MW 335 kDa while the dimeric iodinated follicular form is 660 kDa), each molecule of which contains about 115 tyrosine residues. It’s synthesised, glycosylated and then secreted into the lumen of the follicle, where iodination of the tyrosine residues occurs. Thyroglobulin makes up ½ the protein in the thyroid gland

- Parafollicular cells found between follicles / acini secrete calcitonin (in response to high serum calcium - increases uptake of calcium into bone, and increased deposition)

### Thyroid & Parathyroid Summary — molecular level — Additional Notes

- T4 and T3 enter the cell by diffusion or carrier mediated transport.
- Only T3 acts once in the cell. T4 must be converted to T3 by intracellular deiodinated enzymes.
- This local, tissue specific regulation of T3 production is important in reducing the impact of thyroid deficiency on tissues eg brain, pituitary

- Once converted, T3 binds with high affinity to TR (the nuclear receptor family found in almost every cell type). When T3 is bound, the corepressor (CoR) is released and a coactivator (CoA) is recruited.
- This then binds to the TRE 🡪 activates transcription 🡪 generation of mRNA 🡪 protein synthesis

### Thyroid & Parathyroid Summary — CRETINISM — Additional Notes

- Definition: maternal and foetal hypothyroidism
- Epidemiology: common where dietary iodine deficiency is endemic eg Himalayas, inland China, Africa.
- Aetiology: dietary iodine deficiency in mother. Sporadic cretinism is rare and results from metabolic disorders that limit T3,T4 biosynthesis.
- Pathogenesis: at 10 -12 weeks the thyroid and pituitary are well formed
- 15 - 20 weeks, TSH, T3 T4 are very low / undetectable. The placenta is TSH impermeable so there is low T4 transfer. Some Placental T4 🡪 T3 conversion occurs under Type III
- 30 weeks - peripheral conversion of T4 🡪 T3 due to maturation of enzymes. There is a post delivery surge so the baby is cold. Without thyroid hormones, GH effects are lessened 🡪 ↓ ossification of cartilage, growth of teeth, contours of face and proportions of body. Severity of CNS impairment is related to the time at which thyroid deficiency occurred in utero. Maternal T3, T4 cross the placenta and are critical to foetal brain development. If there is deficiency BEFORE the foetal thyroid gland develops, mental retardation will be severe.
- Clinical features: cretins are short with infantile proportions. coarse facial features, protruding tongue, umbilical hernia, impaired skeletal development. Impaired CNS development 🡪 mental retardation. /soft tisses may grow lots and obstruct breathing
- Treatment: postpartum deficiencies must be corrected within days to avoid mental retardation
