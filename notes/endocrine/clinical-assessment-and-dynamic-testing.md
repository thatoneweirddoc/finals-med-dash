## Clinical Assessment & Dynamic Testing


### Endocrine Master Notes — Clinical Assessment & Dynamic Testing

#### 3.1 History and Examination — Pattern-Spotting

Endocrine disease rarely presents with one symptom; it presents as a **cluster** that points to a gland. Learn the clusters, not the individual symptoms:

| Cluster | Points to |
|---|---|
| Weight **loss** + heat intolerance + tremor + palpitations + diarrhoea | **Hyperthyroidism** |
| Weight **gain** + cold intolerance + fatigue + constipation + dry skin | **Hypothyroidism** |
| Polyuria + polydipsia + weight loss (+ blurred vision) | **Diabetes mellitus** (or DI if no glycosuria) |
| Central obesity + proximal weakness + thin skin/striae + bruising + hypertension | **Cushing's** |
| Fatigue + weight loss + **pigmentation** + postural dizziness + salt craving | **Addison's** |
| Coarsening features + big hands/feet/jaw + sweating + headache | **Acromegaly** |
| Episodic headache + sweating + palpitations + labile hypertension | **Phaeochromocytoma** |
| Hypertension + hypokalaemia (weakness, cramps) | **Conn's (primary aldosteronism)** |
| "Bones, stones, groans, psychic moans" | **Hypercalcaemia** (often hyperparathyroidism) |

**Targeted signs:** visual-field testing (pituitary), thyroid status and eye signs (Graves'), Cushingoid habitus and proximal myopathy, acral/soft-tissue changes (acromegaly), buccal/palmar-crease pigmentation (Addison's), Chvostek/Trousseau (hypocalcaemia), acanthosis nigricans (insulin resistance).

#### 3.2 Static Biochemistry — "Measure the Pair"

The single most important interpretive principle in endocrinology:

> **Always interpret a target-gland hormone together with its trophic (pituitary) hormone.** The *combination* localises the lesion; either number alone is ambiguous.

**Worked logic (using the thyroid axis):**

| Free T4 | TSH | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Low | **High** | **Primary** hypothyroidism (gland fails, pituitary shouts) |
| Low | Low/inappropriately normal | **Secondary/central** hypothyroidism (pituitary fails) |
| High | **Low** | **Primary** hyperthyroidism (gland autonomous, pituitary silenced) |
| High | High/inappropriately normal | TSH-secreting adenoma or thyroid hormone resistance (rare) |

The same "primary vs secondary" grid applies to every axis: **high trophic + low target = primary gland failure; low trophic + low target = pituitary/hypothalamic failure; low trophic + high target = autonomous gland excess.** *Memory: in **primary** disease the trophic hormone moves in the **opposite** direction to the target hormone (intact feedback); in **secondary** disease they move in the **same** direction.*

Other essential pairs: **ACTH + cortisol**; **PTH + calcium**; **LH/FSH + testosterone/oestradiol**; **renin + aldosterone** (as the ratio); **IGF-1** as the stable read-out of GH.

#### 3.3 Dynamic Tests *(high yield — memorise the direction and the cut-offs)*

Apply the Section 1 rule: **suppress to prove excess, stimulate to prove deficiency.**

##### Suppression tests (for suspected hormone EXCESS)

**Dexamethasone suppression test (DST) — Cushing's screening/localisation**
- **Overnight/low-dose (1 mg)** DST: give dexamethasone 2300h, measure 0900h cortisol. A normal axis suppresses to **<50 nmol/L**; **failure to suppress = Cushing's syndrome** (screen).
- **High-dose (8 mg)** DST: separates the *source* — **pituitary Cushing's disease suppresses** (>50% fall, residual CRH sensitivity) whereas **ectopic ACTH and adrenal tumours do not**.

**OGTT with GH — acromegaly**
- Give 75 g glucose; in health GH suppresses to **<1 µg/L (some assays <0.4)**. **Failure of GH to suppress confirms acromegaly.** (IGF-1 is the screening test; OGTT confirms.)

**Saline/fludrocortisone suppression — primary aldosteronism**
- After a positive **aldosterone:renin ratio (ARR)** screen, aldosterone that **fails to suppress** with salt/saline loading confirms autonomous production.

##### Stimulation tests (for suspected hormone DEFICIENCY)

**Short Synacthen (ACTH stimulation) test — adrenal insufficiency**
- Measure cortisol at 0 and 30 (±60) min after 250 µg synthetic ACTH. A peak cortisol **≥~430–550 nmol/L (assay-dependent; classically >500)** excludes primary adrenal insufficiency. A **flat, low response confirms it**. ⚑ Modern immunoassays have lowered the accepted cut-off — quote the local assay range rather than a single legacy number.

**Insulin tolerance test (ITT) — GH and ACTH/cortisol reserve**
- Induce hypoglycaemia (a potent stressor); GH and cortisol should both rise. The "gold standard" for GH deficiency and for the HPA axis, but contraindicated in ischaemic heart disease/epilepsy.

**Water deprivation test — diabetes insipidus**
- Deprive of water and measure urine/plasma osmolality, then give **desmopressin (DDAVP)**:
  - Normal/primary polydipsia: urine concentrates with deprivation.
  - **Cranial DI**: fails to concentrate with deprivation, **then concentrates after DDAVP** (>50% rise) — the ADH is missing but the kidney responds.
  - **Nephrogenic DI**: fails to concentrate with deprivation **and after DDAVP** — the kidney can't respond.

##### Test-direction summary

| Suspected disorder | Test | Result confirming disease |
|---|---|---|
| Cushing's | Low-dose **DST** | Cortisol **fails to suppress** |
| Cushing's disease vs ectopic | High-dose DST (+ ACTH, CRH test) | Pituitary suppresses; ectopic doesn't |
| Acromegaly | **OGTT-GH** | GH **fails to suppress** |
| Primary aldosteronism | **ARR** then saline suppression | Aldosterone **fails to suppress** |
| Adrenal insufficiency | **Short Synacthen** | Cortisol **fails to rise** |
| GH deficiency | ITT / glucagon | GH **fails to rise** |
| Diabetes insipidus | **Water deprivation ± DDAVP** | Fails to concentrate (± DDAVP splits cranial/nephrogenic) |

#### 3.4 Imaging and Localisation

- **Pituitary**: dedicated **MRI** with contrast (microadenoma <10 mm vs macroadenoma ≥10 mm); formal visual-field perimetry.
- **Thyroid**: **ultrasound** with a risk-stratification score (e.g. U-classification/TIRADS) → **FNA cytology** (reported with the Bethesda/Thy system); **radionuclide (technetium/iodine) uptake scan** separates a **hot** autonomous nodule (uptake) from a **cold** nodule (no uptake, higher malignancy risk) and identifies the diffuse uptake of Graves' vs the patchy uptake of toxic MNG vs the **absent uptake of thyroiditis**.
- **Adrenal**: **CT/MRI adrenals** (incidentaloma characterisation by size and Hounsfield units); **adrenal vein sampling** to lateralise in Conn's; **metanephrines first, then imaging** for phaeochromocytoma (never biopsy a possible phaeo).
- **Bone**: **DEXA** (T-score) for osteoporosis (Section 6).
- **Functional/nuclear**: MIBG for phaeochromocytoma/paraganglioma; somatostatin-receptor imaging (e.g. Ga-DOTATATE PET) for neuroendocrine tumours.

**House rule for localisation:** **confirm the biochemical diagnosis *before* imaging.** Adrenal and pituitary "incidentalomas" are common; imaging first risks chasing a non-functioning lesion. Biochemistry says *whether* there is a hormone problem; imaging says *where*.

### Adrenal Summary — Low-Dose Dexamethasone Suppression Test *EXAM HINT (sensitivity 85%) — Clinical Assessment & Dynamic Testing

**Why: **probe patient's glucocorticoid negative feedback system

**How: **patient is given a prescription for 1 mg of dexamethasone and instructed to take it between 11 pm and 12 am. The next morning, a serum cortisol is obtained btw 8 - 9am.

**Result: **Cortisol should be &lt;50 nmol/L because dexamethasone should result in suppression of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis in normal persons and a fall in plasma and urinary cortisol levels. Failure to suppress cortisol levels following low-dose dexamethasone administration is indicative of Cushing syndrome as lowest levels of cortisol occur at night

**Cons: **10 - 15% are false negatives. Also dexamethasone is primarily metabolized by the cytochrome P-450 (CYP) system; several drugs, such as phenobarbital, carbamazepine (Tegretol), and rifampicin (Rifadin), that induce the activity of CYP3A4 can lead to false-positive tests. OCP also interfere with serum cortisol levels owing to an increase in corticosteroid-binding globulin and by increasing dexamethasone metabolism. Obesity, chronic illness, chronic alcoholism and depression can cause false-positive results.

**Pros:** not affected by GFR like the urine test

### Adrenal Summary — CRH stimulation test — Clinical Assessment & Dynamic Testing

performed by administering 1 μg per kg of CRH intravenously

ACTH and cortisol levels are measured before CRH injection and 15, 30, 45, 60, 90 and 120 minutes after injection.

A rise in the cortisol value of 20 percent or more above basal level or a rise in the ACTH value of at least 50 percent above basal level is considered evidence for an ACTH-dependent lesion

### Adrenal Summary — Imaging (CT, MRI, high dose dexamethasone suppression test) — Clinical Assessment & Dynamic Testing

When test results indicate that a patient has an ACTH-independent lesion, abdominal CT or MRI can help localize the site of the lesion.

A tumour that does not respond to low dose but does respond to high doses is Cushing’s disease (ie a pituitary cause)

If the process is ACTH dependent, a high-dose dexamethasone suppression test combined with cranial MRI studies may aid in localizing the site of ACTH overproduction.

#### INFERIOR PETROSAL VENOUS SAMPLING

pituitary MRI with gadolinium enhancement is insufficiently sensitive to detect small (&lt;2 mm) pituitaryACTH-secreting adenomas

so, bilateral inferior petrosal sinus ACTH sampling before and after CRH administration may be required to distinguish these lesions from ectopic ACTH-secreting tumors that may have similar clinical and biochemical characteristics.

An increased ratio (&gt;2) of inferior petrosal:peripheral vein ACTH confirms pituitary Cushing's syndrome

Cons: procedure should not be performed in patients with hypertension or in the presence of a well-visualized pituitary adenoma on MRI

### Thyroid & Parathyroid Summary — Biochemistry — Clinical Assessment & Dynamic Testing

T4 = thyroxine = 3,5,3’,5’ tetraiodothyronine

T3 = 3,5,3’ triiodothyronine

rT3 = 3,3’,5 triiodothyroinine (no biological activity)

T4 🡪 T3 by the removal of one 5’ iodine (5’ deiodination) by deiodinase 1 or 2

20% of T3 is made in the thyroid, 80% in peripheral tissue by tissue specific deiodinase
