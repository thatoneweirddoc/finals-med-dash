## Calcium, Bone & Parathyroid Disorders


### Endocrine Master Notes — Calcium, Bone & Parathyroid Disorders

*Foundation: the PTH / vitamin D / calcitonin loop from Section 1. The whole section is read off the **calcium + phosphate + PTH + ALP** panel.*

#### 6.1 Hypercalcaemia and Hyperparathyroidism

**Symptoms** — "**bones, stones, abdominal groans, thrones and psychic moans**": bone pain, renal stones/nephrocalcinosis, constipation/nausea/pancreatitis/peptic ulcer, polyuria (thrones), fatigue/depression/confusion. ECG: **short QT**.

**Two causes account for ~90%** — separate them with **PTH**:

| | PTH | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary hyperparathyroidism** | **High or inappropriately normal** | Outpatient; usually a single **adenoma**; commonest cause of hypercalcaemia in the community |
| **Malignancy** | **Suppressed (low)** | Inpatient; **PTHrP** (squamous cancers), bony metastases, myeloma; commonest cause in hospital |

**Discriminator:** in **PTH-driven** hypercalcaemia PTH is high/normal-but-inappropriate; in **PTH-independent** (malignancy, vitamin D excess, granulomatous disease, thyrotoxicosis) PTH is **suppressed**. Remember the phosphate rule from Section 1: **primary hyperparathyroidism gives high calcium with LOW phosphate**; malignancy-related hypercalcaemia is variable.

**Types of hyperparathyroidism:**

| Type | Calcium | PTH | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | ↑ | ↑ | Autonomous adenoma/hyperplasia |
| **Secondary** | ↓ or normal | ↑ | Physiological response to **low calcium / vitamin D deficiency / CKD** |
| **Tertiary** | ↑ | ↑↑ | Long-standing secondary (CKD) → autonomous hyperplasia |

**Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcaemia (FHH)** — the key mimic of primary hyperparathyroidism: a benign **CaSR loss-of-function** mutation giving mild hypercalcaemia with **inappropriately normal/high PTH** but **LOW urinary calcium** (low urine calcium:creatinine clearance ratio). *Discriminator: check a **24 h urine calcium** before parathyroid surgery — a low value points to FHH, which needs no surgery.*

**Severe hypercalcaemia** *(emergency, usually >3.5 mmol/L or symptomatic):* **aggressive IV 0.9% saline rehydration** first; then **IV bisphosphonate** (zoledronate) for malignancy; **calcitonin** for rapid short-term lowering; steroids for granulomatous/vitamin-D causes; treat the underlying cause. (Loop diuretics are no longer routine — only if fluid-overloaded. ⚑)

#### 6.2 Hypocalcaemia and Hypoparathyroidism

**Symptoms** — neuromuscular irritability: **perioral/digital paraesthesia, cramps, tetany, Chvostek's sign** (facial-nerve tap → twitch) and **Trousseau's sign** (carpopedal spasm on cuff inflation), seizures; ECG **long QT**.

**Causes:**
- **Hypoparathyroidism** — commonly **post-surgical** (after thyroid/parathyroid surgery), autoimmune, or infiltrative → **low calcium, HIGH phosphate, low PTH**.
- **Vitamin D deficiency** → low calcium, **low phosphate**, high PTH (secondary), high ALP.
- **Chronic kidney disease** (reduced 1α-hydroxylation + phosphate retention), **hypomagnesaemia** (impairs PTH secretion/action — always check and replace Mg²⁺), acute pancreatitis, massive transfusion.
- **Pseudohypoparathyroidism** — end-organ **resistance** to PTH: low calcium, high phosphate but **HIGH PTH**; may have the Albright hereditary osteodystrophy phenotype (short 4th/5th metacarpals, short stature).

**Management:** acute symptomatic → **IV calcium gluconate** with cardiac monitoring; chronic → oral calcium + **activated vitamin D (alfacalcidol/calcitriol)**; correct magnesium.

#### 6.3 Metabolic Bone Disease

##### Osteoporosis ⚑
Reduced bone **mass and quality** → fragility fractures (hip, vertebrae, wrist). **Normal calcium, phosphate, PTH and ALP** (unlike osteomalacia/Paget's) — the labs are typically normal; the diagnosis is by DEXA or a fragility fracture.

- **DEXA T-score** (SD from young-adult peak): **≥ −1 normal; −1 to −2.5 osteopenia; ≤ −2.5 osteoporosis; ≤ −2.5 with a fragility fracture = severe/established.**
- **Risk assessment: FRAX** (10-year fracture probability) guides who to scan/treat, integrated with BMD (NOGG traffic-light thresholds). Assess anyone with a clinical risk factor or a fragility fracture.
- **First-line treatment: oral bisphosphonate (alendronate/risedronate)** or **IV zoledronate**; ensure calcium/vitamin D replete. **Anabolic therapy (romosozumab, teriparatide/abaloparatide)** is for **very high risk** (e.g. recent vertebral fracture, T-score ≤ −3.5, multiple fractures), followed by an antiresorptive. **Denosumab** (RANKL antibody) is an alternative — but **must not be stopped abruptly** (rebound vertebral fractures). ⚑
- **Glucocorticoid rule:** anyone starting **≥7.5 mg prednisolone/day for ≥3 months** needs bone protection.
- Bisphosphonate counselling: take fasting, upright, with water; oesophagitis risk; rare **osteonecrosis of the jaw** and **atypical femoral fractures** with long use.

##### Osteomalacia / rickets
Defective **mineralisation** from **vitamin D deficiency** (or hypophosphataemia): bone pain, proximal myopathy (waddling gait), Looser's zones (pseudofractures); **rickets** in children (bowing, widened epiphyses). Labs: **low/normal calcium, low phosphate, high ALP, high PTH, low vitamin D.** Treat with vitamin D ± calcium.

##### Paget's disease of bone
Disordered, accelerated bone **turnover** → enlarged, deformed, painful bone; **isolated markedly raised ALP with normal calcium/phosphate**. Complications: bone pain, deformity (bowed tibia, skull enlargement), **deafness** (VIII compression), high-output cardiac failure, and (rarely) **osteosarcoma**. Treat symptomatic disease with **bisphosphonates**.

**Bone-panel pattern grid (high yield):**

| Disease | Calcium | Phosphate | ALP | PTH |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Osteoporosis** | Normal | Normal | **Normal** | Normal |
| **Osteomalacia** | Low/normal | **Low** | **High** | High |
| **Primary hyperparathyroidism** | **High** | **Low** | High/normal | **High** |
| **Paget's** | Normal | Normal | **Very high (isolated)** | Normal |

### Endocrinology (ANU) — parathyroid gland — Calcium, Bone & Parathyroid Disorders

- Most common number of parathyroid glands is 4, but it can range from 1 – 12
- Composed primarily of chief cells and fat with thin fibrous capsule dividing the gland into lobules
- Fat becomes more scattered with age
- Chief cells: clear cytoplasm and prominent nucleolus that secretes parathyroid hormone (PTH)
- Oxyphil cells: larger, and have eosinophilic cytoplasm that increases with age
- PTH plays an important role in regulating plasma calcium and phosphate levels, interacting with dietary vitamin D and calcitonin
- Calcium is important for normal cell function, neural transmission, membrane stability, bone structure, blood coagulation and intracellular signalling

### Endocrinology (ANU) — Calcium homeostasis — Calcium, Bone & Parathyroid Disorders

- Normal total serum Ca2+: 2.2-2.6 mmol/L
- 40% protein bound (mostly albumin), 50% ionized, and 10% complexed with PO43- and citrate
- Regulated by parathyroid hormone (PTH) and vitamin D which act on GI tract, bone and kidney
- Role of calcium: ionized calcium is responsible for stabilizing the resting membrane potential of cells

PRESENTATION

- Variable (may be asymptomatic)
- Stones (nephrolithiasis: calcium oxalate &gt; calcium phosphate stones)
- Bones (bone pain, arthralgia)
- Abdominal groans (abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, PUD, constipation, pancreatitis)
- Psychiatric overtones (anxiety, depression, fatigue)
- Note: the above findings are in Vitamin D overdose
- Diminished muscle excitability (cardiac arrhythmias, muscle weakness, paresis)
- Polyuria and dehydration

INVESTIGATIONS

![](notes/images/endocrinology-anu/img015.jpg)

MANAGEMENT

- Treatment of any underlying disorder (e.g., glucocorticoids in granulomatous disease)
- Mild or asymptomatic hypercalcemia: total calcium &lt; 12 mg/dL (&lt; 3 mmol/L) or ionized calcium &lt; 8 mg/dL (&lt; 2 mmol/L)

- Reduced dietary intake of calcium
- Avoidance of thiazide diuretics, lithium, high-calcium diet

- Severe or symptomatic hypercalcemia: total calcium &gt; 14 mg/dL (&gt; 3.5 mmol/L) or ionized calcium &gt; 12 mg/dL (&gt; 3 mmol/L)

- IV hydration with isotonic saline
- Loop diuretics (with monitoring of serum potassium!) in patients with renal insufficiency or heart failure to avoid volume overload (Loop diuretics increase the renal excretion of calcium but also prevent volume overload in at-risk patients.)
- Bisphosphonates (e.g., zoledronic acid, pamidronate), in cases of excessive bone resorption (e.g. hypercalcemia of malignancy, immobilization)
- Calcitonin
- Dialysis in severe cases or renal failure: &gt; 18 mg/dL (&gt; 4.5 mmol/L)

Note: thiazide diuretics are calcium sparing → discontinue them in hypercalcemia!

HYPERCALCAEMIC CRISIS

- Definition: life-threatening condition that should be suspected at total calcium levels &gt; 14 mg/dL (3.5 mmol/L) or ionized calcium &gt; 12 mg/dL (3 mmol/L)
- Symptoms: dehydration (ADH resistance, nausea, and vomiting), fever, psychosis, and ultimately coma
- Treatment:

- Increase urinary Ca2+ excretion with isotonic saline over 24 hours ± loop diuretic but only if hypervolemic
- Diminish bone resorption: bisphosphonates
- Decrease GI Ca2+ absorption: corticosteroids in ↑ vitamin D and haematologic malignancies
- Dialysis: last resort
