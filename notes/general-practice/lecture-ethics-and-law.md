## Lecture: Ethics and law


### General Practice (ANU) — Lecture: Ethics and law

CONSENT:** **legal and ethical obligation required to protect doctor against trespass

- Must be given freely and voluntarily
- Person must have legal capacity to consent, and must be able to 1) take in/retain information, 2) understand information, 3) assess information, balancing needs and risks
- Must be specific/informed

CONFIDENTIALITY** **is an ethical duty

- Respecting patient’s autonomy
- Building an honest/trustworthy relationship
- Achieving diagnosis/treatment

PRIVACY** **is a legal right to impart information to impart information with reliance upon secrecy

- 2002 Health Records and Information Privacy Act
- Exceptions to confidentiality and privacy:
- Express or implied consent of patient (communication among other health professionals)
- Mandatory disclosure under compulsion of law (e.g. mandatory reporting in child abuse for example, court order, subpoena, search warrant, power of EPA)
- Overriding duty to the public (serious risk of harm)

APOLOGY AND LIABILITY

- Legal duty  to disclose medical error
- An apology made on behalf of a person in connection to a matter alleged to be caused by the person does not constitute implied admission of liability and is not admissible in civil proceedings as evidence of fault

OTHER LAWS

- S8 drugs- it is against the law to self-administer or supply S8 drugs for more than 7 days
- It is unethical/unprofessional for a doctor to enter a sexual relationship with a patient, regardless of consent

HEALTH PRACTITIONERS REGULATION NATIONAL LAW NSW NO 86A-SECT 139E

- “Unsatisfactory professional conduct”: conduct is significantly below the standard reasonably expected of a practitioner at equivalent training/level of experience
- “Professional misconduct”: unsatisfactory professional conduct of a serious nature to justify suspension of the practitioner from practicing medicine or the removal of the practitioner’s name from the Register
