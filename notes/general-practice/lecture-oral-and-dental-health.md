## Lecture: oral and dental health


### General Practice (ANU) — Lecture: oral and dental health

- FUNCTION: communication, alimentation (mastication, gustation, digestion, deglutination), respiration, intimacy
- SALIVA PHYSIOLOGY: functions to buffer acid and remineralise tooth structure. Flow rate of saliva affected by:
- Physiologic: circadian rhythm (↓ during sleep), ANS (PNS cholinergic, sympathetic adrenergic), fluid balance
- Pathology: immune-mediate exocrinopthies (Sjogren, Sarcoidosis), infection, DM, fluid restriction, drugs (analgesia, anxiolytic, narcotics, sedatives, diuretics, anticholinergics, anti-emetics), RT
- ORAL COMPLICATIONS OF SYSTEMIC DISEASE: aphthae, lichen planus, pemphigoid, pemphigus, oral cancer
- ORAL AND DENTAL ADVERSE EFFECTS OF DRUGS:  bisphosphonate-related osteonecrosis of the jaws (BRONJ), drug-induced salivary hypofunction
- DENTAL DISEASE: caries, periodontal disease

ORAL CANCER

- Frequent sites: commissures, retromolar trigone and tonsillar pillar, lateral tongue, ventral tongue and floor of mouth

DENTAL CARIES (DENTAL DECAY)

- Weak organic acid demineralisation of tooth’s hydroxyapatite crystal structure
- Acid produced by cariogenic oral bacterial flora (in plaque) from fermentable carbohydrates (sugars) present in food

PERIODONTITIS/PERIODONTAL DISEASE

- Periodontium is the tissue that surround and support teeth
- Bystander immune-mediated tissue destruction of alveolar bone and periodontal ligament, invoked by overgrowth of oral bacteria into gingival crevice/periodontal ligament
- Characterised by gingivitis, bleeding gums, pain, pocket depths >3mm
- Involves progressive loss of alveolar bone around teeth → mobility → tooth loss

ACUTE APICAL PERIODONTITIS/APICAL ABSCESS

- Localised intra-oral swelling evident
- Rx: analgesia, avoid antibiotics, referral for root canal therapy, extraction

PULPITIS

- Inflammation of the dental pulp
- Aetiology: infective (dental caries), trauma (mechanical, thermal, chemical)
- Presentation: pain unrelieved by OTC analgesics
- Rx: NSAIDs, paracetamol + codeine
- Referral: restoration, root canal therapy, extraction

DENTAL ABSCESS/FACIAL CELLULITIS

- Features: “mixed” bacterial flora, dominated by facultative anaerobic species
- Early infections (<3 days) dominated by gram positive streptococci species
- Persistent infections increasingly dominated by anaerobic species
- Presentation: extra-oral swelling, regional tender (reactive) lymphadenopathy, fever (>37.5oC)
- Rx:
- Analgesia
- Antibiotics (amoxil, metronidazole, clindamycin, macrolide, metronidazole)
- Referral for definite treatment (root canal therapy, extraction)

LUDWIG’S ANGINA

- Aggressive cellulitic infection involving floor of mouth/submandibular space
- Life threatening → associated airway compromise, potential spread to mediastinum/pericardium
- Risk factors: mandibular abscess/infection; compromised host

ORAL MANIFESTATIONS/COMPLICATIONS OF DIABETES

- Bacterial infections: caries, periodontal disease, cellulitis, space-occupying infections
- Fungal: oral candidiasis
- Salivary dysfunction: autonomic neuropathy
- Neurosensory dysaesthesia:  taste disturbance, glossodynia
- Oral lichen planus: drug reaction
- Risk of periodontitis 3x higher in diabetics

FDI 2-DIGIT TOOTH IDENTIFICATION

- Quadrant 1: right maxilla
- Quadrant 2: left maxilla
- Quadrant 3: left mandible
- Quadrant 4: right mandible
