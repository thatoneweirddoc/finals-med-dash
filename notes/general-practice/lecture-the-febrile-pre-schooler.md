## lecture: The Febrile Pre-schooler


### General Practice (ANU) — lecture: The Febrile Pre-schooler

#### Differentials

- Infection (always consider UTI in pre-verbal child)
- Connective tissue disease
- Cancer
- Relapsing fever syndromes

#### Red flags

- Age &lt;3 months
- Apnoea
- Increasing difficulty breathing
- A weak suck (that was previously strong)
- Episodic pallor or weakness

#### GUIDING CONCEPTS

- THRESHOLD OF RECOGNITION: meningococcal disease, respiratory syncytial virus, pneumonia, UTI, EBV, hepatitis A
- OCCULT BACTERAEMIA: pneumococcus, meningococcus, salmonella

#### 5 steps to success:

- Clinician achieves clarity of purpose
- Empathy
- Focus attention on the ABCD schema: alertness/activity/a social responsiveness; breathing; circulation; dehydration
- Explain that febrile illness is common
- Describe what is expected to happen next if all goes as well as expected

#### Natural history of respiratory viral illnes

- 1-4 DAYS: fluctuating fever, fluctuating A’s, early runny nose ± sore throat
- 3-4 WEEKS COUGH: initially dry, eventually productive
- DEPARTURE FROM NATURAL HISTORY:
- Progressive unrelieved deterioration in A’s
- Progressive increase in work of breathing
- Unusual episodes of pallor or decreased cap refill
- Failure to pass urine 4 or 5 times in 24h
- Petechial rash that spreads
- Parents should reassess every 4-6h
- Reconsider Dx if child has fever beyond 4th day

#### Natural history of gastrointestinal infection

- 0-6 HOURS: very frequent vomiting. No blood or bile
- 6-30 HOURS: vomiting gradually becomes less frequent
- 6-30 HOURS: diarrhoea begins. Initially frequent, may take weeks to resolve
- DEPARTURE FROM NATURAL HISTORY:
- Persistent and frequent
- Blood or bile
- Progressive unrelieved deterioration in A’s
- Episodes of unusual pallor
- Reduced cap refill
- DDX OF ACUTE VOMITING: sepsis, UTI, head/abd/endocrine/metabolic, exogenous (meds, poisoning, envenomation)
