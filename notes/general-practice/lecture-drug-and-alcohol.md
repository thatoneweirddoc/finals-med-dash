## Lecture: drug and alcohol


### General Practice (ANU) — Lecture: drug and alcohol

#### The 5 A’s framework for behavioural intervention

- Ask – seek permission to discuss the issue
- Assess – quantify the problem; risk assessment; readiness to change
- Advise – specify health implications and agree on goals
- Assist – with referral to community-based programmes or services
- Arrange – follow-up with patient; make next appointment

#### History

- SUBSTANCE USED
- Tobacco
- Alcohol
- Misuse of prescribed drugs
- Illicit drugs: cannabis, stimulants (MDMA, amphetamine, methamphetamine, cocaine), opioids, hallucinogens
- ROUTE OF ADMINISTRATION
- AMOUNT: volume, weight, money spent, amount used in a certain time frame
- FREQUENCY USED: average day/week use, how much yesterday, the day before etc., when last used
- DURATION OF CURRENT USe
- AGE FIRST USED/AGE WHEN USE BECAME PROBLEMATIC

#### diagnosis

- HARMFUL USE is associated with clear physical or psychological harm
- DEPENDENCE: ≥3 within the last year of 1) strong desire to use, 2) loss of control, 3) withdrawal, 4) tolerance, 5) salience, 6) use despite harm

#### Motivational enhancement therapy

- Express empathy
- Elicit ambivalence
- What’s good about your drug use?
- What’s not so good?
- Explore discrepancies
- Elicit self-motivational statements
- Counselling micro-skills
- Active listening, supportive counselling that is non-judgemental
- Roll with resistance

#### Motivational interviewing – AREDS

- Avoid arguing
- Roll with resistance
- Express empathy
- Develop discrepancy
- Support self-efficacy

#### Goals must be:

- Realistic and achievable
- Specific and observable
- Client’s goals rather than those of others

WHOSE GOALS?

- Patient vs family vs therapist
- Short-term vs long-term
- Drug-specific vs other health and lifestyle issues
- Look at the social context
- Spouse told patient to come in, court-mandate, etc.

THINGS TO CONSIDER

- If patient doesn’t want to change, agree to disagree and leave the door open for future contact
- Harm minimisation in interim e.g. avoidance of driving when intoxicated, safety when intoxicated, child protection, thiamine in alcohol dependence, safe-injecting, avoid over-heating, avoid dehydration with stimulants

NHMRC GUIDELINES

- Guideline 1:  ≤ 2 standards for healthy people ↓ the lifetime risk of harm from alcohol-related disease or injury
- Guideline 2: ≤ 4 standards for healthy people on a single occasion ↓ the risk of alcohol related injury arising from that occasion.
- Guideline 3: Children ≤15 y/o are at the greatest risk of harm from drinking  and 15–17 y/o should delay initiation of drinking for as long as possible.
- Guideline 4: For pregnant/planning pregnancy/breastfeeding, not drinking is the safest option.

ON DEPENDENCE

- Types of drinkers: dependent → hazardous/harmful → low risk → non-drinker
- Questionnaires: CAGE, AUDIT-C (8+= high risk of harm 🡪 brief interventions, assess for dependency, specialist referral)
- Audit intervention for non-dependence: (mneomonic FLAGS): Feedback, Listen, Advice, Goals, Strategies

#### opioids/benzodiazepine

- DOCTOR SHOPPERS IDENTIFICATION (EXAMPLES)
- “Travelling through”, “lost prescription”
- Exaggerating/feigning medical problems- using old reports, declining physical examination/permission to obtain past reports
- Doctor can use Medicare Prescription Shopping Information Service (24 hour hotline) if suspicious

#### Assess 6A’s regularly

- ACTIVITIES: functional improvement
- ANALGESIA: assess change in pain levels (can use formal scales), quality of sleep
- ADVERSE EFFECT: side-effects, hospitalisation
- ABERRANCE BEHAVIOUR: multiple sources, re-presenting early, lost prescriptions, U/A negative for the prescribed drug and/or positive for non-prescribed drugs, injecting medications.
- AFFECT: mood and mental health
- ACCURATE RECORDS: state/commonwealth regulations, communications with other workers
