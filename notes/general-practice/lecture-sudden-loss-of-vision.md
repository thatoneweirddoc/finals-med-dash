## lecture: sudden loss of vision


### General Practice (ANU) — lecture: sudden loss of vision

#### History

- How was it discovered
- Time course
- Pain in eye, pain on eye movement
- Floaters/flashes of light
- Headache, pain in temple
- Hx trauma, previous ocular history, myopia
- General health: CVD, hypertension/cholesterol, diabetes, MS

#### Examination

![](notes/images/general-practice-anu/img016.jpg)

- General: BP, pulses including carotid
- Visual acuity: glasses, pinhole
- Vision: light perception/projection
- Colour vision, red saturation
- Pupils: direct, consensual, RAPD
- Slit lamp exam
- Face: nose bleeds, ptosis, localised tenderness, sensation, numbness upper teeth/gum (infraorbital nerve), palpate eyelids for crepitus
- Ophthalmoscopy: disc (colour, contours, cupping), vasculature, 4 quadrants, macul

#### brain ct

- INDICATIONS: suspicion of ↑ICP, proptosis, chemosis, homonymous field defect or eye movement disorders
- Contrast CT in patients with adequate RFTs when vascular, inflammatory or inflammatory aetiology suspected

#### Retinal detachment

- DEFINITION: detachment of inner layer of retina (neuro-retina) from retinal pigment epithelium
- CAUSES: tears/holes in retina (rhegmatogenous retinal detachment) → sub-retinal fluid accumulates, retina temporarily loses its function
- RISK FACTORS: myopia, trauma, previous ophthalmic surgery, intraocular tumour, vitreous haemorrhage, age, DM
- O/E: grey colour on ophthalmoscope
- PRESENTATION: prodromal flashes of light and floaters; loss/deterioration of central vision; scotoma; loss of peripheral visual field; relative afferent pupillary defect
- TREATMENT:
- Retinal tear with laser photocoagulation or cryopexy
- Retinal detachment: emergency operative management

#### Optic Neuropathy due to Giant Cell Arteritis

- GCA: granulomatous vasculitis of large and medium-sized arteries
- AETIOLOGY/RISK FACTORS: women, &gt; 50y/o, PMR
- PRESENTATION: jaw claudication, blurry vision/loss of vision, headache, superficial temporal artery tenderness, thickening or nodularity, systemically unwell
- O/E: swollen tender temporal artery, relative afferent pupillary defect, pale swollen optic discs
- IX: ↑ESR, ↑CRP, thrombocytosis, temporal artery biopsy
- RX: systemic corticosteroids
- COMPLICATION: optic nerve ischaemia

#### transient embolic phenomenon

- AMAUROSIS FUGAX: painless temporary loss of vision in one/both eyes
- IX: carotid duplex, echocardiogram, CTA, CVS risk factors, consider thrombophilia/hyperviscosity syndromes

#### Central Retinal Artery Occlusion

- DEFINITION: occlusion of the central retinal artery and/or its branches, usually from thromboembolic phenomena
- PRESENTATION: sudden, painless visual loss; relative afferent pupillary effect; visual field defects depend on branch
- OPHTHALMOSCOPY:  cherry red spot on macular and pale, oedematous retina
- TREATMENT: usually ineffective due to irreversible ischemic damage but can try eyeball massage, carbogen therapy, ↓ intraocular pressure with drugs/surgery (paracentesis of anterior chamber), hemodilution, vasodilators
- IX: carotid doppler, echocardiography, rule out temporal arteritis

#### Central Retinal Vein Occlusion

- DEFINITION: occlusion of central venous vein, usually by thrombus
- Ischemic (haemorrhagic) vs non-ischemic (more common; venous stasis retinopathy)
- PRESENTATION: non-ischemic presents with mild to moderate visual loss and ischemic presents with loss of vision and afferent pupillary defect with extensive retinal haemorrhage on opthalmoscopy
- BLOOD AND THUNDER APPEARANCE: extensive haemorrhages, dilated tortuous veins
- IX: fluorescein angiography
- TREATMENT: laser therapy, intravitreal VEGF injection

#### Vitreous haemorrhage

- DEFINITION: bleeding into the vitreous chamber, which most often occurs from the retinal vessel
- RISK FACTORS: trauma, posterior vitreous detachment, diabetic retinopathy, hypertensive retinopathy, blood dyscrasias, intraocular neoplasms
- PRESENTATION: shadow in vision, spots, completely black
- TREATMENT: rest, head up, wait for haemorrhage to clear, treat underlying cause (diabetic retinopathy, retinal tear)

#### optic neuritis

- DEFINITION: 70% with known multiple sclerosis and may be the first presentation of demyelinating disease
- PRESENTATION: unilateral sudden vison loss, retrobulbar pain, ↓colour perception, relative afferent papillary defect
- TREATMENT: IV methylprednisolone (1g/day for 3d) followed by oral prednisolone for 11d, Interferon-ß to delay MS

#### other

PAPILLEDEMA

- Optic disc swelling secondary to ↑ ICP
- Common cause is idiopathic intracranial HTN
