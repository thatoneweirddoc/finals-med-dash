## lecture: eye problems in gp


### General Practice (ANU) — lecture: eye problems in gp

#### Infraorbital fracture:

- IX: CT orbits and brain (axial, coronal)
- URGENT OPHTHALMOLOGIST REVIEW if intraocular haemorrhage, globe rupture, orbital wall fracture

#### Red Eye in GP

- CHALAZION: cyst-like nodule inside eyelid that forms around Meibomian Gland that is benign and painless
- BLEPHARITIS: inflammation of eyelids and associated adnexal structures; usually chronic
- RED EYE
- Microbial keratitis: contact lenses; intensive topical ABx
- Marginal keratitis: topical steroids
- No contact lenses: ciliary injection, conjunctival injection
- CONJUNCTIVAL: allergy, bacterial, viral
- Unilateral: bacterial (pus)
- Bilateral: viral or allergic
- Viral: follicles, lymph node, contact, URTI
- Allergic: Itchy, allergic tendency
- Follicles: viral, chlamydia, toxic, molluscum contagiosum
- Papillae: bacterial, allergy, irritation
- CILIARY: keratopathy (inflamed cornea), iritis, acute angle closure
- CORNEAL ABRASION
- Mid-dilated → acute angle closure
- Peaked pupil → penetrating eye injury
- Constricted → Iritis
- SCLERITIS: NSAIDs
- EPISCLERITIS: topical Steroids
- REFER TO OPHTHALMOLOGIST IF: pain, ciliary injection, ↓ visual acuity, pupil changes, unresponsive to treatment
