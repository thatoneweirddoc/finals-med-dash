## prescribing in general practice


### General Practice (ANU) — prescribing in general practice

#### National Medicines Policy

- Quality, safety and efficacy
- Viable and responsible pharmaceutical industry
- Equity of access

#### Quality use of medicines

- RIGHT MANAGEMENT: consider the place of medicines (risk calulators), therapeutic guidelines
- RIGHT MEDICINE: risks/benefits, individual, comorbidities, other medications, costs for individual and health system
- SAFE AND EFFECTIVE USE: monitoring outcomes, minimising misuse/overuse/underuse, improving patient’s ability to solve problems related to their medications

#### Example: Antihypertensives

- HIGH RISK (>15%) start antihypertensive. Start with ACE-I, CCB, or if 65 or older, thiazide diuretic (low dose)
- MODERATE RISK (10-15%) consider antihypertensive if 3-6month lifestyle changes doesn’t reduce risk
- OR if BP persists >160/100, FHx of CVD , at risk population (ATSI, Maori, South Asian, Middle Eastern, Pacific Islander)
- Allow ~ 4-6wks to gauge response to treatment

#### Home Medicine Review

- Initiated by GP, pharmacist visits patient at home
- BENEFITS: free, improves safety, decreases medical error
- RISK FACTORS FOR ADVERSE DRUG REACTIONS: physiological changes with ageing, multimorbidity, female, recent hospitalization, depression
- 1/3 of unplanned hospital admissions involving elderly are medicine-related
- Risk of falling and adverse drug reactions increases with more meds

#### SIMPLE model for adherance

- Simplifying regimen characteristics
- Imparting knowledge
- Modifying patient beliefs
- Patient communication
- Leaving the bias
- Evaluating adherence
