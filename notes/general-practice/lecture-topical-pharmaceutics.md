## LECTURE: TOPICAL PHARMACEUTICS


### General Practice (ANU) — LECTURE: TOPICAL PHARMACEUTICS

#### Corticosteroids

- Topical formulations
- CREAM: oil and water emulsion; penetrates stratum cornum
- OINTMENT: oil + water and effective barrier against moisture loss
- GEL: liquifies upon contact with skin
- PASTE: oil + water + powder
- POWDER: subdivided solid substance
- Vehicles
- MILD: hydrocortisone 0.5-1%, hydrocortisone acetate 0.5-1%
- MODERATE: betamethasone valerate 0.02/0.05%, clobetasone, desonide, triamcinolone
- POTENT: betamethasone valerate 0.1%, betamethasone dipropionate, mometasone, methylprednisolone 0.1%
- VERY POTENT: betamethasone dipropionate
- Penetration depends on stratum corneum thickness, local occlusive factors, warmth, moisture, area (mucus membrane)
- FINGERTIP UNITS TO COVER BODY AREAS: neck + face: 2.5; chest + abdo: 7; arm: 3; back + buttocks: 7; hand: 1
- ADVERSE EFFECTS OF CORTICOSTEROIDS (LOCAL AND SYSTEMIC)
- Loss of dermal collagen → skin atrophy, formation of striae, fragility, easy bruising
- Telangiectasia
- Promotion of underlying infection
- Idiosyncratic reactions
- DURATION OF THERAPY
- Exacerbations: 7-14 days, then emollient for maintenance
- Unresponsive: up to 3 weeks
- Chronic dermatitis: 2 consecutive days/week
- Severe: max 3 weeks continuous without review
- Long term use associated with tolerance and risk of rebound

#### Eye drops & ointments

- NASAL DRAINAGE MORE COMMON WITH DROPS: systemic absorption
- OINTMENTS: longer duration of effect, appropriate for night, used in children, sustained drug concentrations. Cons: difficult to self-administer, blur vision, more likely to cause contact dermatitis
- PATIENT COUNSELLING FOR DROPS: Write date of opening on container. Wash hands, remove from fridge earlier. Sit or lie down. Form pouch with lower lid. Shake drops well. Avoid product touching eyes, skin, etc. Use clean tissues for discharge. Add second drop only if 1st missed. Try not to blink. Close eyes and double DOT for at least 3 mins. Wait 3-5 mins b/n different administrations
- DOUBLE DOT: Don’t Open eyes Technique + Digital Occlusion of Tear duct → ↓ systemic absorption by 2/3
- Remove contact lenses pre-administration and for at least 15 mins post
- PT COUNSELLING FOR OINTMENTS: use ~1cm along pouch, blink to spread; use after drops; no contact lenses
- CLASSES OF EYE DROPS
- Prostaglandin Analogues – latanoprost, bimatoprost, travoprost

Systemic effects: H/A, asthma, dyspnoea

- β-blockers – timolol, betaxolol

Systemic effects: bradycardia, hypotension, syncope, fatigue, bronchospasm

- Agonists – apraclonidine, brimonidine

Systemic effects: dry mouth, taste disturbance, H/A, dizziness, drownsiness, hypotension, palpitations

- Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors – brinzolamide

Systemic effects: bitter taste, GI disturbance, H/A, dizziness

- Cholinergic – pilocarpine. Systemic effect: frontal H/A

Many calls to poisons line re meds incorrectly administered to eye

- SSRIs: mydriasis, increased intraocular pressure, angle-closure glaucoma, oculogyric crisis

#### Patches

- Opioid - 3 days/weekly
- Rivastigmine - daily
- Rotigotine - daily
- NRT - daily
- GTN - daily
- Oxybutynin - 2 per week
- HRT - 2 per week
- Testosterone – daily

FENTANYL PATCH

- Very potent and not for opioid naïve
- For stable pain (72h analgesia)
- 6 days for steady state after dose change (takes up to 18-24h for effect and remains 15-20h after removal)
- Metabolised in liver with no active metabolites
- Lozenges, buccal

FENTANYL PATCH COUNSELLING:

- Rotate sites, date patches, amd don’t’ forget to remove old one
- Variable rates of delivery
- Effects of heat, sweating, shaving
- Do not cut
- Caution: child and pets
- Illicit use
- Consider HMR if available
- Can use transdermal patch monitoring char
