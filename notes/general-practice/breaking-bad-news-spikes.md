## Breaking Bad News - SPIKES


### General Practice (ANU) — Breaking Bad News - SPIKES

S – SETTING UP THE INTERVIEW

- Arrange for some privacy and sit down
- Involve significant others

P – PERCEPTION:

- Assess patient’s perception of their medical situation by asking open-ended questions
- Based on this information, you can rectify any misconceptions

I – INVITATION:

- While a majority of patients express a desire for full information about their diagnosis, some patients do not have a psychological coping mechanism
- E.g. Would you like me to give you all the results or spend more time on the treatment plan?

K – KNOWLEDGE

- Giving knowledge and information to the patient
- Warning the patient that bad news is coming may lessen the shock that can follow

E – EMOTIONS

- Addressing the patients emotions with empathetic responses

S – STRATEGY AND SUMMARY

- Before discussing a treatment plan, ask if the patient is ready for such a discussion
- Discuss a clear plan for the future to relieve anxiety and uncertainty

### Geriatrics Summary — Breaking Bad News - SPIKES

- Medical emergenciesAltered mental status

*Delirium is an acute confused state. *

*A delirious, drowsy patient is a medical emergency*

- Definitiondisturbance in CONSCIOUSNESS and ATTENTION
- change in COGNITION (memory, orientation, language) or development of perceptual disturbance not better accounted for by pre existing / evolving dementia
- ACUTE: developing over a short period of time (hours to days)
- FLUCTUATING course and consciousness
- ORGANIC: history exam or labs suggest this is the direct physiological consequence of a general medical condition or substance intoxication/withdrawal state.

- Associated featuressleep wake cycle disturbance
- psychomotor behaviour disturbance
- emotional disturbance including rapid shifts in emotional state

- Complications - higher mortality
- higher rates of institutionalisation
- higher complication rates of other conditions
- longer LOS

- Causes - usually multifactorial#1 hypoxia
- drugs esp anticholinergic or dopaminergic; polypharmacyhigh risk: benzos, antidepressants, anti parkinsonian (levodopa, carbidopa), analgesia, lithium, steroids, methyldopa for HTN,
- medium risk: anti - Tb (rifampicin / isoniazid), anti fungal (hydrochloroquine,), antiviral (amantadine, acyclovir, zidovudine), anticholinergic, antihistamine, anti spasmodics, a blockers (tamsulosin), B blockers, anti arrhythmics eg digoxin, amiodarone, disopyramide and lignocaine, major tranquilisers eg chlorpromazine (Largactil),
- low risk: anticonvulsants (phenytoin, carbamazepine, valproate, primidone), sedatives eg haloperidol, Droperidol, antibiotics eg ben pen, cotrimoxazole, amphotericin; antihistamines eg cimetidine, ranitidine, famotidine, ACEI, CCB, diuretic individually do not cause delirium…but
- triple whammy: ACEI + NSAID + thiazide diuretic 🡪 electrolyte disturbance
- certain chemotherapy agents can eg methotrexate, vinca alkaloids, FU,
- OHAs can cause hypoglycaemia and hyponatraemia which do cause delirium

- overdose -  lithium, salicylates, or anticholinergics

- Reversing drug deliriumImplement non-pharmacological intervention for support.
- Review all the medications and look for a temporal relationship
- Review medications for a recent addition or change in dose
- Stop/taper the medication causing delirium.

Discontinue all anticholinergic medicines, if possible.

Reassess pain; adding analgesic may be necessary, or reduce the dose or discontinue narcotics if high doses have been used.

Discontinue all benzodiazepines if use &lt;1 week; if &gt;1 week taper slowly as tolerated and use non- pharmacological management for sleep.

Reduce the dose of other delirogenic medication as appropriate.

- Calculate CrCl and adjust the dosage of renally eliminated medications.
- Specific antidotes in poisoning or toxic conditions.
- Minimise polypharmacy and use non-delirogenic drugs when possible.
- If drugs with known risk of inducing delirium is used close observation and monitoring is essential.

- Medical emergencies present differently in geriatric patients

- medications they are taking may mask changes in vital signs
- less physiological reserve 🡪 dehydration etc can have large impact
- have comorbidities which impact on how you might manage conditions
- renal function may ↓ ability to use contrast with CT scans
- can have acute changes to a chronic conditions 🡪 delay in recognition
- may have cognitive problems which make it harder for them to verbalise symptoms
- may be dependent on others eg nursing home staff, children who don’t live with them to access medical care which can lead to delays in recognition and assessment
- concurrent dementia and delirium can make history taking and thus workup more difficult

- Holistic approach to management in geriatricsBiological effects of agingRenal 🡪 decrease RBF, GFR, CrCl
- Muscles 🡪 atophy, weakness, more fibrosis
- Bones 🡪 less remodelling, more loss, less deposition
- Vision 🡪 night vision,
- Memory 🡪 poor word finding, switching tasks, new information,

- Aging and drug metabolismSeniors experience many receptor changes due to aging. For example, cholinergic receptors appear to be more sensitive. Pharmacodynamic changes can therefore produce a more pronounced drug effect for a given serum drug level.
- Anticholinergic medications also appear to have exaggerated adverse effects secondary to receptor site changes. Increased drug sensitivity to anticholinergic, narcotic, or sedating medications, can lead to delirium. In the elderly, changes in receptor function occurs across organs. The net effect of these changes is heightened sensitivity of the brain to adverse drug effects

- Respite care respite services provide families and unpaid carers of a person with disability with planned, short-term, time-limited breaks from their usual caring role. They are services that assume the caring role during the period of respite with the intention that families/carers resume care at the end of the respite period.
- Types - in home respite, centre based day respite eg if carer wishes to re enter the workforce, overnight / weekend respite in a respite house or host family’s home, community access respite, residential respite care (short stays in aged care homes on planned or emergency basis eg if carer becomes ill), transition care is short term for people being discharged from hospital who are not quite independent yet
