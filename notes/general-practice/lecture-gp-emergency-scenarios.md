## Lecture: GP emergency scenarios


### General Practice (ANU) — Lecture: GP emergency scenarios

#### RESOURCES IN A GP PRACTICE

- PEOPLE: other doctors, reception staff, nurses, people in waiting room
- EQUIPMENT: O2, stethoscope, drugs, blanket/pillows, ambulance equipment, ECG, emergency trolley, IV access, phone
- THE DOCTOR’S BAG: auroscope, ophthalmoscope, sphygmomanometer, thermometer, syringes, gloves, airway equipment, medications (emergency meds e.g. adrenaline, atropine, glucose 50%. Diazepam, frusemide, benzylpenicillin, hydrocortisone, metoclopramide, morphine, naloxone, etc)

#### Case: anaphylaxis from insect bite

- DRSABCD
- Use adrenaline in the Doctor’s bag
- 0.01ml/kg of 1/1000 IM adrenaline into lateral thigh
- Repeat after 5 mins if not improving
- IV adrenaline only unless cardiac arrest imminent
- Child &gt; 12 -  500mcg IM (0.5ml)
- Child 6-12 -  300mcg IM (0.3ml)
- Child &lt; 6 -  150mcg IM (0.15ml)
- If adrenaline insufficient
- Promethazine 25mg IM or slow IV
- Hydrocortisone 200mg (adults) IM or slow IV (takes ~6h to work)
