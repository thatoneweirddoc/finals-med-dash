## Lecture: Men’s bodies


### General Practice (ANU) — Lecture: Men’s bodies

#### Statistics

- Men die 6 years earlier than woman and 25% of men did not see a GP in the past year (10% women)
- Men have ↑ risk of CHD, dementia, Alzheimer’s disease, CVD, lung cancer and COPD

#### Factors affecting men’s access to health care

- Social expectations
- Work
- Embarrassment
- Lifestyle
- Health literacy
- Late presentations
- Health services – orientations
- Stereotyping and subgroups

#### Gynaecomastia

CAUSES

- Physiological
- Pseudogynaecomastia (fat or muscle, no resistance when pinch beneath nipple)
- Chronic liver disease or renal failure
- Tumour: breast, adrenal, testicular, paraneoplastic syndrome
- Endocrinological: thyroid, hypogonadism
- Drugs e.g. methotrexate, ACEI, calcium channel blockers, amiodarone, haloperidol, tricyclics, PPIs, metoclopramide, alcohol, anabolic steroids, oestrogens, amphetamines, etc

CLINICAL ASSESSMENT

- History: symptoms of hypogonadism, hyperthyroidism, meds, non-prescribed drugs, PMHx
- Examination: general, secondary sexual characteristics, neck, breasts, axilla, abdomen, genitalia
- Red flags: asymmetrical, hard, fixed, axillary involvement, associated discharge
- Investigations: mammography/US, morning testosterone, LH, FSH, oestradiol, HCG, TSH

#### anabolic steroids

- Oral or injectable
- Cycling, stacking and pyramiding
- Reversible SE: ↑appetite, anxiety, aggression, acne, libido change, scrotal pain & impotence, oedema
- Irreversible SE: hirsutism, striae, baldness, cardiac disease, gynaecomastia, testicular atrophy, infertility may improve
- May also use tamoxifen, HCG, diuretics, opioids to counteract SE
- Long-term risks: cardiac disease, cholestasis, hepatocellular carcinoma, tendon rupture, mental illness (depression)

#### bigorexia (muscle dysmorphia)

- Subtle body dysmorphic disorder, related to OCD: preoccupation that one’s body not sufficiently lean/muscular
- Features: ↑ working out, obsession over diet, performance-enhancing substances

#### Erectile Dysfunction

- Epidemiology: 20% of men > 40 y/o; < 20% present for treatment
- Consequences of delayed treatment:
- Poorer response to treatment
- Increased emotional impact
- Effect on relationship with partner
- Missed opportunity for intervention for underlying comorbidity
- Causes: cardiovascular, endocrinological, neurological, urological, pharmacological (smoking, alcohol, marijuana, cocaine), psychosocial (performance anxiety, relationship stress)
- History: above condition, sexual history, medication, psychosocial stressors
- Examination: general, secondary sexual characteristics, cardiovascular, genitalia, CV risk assessment
- Red flags: IHD, malignancy
- Investigations: ± lipids, diabetes screen, TSH, morning testosterone, LH, FSH, oestradiol, PSA, sexual health inventory for men questionnaire
- Drugs associated with ED: SSRIs, MOAI, tricyclics, B-blockers, antiarrhythmic, diuretics, steroids, anti-androgens, ranitidine
- Management: SNAP, counselling, phosphodiesterase inhibitors (sildenafil), vacuum pump devices, intracavernosal injections (alprostadil), prostheses

#### lethargy

RED FLAGS:

- Recent onset fatigue in previously well, older pt
- Unexplained weight loss
- Abnormal bleeding
- Dyspnoea
- Unexplained lymphadenopathy
- Fever
- Recent onset or progression of cardiovascular, gastro, neuro, rheum symptoms

*may be depression esp. in older men. Ask about psychosocial circumstances

#### Depression in men

- 12.2% of Australian males > 16 years expected to experience mood disorder in their lifetime
- Suicide rate higher in men
- Describe somatic symptoms more often than woman
