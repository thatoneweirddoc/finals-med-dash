## Lecture: sexual assault


### General Practice (ANU) — Lecture: sexual assault

- By force, threat, victim incapable of consenting, coercion and bullying, by pretence, children
- Historical sexual assault can be disclosed at any time
- RED FLAGS: partial disclosure, genital injury, possible STI, history does not fit the findings, multiple presentations, late presentations, sexual behaviour
- NON-SPECIFIC PRESENTATION: red bottom/vulva, dysuria, blood on defecation, vague allegation, distressed
- TREATMENT ISSUES: emotional state, pregnancy prevention, physical care, STI prevention
- LIFETIME HEALTH EFFECTS: trauma, mental, sexual, physical, epigenetics
- Forensic issues
- Careful history, document time and place of exam
- Objectivity, honesty, recognising your limitations
- Examination/draw/photography injury (head to toe, genital): It is impossible to age bruises!
- Toxicology
- Specimen collection and sampling for DNA
- Careful documentation to allow 2nd opinion
- Explaining to lay audience – police, jury, etc
- NSW Sexual assault kit: documents, swabs, clothing for DNA/trace evidence, procedures for chain of evidence
- Forensic exam: with pts consent, to assist police investigation, protect evidence
