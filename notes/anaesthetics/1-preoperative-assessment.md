## 1. preoperative assessment


### Anaesthetics (ANU) — 1. preoperative assessment

#### learning objectives

- Understand the importance ofFasting Guidelines
- The effects of pre-existing illness and appropriate optimisation
- Management of usual medications and how they may interact with anaesthesia
- Role of the Pre-Anaesthetic Clinic

- Be able to Elicit relevant history and examination
- Explain risks of anaesthesia
- Understand patient’s concerns
- Request appropriate investigations and understand the relevance of the result to anaesthesia
- Communicate relevant information to other teams involved in patient management
- Show an appreciation of the need for post-operative plans for the patient
- Understand the effect of the social situation of the patient
- Understand the medico-legal issues

#### fASTING GUIDELINES

(Recommended by ANZCA)

- Adults and Children>6months, Elective Procedure- Breast Milk and/or Limited solid food six hours prior to anaesthesia, clear fluids up to two hours prior to anaesthesia
- Infants <6 months, Elective Procedure- Formula up to four hours, breast milk up to three hours, clear fluids up to two hours
- Prescribed medications may be taken with a sip of water less than two hours prior to anaesthesia unless otherwise directed (for example oral hypoglycaemics and anticoagulants).
- An H2-antagonist, proton pump inhibitor or other agent that decreases gastric secretion and acidity should be considered for patients with an increased risk of gastric regurgitation.

#### Pre-operative optimisation

Prior to elective surgery, **fluid/electrolyte balance, comorbidities** and **medications** need to be considered/adjusted

- HTN
- CAD- perioperative β- Blockers may reduce cardiac events and mortality, however may increase stroke risk. Usually β- Blockers continued if taken routinely prior to surgery or initiated in patients with CAD.
- EndocrineDiabetes- Intraoperative glucose management plan, glucose control, extent of end organ damage
- Hyperthyroidism- β- Blocker and pre-operative prophylaxis
- Adrenocortical insufficiency e.g. Addison’s- consider steroid supplementation

- Respiratory DiseasesSmoking, which affects mucus secretion/clearance
- Asthma- Avoid non-selective β- Blockers (cardioselective β- Blockers do not increase risk of bronchospasm in the short-term)
- COPD- Anaesthesia, surgery and analgesia predispose the patient to atelectasis, bronchospasm, pneumonia, prolonged need for mechanical ventilation and respiratory failure. Optimise with bronchodilators ± ICS ± antibiotics.
- Delay surgery in the case of URTI or acute exacerbations

- Obesity and Obstructive Sleep ApneaIncrease risk of difficult ventilation, intubation and post-operative respiratory complications

- Hematological DisordersCoagulopathies- Discontinue or modify anticoagulation therapies (warfarin, clopidogrel, ASA) in advance of elective surgeries. Administration of reversal agents if necessary: vitamin K, FFP, prothrombin complex concentrate, recombinant activated factor VII

- AspirationIncreased risk of aspiration with: decreased LOC, trauma, meals within 8 h, suspected sphincter incompetence (GERD, hiatus hernia, nasogastric tube), increased abdominal pressure (pregnancy, obesity, bowel obstruction, acute abdomen) and/or  laryngeal mask vs. endotracheal tube (ETT)

#### Management of usual medications and how they may interact with anaesthesia

Pay particular attention to **cardiac and respiratory medications**, **opioids and drugs** with many side ffects and interactions

**Pre-operative medications to consider- Prophylaxis  **

- Risk of GE reflux: sodium citrate 30 mL PO or ranitidine 150-300 mg PO or metoclopramide 10 mg PO 30 min to 1 h pre-operatively
- Risk of infective endocarditis, GI/GU interventions: antibiotics
- Risk of adrenal suppression: steroid coverage
- Anxiety: consider benzodiazepines
- COPD, asthma: bronchodilators
- CAD risk factors: nitroglycerin and β-blockers

**Pre-operative medications to stop**

- Oral hypoglycemic, Antidepressants, ACE Inhibitors, ARBs : stop on morning of surgery
- Warfarin (consider bridging with heparin), Anti-platelet agents (e.g. clopidogrel)  Discuss perioperative use of ASA, NSAIDs with surgeon
- In patients undergoing non-cardiac surgery, starting or continuing low-dose aspirin in the perioperative period does not appear to protect against post-operative MI or death, but increases the risk of major bleeding  (Note: Not applicable to patients with bare metal stents or drug-eluting coronary stents)

**Pre-operative medication to adjust**

- Insulin (consider insulin/dextrose infusion or holding dose)
- Prednisone
- Bronchodilators

#### Preanaesthetic Clinic

A pneumonic has been suggested for pre-anesthetic assessment:

- A - Affirmative history; Airway
- B - Blood hemoglobin, blood loss estimation, and blood availability; Breathing
- C - Clinical examination; Co-morbidities
- D - Drugs being used by the patient; Details of previous anesthesia and surgeries
- E - Evaluate investigations; End point to take up the case for surgery
- F - Fluid status; Fasting
- G - Give physical status; Get consent

#### History, Examination, Investigations

##### History

- Age, gender
- Indication for surgery
- Surgical/anesthetic Hx: previous anesthetics, any complications, previous intubations, medications, drug allergies
- FHx: abnormal anesthetic reactions, malignant hyperthermia, pseudocholinesterase deficiency
- PMHxCNS: seizures, TIA/strokes, spinal disease, aneurysm
- CVS: angina/CAD, MI, CHF, HTN,  valvular disease, dysrhythmias, PVD, conditions requiring endocarditis prophylaxis, exercise tolerance
- RESPIRATORY: smoking, asthma, COPD, recent upper respiratory tract infection, sleep apnea
- GI: GERD, liver disease,
- RENAL: insufficiency, dialysis, chronic kidney disease
- HEMATOLOGIC: anemia, coagulopathies
- MSK: conditions associated with difficult intubations e.g. Rheumatoid arthritis, cervical tumors, previous cervical spine surgery, myasthenia gravis
- ENDOCRINE: diabetes, thyroid disorders, adrenal disorders
- OTHER: morbid obesity, pregnancy, ethanol/other drug use

##### Physical Exam

- Weight, height, BP, pulse, respiratory rate
- Focused physical exam of the CNS, CVS, and respiratory systems
- General assessment of nutrition, hydration, and mental status
- Airway assessment
- Asses intubation difficulty
- Cervical spine stability and neck movement
- 3-2-1 Rule: Thyromental distance, Mouth opening, Anterior jaw subluxation
- Tongue size
- Dentition, dental appliances, existing chipped/loose teath
- Nasal passage patency
- Assess difficulty of ventilation
- Examination of anatomical sites relevant to lines and blocksBony landmarks and suitability of anatomy for regional anesthesia (if relevant)
- Sites for IV, central venous pressure (CVP), and pulmonary artery (PA) catheters

#### risks of anaesthesia

- Pain
- Nausea and vomiting
- Damage to teeth
- Sore throat and laryngeal damage
- Anaphylaxis to anaesthetic agents
- Cardiovascular collapse
- Respiratory depression
- Aspiration pneumonitis
- Hypothermia (triggered specifically by volatile anesthetic agents)
- Hypoxic brain damage
- Nerve injury
- Awareness during anaesthesia
- Embolism - air, thrombus, venous or arterial
- Backache
- Headache
- Confusion and agitation
- Hypotension/Hypertension
