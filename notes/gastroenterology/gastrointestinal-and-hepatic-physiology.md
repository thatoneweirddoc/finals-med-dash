## Gastrointestinal & Hepatic Physiology


### Gastroenterology Master Notes — Gastrointestinal & Hepatic Physiology

*The exam tests physiology as the substrate for the drugs and the diseases: acid secretion → PPIs and ulcers; bile/enterohepatic circulation → gallstones and B₁₂; bilirubin handling → the jaundice split; portal anatomy → varices. Learn the loop, and the clinical section reads off it.*

#### 1.1 Secretion

##### Gastric acid
The **parietal cell** (body/fundus) secretes H⁺ via the **H⁺/K⁺-ATPase ("proton pump")** — the final common step and the target of PPIs. Three stimulants converge on it, each with its own receptor:

| Stimulant | Source | Receptor on parietal cell | Blocked by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Histamine** | ECL cells | H₂ | H₂-antagonists (ranitidine, famotidine) |
| **Gastrin** | G cells (antrum) | CCK₂/gastrin | — (indirectly, via ↓ECL histamine) |
| **Acetylcholine** | Vagus | M₃ (muscarinic) | (antimuscarinics) |

Histamine is the **dominant/amplifying** pathway — which is why H₂-blockers work and why gastrin and ACh act partly *through* ECL histamine release. **Somatostatin** (D cells) is the master brake: it inhibits gastrin, histamine and acid. **Prostaglandins** (mucosal) inhibit acid and stimulate the protective mucus/bicarbonate layer — hence **NSAIDs (↓prostaglandins) cause ulcers**.

**Three phases of secretion:** **cephalic** (sight/smell/taste → vagal, ~30%), **gastric** (distension + peptides → gastrin, ~60%), **intestinal** (small, then inhibitory as chyme enters duodenum). **Intrinsic factor**, also from the parietal cell, is essential for **B₁₂ absorption in the terminal ileum** — lose the parietal cells (autoimmune/pernicious anaemia, gastrectomy) and you lose both acid and B₁₂.

##### Pancreatic exocrine secretion
Two components, two hormones:
- **Acinar cells → digestive enzymes** (amylase, lipase, proteases as inactive zymogens — trypsinogen activated by enterokinase to trypsin, which activates the rest). Driven by **CCK** (from I cells, in response to fat and protein).
- **Ductal cells → bicarbonate-rich alkaline fluid** that neutralises gastric acid. Driven by **secretin** (from S cells, in response to duodenal acid).

*Memory hook: **S**ecretin from **S**-cells for **S**odium bicarbonate (the "wash"); **C**CK for **C**ontents/enzymes and gall-bladder **C**ontraction.*

##### Bile and the enterohepatic circulation
Hepatocytes secrete **bile** (bile salts, cholesterol, phospholipids, bilirubin) into canaliculi → the biliary tree → stored/concentrated in the gall bladder → released on **CCK** (gall-bladder contraction + sphincter of Oddi relaxation) after a fatty meal. **Bile salts emulsify fat** into micelles for absorption. ~95% of bile salts are **reabsorbed in the terminal ileum** and recycled (the **enterohepatic circulation**) — so **terminal ileal disease/resection (Crohn's) causes bile-salt malabsorption** (→ watery diarrhoea, gallstones, and fat/fat-soluble-vitamin malabsorption).

#### 1.2 Motility

- **Swallowing:** oral (voluntary) → pharyngeal → oesophageal (peristalsis). The **lower oesophageal sphincter (LOS)** relaxes to let the bolus through; failure of LOS relaxation = **achalasia**; incompetent LOS = **GORD**.
- **Stomach:** receptive relaxation/**accommodation** (vagally mediated — lost after vagotomy), mixing (antral contractions), and controlled **emptying**. Liquids empty fast, solids slower.
- **Migrating motor complex (MMC):** the "housekeeper" — cyclical fasting-state sweeps that clear residue; driven by **motilin** (the target of erythromycin as a prokinetic).
- **Small/large bowel:** segmentation (mixing) and peristalsis (propulsion); the **gastrocolic reflex** (eating → colonic mass movement → urge to defecate). **Defecation** relies on the recto-anal inhibitory reflex and voluntary external anal sphincter (pudendal, S2–4).
- **The enteric nervous system** ("the gut's own brain": myenteric/Auerbach = motility; submucosal/Meissner = secretion) runs the gut semi-autonomously, modulated by the vagus and sympathetics. Loss of enteric ganglia = **Hirschsprung's** (aganglionic segment → functional obstruction).

**The gut hormones worth knowing:**

| Hormone | Source | Main action | Exam hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gastrin** | G cells (antrum) | ↑Acid | ↑↑↑ in **Zollinger–Ellison** |
| **CCK** | I cells (duodenum) | Gall-bladder contraction, pancreatic enzymes, satiety | Fat/protein trigger |
| **Secretin** | S cells (duodenum) | Pancreatic/biliary **bicarbonate**, ↓acid | Acid trigger |
| **GIP** | K cells | ↑Insulin (incretin) | — |
| **GLP-1** | L cells (ileum) | ↑Insulin, ↓glucagon, ↓emptying, satiety | Basis of **GLP-1 agonists** |
| **Somatostatin** | D cells | Inhibits nearly everything | Octreotide (varices, VIPoma) |
| **Motilin** | M cells | MMC / gastric emptying | Erythromycin agonist |
| **Ghrelin** | Stomach (fundus) | **Hunger** ("ghrowling") | ↑ before meals |

#### 1.3 Digestion and Absorption

- **Carbohydrate:** salivary/pancreatic amylase → oligosaccharides → brush-border disaccharidases (lactase, sucrase) → monosaccharides absorbed (SGLT1, GLUT). **Lactase deficiency → lactose intolerance** (osmotic diarrhoea, bloating).
- **Protein:** pepsin (stomach) + pancreatic proteases → amino acids/peptides absorbed.
- **Fat:** bile-salt emulsification + pancreatic lipase → micelles → absorbed → chylomicrons. Fat malabsorption → **steatorrhoea** (pale, greasy, floating, offensive stool) + deficiency of **fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, K** (K → coagulopathy).

**Site of absorption (high yield):**

| Nutrient | Main site |
|---|---|
| Iron, folate, calcium | **Duodenum / proximal jejunum** |
| Most nutrients (carbs, protein, fat) | Jejunum |
| **Vitamin B₁₂ (with intrinsic factor), bile salts** | **Terminal ileum** |
| Water, electrolytes, short-chain fatty acids | Colon |

*Discriminator: **terminal ileal disease (Crohn's, resection) = B₁₂ deficiency + bile-salt diarrhoea + gallstones**; proximal small-bowel disease (coeliac) = iron/folate deficiency.*

#### 1.4 Liver Physiology and Bilirubin

The liver has a **dual blood supply** — ~75% from the **portal vein** (nutrient-rich, deoxygenated, draining the gut) and ~25% from the **hepatic artery** — mixing in the sinusoids. The functional acinus is zoned: **zone 1 (periportal)** is best oxygenated; **zone 3 (pericentral/centrilobular)** is most susceptible to **ischaemia** and to **paracetamol/toxic** injury (highest cytochrome P450 activity).

**Functions (a memory frame):** **Synthetic** (albumin, clotting factors — all except VIII, so a rising **INR/PT** is an early marker of failing synthesis), **Metabolic/detoxifying** (drugs; **ammonia → urea** — lost in liver failure → encephalopathy), **Storage** (glycogen, vitamins A/D/B₁₂, iron, copper), **Immune** (Kupffer cells), and **Excretory** (bilirubin, bile).

**Bilirubin metabolism — the basis of the jaundice split:**
1. Haem breakdown → **unconjugated (indirect)** bilirubin — fat-soluble, albumin-bound, **not water-soluble → not in urine**.
2. Hepatocyte **conjugation** by UGT1A1 → **conjugated (direct)** bilirubin — water-soluble.
3. Excreted in bile → gut bacteria → **urobilinogen** (some reabsorbed) and **stercobilin** (brown stool).

| Type | Bilirubin raised | Urine | Stool | Classic causes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-hepatic** | Unconjugated | Normal (no bilirubin; ↑urobilinogen) | Normal | Haemolysis, **Gilbert's** |
| **Hepatic** | Both | Dark (bilirubin +) | Normal/pale | Hepatitis, cirrhosis |
| **Post-hepatic (obstructive)** | Conjugated | **Dark** (bilirubin +) | **Pale** | Gallstone, pancreatic Ca, stricture |

*Discriminator: **pale stool + dark urine + itch = obstructive (conjugated)**; unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia with otherwise normal LFTs and no bilirubinuria = **Gilbert's or haemolysis**.*

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — GASTROINTESTINAL STROMAL TUMOUR — Gastrointestinal & Hepatic Physiology

- Most common mesenchymal neoplasm of GI tract
- Derived from interstitial cells of Cajal (Auerbach’s plexus 🡪 autonomous pacemaker function 🡪 peristalsis)
- RISK FACTORS/AETIOLOGY: tyrosine kinase mutations (75-80%), Type IA neurofibromatosis, Carney’s triad
- Most common in stomach (50%), proximal SI (25%) but can occur anywhere along GI tract
- SIGNS/SYMPTOMS: vague abdominal mass, feeling of abdominal fullness with secondary bleeding or anaemia
- Often incidental finding on CT, laparotomy, endoscopy
- TREATMENT: Surgical resection (resection &gt; 2cm, endoscopy &lt;2cm), localised GIST (surgical resection, lymphadenectomy, adjuvant chemo with Imatinib), advanced disease (palliative intent chemo Imatinib, metastectomy)
- PROGNOSIS: Metastatic potential depends on tumour size (&gt;10cm), mitotic activity (&gt;5 figures), nuclear pleomorphism, location (extra-gastric location); metastases to liver, omentum, peritoneum, nodal
