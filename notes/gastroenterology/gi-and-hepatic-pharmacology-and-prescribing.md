## GI & Hepatic Pharmacology & Prescribing


### Gastroenterology Master Notes — GI & Hepatic Pharmacology & Prescribing

*The consolidated drug section. Organised by target; the discriminators are the mechanism, the classic adverse effect, and the exam "trap."*

##### 9.1 Acid suppression and gut motility drugs

**⚑ Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs — omeprazole, lansoprazole, pantoprazole):** irreversibly block the **H⁺/K⁺-ATPase**; first-line for GORD, peptic ulcers, *H. pylori* regimens, and NSAID gastroprotection. **Long-term harms to know:** hypomagnesaemia, **B₁₂/iron malabsorption**, increased **fracture** risk, **C. difficile** and enteric infection, **microscopic colitis**, hyponatraemia, and masking of gastric cancer symptoms. **Deprescribe/review long-term PPIs** where no ongoing indication (⚑ — over-prescription is a real exam/QI theme).

**H₂-receptor antagonists (famotidine):** block histamine H₂ on the parietal cell — weaker acid suppression. *(Ranitidine was withdrawn over NDMA contamination — don't quote it as first-line. ⚑)*

**Antacids/alginates** (Gaviscon) — symptomatic reflux relief.

**Prokinetics:** **metoclopramide** (D₂ antagonist — **extrapyramidal side effects**, avoid in young/Parkinson's), **domperidone** (D₂, doesn't cross BBB but **QT prolongation**), **erythromycin** (motilin agonist).

**Antiemetics — by receptor target (high yield):**

| Drug | Target | Best for | Watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ondansetron** | 5-HT₃ antagonist | Chemo, post-op | Constipation, **QT** |
| **Metoclopramide/domperidone** | D₂ antagonist | Gastric stasis, GI causes | EPSEs / QT |
| **Cyclizine** | H₁ antihistamine | Motion, vestibular, mechanical | Antimuscarinic |
| **Hyoscine** | Antimuscarinic | Motion sickness, secretions | Dry mouth |
| **Aprepitant** | NK₁ antagonist | Chemo (delayed) | — |

**Laxatives:** bulk (ispaghula), osmotic (**lactulose, macrogol**), stimulant (senna, bisacodyl), softeners. **Antidiarrhoeals:** **loperamide** (avoid in acute infective/inflammatory colitis — precipitates megacolon). **Antispasmodics:** mebeverine, hyoscine (IBS).

##### 9.2 IBD, biologic and immunomodulator therapy

- **5-ASAs (mesalazine, sulfasalazine):** topical anti-inflammatory for **UC** (induction + maintenance); sulfasalazine can cause reversible **oligospermia**, rash.
- **Corticosteroids:** flares only (not maintenance); **budesonide** (high first-pass metabolism → fewer systemic effects) for ileocaecal Crohn's/microscopic colitis.
- **Thiopurines (azathioprine, mercaptopurine):** steroid-sparing maintenance — **check TPMT activity first** (low TPMT → myelosuppression); monitor FBC/LFTs; risk of pancreatitis, hepatotoxicity, lymphoma/skin cancer.
- **Methotrexate:** Crohn's maintenance; folate, teratogenic, hepatotoxic, pneumonitis.
- **⚑ Biologics & small molecules (the modern step-up):** **anti-TNF** (infliximab, adalimumab, golimumab), **anti-integrin** (vedolizumab — gut-selective), **anti-IL-12/23** (ustekinumab) and **anti-IL-23** (risankizumab, mirikizumab), **JAK inhibitors** (tofacitinib, upadacitinib, filgotinib), **S1P modulators** (ozanimod, etrasimod). **Pre-biologic safety screen:** **latent TB (IGRA), hepatitis B/C, VZV**, and vaccination status — **anti-TNF can reactivate TB and hepatitis B.**

##### 9.3 Liver, pancreas and prescribing safety

**Hepatotoxins / DILI:** **paracetamol** (dose-dependent), and idiosyncratic — isoniazid, rifampicin, methotrexate, amiodarone, statins, augmentin (cholestatic), anti-epileptics, flucloxacillin. **N-acetylcysteine** is the paracetamol antidote (also used in non-paracetamol ALF).

**Drugs in liver/complication management:**
- **Lactulose + rifaximin** — hepatic encephalopathy.
- **Terlipressin** — variceal bleeding and hepatorenal syndrome (+ albumin).
- **Spironolactone (± furosemide)** — ascites.
- **Non-selective β-blockers (propranolol/carvedilol)** — variceal prophylaxis.
- **Ursodeoxycholic acid** — PBC (and gallstone dissolution in selected cases).
- **Pancreatic enzyme replacement (Creon)** — exocrine insufficiency.

**Prescribing safety themes:**
- **Prescribing in liver disease:** avoid/adjust hepatically-metabolised and sedating drugs (precipitate encephalopathy — **avoid opioids/benzodiazepines**); NSAIDs worsen ascites/renal function and bleeding risk; watch **coagulopathy** and **drug-induced cholestasis**.
- **In the GI-bleed patient:** stop NSAIDs/antiplatelets/anticoagulants where possible and reverse; PPI after endoscopic therapy.
- **General GI safety traps:** metoclopramide EPSEs; loperamide in colitis; abrupt long-term PPI issues; **TPMT before thiopurines**; TB/hepatitis screen before anti-TNF.

##### Part A — By Presenting Syndrome

###### A1. Dysphagia
| If you also see… | Think | Clincher |
|---|---|---|
| Progressive **solids → liquids** + weight loss | **Oesophageal cancer / stricture** | OGD + biopsy |
| **Solids and liquids together** from the start | **Achalasia / motility** | Barium "bird's-beak", manometry |
| Atopic young adult, food bolus stuck | **Eosinophilic oesophagitis** | ≥15 eosinophils/hpf |
| Iron-deficiency anaemia + web | **Plummer–Vinson** | — |
| Regurgitation of undigested food, halitosis, neck lump | **Pharyngeal pouch (Zenker's)** | Barium (don't blind-scope) |

###### A2. Dyspepsia / epigastric pain
- **Pain relieved by food** → **duodenal ulcer**; **worse with food + weight loss** → **gastric ulcer** (biopsy — could be cancer).
- **ALARM features** (anaemia, weight loss, dysphagia, melaena, ≥55 new) → **urgent OGD**.
- Radiating to the **back** → pancreatitis (lipase) or AAA. Epigastric + cardiac risk → exclude **MI**.

###### A3. Right-upper-quadrant pain
| Clue | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| Post-fatty-meal pain <6 h, no fever | **Biliary colic** |
| Constant pain + fever + **Murphy's sign** | **Acute cholecystitis** |
| Pain + fever + **jaundice** (Charcot's triad) | **Ascending cholangitis** |
| + hypotension + confusion (Reynolds' pentad) | **Severe cholangitis** → urgent ERCP |
| RUQ pain + deranged transaminases | **Hepatitis** |
| RUQ + right-shoulder tip, sexually active woman | **Fitz-Hugh–Curtis** (perihepatitis) |

###### A4. Jaundice
- **Pale stool + dark urine + itch** = **obstructive (post-hepatic)** → US then MRCP/ERCP.
- **Painless** obstructive jaundice + palpable gallbladder (**Courvoisier**) = **pancreatic/biliary malignancy**.
- Jaundice + **AST:ALT >2** = alcohol; + thousands of transaminases = ischaemia/paracetamol/acute viral.
- Isolated **unconjugated** with normal LFTs, on fasting = **Gilbert's**; with anaemia/↑LDH/↓haptoglobin = **haemolysis**.

###### A5. GI bleeding
| Clue | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| **Melaena + raised urea** | **Upper GI bleed** (ulcer/varices) |
| Retching/vomiting **then** haematemesis | **Mallory–Weiss** |
| Chronic liver disease + massive haematemesis | **Variceal bleed** (terlipressin + antibiotics) |
| Painless large-volume fresh PR blood, elderly | **Diverticular** / angiodysplasia |
| Altered habit + weight loss + PR blood | **Colorectal cancer** |
| Bloody diarrhoea + pain | **Colitis** (IBD/infective/ischaemic) |
| Bright red on paper only | **Haemorrhoids / fissure** |

###### A6. Chronic diarrhoea
- **Bloody + urgency + systemic** → **IBD** (calprotectin ↑).
- **Steatorrhoea + weight loss** → malabsorption (**coeliac**, **pancreatic insufficiency** — low elastase).
- **No red flags, normal calprotectin, pain + habit change** → **IBS** (Rome IV).
- Terminal-ileal history/resection → **bile-salt malabsorption**; recurrent antibiotics → **C. difficile**.

###### A7. The acute abdomen
| Pattern | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| Colicky pain, vomiting, distension, absolute constipation | **Bowel obstruction** |
| Sudden severe pain, rigid board-like abdomen, free air | **Perforation/peritonitis** |
| **Pain out of proportion** + AF + ↑lactate | **Mesenteric ischaemia** |
| Central pain **migrating to RIF** + fever | **Appendicitis** |
| LIF pain + fever ("left-sided appendicitis") | **Diverticulitis** |
| Any woman of childbearing age | Always exclude **ectopic (β-hCG)** |

|---|---|

##### Part C — Confusable Pairs *(reduced to the one separating feature)*

| Pair | The single separating feature |
|---|---|
| **Duodenal vs gastric ulcer** | Duodenal **relieved** by food; gastric **worse** with food (+ biopsy — cancer risk) |
| **Crohn's vs ulcerative colitis** | Crohn's = **transmural, skip lesions, granulomas, whole GI**; UC = **mucosal, continuous, colon only** |
| **Smoking in IBD** | **Worsens Crohn's; protects in UC** |
| **IBS vs IBD** | **Faecal calprotectin** (raised in IBD, normal in IBS) |
| **Mechanical vs motility dysphagia** | Mechanical = solids→liquids progressive; motility = **solids + liquids together** |
| **Oesophageal adeno vs squamous** | Adeno = **lower third, GORD/Barrett's**; squamous = **upper ⅔, smoking/alcohol** |
| **Biliary colic vs cholecystitis** | Cholecystitis adds **fever + Murphy's sign** (inflammation) |
| **Cholecystitis vs cholangitis** | Cholangitis adds **jaundice** (Charcot's triad — CBD infection) |
| **Choledocholithiasis: MRCP vs ERCP** | **MRCP diagnoses, ERCP treats** |
| **Amylase vs lipase** | **Lipase** more sensitive/specific (amylase can be normal if late/alcoholic) |
| **PBC vs PSC** | PBC = **AMA, woman, itch**; PSC = **pANCA, man, UC, beaded ducts** |
| **Autoimmune hepatitis vs PBC** | AIH = **hepatocellular (↑ALT, ASMA)**; PBC = **cholestatic (↑ALP, AMA)** |
| **Haemochromatosis vs Wilson's** | Haemochromatosis = **iron, older, bronze diabetes**; Wilson's = **copper, <40 y, KF rings/neuro** |
| **Pre- vs post-hepatic jaundice** | Post-hepatic = **pale stool + dark urine + itch** (conjugated, bilirubinuria) |
| **Gilbert's vs haemolysis** | Both unconjugated; haemolysis has **anaemia + ↑LDH + ↓haptoglobin** |
| **Acute vs chronic pancreatitis** | Chronic = **calcification + low faecal elastase + steatorrhoea/diabetes** |
| **Small- vs large-bowel obstruction** | AXR: **valvulae conniventes (full width)** vs **haustra (partial)**; SBO vomits early |
| **Sigmoid vs caecal volvulus** | Sigmoid = **elderly, coffee-bean, decompress by tube**; caecal = younger, surgery |
| **Simple obstruction vs strangulation** | Strangulation adds **fever, tachycardia, peritonism, ↑lactate** |
| **Diverticulitis vs appendicitis** | Side: **LIF** (diverticulitis) vs migrating to **RIF** (appendicitis) |
| **Upper vs lower GI bleed** | **Melaena + raised urea = upper**; fresh red PR + normal urea = lower |
| **Courvoisier: stone vs cancer** | **Palpable painless gallbladder → malignancy** (a stone-scarred GB won't distend) |
| **Hep A/E vs B/C/D** | A/E = **faeco-oral, acute**; B/C/D = **blood-borne, chronic** |
| **NAFLD/NASH vs MASLD/MASH** | Same disease — **new positive definition needing a cardiometabolic risk factor** ⚑ |

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — crohn’s disease — GI & Hepatic Pharmacology & Prescribing

- DEFINITION: Chronic transmural GIT inflammation that may involve the entire GIT from mouth to perianal area
- EPIDEMIOLOGY: Bimodal distribution (15-40 years, 60-80 years), M=F, ↑ incidence in Caucasians and Ashkenazi Jews
- PATHOGENESIS: Current theory involves inappropriate mucosal immune response to commensal bacteria in the gut lumen in a genetically susceptible host that might have been exposed to environmental triggers. Initially inflammatory infiltrate around intestinal crypts 🡪 ulceration of superficial mucosa 🡪 progresses to involve deeper layers 🡪 thickens the bowel wall 🡪 scarring, luminal narrowing, stricture formation 🡪 obstruction + ↓absorption.
- Most common location is ileum and ascending colon.
- VIENNA CLASSIFICATION OF CD

![](notes/images/general-surgery-and-gastroenterology-anu/img012.jpg)

- RISK FACTORS: Caucasian, age, FHx, smoking, OCP, ↑refined sugar
- HISTORYCharacteristic triad – abdominal pain (cramping/constant usually in RLQ/peri-umbilical regions; exacerbated by eating), diarrhoea (possible constipation/obstipation in obstruction), weight loss
- Fevers, fatigue, haematochezia (microscopic/gross), oral (ulcers, mouth/gum pain), oesophageal (dysphagia, odynophagia), gastroduodenal (upper abd pain, gastric outlet obstruction), perianal (pain, skin tags, anal fissures, abscesses)

- INVESTIGATIONS
- FBC (anaemia, leucocytosis), iron studies (to demonstrate consistency with iron deficiency), serum B12, serum folate, CMP (in severe/chronic disease 🡪 ↓albumin, ↓cholesterol, ↓ Ca2+), CRP and ESR, stool testing, AXR (bowel wall thickening, bowel/colonic dilatation, calcification, sacroiliitis, abscess), CT abdomen (localisation of disease, fistula, abscess, extra-mural complications), MRI abdomen/pelvis (when IV contrast contraindicated)
- Colonoscopy/gastroscopy: exploration, tissue biopsy, skip lesions (focal ulceration adjacent to normal mucosa), polypoid mucosal changes (cobblestone appearance)
- DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSES: UC, IBS, CRC, infectious colitis (diarrhoea, acute Sx, infections with Shigella/ Salmonella/  Campylobacter/ E.Coli/ C.Diff/ CMV)
- TREATMENT AND MANAGEMENT
- Lifestyle: smoking cessation, fluids in acute exacerbations, enteral diet for symptomatic management, supplementation (Vit D, Ca2+, Mg2+, Zn, Fe, B12)
- PharmacologyFor mild disease: 5-ASA compounds (aminosalicylates) e.g. sulfazaline/mesalamine (↑ effective in mild UC)
- For moderate disease: immunomodulators e.g. 6-mercaptopurine (6-MP, azathioprine, and methotrexate (less often) often used to maintain remission and as steroid-sparing agents; may require &gt;3 mo for beneficial effect
- For severe disease: biologics (anti-TNF agents) e.g. Infliximab IV or adalimumab SC and proven for effective treatment of fistulae and medically refractory CD
- For flares: steroids e.g. prednisolone, methylprednisolone or IV hydrocortisone + antibiotics (metronidazole/ ciprofloxacin)

- Surgery: generally reserved for medically refractory disease and complications; not curative
- COMPLICATIONS:
- Fistulas (enterovesical, retroperitoneum, enterovaginal, enterocutaneous)
- Extraintestinal (arthritis large joints, eye involvement uveitis/iritis/episcleritis, skin involvement erythaema nodosum/pyoderma gangrenosum, primary sclerosing cholangitis, secondary amyloidosis)
- Malnutrition (↓ Vit D 🡪 osteopenia,  ↓B12 🡪 pernicious anaemia)
- PROGNOSIS: patients typically have intermittent exacerbation followed by remission, mild ↓ in life expectancy
