## GI Bleeding & the Acute Abdomen


### Gastroenterology Master Notes — GI Bleeding & the Acute Abdomen

#### 6.1 Upper GI bleeding

**Upper GI bleed = bleeding proximal to the ligament of Treitz** → **haematemesis** (fresh red or "coffee-ground") and/or **melaena** (black, tarry, offensive — digested blood; ~50 mL is enough). Brisk upper bleeding can present as fresh PR blood (haematochezia) with haemodynamic compromise.

**Causes (know the top few):**

| Cause | Clue |
|---|---|
| **Peptic ulcer** (commonest) | NSAID/*H. pylori*; posterior duodenal → gastroduodenal artery |
| **Oesophageal/gastric varices** | Chronic liver disease/portal hypertension; often massive |
| **Mallory–Weiss tear** | **Vomiting/retching → then haematemesis** (e.g. after alcohol) |
| **Oesophagitis/gastritis** | Reflux, alcohol, drugs |
| **Malignancy** | Weight loss, dyspepsia |
| **Dieulafoy lesion / angiodysplasia** | Recurrent obscure bleeding |

**Immediate management (ABCDE + resuscitate first, scope second):**
1. **A–B–C**, two large-bore cannulae, bloods incl. **crossmatch**, **group & save**, U&E (**urea disproportionately raised** — digested blood protein), LFTs, clotting, VBG/lactate.
2. **Fluid resuscitation**; **⚑ restrictive red-cell transfusion — transfuse at Hb <70 g/L, target 70–90 g/L** (a restrictive strategy improves survival — Villanueva; over-transfusion worsens variceal bleeding). Correct coagulopathy/platelets; reverse anticoagulation as indicated.
3. **Risk-score:**
   - **Glasgow–Blatchford score (GBS)** — used **pre-endoscopy** to decide admission; **a score of 0–1 may allow safe outpatient management**. Based on urea, Hb, systolic BP, pulse, melaena, syncope, hepatic/cardiac disease.
   - **Rockall score** — **post-endoscopy**, predicts rebleeding/mortality (age, shock, comorbidity, diagnosis, stigmata of recent haemorrhage).
4. **Endoscopy within 24 h** (immediately after resuscitation if unstable/variceal).

**Non-variceal (e.g. peptic ulcer):** **endoscopic haemostasis** — two modalities (adrenaline injection **plus** a second: clips/thermal/haemospray). **⚑ Give PPI *after* endoscopic therapy** for high-risk stigmata (not routinely before). Test and treat *H. pylori*; stop NSAIDs.

**Variceal bleed (a distinct pathway — high yield):**
- **Terlipressin** (splanchnic vasoconstrictor) **+ prophylactic antibiotics** (e.g. ceftriaxone — reduces mortality) started **immediately**, even before endoscopy.
- **Endoscopic band ligation** (oesophageal); **injection/glue** or **balloon tamponade (Sengstaken–Blakemore)** as a bridge for uncontrolled bleeding; **TIPS** (transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt) for refractory bleeding.
- **Secondary prevention:** **non-selective β-blocker (propranolol/carvedilol)** + repeat banding.

#### 6.2 Lower GI bleeding

**Bleeding distal to the ligament of Treitz** → usually **fresh/dark red PR blood** (haematochezia). **Causes by likelihood/age:**

| Age / pattern | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Older, painless, large-volume | **Diverticular disease** (commonest), **angiodysplasia** |
| Older, altered habit/weight loss | **Colorectal cancer** |
| Any age, bloody diarrhoea + pain | **Colitis** (IBD, infective, ischaemic) |
| Bright red on paper/around stool | **Haemorrhoids / anal fissure** |
| Young | Meckel's diverticulum, IBD, polyps |

**Approach:** resuscitate as above; **exclude a brisk upper GI source** (a bleeding duodenal ulcer can mimic lower bleeding — consider OGD/NG aspirate, and note the **raised urea** of an upper bleed). Investigate with **colonoscopy** (once stable/prepped); **CT angiography** for active brisk bleeding (can go to embolisation). Most lower GI bleeds settle spontaneously.

*Discriminator: **melaena + raised urea = upper**; fresh red PR blood with normal urea = lower. **Massive haematochezia with shock can still be a fast upper bleed** — don't be caught out.*

#### 6.3 The acute abdomen — obstruction, perforation, ischaemia

**Bowel obstruction:**

| | **Small-bowel obstruction** | **Large-bowel obstruction** |
|---|---|---|
| Commonest causes | **Adhesions** (previous surgery), **hernias** | **Colorectal cancer**, **volvulus**, diverticular stricture |
| Vomiting | **Early**, bilious | Late |
| Distension | Less | **More** |
| Pain | Colicky, central | Colicky, lower |
| AXR | Central loops, **valvulae conniventes** (cross the full width) | Peripheral loops, **haustra** (partial) |

Features: **colicky pain, vomiting, distension, absolute constipation** (no flatus), tinkling then absent bowel sounds. Management: **"drip and suck"** (NBM, IV fluids, **NG tube** decompression), correct electrolytes, imaging (**CT** to find cause/level and spot ischaemia); surgery for a cause needing it or for **strangulation/ischaemia** (fever, tachycardia, peritonism, ↑lactate — the sign a simple obstruction has become an emergency).
- **Sigmoid volvulus** — elderly/constipated/institutionalised; **"coffee-bean" sign** on AXR; decompress with a **flatus tube/sigmoidoscopy**.
- **Caecal volvulus** — younger; usually needs surgery.
- Mnemonic for the causes of distension — **the 6 Fs:** Fat, Fluid, Flatus, Faeces, Fetus, "Fatal growth" (tumour).

**Perforation and peritonitis:** sudden severe pain, rigid "board-like" abdomen, involuntary guarding, rebound tenderness, absent bowel sounds, shock. **Free air under the diaphragm on erect CXR** (or free gas on CT). Causes: perforated peptic ulcer, diverticulitis, appendicitis, obstruction, cancer, ischaemia. Management: resuscitate, IV antibiotics, analgesia, and usually **urgent laparotomy**.

**Mesenteric ischaemia (acute):** the classic **"pain out of proportion to examination"** — severe central pain with an initially soft abdomen. Causes: **SMA embolus (AF!)**, SMA thrombosis, mesenteric venous thrombosis, non-occlusive (low-flow). **Metabolic acidosis + raised lactate** is the red flag. Diagnose **CT angiography**; this is a surgical emergency (revascularisation/resection) — high mortality if delayed.
- **Ischaemic colitis** (a milder, more common cousin) — the **splenic-flexure watershed**, transient bloody diarrhoea + LIF pain, "thumbprinting" on imaging; usually managed conservatively.

**Appendicitis:** central (periumbilical, visceral) pain **migrating to the right iliac fossa** (McBurney's, somatic), anorexia, low-grade fever, RIF tenderness/guarding, Rovsing's/psoas signs. Largely a **clinical diagnosis** (imaging/US/CT if uncertain, especially to avoid missing other pathology in women); treat with **appendicectomy** (± antibiotics).

**The acute abdomen by quadrant (a rapid triage):**

| Location | Think first |
|---|---|
| **RUQ** | Cholecystitis/biliary, hepatitis, right lower-lobe pneumonia |
| **Epigastric** | Peptic ulcer/perforation, pancreatitis, MI, AAA |
| **RIF** | Appendicitis, ectopic pregnancy, ovarian, Crohn's, mesenteric adenitis |
| **LIF** | Diverticulitis, colitis, ovarian, constipation |
| **Loin → groin** | Renal/ureteric colic, AAA |
| **Central/diffuse + shock** | Mesenteric ischaemia, ruptured **AAA**, perforation |

*Always exclude the killers early: **ruptured AAA, mesenteric ischaemia, ectopic pregnancy (β-hCG in every woman of childbearing age), MI, perforation**.*

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators — Section 6

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Black tarry offensive stool | **Melaena** (upper GI bleed) |
| Urea disproportionately raised with GI bleed | **Upper** GI bleed (digested blood protein) |
| Vomiting/retching **then** haematemesis | **Mallory–Weiss tear** |
| Transfusion threshold in GI bleed | **Hb <70 g/L**, target 70–90 ⚑ |
| Pre-endoscopy risk score (can allow outpatient) | **Glasgow–Blatchford (0–1)** |
| Post-endoscopy risk score | **Rockall** |
| When to give PPI in bleeding ulcer | **After** endoscopic therapy ⚑ |
| Immediate drugs in variceal bleed | **Terlipressin + prophylactic antibiotics** |
| First-line endoscopic therapy for oesophageal varices | **Band ligation** |
| Bridge for uncontrolled variceal bleed | **Balloon tamponade (Sengstaken–Blakemore)** → TIPS |
| Secondary prevention of variceal bleed | **Non-selective β-blocker + banding** |
| Commonest cause of lower GI bleed | **Diverticular disease** |
| Small-bowel vs large-bowel AXR sign | **Valvulae conniventes** (full width) vs **haustra** |
| Obstruction management | **"Drip and suck"** (NBM, IV fluids, NG) |
| "Coffee-bean" sign on AXR | **Sigmoid volvulus** |
| Board-like rigid abdomen + free air | **Perforation/peritonitis** |
| Pain **out of proportion** + AF + ↑lactate | **Acute mesenteric ischaemia (SMA embolus)** |
| Central pain migrating to RIF | **Appendicitis** |
| Must-exclude test in any woman with acute abdomen | **β-hCG** (ectopic) |
