## Hepatology


### Gastroenterology Master Notes — Hepatology

#### 7.1 Jaundice and the approach to abnormal LFTs

**Jaundice** (visible when bilirubin >~50 µmol/L) splits three ways — this algorithm underlies most hepatology data questions (see also Section 1.4):

| Type | Bilirubin | Other LFTs | Urine/stool | Causes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-hepatic** | Unconjugated ↑ | Otherwise normal | Normal urine, normal stool | **Haemolysis, Gilbert's** |
| **Hepatic** | Both ↑ | ↑↑ALT/AST | Dark urine | Hepatitis, cirrhosis, drugs |
| **Post-hepatic** | Conjugated ↑ | ↑ALP/γGT | **Dark urine, pale stool, itch** | **Gallstone, pancreatic/biliary Ca, stricture, PBC/PSC** |

**Inherited hyperbilirubinaemias:** **Gilbert's syndrome** (common, benign; reduced UGT1A1 → **isolated unconjugated** hyperbilirubinaemia, jaundice on **fasting/stress/illness**, otherwise normal LFTs — no treatment, reassure); Crigler–Najjar (severe unconjugated); **Dubin–Johnson / Rotor** (conjugated, benign).

#### 7.2 Acute liver failure

**Acute liver failure = acute liver injury + coagulopathy (INR ≥1.5) + hepatic encephalopathy in someone without pre-existing cirrhosis** (classified by the jaundice-to-encephalopathy interval: hyperacute/acute/subacute).

**Causes:** **paracetamol overdose** (commonest in the UK/West), viral hepatitis (commonest worldwide — A/B/E), drug reactions (idiosyncratic DILI), ischaemia, Wilson's, Budd–Chiari, acute fatty liver of pregnancy, amanita mushroom poisoning. Complications drive the danger: **encephalopathy + cerebral oedema, coagulopathy/bleeding, hypoglycaemia, AKI, sepsis, multi-organ failure**.

**⚑ Paracetamol overdose (very high yield):**
- Toxic metabolite **NAPQI** depletes glutathione → hepatocyte necrosis (zone 3).
- **Plot the paracetamol level against time on the treatment nomogram** — a **single line at 100 mg/L at 4 h → 15 mg/L at 15 h** is now used for everyone (the "high-risk" line was abolished in the UK, treating more people). Bloods: paracetamol level (≥4 h post-ingestion), LFTs, **INR**, U&E, VBG/lactate, glucose.
- **Antidote: N-acetylcysteine (NAC)** — replenishes glutathione. Give **immediately if**: level above the nomogram line; **staggered/uncertain-time** overdose; or presenting late with abnormal INR/ALT. Don't wait for a level if presentation is late or staggered.
- **King's College criteria for transplant referral (paracetamol ALF):** **arterial pH <7.30** after resuscitation, **OR all three of** — **INR >6.5 (PT >100 s)**, **creatinine >300 µmol/L**, and **grade III/IV encephalopathy**. (An arterial **lactate >3.5 mmol/L** early, or >3.0 after fluid resuscitation, is an additional adverse marker.)

#### 7.3 Cirrhosis and portal hypertension

**Cirrhosis** = diffuse **fibrosis + regenerative nodules** (irreversible architectural distortion) → two problems: **portal hypertension** and **hepatic synthetic/detoxifying failure**. Commonest causes: **alcohol, MASLD/MASH, chronic viral hepatitis (B/C)**; then autoimmune, PBC/PSC, haemochromatosis, Wilson's, α₁-antitrypsin.

**Compensated vs decompensated** — **decompensation** is defined by the appearance of **jaundice, ascites, variceal bleeding, or encephalopathy**. **Severity scores:**
- **Child–Pugh (A/B/C)** — **"ABCDE": Albumin, Bilirubin, Clotting (INR/PT), Distension (ascites), Encephalopathy**. Grades severity/surgical risk.
- **⚑ MELD / MELD-Na** — bilirubin, INR, creatinine (+ **sodium**) — used for **transplant prioritisation** and prognosis; MELD-Na now standard (adds hyponatraemia as an adverse factor).

**The complications set (learn these six as a block):**

**1. Variceal bleeding** — see Section 6.1 (terlipressin + antibiotics + banding; NSBB + banding for prevention).

**2. Ascites** — portal hypertension + **hypoalbuminaemia** + splanchnic vasodilatation/RAAS activation → Na⁺/water retention. Diagnose and **always tap (diagnostic ascitic tap)**:
- **Serum–ascites albumin gradient (SAAG)**: **≥11 g/L = portal hypertension** (transudate: cirrhosis, heart failure, Budd–Chiari); **<11 g/L** = other (malignancy, TB, pancreatitis, nephrotic).
- **Management:** **salt restriction + spironolactone** (± furosemide), **fluid restriction if Na⁺ <125**, therapeutic paracentesis (with **albumin cover** for large volumes), and **TIPS** for refractory ascites.

**3. Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP)** — infection of ascites **without a surgical source**. Suspect in any decompensation/fever/abdominal pain/encephalopathy in an ascitic patient. **Diagnosis: ascitic neutrophils (PMN) ≥250 cells/mm³.** Commonest organism **E. coli**. **Treat with IV cefotaxime/piperacillin-tazobactam + IV albumin** (reduces hepatorenal syndrome/mortality). **Secondary prophylaxis: oral ciprofloxacin/norfloxacin.**

**4. Hepatic encephalopathy** — **ammonia** and other toxins cross to the brain → confusion, **asterixis (flap)**, altered consciousness (graded I–IV). **Precipitants (treat these!): GI bleed, infection/SBP, constipation, dehydration/diuretics, electrolyte disturbance, sedatives, high protein load.** **Treatment: lactulose** (traps ammonia in the gut, ≥2–3 soft stools/day) + **rifaximin** (non-absorbed antibiotic, reduces ammonia-producing flora) + correct the precipitant.

**5. Hepatorenal syndrome (HRS)** — functional renal failure from splanchnic vasodilatation → renal vasoconstriction, in advanced liver disease, **with structurally normal kidneys** (diagnosis of exclusion — no other cause, no response to volume). Treat: **terlipressin + albumin**; definitive = **liver transplant**.

**6. Hepatocellular carcinoma** — see 7.3 surveillance below.

**⚑ HCC surveillance:** **6-monthly liver ultrasound (± AFP)** in patients with **cirrhosis** (and in some non-cirrhotic chronic hepatitis B). Diagnosis is often **radiological** (characteristic arterial enhancement + washout on multiphase CT/MRI) — biopsy is frequently avoided. AFP is neither sensitive nor specific alone.

#### 7.4 Viral hepatitis

**The core split — route and chronicity:**

| Virus | Genome/route | Acute/chronic | Key points |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hep A** | RNA, **faeco-oral** | Acute only | Travel/shellfish; **vaccine**; self-limiting |
| **Hep B** | **DNA**, blood/sexual/vertical | Acute → **chronic** (esp. neonatal) | Vaccine (surface antigen); serology below; ↑HCC |
| **Hep C** | RNA, **blood-borne** (IVDU) | Mostly **chronic** | **Curable with DAAs** ⚑; no vaccine |
| **Hep D** | RNA, needs **HBsAg** | Co/super-infection with B | Worsens B |
| **Hep E** | RNA, **faeco-oral** (pork, water) | Acute; **chronic in immunosuppressed** | Dangerous in **pregnancy** |

**⚑ Hepatitis B serology (a guaranteed exam grid):**

| Marker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **HBsAg** | **Surface antigen — current infection** (acute or chronic if >6 months) |
| **Anti-HBs** | **Immunity** — vaccination or cleared infection |
| **Anti-HBc IgM** | **Acute/recent** infection |
| **Anti-HBc IgG** | **Past exposure** (present in chronic and resolved; absent after vaccination) |
| **HBeAg** | **High infectivity / active replication** |
| **Anti-HBe** | Lower infectivity (seroconversion) |
| **HBV DNA** | Viral load (monitors treatment) |

*Discriminators: **Anti-HBs alone (no anti-HBc) = vaccinated.** HBsAg + anti-HBc IgM = acute. HBsAg >6 months + anti-HBc IgG = chronic. HBeAg positive = highly infectious.* Chronic B treated with **tenofovir/entecavir** (or pegylated interferon) — suppressive, not curative.

**⚑ Hepatitis C** — screen with **anti-HCV antibody**, confirm active infection with **HCV RNA (PCR)**. **Direct-acting antivirals (DAAs)** — **pangenotypic regimens** such as **sofosbuvir/velpatasvir (12 weeks)** or **glecaprevir/pibrentasvir (8 weeks)** — achieve **>95% cure (SVR12)**, well tolerated. This has transformed HCV from a chronic disease into a curable one (older interferon/ribavirin regimens are obsolete).

#### 7.5 Alcohol-related and metabolic liver disease

**Alcohol-related liver disease** — a spectrum: **fatty liver (steatosis, reversible) → alcoholic hepatitis → cirrhosis.** Clues: **AST:ALT >2**, ↑γGT, ↑MCV, ↑IgA. **Alcoholic hepatitis** (jaundice, tender hepatomegaly, systemically unwell) — severity by **Maddrey's discriminant function** (≥32 = severe) or **Glasgow Alcoholic Hepatitis Score**; severe disease may be treated with **corticosteroids** (assess response with the Lille score). Manage alcohol withdrawal (**chlordiazepoxide**, **thiamine/Pabrinex** to prevent **Wernicke's**), nutrition, and abstinence (the single most important intervention).

**⚑ MASLD / MASH (the 2023 renaming):** what used to be **NAFLD → is now MASLD** (metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease), and **NASH → MASH** (steatohepatitis). The change is more than cosmetic: diagnosis now requires **hepatic steatosis PLUS ≥1 cardiometabolic risk factor** (overweight/obesity, type 2 diabetes/dysglycaemia, hypertension, dyslipidaemia) — a **positive** definition rather than "fatty liver by exclusion of alcohol." A new category, **MetALD**, covers MASLD with a moderate/increased alcohol intake. It is now a leading cause of chronic liver disease. Management: **weight loss (≥7–10%)**, treat the metabolic risk factors, exercise; assess fibrosis (**FIB-4/elastography**). *(Any older material calling this "NAFLD/NASH" is out of date — flag ⚑.)*

#### 7.6 Autoimmune, cholestatic and inherited liver disease

**Autoimmune hepatitis (AIH)** — young/middle-aged **women**, may present acutely or as chronic hepatitis/cirrhosis; **↑IgG, ASMA / anti-LKM1**, interface hepatitis on biopsy. **Steroid-responsive** (prednisolone + azathioprine).

**The two cholestatic autoimmune diseases (don't mix them up):**

| | **Primary biliary cholangitis (PBC)** | **Primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)** |
|---|---|---|
| Who | **Middle-aged women** | **Men**, associated with **UC** |
| Target | Small intrahepatic bile ducts | Intra- **and** extra-hepatic ducts |
| Antibody | **Anti-mitochondrial antibody (AMA)** | **pANCA** (no specific antibody) |
| Imaging | — | **MRCP: "beaded" strictures** |
| Hallmark symptom | **Itch + fatigue**, xanthelasma | Cholestasis, cholangitis episodes |
| Cancer risk | — | **Cholangiocarcinoma**, colorectal (with UC) |
| Treatment | **Ursodeoxycholic acid** (+ obeticholic acid) | ERCP for dominant strictures; transplant; no drug alters course |

**The three inherited metabolic liver diseases (each has a signature):**

| Disease | Defect | Signature findings | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Haemochromatosis** | **HFE (C282Y)** → iron overload (AR) | **↑Ferritin, ↑transferrin saturation**; "bronze diabetes," arthralgia, cardiomyopathy, hypogonadism | **Venesection** |
| **Wilson's disease** | ATP7B → copper overload (AR); think **<40 y** | ↓Caeruloplasmin, ↑urinary copper, **Kayser–Fleischer rings**, neuropsychiatric signs | **Penicillamine / trientine, zinc** |
| **α₁-antitrypsin deficiency** | SERPINA1 (PiZZ) | Liver disease **+ early emphysema**; low serum A1AT; PAS-positive globules | Supportive; ± transplant |

*Discriminators: young patient + liver + **neuropsychiatric/eye signs = Wilson's**; middle-aged + **bronze skin/diabetes/arthralgia = haemochromatosis**; liver + **basal emphysema = α₁-antitrypsin**.*
