### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — HIATUS HERNIA — Additional Notes

- DEFINITION: Protrusion of the upper stomach into the thorax, through a tear/weakness in the diaphragm
- RISK FACTORS: age (~60% of individuals >50), ↑ abdominal pressure (in obesity, pregnancy, coughing, heavy lifting), smoking, structural abnormalities
- PATHOGENESIS/CLASSIFICATION

![](notes/images/general-surgery-and-gastroenterology-anu/img001.jpg)

- Type I – sliding hiatal hernia (95%): Protrusion of gastro-oesophageal junction followed by the body of the stomach through the oesophageal hiatus and above the diaphragm; ↓ lower oesophageal sphincter pressure ↑ GORD risk
- Type II – rolling (1%): Protrusion of the fundus and/or body of the stomach into the chest, with maintenance of the gastro-oesophageal junction below the diaphragm; ↑ risk of intermittent strangulation/ischemia/ischemic necrosis of stomach (🡪 mediastinitis, death)
- Type III – mixed (3%): Both GO junction + fundus above the diaphragmatic hiatus
- Type IV – mixed + complex (1%): other viscera (e.g. colon, spleen, pancreas, small intestine) within the hernia sac

- HISTORY: Sliding (Sx of GORD), Paraoesophageal (retrosternal fullness, N/V), GI bleeding/fever (O= ischemia/potential necrosis of stomach)
- EXAMINATION: bowel sounds in chest
- INVESTIGATIONSUpper GI series: first drink is very carbonated 🡪 gas build up 🡪 expands the stomach 🡪 second drink is a contrast agent 🡪 X-Ry 🡪 positive when stomach is partially/completely intrathoracic
- CT/MRI: better visualization if  upper GI is negative but diagnosis still suspected

- DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSES: angina pectoris, GORD, pneumonia, gastric outlet obstruction
- TREATMENTSliding hiatus hernia: surgical repair not indicted in asymptomatic patients; treat as per GORD in symptomatic patients (medical/surgical)
- Paraesophageal hiatus hernia: surgical repair if failed medical management of GORD, dysphagia, early satiety, postprandial pain, vomiting

- COMPLICATIONS: GORD (most common), esophagitis, peptic stricture, Barrett’s oesophagus, oesophageal carcinoma and extra-oesophageal complications such laryngitis, pneumonia, asthma or coughing, strangulation (ischemia/necrosis)
- PROGNOSIS: good to excellent long-term outcomes in relatively small hiatus
- PREVENTION: avoid meals that are large/before bedtime, avoid nicotine/caffeine/fatty foods (↓LES tone)

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — inguinal hernia — Additional Notes

- DEFINITION: Protrusion of abdominal or pelvic contents through a weak point in the abdominal muscles
- EPIDEMIOLOGY: M>F, ↑ incidence with age

| Direct Inguinal | Indirect Inguinal | Femoral Hernia |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acquired weakness in the aponeurosis of the transversalis fascia (degeneration + fatty changes), “wear and tear”, ↑ intraabdominal pressure | Congenital persistence/failure to regress of processus vaginalis in 20% of adults | Pregnancy (weak pelvic floor muscles), ↑intraabdominal pressure |
| Through Hesselbach’s triangle medial to inferior epigastric artery; usually doesn’t descend into scrotal sac Hernia sac comes through the inguinal floor, medial to the inferior epigastric artery and the deep inguinal ring | Originates in deep inguinal ring lateral to inferior epigastric artery Often descends into scrotal sac (or labia majora) | Into femoral canal, below inguinal ligament but may override it; Medial to femoral vein within femoral canal |
| Mainly contain preperitoneal fat (most do not have a true peritoneal lining/bowel), May press onto nerve (🡪 Sx) | Visible bulge created by empty peritoneal sac in the inguinal canal; which follows the spermatic cord tract into scrotal sac in men/ round ligament 🡪 pubic tubercle in women | Commonly strangulated/incarcerated, pain in medial thigh |

- ![](notes/images/general-surgery-and-gastroenterology-anu/img006.jpg)
ANATOMY OF INGUINAL CANAL (spermatic cord + ilioinguinal nerve)

![](notes/images/general-surgery-and-gastroenterology-anu/img007.jpg)

- 3 arteries: Testicular Artery, Cremasteric Artery, Artery to Vas
- 3 veins: Pampiniform plexus, Cremasteric Vein, Vein of Vas
- 3 nerves: genital branch of genitofemoral nerve, sympathetics, ilioinguinal nerve (technically runs outside the spermatic cord)
- 3 fascias: External Spermatic Fascia, Cremasteric fascia, Internal Spermatic fascia
- 3 others: Vas Deferens, Lymphatics, Tunica Vaginalis
- Hesslebach Triangle: Medial border: aponeurosis of rectus abdominus. Lateral border: Inferior epigastric. Inferior border: Inguinal ligament
- RISK FACTORS: prematurity, smoking, old age, heavy lifting, CTD (SLE, scleroderma), ↑ intra-abdominal pressure (chronic cough, pregnancy, obesity)
- HISTORY: groin discomfort/bulge, slow/gradual onset of pain, able to be reduced (temporary relieves pain), pain when urinating/passing bowels/coughing
- COMPLICATIONS:
- Incarcerated hernia: Intestinal flow is completely obstructed 🡪 painful and tender, non-reducible, small bowel obstruction, nausea, vomiting, constipation
- Strangulated hernia: blood and intestinal flow is completely obstructed 🡪 ↑ risk of gangrene/perforation/peritonitis; painful and tender, continued pain despite reduction, systemic toxicity, fever, malaise
- HERNIA EXAM
- Inspection: position patient standing up and inspect for lump/sliding motion
- Palpate: Lump/Inguinal ligament region and describe lump (size, shape, consistency, contours, colour, tenderness, temperature, transillumination).
- Feel cough impulse
- Reducibility: Locate deep inguinal ring (midway b/w ASIS and public tubercle) and press firmly on the lump. Starting inferiorly, try and lift in up and compress it towards the deep inguinal ring. Once it is reduced, slide your fingers up and maintain pressure over the deep inguinal ring. Ask the patient to cough. If the hernia reappears it is a direct hernia; if not, it is indirect. Release and watch hernia reappear (indirect will slide down obliquely; direct will project forward)
- INVESTIGATIONS: usually clinical but if unclear can pursue groin U/S or CT/MRI of groin
- DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSES: Lymphadenopathy, femoral aneurysm, undescended testis, testicular tumour, torsion
- TREATMENT AND MANAGEMENT
- Symptomatic relief- analgesia, compression belt, abdominal binder, treat chest infection, no heavy lifting
- Surgical (laparoscopic/open)- with or without the addition of mesh to produce a tension-free defect. Laparoscopic repairs have ↓ recovery time but ↑ complication rates. Lichtenstein repair (gold standard open mesh hernia repair) is where inguinal canal incised, subcut divided, epigastric vessels ligated, external oblique incised, cord dissected from transversalis fascia + retracted, indirect hernia sac removed, mesh placed. Other procedures include Shouldice repair, Bassini repair, Mcvay Repair.
- COMPLICATIONS OF REPAIR: recurrence (15-20%), scrotal haematoma, nerve entrapment, femoral vein occlusion, ischemic colitis
- OTHER HERNIAS Richter's hernia: only part of circumference of bowel (usually anti-mesenteric border) is incarcerated or strangulated so may not be obstructed. A strangulated Richter's hernia may self-reduce and thus be overlooked, leaving a gangrenous segment at risk of perforation
- Sliding hernia: part of wall of hernia formed by protruding viscus (usually cecum, sigmoid colon, bladder)
- Umbilical (10-30%): through umbilical fibromuscular ring in infants
- Incisional (10%): breakdown of fascial closure after surgery
- Spigelian: through the lateral edge of rectus sheath due to defect in spigelian fascia
- Obturator: through obturator foramen following obturator nerves and muscle, more common in females
- Epigastric: through linea alba above umbilicus

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — fobt — Additional Notes

- ADVANTAGES: non-invasive, quick, easy, cheap
- DISADVANTAGES: ↓ specific, false positive test results, requires follow-up investigations if positive
- Australians turning 50, 55, 60 or 65, who hold a Medicare/DVA card have a free FOBT kit sent which they can send to pathology. A positive result is followed by imaging of the whole colon, usually with colonoscopy
- The National Bowel Cancer Screening Program is currently inviting Australians turning 50, 55, 60, 64, 65, 70, 72 or 74 years of age to take part through FOBTs. However, NHMRC recommends FOBT screening every two years from 50
- Other screening strategies include 5-yearly flexible sigmoidoscopy with or without FOBT, double-contrast barium enema or 10-yearly colonoscopy

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — pilonidal disease — Additional Notes

- DEFINITION: chronic recurring abscess or chronic draining sinus in sacrococcygeal area/ natal cleft of buttocks that contains hair and skin debris
- EPIDEMIOLOGY: young men (15-40 y/o)
- AETIOLOGY: obstruction of the hair follicles in this area 🡪 cysts, sinuses, abscess
- CLINICAL FEATURES: asymptomatic until acutely infected 🡪 pain, tenderness, purulent discharge, inspissated hair
- TREATMENT: acute abscess (I&D, wound packed open), surgery (failure to I&D and in recurrent disease 🡪 pilonidal cystotomy (excision of sinus tract and cyst; wound closed by secondary intention, primary closure with tissue flap)

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — cholangiocarcinoma — Additional Notes

- DEFINITION: malignancy of extra- or intrahepatic bile ducts
- RISK FACTORS: 50-70 y/o, UC, PSC, liver fluke, intrahepatic stones
- Majority adenocarcinoma
- CLINICAL FEATURES: gradual Sx of biliary obstruction (jaundice, pruritus, dark urine, pale stools), anorexia, weight loss, RUQ pain, Courvoisier’s sign in CBD obstruction, hepatomegaly, metastases into portal vein/hepatic artery
- Klatskin tumour at the bifurcation of common hepatic duct
- INVESTIGATIONS
- LFTs: obstructive picture
- U/S or CT: dilated bile ducts
- ERCP/PTC: ?resectability, biopsy
- CXR/bone scan: metastatic work-up
- TREATMENT:
- Resectable: biliary drainage and wide excision margin (can do duct resection, Roux-en-Y hepaticojejunostomy, liver resection, Whipple Procedure depending on location)
- Unresectable: stent or choledochojejunostomy (surgical bypass)
- Chemotherapy ± radiotherapy
- Transplantation in Klatskin tumours

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — hemochromatosis — Additional Notes

- DEFINITION: ↑ iron storage to 20-40g (normal 1g)  causing multi-organ system dysfunction (particularly liver)
- AETIOLOGY: Primary (hepcidin deficiency), Secondary (transfusions, hemolytic anaemia, excessive intake)
- CLINICAL FEATURES: Arthralgia, Bronze skin (due to melanin), Cardiomyopathy, Cirrhosis, Diabetes (pancreatic damage), Hypogonadism (anterior pituitary damage)
