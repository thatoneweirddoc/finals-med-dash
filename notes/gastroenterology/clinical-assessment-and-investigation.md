## Clinical Assessment & Investigation


### Gastroenterology Master Notes — Clinical Assessment & Investigation

#### 3.1 History and Examination

**Cardinal symptoms and what they localise:**

| Symptom | Key discriminators |
|---|---|
| **Dysphagia** | Solids-then-liquids progressive = **mechanical** (stricture/cancer); solids **and** liquids from the start = **motility** (achalasia). Painful swallowing (odynophagia) = oesophagitis/candida. |
| **Dyspepsia** | Epigastric pain/burning; relation to meals (duodenal ulcer often *relieved* by food; gastric ulcer *worsened*). Screen for **ALARM** features. |
| **Abdominal pain** | Site (map to foregut/midgut/hindgut), character (colicky = obstruction/biliary; constant = inflammation/peritonism), radiation (epigastric → back = pancreatitis/AAA). |
| **Altered bowel habit** | New change >6 weeks in >50s = red flag; alternating = IBS/obstruction; nocturnal/weight loss = organic. |
| **Rectal bleeding** | Bright red on paper/coating stool = anorectal; mixed in/darker = higher; **melaena** (black tarry) = upper GI. |
| **Jaundice** | Pale stool + dark urine + itch = obstructive; painless = malignancy until proven otherwise (Courvoisier). |

**⚑ ALARM / red-flag symptoms** mandating **urgent upper GI endoscopy / 2-week-wait referral**: **A**naemia (iron-deficiency), **L**oss of weight, **A**norexia, **R**ecent onset/progressive symptoms, **M**elaena/haematemesis, **S**wallowing difficulty (dysphagia). Add **new dyspepsia in anyone ≥55** with weight loss, and any upper-abdominal mass. (NICE NG12.)

**Signs of chronic liver disease** (worth a systematic hand-to-abdomen sweep): palmar erythema, **Dupuytren's**, leuconychia, clubbing, **spider naevi** (>5, SVC distribution), gynaecomastia, **caput medusae**, splenomegaly, ascites, testicular atrophy. **Signs of decompensation:** jaundice, **asterixis** (flap) + encephalopathy, ascites, variceal bleeding, easy bruising (coagulopathy).

#### 3.2 Liver Function Tests and Their Interpretation

**First split every abnormal LFT into a *pattern*, not a list of numbers:**

| | **Hepatocellular** | **Cholestatic (obstructive)** |
|---|---|---|
| Dominant rise | **ALT/AST** (transaminases) | **ALP + γGT + bilirubin** |
| Meaning | Hepatocyte injury | Bile-flow obstruction (intra- or extra-hepatic) |
| Examples | Viral/alcoholic/autoimmune hepatitis, ischaemia, drugs, MASH | Gallstone, pancreatic/biliary cancer, PBC, PSC, drugs |

**Reading the enzymes:**
- **ALT** is more liver-specific than AST. **AST:ALT ratio >2** suggests **alcoholic** liver disease (and advanced fibrosis); **ALT > AST** is typical of viral hepatitis and MASLD.
- **Transaminases in the thousands** = a narrow differential: **ischaemia ("shock liver"), paracetamol/toxins, acute viral hepatitis** (± autoimmune).
- **Raised ALP** — is it liver or bone? Check **γGT**: **↑ALP + ↑γGT = hepatobiliary**; ↑ALP with **normal** γGT = **bone** (Paget's, mets, growth) or placenta (pregnancy).
- **Bilirubin** — see the pre-/intra-/post-hepatic split (Section 1.4).

**⚑ True tests of hepatic *function* (not just damage)** — these tell you how sick the liver actually is:
- **Albumin** (low = chronic synthetic failure, but also sepsis/nutrition) — long half-life.
- **Prothrombin time / INR** — the **most useful acute marker of synthetic function** (clotting factors have a short half-life, so PT/INR rises early in acute liver failure). *ALT/ALP tell you there is injury; INR and bilirubin tell you the liver is failing.*

**Non-invasive fibrosis assessment (⚑ replaces routine biopsy for staging in many settings):** simple scores (**FIB-4**, NAFLD fibrosis score), the **Enhanced Liver Fibrosis (ELF)** blood test, and **transient elastography (FibroScan)**. A "**non-invasive liver screen**" for unexplained abnormal LFTs = viral serology (Hep B/C), autoantibodies + immunoglobulins (AIH, PBC), ferritin/transferrin saturation (haemochromatosis), caeruloplasmin (Wilson's, if <40y), α₁-antitrypsin, coeliac serology, lipids/glucose (MASLD), and an **ultrasound**.

#### 3.3 Endoscopy, Imaging and Stool/Serology

**Endoscopy:**
- **OGD (gastroscopy)** — dyspepsia with ALARM features, dysphagia, haematemesis/melaena, surveillance (Barrett's, varices), biopsy (coeliac, *H. pylori*).
- **Colonoscopy** — the gold standard for colorectal assessment; investigation of altered bowel habit/rectal bleeding/iron-deficiency anaemia, IBD assessment, polyp/cancer diagnosis and removal, surveillance.
- **Flexible sigmoidoscopy** — left colon/rectum (e.g. acute UC where full colonoscopy risks perforation).
- **Capsule endoscopy** — obscure small-bowel bleeding/Crohn's (contraindicated if stricturing — risk of capsule retention).

**Imaging:**

| Modality | Best for |
|---|---|
| **Ultrasound** | First-line for gallstones, biliary dilatation, liver texture/ascites, portal flow |
| **CT** | Acute abdomen (obstruction, perforation, ischaemia, collections), cancer staging |
| **MRCP** | **Non-invasive** imaging of the biliary/pancreatic ducts (stones, strictures, PSC beading) |
| **ERCP** | **Therapeutic** biliary intervention (stone extraction, stenting, sphincterotomy) — not first-line diagnostic (risk of pancreatitis) |
| **EUS** | Staging oesophageal/pancreatic tumours; sampling; assessing CBD stones |
| **Erect CXR / AXR** | Free air under the diaphragm (perforation); dilated loops/obstruction |

*Discriminator: **MRCP looks, ERCP acts.** Use MRCP to diagnose, ERCP to treat.*

**Stool and serology (know the "one test" for each disease):**

| Test | Points to |
|---|---|
| **Faecal calprotectin** | **Organic bowel inflammation** — distinguishes **IBD from IBS** (raised in IBD, normal in IBS) |
| **Faecal immunochemical test (FIT)** | Occult blood — bowel-cancer **screening** and symptomatic triage |
| **Faecal elastase** (low) | **Pancreatic exocrine insufficiency** (chronic pancreatitis) |
| ***C. difficile* toxin/GDH** | Antibiotic-associated / pseudomembranous colitis |
| **Anti-tTG (IgA) + total IgA** | **Coeliac disease** (must be **on a gluten-containing diet**) |
| **Anti-mitochondrial antibody (AMA)** | **Primary biliary cholangitis** |
| **ASMA / anti-LKM1, ↑IgG** | **Autoimmune hepatitis** |
| **pANCA + MRCP beading (+ IBD)** | **Primary sclerosing cholangitis** |
| **Ferritin ↑, transferrin sat ↑** | **Haemochromatosis** |
| **Caeruloplasmin ↓** (<40y) | **Wilson's disease** |

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — alcoholic liver disease — Clinical Assessment & Investigation

- DEFINITION: liver damaged as a result of chronic, heavy alcohol ingestion
- PATHWAYS OF ALCOHOL METABOLISM
- EtOH 🡪 alcohol dehydrogenase 🡪 acetalydehyde 🡪 acetaldehyde dehydrogenase 🡪 acetate; Reduction of NAD to NADH (however when there is ↑NADH in relation to NAD this inhibits gluconeogenesis and increases fatty acid oxidation to promote fatty infiltration in the liver)
- Cytochrome P450 E21 pathway that enerates free radicals through oxidation of NADPH to NADP. Chronic alcohol use upregulates cytochrome P450 E21, thus producing more free radicals
- Via hepatic macrophages (Kupffer cells): Chronic EtOH exposure activates this third site of metabolism 🡪 TNF-α production 🡪 ROS in the mitochondria

![](notes/images/general-surgery-and-gastroenterology-anu/img022.jpg)

- STAGES
- Fatty liver: abnormal lipid retention within hepatocytes due to shift from metabolism to catabolism that ↑ lipid globule size and displace hepatocytes; nil jaundice, mildly tender, hepatomegaly, mildly ↑ transaminases
- Alcoholic hepatitis (reversible): Inflammation of liver due to excessive EtOH 🡪 hepatocytes swell up with ↑ fat, water, protein and malllory filaments 🡪 block bile ducts + cholestasis 🡪 Kupffer cells become overloaded 🡪 neutrophils/macrophages migrate and trigger an inflammatory response 🡪 necrotic damage
- Alcoholic cirrhosis (irreversible): hepatic cell death, inflammation, nodular regeneration, scarring/fibrosis; deranged vascular perfusion,  ↓ function of new hepatocytes, green tint from bile stasis
- HISTORY: RUQ abdominal discomfort, weight loss (malnutrition), weight gain (ascites)
- ABDOMINAL EXAM reveals stigmata of chronic liver disease: finger clubbing, palmar erythaema, Dupuytren’s contracture, asterixis, signs of jaundice, venous collaterals, spider naevi, gynaecomastia, ascites and pitting oedema, hepatomegaly for fatty liver and hepatitis, but liver size is decreased with cirrhosis
- INVESTIGATIONS: CAGE, LFT (AST:ALT &gt; 2:1, ↑GGT, ↑bilirubin, ↓albumin), FBC (↓Hb, ↑MCV, ↑WBC), U/S  for cirrhosis, CT for hepatosplenomegaly/varices/nodular texture/ascites, liver biopsy (swollen hepatocytes, Mallory bodies, neutrophils, fibrosis in speace of Disse), Hepatitis serology (Hep B surface antigen, anti-Hep B IgG, Hep X antibodies), fluid/stool chart, serial weights (ascites), copper studies (wilson’s), iron studies (haemochromatosis), INR to assess function
- DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSES: viral hepatitis, autoimmune hepatitis, haemochromatosis, biliary obstruction, cholecystitis
- TREATMENT AND MANAGEMENT

- Alcohol cessation –naltrexone for addiction, Valium for withdrawal, rehab, AA, Drug &amp; EtOH referral, counselling
- Nutritional support –dietician referral, thiamine, B12, folate, correct electrolyte and glucose abnormalities
- Weight reduction; smoking cessation
- Cease drugs metabolised by liver and
- Prednisolone (in those with ↑INR and ↑bilirubin) and monitor INR (may need Vit K)
- Monitor GCS for Wernicke’s and assess renal function
- Replace albumin with Licosim
- Lactulose (removes NH3 into faeces by acidifying it) 🡪 for encephalopathy
- PPI and H2 agonist to prevent variceal bleed
- TIPS to reduce portal HTN
- Liver transplant for end-stage

- COMPLICATIONS: oesophagus (Mallory-Weiss tear, oesophageal varicies), stomach (gastritis), pancreat (acute/chronic pancreatitis), liver (EtOH hepatitis, fatty liver, cirrhosis, hepatic encephalopathy, portal HTN, ascites, hepatocellular carcinoma), hepatorenal syndrome  (liver disease mediators 🡪 splanchnic vasodilation 🡪 RAAS 🡪 vasoconstriction of renal arteries 🡪 rapid deterioration in kidney function 🡪 renal failure)
- PROGNOSIS: fatty liver and hepatitis usually reversible with EtOH abstinence

Note: non-alcoholic steatohepatitis risk factors are obesity, DM, increased LDL, FHx
