## Biliary Tract & Pancreatic Disease


### Gastroenterology Master Notes — Biliary Tract & Pancreatic Disease

#### 8.1 Gallstones and the biliary tree

**Gallstones** — mostly **cholesterol** (or mixed); pigment stones with haemolysis. **Risk = the 5 Fs: Female, Fat, Forty, Fertile, Family history** (± rapid weight loss, ileal disease/bile-salt loss). Most are **asymptomatic** (leave alone).

**The spectrum — separate them by the presence of pain, fever, and jaundice (high yield):**

| Condition | Where the stone is | Pain | Fever/WCC | Jaundice | Key sign/test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Biliary colic** | Neck/cystic duct (transient) | RUQ, post-fatty meal, **<6 h, no fever** | No | No | US stones; settles |
| **Acute cholecystitis** | Impacted in cystic duct → inflamed GB | RUQ, **constant >6 h** | **Yes** | Usually no | **Murphy's sign +**; US: thick wall, pericholecystic fluid |
| **Choledocholithiasis** | **CBD** | RUQ | ± | **Yes (obstructive)** | ↑bilirubin/ALP; dilated CBD; **MRCP → ERCP** |
| **Ascending cholangitis** | CBD + **infection** | RUQ | **Yes** | **Yes** | **Charcot's triad** (RUQ pain + fever + jaundice); emergency |

*Discriminators: **biliary colic = pain only**; **cholecystitis = pain + fever + Murphy's** (but no jaundice); **cholangitis = pain + fever + jaundice (Charcot's triad)** — add **hypotension + confusion = Reynolds' pentad** (severe, needs urgent biliary drainage/ERCP).*

**Management:** cholecystitis → analgesia, IV antibiotics, and **laparoscopic cholecystectomy** (ideally within 1 week / same admission ⚑). CBD stones → **ERCP** clearance ± cholecystectomy. Ascending cholangitis → resuscitate, IV antibiotics, **urgent biliary decompression (ERCP)**.

**Other biliary:**
- **Murphy's sign:** arrest of inspiration on RUQ palpation (cholecystitis).
- **Mirizzi syndrome** — stone in the cystic duct compressing the CBD → obstructive jaundice.
- **Gallstone ileus** — a large stone erodes into the bowel (cholecysto-enteric fistula) → small-bowel obstruction; **Rigler's triad** (pneumobilia, obstruction, ectopic stone).
- **Courvoisier's law:** a **palpable, painless** gallbladder with jaundice is **unlikely to be gallstones** — think **malignant obstruction** (pancreatic/biliary cancer), because a chronically stone-diseased gallbladder is fibrotic and won't distend.
- **Cholangiocarcinoma** (PSC-associated, ↑CA19-9) and **gallbladder cancer** (porcelain gallbladder) — late, poor prognosis.

#### 8.2 Acute pancreatitis

**Acute inflammation from premature enzyme activation** → autodigestion. **Causes — "I GET SMASHED"**: **I**diopathic, **G**allstones, **E**thanol, **T**rauma, **S**teroids, **M**umps, **A**utoimmune, **S**corpion, **H**yperlipidaemia/hypercalcaemia, **E**RCP, **D**rugs. **Gallstones and alcohol account for ~80%.**

**Diagnosis (2 of 3):** characteristic **epigastric pain radiating to the back**, **serum lipase (or amylase) >3× upper limit** (lipase more specific/sensitive), and/or characteristic **imaging** (CT/US). *Amylase can be normal if late-presenting or in alcoholic pancreatitis — lipase is better.*

Signs: epigastric tenderness, and (in severe/haemorrhagic disease) **Cullen's sign** (periumbilical bruising) and **Grey Turner's sign** (flank bruising).

**⚑ Severity assessment:**
- **Glasgow–Imrie score (PANCREAS)** at 48 h — **PaO₂ <8 kPa, Age >55, Neutrophils (WCC >15), Calcium <2, Renal (urea >16), Enzymes (LDH/AST high), Albumin <32, Sugar (glucose >10)**. **≥3 = severe** → HDU/ITU. (Ranson's and **APACHE II** and **CRP >150** are also used.)
- **Revised Atlanta classification** grades severity by **organ failure and complications**: **mild** (no organ failure/complications), **moderately severe** (transient organ failure <48 h and/or local complications), **severe** (**persistent organ failure >48 h**).

**Management:** **aggressive IV fluid resuscitation**, analgesia, oxygen, **early enteral nutrition** (NBM only if not tolerated; parenteral is last resort), treat the cause (**ERCP/cholecystectomy for gallstone pancreatitis**; alcohol withdrawal). **Antibiotics are not routine** — only for proven infected necrosis. Complications: **pancreatic necrosis** (± infection), **pseudocyst** (fluid collection weeks later — amylase-rich; drain if symptomatic), abscess, haemorrhage, **ARDS**, and later chronic pancreatitis/diabetes.

#### 8.3 Chronic pancreatitis and pancreatic cancer

**Chronic pancreatitis** — irreversible fibrosis (commonest cause **alcohol**; also genetic, autoimmune/IgG4, obstruction). Presents with **chronic epigastric pain radiating to the back**, and the two failures: **exocrine** (steatorrhoea, weight loss, **low faecal elastase** → **pancreatic enzyme replacement, Creon**) and **endocrine** (diabetes). Imaging: **pancreatic calcification** on CT/AXR. Manage pain, alcohol cessation, enzyme/insulin replacement.

**Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma** — usually **head of pancreas**; classically **painless obstructive jaundice + weight loss** (**Courvoisier-positive** — palpable painless gallbladder), later epigastric/back pain, new-onset diabetes, thrombophlebitis migrans (Trousseau's sign). Risk: **smoking**, chronic pancreatitis, age, hereditary. Tumour marker **CA19-9**. Diagnose with **CT (pancreatic protocol) + EUS ± biopsy**. Most present **late/unresectable**; only ~20% are candidates for **Whipple's (pancreaticoduodenectomy)**; overall **poor prognosis**.

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators — Section 8

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Gallstone risk factors | **5 Fs: Female, Fat, Forty, Fertile, Family history** |
| RUQ pain <6 h, no fever, settles | **Biliary colic** |
| RUQ pain + fever + Murphy's, no jaundice | **Acute cholecystitis** |
| RUQ pain + fever + **jaundice** | **Ascending cholangitis (Charcot's triad)** |
| Charcot's triad + hypotension + confusion | **Reynolds' pentad** (severe cholangitis) |
| Definitive treatment of cholecystitis | **Laparoscopic cholecystectomy** |
| Emergency treatment of cholangitis | **Urgent biliary drainage (ERCP)** |
| Palpable **painless** gallbladder + jaundice | **Malignant obstruction** (Courvoisier's law) |
| Stone eroding into bowel → obstruction | **Gallstone ileus** (Rigler's triad) |
| Pancreatitis causes mnemonic | **I GET SMASHED** (gallstones + alcohol ~80%) |
| Best diagnostic enzyme in pancreatitis | **Lipase** (>3× ULN) |
| Periumbilical / flank bruising | **Cullen's / Grey Turner's sign** |
| Severity score at 48 h | **Glasgow–Imrie (PANCREAS), ≥3 = severe** |
| Atlanta "severe" pancreatitis | **Persistent organ failure >48 h** |
| Antibiotics in acute pancreatitis | **Only for infected necrosis** (not routine) |
| Fluid collection weeks after pancreatitis | **Pseudocyst** |
| Low faecal elastase + calcification + steatorrhoea | **Chronic pancreatitis** (give Creon) |
| Painless obstructive jaundice + weight loss + CA19-9 | **Pancreatic head cancer** |
| Curative pancreatic cancer operation | **Whipple's (pancreaticoduodenectomy)** |
