## Biliary Tract & Pancreatic Disease


### Gastroenterology Master Notes — Biliary Tract & Pancreatic Disease

#### 8.1 Gallstones and the biliary tree

**Gallstones** — mostly **cholesterol** (or mixed); pigment stones with haemolysis. **Risk = the 5 Fs: Female, Fat, Forty, Fertile, Family history** (± rapid weight loss, ileal disease/bile-salt loss). Most are **asymptomatic** (leave alone).

**The spectrum — separate them by the presence of pain, fever, and jaundice (high yield):**

| Condition | Where the stone is | Pain | Fever/WCC | Jaundice | Key sign/test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Biliary colic** | Neck/cystic duct (transient) | RUQ, post-fatty meal, **<6 h, no fever** | No | No | US stones; settles |
| **Acute cholecystitis** | Impacted in cystic duct → inflamed GB | RUQ, **constant >6 h** | **Yes** | Usually no | **Murphy's sign +**; US: thick wall, pericholecystic fluid |
| **Choledocholithiasis** | **CBD** | RUQ | ± | **Yes (obstructive)** | ↑bilirubin/ALP; dilated CBD; **MRCP → ERCP** |
| **Ascending cholangitis** | CBD + **infection** | RUQ | **Yes** | **Yes** | **Charcot's triad** (RUQ pain + fever + jaundice); emergency |

*Discriminators: **biliary colic = pain only**; **cholecystitis = pain + fever + Murphy's** (but no jaundice); **cholangitis = pain + fever + jaundice (Charcot's triad)** — add **hypotension + confusion = Reynolds' pentad** (severe, needs urgent biliary drainage/ERCP).*

**Management:** cholecystitis → analgesia, IV antibiotics, and **laparoscopic cholecystectomy** (ideally within 1 week / same admission ⚑). CBD stones → **ERCP** clearance ± cholecystectomy. Ascending cholangitis → resuscitate, IV antibiotics, **urgent biliary decompression (ERCP)**.

**Other biliary:**
- **Murphy's sign:** arrest of inspiration on RUQ palpation (cholecystitis).
- **Mirizzi syndrome** — stone in the cystic duct compressing the CBD → obstructive jaundice.
- **Gallstone ileus** — a large stone erodes into the bowel (cholecysto-enteric fistula) → small-bowel obstruction; **Rigler's triad** (pneumobilia, obstruction, ectopic stone).
- **Courvoisier's law:** a **palpable, painless** gallbladder with jaundice is **unlikely to be gallstones** — think **malignant obstruction** (pancreatic/biliary cancer), because a chronically stone-diseased gallbladder is fibrotic and won't distend.
- **Cholangiocarcinoma** (PSC-associated, ↑CA19-9) and **gallbladder cancer** (porcelain gallbladder) — late, poor prognosis.

#### 8.2 Acute pancreatitis

**Acute inflammation from premature enzyme activation** → autodigestion. **Causes — "I GET SMASHED"**: **I**diopathic, **G**allstones, **E**thanol, **T**rauma, **S**teroids, **M**umps, **A**utoimmune, **S**corpion, **H**yperlipidaemia/hypercalcaemia, **E**RCP, **D**rugs. **Gallstones and alcohol account for ~80%.**

**Diagnosis (2 of 3):** characteristic **epigastric pain radiating to the back**, **serum lipase (or amylase) >3× upper limit** (lipase more specific/sensitive), and/or characteristic **imaging** (CT/US). *Amylase can be normal if late-presenting or in alcoholic pancreatitis — lipase is better.*

Signs: epigastric tenderness, and (in severe/haemorrhagic disease) **Cullen's sign** (periumbilical bruising) and **Grey Turner's sign** (flank bruising).

**⚑ Severity assessment:**
- **Glasgow–Imrie score (PANCREAS)** at 48 h — **PaO₂ <8 kPa, Age >55, Neutrophils (WCC >15), Calcium <2, Renal (urea >16), Enzymes (LDH/AST high), Albumin <32, Sugar (glucose >10)**. **≥3 = severe** → HDU/ITU. (Ranson's and **APACHE II** and **CRP >150** are also used.)
- **Revised Atlanta classification** grades severity by **organ failure and complications**: **mild** (no organ failure/complications), **moderately severe** (transient organ failure <48 h and/or local complications), **severe** (**persistent organ failure >48 h**).

**Management:** **aggressive IV fluid resuscitation**, analgesia, oxygen, **early enteral nutrition** (NBM only if not tolerated; parenteral is last resort), treat the cause (**ERCP/cholecystectomy for gallstone pancreatitis**; alcohol withdrawal). **Antibiotics are not routine** — only for proven infected necrosis. Complications: **pancreatic necrosis** (± infection), **pseudocyst** (fluid collection weeks later — amylase-rich; drain if symptomatic), abscess, haemorrhage, **ARDS**, and later chronic pancreatitis/diabetes.

#### 8.3 Chronic pancreatitis and pancreatic cancer

**Chronic pancreatitis** — irreversible fibrosis (commonest cause **alcohol**; also genetic, autoimmune/IgG4, obstruction). Presents with **chronic epigastric pain radiating to the back**, and the two failures: **exocrine** (steatorrhoea, weight loss, **low faecal elastase** → **pancreatic enzyme replacement, Creon**) and **endocrine** (diabetes). Imaging: **pancreatic calcification** on CT/AXR. Manage pain, alcohol cessation, enzyme/insulin replacement.

**Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma** — usually **head of pancreas**; classically **painless obstructive jaundice + weight loss** (**Courvoisier-positive** — palpable painless gallbladder), later epigastric/back pain, new-onset diabetes, thrombophlebitis migrans (Trousseau's sign). Risk: **smoking**, chronic pancreatitis, age, hereditary. Tumour marker **CA19-9**. Diagnose with **CT (pancreatic protocol) + EUS ± biopsy**. Most present **late/unresectable**; only ~20% are candidates for **Whipple's (pancreaticoduodenectomy)**; overall **poor prognosis**.

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — ACUTE PANCREATITIS — Biliary Tract & Pancreatic Disease

- EPIDEMIOLOGY: ↑ in incidence, age and sex dependent on aetiology (EtOh-related males, biliary tract-related females)
- AETIOLOGY: I – idiopathic, G – gallstones, E – ethanol, T – trauma, S – steroids, M – mumps/malignancies/microbiology, A – autoimmune (SLE, polyarteritis nodosa, Crohn’s Disease), S – scorpion stings/spider bites, H – hyperlipidaemia/hyperCa2+ (metabolic disorders), E – ERCP D – drugs (azathioprine, frusemide, oestrogen, antibiotics, acetaminophen, salicylates)
- PATHOGENESIS: Pancreatic acinar cells in the exocrine pancreas produce enzymes which are packaged into zymogens and released into the pancreatic duct via ductal cells. Proenzyme is activated at duodenal brush border, where trypsinogen is converted to trypsin, facilitating activation of other proenzymes. To prevent premature activation, zymogens are segregated into compartments, and contain ↓pH and ↓Ca2+. Once cellular injury pattern has initiated, cellular membrane trafficking becomes chaotic, leading to fusion of lysosomal and zymogen granules, enabling activation of trypsinogen to trypsin. Secretory vesicles are extruded across the basolateral membrane into the interstitium, where molecular fragments act as chemoattractants for inflammatory cells. Activated neutrophils exacerbate the problem by releasing superoxides/proteolytic enzymes 🡪 ↑vascular permeability 🡪 haemorrhage, oedema, pancreatic necrosis

In gallstone pancreatitis, mechanical obstruction of the pancreatic duct whereas in ethanol-related pancreatitis, pathogenesis is unknown. In rare genetic diseases, mutations prevent the physiological trypsin breakdown required normally to stop proteolysis

- CLASSIFICATIONS
- Oedematous – pancreatic parenchyma and surrounding retroperitoneal structures are
- Haemorrhagic – bleeding into the parenchyma engorged with interstitial fluid and inflammatory cell infiltration
- PATHOLOGY
- Mild (interstitial)- peri-pancreatic fat necrosis, interstitial oedema
- Severe (necrotic)- peri/intra-pancreatic fat necrosis/haemorrhage/infection; systemic toxic factors and peritoneal space (multi-organ failure)
- HISTORY
- Sharp epigastric pain that radiates to the back, eased by forward flexion (Inglefinger’s sign)
- Post-prandial, fever, N/V, steatorrhoea, anorexia, ↓BP, jaundice (bile duct obstruction), hypovolemic shock (🡪 renal failure), ARDS (SIRS, sepsis, severe sepsis, septic shock, multi-organ dysfunction), coma, tetany (transient hypoCa2+)
- EXAMINATION: rigidity, guarding, abd distension (from paralytic ileus), Cullen’s sign (periumbilical ecchymosis), Grey-Turner’s signs (flank ecchymosis), ↓BP, ↑HR
- ![](notes/images/general-surgery-and-gastroenterology-anu/img019.jpg)
INVESTIGATIONS
- ↑ Lipase (?EtOH- related; x3 reference range; starts rising 4-8 hrs after onset peaks at 24 hrs and lasts for 8-14 days)
- ↑ Amylase (more pancreas-?gallstone-related; x3 reference range; starts rising 2-12 hrs peaks at 48 hrs and lasts for 3-5 days)
- FBC, CRP (severity), IgG (acute), ALT&gt;100 (pancreatitis)
- AXR (calcification; sentinel loop which is isolated distension of gut segment adjacent to pancreas)
- CXR (usually L sided pleural effusion)
- U/S (inflammation, calcification, fluid collection, stones)
- CT contrast (gold standard to visualize fat stranding, viable tissue, necrosis, micro-calcification, circulation disruption, necrosis)
- FNAB (?necrosis, causative organism)
- If Glasgow&gt;3=severe pancreatitis 🡪 ICU transfer
- DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSES: peptic ulcer, intestinal obstruction, AAA, cholangitis, choledocholithiasis, cholecystitis
- TREATMENT AND MANAGEMENT
- NBM, NG/NJ tube to aspirate gastric secretions (no necessary)
- IV fluids (sequestration of fluid in retroperitoneum hence maintaining hemodynamic stability) + electrolytes (Ca2+, Mg2+). Monitor urine output (check for third spacing, ensure end organ function)
- Nutritional support- TPN avoiding pancreatic stimulation
- Analgesia (morphine sulfate), antiemetic (ondansetron)
- Oxygen (if hypoxic)
- With gallstones – cholecystectomy, endoscopic spincterotomy
- With alcohol-induced – benzodiazepine, thiamine, vitamin, mineral
- With infected pancreatic necrosis – necrosectomy, WIpple’s procedure
- COMPLICATIONS: chronic pancreatitis, ARF, necrotizing pancreatitis, L pleural effusion, atelectasis, ARDS (inflammatory mediators effects on alveoli)
- PROGNOSIS: improvement within 3-7 day conservative management, long-term based on aetiology/lifestyle
- PREVENTION: ↓ fat diet, ↓EtOH

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — CHRONIC PANCREATITIS — Biliary Tract & Pancreatic Disease

- DEFINITION: persistent/irreversible pancreatic inflammation with cell loss (from necrosis), inflammation, fibrosis
- AETIOLOGY: idiopathic, EtOH (&gt;90%), cystic fibrosis
- PATHOGENESIS: unrelenting injury to the pancreas 🡪 fibrosis and chronic pancreatitis and ↓ amylase and lipase
- RISK FACTORS: EtOH, smoking, coeliac disease
- HISTORY: dull epigastric pain that radiates to the back, and relieved by sitting forward; weight loss and steatorrhea (different from acute), N/V, jaundice, ascites, GI bleed, malabsorption (chronic diarrhoea, steatorrhea, weight loss, fatigue), endocrine insufficiency (frank initially vs pancreatogenic after surgery types of diabetes)
- EXAMINATION: abd distension, skin lesions (circulating pancreatic lipase 🡪 fat necrosis)
- INVESTIGATIONS
- Bloods- normal amylase/lipase (fibrin has destroyed much of the enzyme- forming capacity; may be elevated in acute exacerbations), ↑ glucose/ALP/bilirubin (biliary obstruction)
- Abd U/S- structural changes, cavities, duct irregularity, calcification
- CT Scan- focal/diffuse enlargement of the pancreas
- Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP): most accurate test, shows beading off main pancreatic duct (from alternating dilatation and stenosis)
- Other- 72 hour faecal fat test for exocrine function; secretin test (gold standard)- measures exocrine function but difficult to perform, unpleasant, expensive; pancreatic polypeptide response to a test meal (blunted PP response to feeding in severe disease)
- Diabetes mellitus- in 75% with calcific pancreatitis and 30% of non-calcific (due to ↑ glucose tolerance and ↓ insulin level)
- DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSES: acute pancreatitis, pancreatic cancer, biliary colic, peptic ulcer, AAA
- TREATMENT AND MANAGEMENT
- Lifestyle (psychiatric, ↓fatty foods, ↓smoking, ↓EtOH)
- Pain management- oral analgesia (narcotics titrated to lowest effective dose) with analgesia-enhancing agents e.g. gabapentin
- Tread IDMM with insulin if needed
- Oral enzyme replacement (especially lipase) by resting pancreas negative feedback
- Somatostatin to ↓ exocrine secretion and CKK release
- Interventional- block visceral afferent nerve conduction to treat obstructions of the main pancreatic duct
- Spinchterotomy- if duct dilated, remove stones from pancreatic duct
- Surgery- Indications: failure of medical tx, debilitating pain, pseudocyst complications (persistence, haemorrhage, infection, rupture), CBD obstruction, fistula, pancreatic cancer
- Minimal - pancreatic duct decompression, extracorporeal shockwave lithotripsy, celiac plexus block
- Options:  pancreticojejunostomy (drain pancreatic duct if dilated), resect pancreas if duct contracted (most severe disease usually in the head of the gland) with Whipple Procedure (pancreticoduodenectomy) ± Roux-en-Y pancreaticojejunostomy, or total pancretectomy (where aforementioned has failed to give pain relief)

- COMPLICATIONS: exocrine insufficiency, DM, calcification, duct obstruction, cancer
- PROGNOSIS: pain disappeas over time, 10 year survival is 20-30%

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — Pancreatic cancer — Biliary Tract & Pancreatic Disease

- EPIDEMIOLOGY: 4th most common in relation to mortality
- RISK FACTORS: age, smoking, ↑ fat, ↓ fibre, EtOH, DM, chronic pancreatitis, gastrectomy, cholecystectomy, African
- PATHOLOGY
- Ductal adenocarcinoma most common 75-80%; can have others e.g. endocrine, acinar cell, mucinous cystic and intra-ductal papillary mucinous
- CLINICAL FEATUREs
- Vague abd pain with weight loss ± jaundice in a patient over 50 yr old is pancreatic cancer until proven otherwise
- Head (70%)- weight loss, obstructive jaundice, steatorrhea, vague constant and mid-epigastric pain (worse at night, radiate to back), Courvoisier’s sign (painless jaundice, with palpable epigastric mass = pancreatic cancer, Trousseau's Sign (spontaneous peripheral venous thrombosis, often associated with pancreatic/other)
- Body/tail (30%)- tends to present later + usually inoperable, weight loss, vague epigastric pain, &lt;10% jaundiced, sudden onset DM
- FHx
- INVESTIGATIONS: ↑ALP, ↑bilirubin, Ca19-9 (most useful serum marker), U/S or CT (evaluates metastases + resectability), ± ERCP/MRI/EUS/Bone Scan for staging/metastases
- TREATMENT
- Resectable (10-20%)
- No involvement of liver, peritoneum, vasculature or distant sites
- Whipple procedure (pancreoduodenectomy) for cure &lt;5% mortality
- Distal pancreatectomy ± splenectomy, lymphadenectomy if carcinoma of midbody/tail
- Non-resectable Palliation: Endoscopic stenting or double bypass procedure (choledochoenterostomy + gastroenterostomy)‚
- Chemotherapy (gemcitabine + nab-paclitaxel, FOLFIRNOX) ± radiotherapy

- PROGNOSIS : 5 year survival is 1%

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — Jaundice — Biliary Tract & Pancreatic Disease

- CLINICAL PRESENTATION: dark urine/pale stools, pruritis (cholestasis), abdominal pain (stone/pancreatic tumour causing obstructive jaundice), painless (?pancreatic cancer), kernicterus
- KEY QUESTIONS: Alcohol, Billirubin, Cirrhosis (VARICES), Drug overdose (e.g. panadol, nEoplasms, Familial (autoimmune, hemochromatosis, a1-antitrypsin deficiency, Gilbert’s syndrome, Wilson’s), Gall bladder/pancreas, Hepatitis (IVDU, unprotected sex, overcrowding, poor sanitation)
- INVESTIGATIONS: FBC, EUC, INR, BSL, LFTs (↑AST/ALT=liver damage; GGT=EtOH), serology for Ab in Viral Hepatitis, amylase/lipase, US/CT, endoscopic US, MRCP/ERCP, percutaneous transhepatic cholangiography, liver biopsy

|  | Pre-hepatic | Hepatic | Post-hepatic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Total bilirubin | N/↑ | ↑ | ↑ |
| Conjugated bilirubin | N | ↑ | ↑ |
| Unconjugated bilirubin | ↑ | ↑ | N |
| Urine bilirubin | Not present | Present | Present |
| Urine urobilinogen | N/↑ | ↓ | ↓ |
| Urine colour | N | Dark | Dark |
| Stool colour | N | N/pale | Pale |
| LFTs | N | ↑ | ↑ |

- PRE-HEPATIC JAUNDICE when there is ↑ haemolysis 🡪 liver cannot process ↑ bilirubin 🡪 ↑ unconjugated bilirubin (not water soluble) in blood-stream. Caused by ↑ haemolysis (thalassemia, sickle-cell, spherocytosis, G6PD deficiency, malaria, uaemia) or ↓ uptake/conjugation (Gilbert’s syndrome, porto-systemic shunt)
- HEPATIC JAUNDICE when there is acute/chronic liver injury 🡪 ↓ ability to metabolize and excrete bilirubin thus an ↑ unconjugated and conjugated bilirubin. Caused by cirrhosis (alcoholic, Wilson’s, haemochromatosis), infection (viral hepatitis), and drug-induced (paracetamol toxicity)
- POST-HEPATIC JAUNDICE caused by an interruption to the drainage of bile containing conjugated bilirubin that prevents delivery of bile from liver/gallbladder to duodenum. Caused by obstruction in lumen (choledocholithiasis), obstruction in wall (cholangiocarcinoma, choledocal cyst), and obstruction by compression (head of pancreas cancer, Mirrizi syndrome)

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — cholelithiasis (gallstones) — Biliary Tract & Pancreatic Disease

- ![](notes/images/general-surgery-and-gastroenterology-anu/img021.jpg)
DEFINITION: presence of gallstones in the gallbladder
- EPIDEMIOLOGY: 10-15% of population, M&gt;F, ↑ in elderly
- PATHOGENESIS
- Bile is produced in pancreas and stored in gallbladder. Composition of bile is 92% water, 6% bile salts, 0.3% bilirubin and 1.7% fats (cholesterol, fatty acid, lecithin) and it aids in the digestion of lipids.
- CHOLESTEROL GALLSTONES: Formation dependent on cholesterol supersaturation of bile,  crystalisation-promoting factors within bile, and stasis of gall bladder (supersaturation of bile 🡪 cholesterol crystallisation 🡪 sludge🡪 stone)
- Risk Factors: 5Fs (fair, fat, forties, female- oestrogen , OCP, fertile- multiparity)
- BLACK PIGMENT GALLSTONES: Calcium bilirubinate + network of mucin glycoproteins interfaced with salts such as CaCO3/Ca3(PO4)2. Seen in  haemolytic conditions in which there is chronic ↑ bilirubin s e.g. cirrhosis, chronic haemolysis
- BROWN PIGMENT GALLSTONES: calcium bilirubinate + calcium salts of fatty acids; almost always found in bile stasis (strictures, dilation) and/or biliary infection
- CLINICAL PRESENTATION: asymptomatic (80%), biliary colic (10-25%), cholecystitis, choledocholithiasis (8-15%), cholangitis, gallstone pancreatitis, gallstone ileus, empyema of gallbladder, liver abscess, gall bladder perforation with bile peritonitis
- HISTORY: Biliary colicky pain when gallstone transiently impacted in cystic duct (chronic RUQ dull pain exacerbated after fatty meal, radiates to R shoulder tip/scapula), N/V, potentialchest pain, nil peritoneal/systemic findings
- INVESTIGATIONS
- Bloods (to rule out complications)- FBC, EUC, LFT (↑GGT, ALP), pancreatic enzymes (?pancreatitis), INR/PT
- Urinalysis
- Abdominal U/S (inflammation, obstruction, localisation of stone):Gallbladder wall thickening &gt;4mm, oedema (double-wall sign), sludge, pericholecystic fluid, sonographic Murphy’s sign
- Magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP): non-invasive to visualise ampullary region, biliary and pancreatic anatomy
- Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP): can obtain biopsy sample (risk of pancreatitis 10%)
- HIDA Scan (less common but technetium-99 injected into vein and excreted into bile 🡪 visualisation of biliary tree occlusion)
- Percutanteous Transhepatic Cholangiography  (via hepatic parenchyma for proximal bile duct obstruction or when ERCP fails/unavailable; requires prophylactic ABx, contraindicated in coagulopathy/ascites/sepsis/lung disease)
- DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSES: PUD, acute cholecystitis, choledocholithiasis, acute pancreatitis, acute cholangitis
- TREATMENT AND MANAGEMENT: only if symptomatic; analgesia, NBM, IVF, Abx (Cef/met), endoscopic sphincterotomy (free passage of common bile duct stones), Ursodeoxycholic acid (for cholesterol stones to ↓ cholesterol absorption, dissolve cholesterol stones for uncomplicated and infrequent biliary colic or those with ↑ surgical morbidity/mortality risk; slow onset and ↑recurrence rate), percutaneous dissolution/cholecystolithotomy (40-70% recurrence at 5 years); elective lap cholecystectomy, ERCP
- COMPLICATIONS:
- Acute cholecystitis and acute cholangitis
- Choledocholithiasis: gallstone within the common bile duct which has ↑ pain and jaundice; can be primary (formation) or secondary (passage from gallbladder to CBD) with lab tests ↑ALT/AST early on which may progress to Cholestatic picture (↑bilirubin, ↑ALP, ↑GGT); Tx with ERCP/stone removal/elective cholecystectomy
- Mirizzi syndrome: large gallstone in cystic duct or neck of gallbladder 🡪 compression of CBD or common hepatic duct 🡪 obstructive jaundice
- Gallstone ileus: gallstone erodes through the gallbladder wall 🡪 into the duodenum 🡪 small bowel obstruction
- Acute pancreatitis: small stones obstruct the bile ducts 🡪 increases pancreatic pressure 🡪 causes reflux of pancreaticobilliary secretions into the pancreatic duct

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — acute cholecystitis — Biliary Tract & Pancreatic Disease

- DEFINITION: Inflammation of the gallbladder
- AETIOLOGY: Gallstones (95%), infections, starvation, TPN
- RISK FACTORS: ↓ fibre, ↓ physical activity, DM, trauma
- PATHOGENESIS: Stasis of gallbladder 🡪 bile becomes trapped 🡪 irritation and increased pressure 🡪 inflammatory process and oedema 🡪 bacterial invasion 🡪 gallbladder wall becomes thickened with subserosal haemorrhage, pericholecystic fluid present 🡪 mucosa may show hyperaemia and patchy necrosis (loss of venous initially and then arterial) 🡪 5-10% develop ischemia and necrosis 🡪 perforation (less likely to perforate due to greater compliance and volume)
- HISTORY: RUQ pain (intense, prolonged &gt; 6 hrs 🡪 radiates to R shoulder tip; post-prandial), fever, anorexia, N/V
- EXAMINATION
- Boas Sign- hyperaesthesia (↑ or altered sensitivity) below the R scapula can be a symptom in acute cholecystitis
- Murphy’s sign – inspire deeply while the examiner palpates the area of the gallbladder fossa just beneath the liver edges
- Other- palpable gallbladder, peritonism, ↑HR, febrile
- INVESTIGATIONS: FBC (↑WBC with left shift), ↑CRP, EUC, LFT, albumin, INR, amylase/lipase, U/S (thickened walls &gt; 3 mm, pericolic fluid, CBD dilation &gt; 6mm, shadow of stone, sonographic Murphy’s sign); HIDA scan (if U/S is inconclusive to show cystic duct obstruction); CT scan (less sensitive then U/S)
- DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSES: biliary colic, acute cholangitis, peptic ulcer, acute pancreatitis, appendicitis
- TREATMENT AND MANAGEMENT:
- Conservative- analgesia (NSAIDs), IVF, electrolyte correction, NBM, antibiotics (ceftriaxone + metronidazole)
- Lap cholecystectomy- four small cuts 0.5-2.5 cms made in the abdomen; a telescope passed into one to see the abdomen and ports into the other cuts; CO2 is blown into abdomen to lift the abdominal wall from the liver, gallbladder, small bowel, stomach and other organs; forceps and scissors used to help remove gall bladder; metal clips to block ducts and arteries leading to gall bladder (which stay in body); gallbladder removed and CO2 allowed to escape following by closure with staples/stitches. Sometimes, contrast medium is injected and x-rays are taken of the bile duct
- RISKS: infection, shoulder pain, excess bleeding, perforation, air embolus, hernia, adhesions, lung collapse, heart attack/stroke, DVT, anaphylaxis to contrast/anaesthetic
- COMPLICATIONS: gangrenous cholecystitis, perforation of gallbladder

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — ascending cholangitis — Biliary Tract & Pancreatic Disease

- DEFINITION: ascending bacterial infection in association with partial or complete obstruction of the bile ducts
- AETIOLOGY: cholelithiasis, benign stasis, iatrogenic (during cholecystectomy)
- PATHOGENESIS: biliary obstruction and stasis🡪 increases intrabiliary pressure 🡪 increases permeability of ductules 🡪 permits translocation of bacteria into biliary tract; &gt;90% of cases involve mixed growth of gram-negative (e.g. E.coli and Klebsiella) and gram-positive (e.g. Enterococcus)
- Mnemonic common bacteria in biliary tract KEEPS (Klebsiella, Enterococcus, E.Coli/Enterobacter, Proteus/Pseudomonas, Serratia)
- HISTORY
- Charcot’s triad – RUQ pain + fever + jaundice (usually the one missing)
- Reynold’s pentad – Charcot’s triad + confusion + hypotension
- N/V, abdominal distension, ileus, acholic stools, tea-coloured urine (↑direct bilirubin)
- INVESTIGATIONS: FBC (↑ WBC), LFTs (↑bilirubin, ↑ALP/GGT, potentially ↑AST/ALT), ↑CRP, U/S (dilated bile duct, common bile duct stones), blood culture, CT contrast (level + reason for obstruction, bile culture, removal of stones if needed, drainage of bile with catheter), ERCP
- DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSES: acute cholecystitis, acute pancreatitis, acute diverticulitis, appendicitis, hepatic abscess, peptic ulcer disease
- TREATMENT: analgesia, NBM, IVF, IV ABx (empiric coverage for colonic bacteria), ± NGT, biliary decompression (ERCP + sphincterotomy with stone extraction and/or stent insertion), cholecystectomy (in all patients unless contraindicated)

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — gallstone ileus — Biliary Tract & Pancreatic Disease

- PATHOGENESIS: ↑ inflammation causing a cholecystoenteric fistula (usually duodenal) 🡪 gallstone enters gut 🡪 impacts ileocecal valve 🡪 true bowel obstruction
- RIGLER’S TRIAD: pneumobilia, SBO, gallstone
- BOUVERET’S SYNDROME: gastric outlet/duodenal obstruction caused by a gallstone passing through cholecytogastric/ cholecystoduodenal fistula
- INVESTIGATIONS: AXR (pneumobilia, gallstone, air fluid levels, dilated small intestine), CT (biliary tract air, obstruction, gallstone)
- TREATMENT: fluid resus, NGT decompression, surgery (enterolithotomy + removal of stone), close fistula/manage expectantly, cholecystectomy not usually performed

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — carcinoma of the gall bladder — Biliary Tract & Pancreatic Disease

- RISK FACTORS: ↑ stones, age, female, polyps, chronic infection, abnormal pancreaticobilliary duct junction
- Majority adenocarcinoma
- SYMPTOMS: asymptomatic until late 🡪 RUQ pain, palpable RUQ mass, Courvoisier’s gallbladder, jaundice (50%), local extension to liver/stomach/duodenum, metastasis to liver/lung/bone
- INVESTIGATIONS
- U/S: mural thickening, calcifications, fixed mass
- EUS: staging, sampling bile for cytology
- Abd CT: polypoid mass, mural thickening, liver invasion, nodal involvement, distant metastases
- MRI/MRCP: malignant vs benign
- TREATMENT: open cholecystectomy to avoid tumour seeding of peritoneal cavity ± en bloc wedge resection of 2-5cm underlying liver ± dissection of hepatoduodenal lymph nodes
- PROGNOSIS: Poor 5 -year survival (10%) due to late detection
