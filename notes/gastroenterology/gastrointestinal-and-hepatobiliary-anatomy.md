## Gastrointestinal & Hepatobiliary Anatomy


### Gastroenterology Master Notes — Gastrointestinal & Hepatobiliary Anatomy

*Written as prose + tables (paired with the anatomy SVG sheet). Anatomy earns marks in three predictable places: referred/localising pain by embryological origin, the arterial watershed zones behind ischaemic bowel, and the **portosystemic anastomoses** behind varices.*

#### 2.1 The gut tube and its embryological blood supply

The gut develops as three regions, each with its **own artery** and **own referred-pain level** — this is why abdominal pain localises the way it does:

| Region | Extent | Artery | Referred (visceral) pain |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Foregut** | Mouth → mid-2nd part of duodenum (+ liver, gall bladder, pancreas, spleen) | **Coeliac trunk** (T12) | **Epigastric** |
| **Midgut** | Mid-duodenum → proximal 2/3 transverse colon | **SMA** (L1) | **Periumbilical** |
| **Hindgut** | Distal 1/3 transverse colon → upper rectum | **IMA** (L3) | **Suprapubic/hypogastric** |

*Classic application: **appendicitis** — early **periumbilical** pain (midgut visceral) that later **localises to the right iliac fossa (McBurney's point)** once the parietal peritoneum is irritated (somatic).* Foregut acid/ulcer pain is **epigastric**.

**Watershed zones** (between two arterial territories, vulnerable to ischaemia): the **splenic flexure** (SMA/IMA — "Griffiths' point") and the **rectosigmoid junction** (IMA/internal iliac — "Sudeck's point"). Ischaemic colitis classically hits the **splenic flexure**.

**Peritoneum:** the **peritoneal cavity** (potential space) with intraperitoneal organs (stomach, most small bowel, transverse & sigmoid colon, spleen) vs **retroperitoneal** ones. *Memory hook for retroperitoneal — **"SAD PUCKER"**: Suprarenal glands, Aorta/IVC, Duodenum (2nd–4th parts), Pancreas (except tail), Ureters, Colon (ascending/descending), Kidneys, (o)Esophagus, Rectum.* The **lesser sac** communicates with the greater sac through the **epiploic (omental) foramen of Winslow**.

#### 2.2 Oesophagus, stomach and duodenum

- **Oesophagus** (~25 cm): three **constrictions** where things lodge/strictures form — cricopharyngeus, aortic arch/left main bronchus, diaphragmatic hiatus. Upper third **striated** muscle, lower third **smooth**. The **Z-line (squamocolumnar junction)** is where oesophageal squamous meets gastric columnar epithelium — the site of **Barrett's** metaplasia. The lower oesophagus drains partly into the **portal system** (left gastric vein) → **oesophageal varices**.
- **Stomach:** cardia, fundus, body, antrum, pylorus. **Gastric gland cell types:** parietal (acid + IF), chief (pepsinogen), G cells (gastrin, antrum), ECL (histamine), D cells (somatostatin), mucous/foveolar (mucus).
- **Duodenum** (C-shaped, mostly retroperitoneal): four parts wrapping the pancreatic head. The **ampulla of Vater** (2nd part) is where the **common bile duct + main pancreatic duct** enter, guarded by the **sphincter of Oddi**. The **duodenojejunal flexure** is fixed at the **ligament of Treitz** — the anatomical **upper/lower GI-bleed boundary** (bleeding proximal to it = "upper").

#### 2.3 Small bowel, large bowel and anorectum

| Feature | Jejunum | Ileum |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Upper/left | Lower/right (→ terminal ileum at caecum) |
| Wall/lumen | Thicker wall, wider lumen | Thinner, narrower |
| Plicae circulares | Tall, frequent | Sparse |
| Vasa recta / arcades | Long vasa recta, few arcades | Short vasa recta, many arcades |
| Lymphoid tissue | Less | **Peyer's patches** (→ typhoid, terminal-ileal Crohn's) |

The **large bowel** is recognised by **taeniae coli**, **haustra**, and **appendices epiploicae**. The **appendix** arises from the caecum; the taeniae converge on its base (surgical landmark). **McBurney's point** = 1/3 along a line from the ASIS to the umbilicus.

**Anal canal — the dentate (pectinate) line is a watershed**, and everything about it splits above/below:

| | Above dentate line | Below dentate line |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Endoderm (hindgut) | Ectoderm |
| Epithelium | Columnar | Squamous |
| Sensation | **Insensate** (autonomic) | **Somatic (very painful)** — pudendal |
| Venous drainage | Portal (superior rectal) | Systemic (inferior rectal) |
| Cancer type | Adenocarcinoma | Squamous cell carcinoma |

*Application: internal haemorrhoids (above) are **painless** and bleed; an anal fissure/external thrombosed pile (below) is **exquisitely painful**. The rectum is another **portosystemic anastomosis** → rectal varices (not the same as haemorrhoids).*

#### 2.4 Liver, biliary tree and pancreas

- **Liver:** functionally divided by the portal/hepatic-vein anatomy into **8 Couinaud segments** (each with its own inflow/outflow — the basis of segmental resection). The **porta hepatis** transmits the **portal triad**: portal vein (posterior), hepatic artery (left), common bile duct (right — "**ducts to the right**").
- **Biliary tree:** canaliculi → interlobular ducts → right & left hepatic ducts → **common hepatic duct** → joined by the **cystic duct** → **common bile duct (CBD)** → through the pancreatic head → ampulla of Vater. **Calot's (cystohepatic) triangle** — bordered by the cystic duct, common hepatic duct, and inferior liver edge — contains the **cystic artery**; it is the key dissection landmark in cholecystectomy.
- **Pancreas** (retroperitoneal, except the tail): head (+ uncinate) cradled in the duodenal C, neck, body, tail (→ splenic hilum). Main duct (Wirsung) joins the CBD at the ampulla. Its intimate posterior relations — CBD, portal/SMA vessels, duodenum — explain why **pancreatic head cancer causes painless obstructive jaundice** and why the disease presents late.

**Portosystemic anastomoses — the four sites and their varices (very high yield):**

| Site | Portal vein tributary | Systemic vein | Clinical result of portal hypertension |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lower oesophagus** | Left gastric | Azygos | **Oesophageal varices** (the dangerous ones) |
| **Umbilicus** | Paraumbilical | Epigastric | **Caput medusae** |
| **Rectum** | Superior rectal | Middle/inferior rectal | **Rectal varices** |
| **Retroperitoneal/bare area** | Colic, splenic | Retroperitoneal | (silent) |

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — Gastroesophageal Varices — Gastrointestinal & Hepatobiliary Anatomy

- DEFINITION: dilated portosystemic collaterals, usually in the lower 1/3 of the oesophagus. Usually 30% of cirrhotic patients have varices and it accounts for 10-30% of all cases of upper GI bleeding.
- PATHOPHYSIOLOGY : Intrahepatic vascular resistance (due to deranged hepatic vascular architecture) and splanchnic blood flow become increased in cirrhosis 🡪 portal hypertension 🡪 portosystemic collaterals 🡪 exacerbated by decreased albumin due to functional loss of liver in cirrhosis causing imbalance in osmotic pressures 🡪 tensions become too great and these varices rupture 🡪 oesophageal bleed
- CLINICAL PRESENTATION: massive hematemesis, EtOH abuse, portal HTN, ascites, vomiting
- INVESTIGATIONS: FBC, EUC, coagulation studies, Fe studies, group and hold, LFTs
- MANAGEMENT
- Acute Treatment: haemodynamic stability (IVF, blood transfusion), somatostatin-ocreotide (↓vasodilatory hormones 🡪 splanchnic vasoconstriction and ↓ portal inflow), Abx (third gen e.g. cephalosporin), endoscopic variceal ligation (adrenaline injection, banding, diathermy), PPI, stop NSAIDS/anticoags
- Chronic Treatment: Transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS) may be beneficial to ↓ portal HTN

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — anal fissure — Gastrointestinal & Hepatobiliary Anatomy

- DEFINITION: split/tear in the anal mucosa or anal canal
- CLASSIFICATION: acute (spontaneously heal after 1/2 weeks), chronic (persisting for longer than 6 weeks, often associated with indurated edges, skin tag, visible anal spincter fibres at base)
- EPIDEMIOLOGY: often young adults aged 15 – 40, M=F
- AETIOLOGY: passage of hard stool, nitric oxide synthase deficiency, chronic constipation, excessive wiping, inflammation, Crohn’s disease, anal injury, pregnancy, rectal cancer, opiate analgesia
- PATHOGENESIS: 90% posterior midline of anal canal (poor circulation); other 10% in anterior midline; circulation further reduced by the spasm of the internal anal sphincter which results in insufficient blood supply (🡪 ischemia) to heal the split in the skin. Patient then avoids defaecation in fear experiencing the pain 🡪  formation of harder stools which tears the anoderm at the next time of defaecation 🡪 self-perpetuating cycle
- HISTORY: pain/tearing sensation on defecation, fresh blood on stool/paper, anal spasm
- DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSES: constipation, sarcoidosis (abnormal nodules), Crohn’s disease, anal carcinoma
- TREATMENT AND MANAGEMENT: ↑ fibre, ↓ fluid intake, analgesia, stool softeners, GTN (prevents sphincter spasm, increases blood flow and promotes healing), surgical sphincterectomy/botulinum toxin

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — haemorrhoids — Gastrointestinal & Hepatobiliary Anatomy

- ![](notes/images/general-surgery-and-gastroenterology-anu/img015.jpg)
DEFINITION: vascular structures in the anal canal which become problematic when swollen/inflamed
- Internal – superior haemorrhoidal veins, above dentate line, portal circulation
- External – inferior haemorrhoidal veins, below dentate line, systemic circulation
- AETIOLOGY: ↑ intra-abdominal pressure (chronic constripation, pregnancy, obesity, portal HTN, heavy lifting)
- PATHOGENESIS: normal anatomical and functional components of the anal canal and pathological only when they cause symptoms. Increase in pressure 🡪 decreases venous return 🡪 engorgement of vascular cushions 🡪 rectal bleeding when epithelial lining torn
- HISTORY
- Internal haemorrhoids- painless rectal bleeding, rectal fullness/discomfort, mucous discharge, engorged vascular cushions usually at 3,7, 11 o’clock positions in lithotomy position1st degree: bleed but do not prolapse through the anus
- 2nd degree: bleed, prolapse with straining, spontaneous reduction
- 3rd degree: bleed, prolapse, requires manual reduction
- 4th degree: bleed, permanently prolapsed, cannot be manually reduced
- External haemorrhoids- severe perianal pain in thrombosed haemorrhoids, perianal massDilated venules (mildly symptomatic) vs. thrombosed haemorrhoids (v painful)

- DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSES: anal fissure, rectal prolapse
- TREATMENT AND MANAGEMENT: avoid straining, ↑ fibre, topical corticosteroids, stool softeners, topical steroids, surgical (rubber band ligation/sclerotherapy, haemorrhoidectomy in severe disease)
- COMPLICATIONS: anaemia, thrombosis (acute perianal pain and appearance of a tender nodule adjacent to the anal canal; resolves itself within a few weeks but may leave perianal skin tag)

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — anal neoplasm — Gastrointestinal & Hepatobiliary Anatomy

SCC OF ANAL CANAL (ABOVE DENTATE LINE)

- Most common (75%)
- HPV infection 🡪 ↑ risk for anal squamous intraepithelial lesion (high grade and low grade)
- Clinical features: anal bleeding, pain, mass, ulceration, pruritis, 25% asymptomatic
- Treatment: chemo, radio, surgery
- Prognosis: 80% 5-yr survival

MALIGNANT MELANOMA OF ANAL CANAL

- 3rd most common site for primary malignant melanoma after skin, eyes
- Aggressive, distant metastases
- Treatment: wide excision or APR ± chemoradiation
- Prognosis:&lt;5%5yrsurvival

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — ANAL FISTULA — Gastrointestinal & Hepatobiliary Anatomy

- ![](notes/images/general-surgery-and-gastroenterology-anu/img017.jpg)
DEFINITION: abnormal communication between the epithelialized surface of the anal canal and the perianal skin
- AETIOLOGY: post-operative, trauma, anal fissure, malignancy, radiation proctitis
- PATHOPHYSIOLOGY: Originate from anal glands (b/ww internal and external anal sphincter and drain into anal canal)🡪 glands become blocked 🡪 build-up of contents in tunnel 🡪 abscess
- SIGNS AND SYMPTOMS: pain, bloody/purulent discharged, pruritis ani, systemic Sx in infection
- DIAGNOSIS: generally clinical (opening of fistula, painful, redness, induration, discharge), but can use anoscopy, probing (to find both openings), fistulogram, proctoscopy, sigmoidoscopy
- TREATMENTIdentificationInternal opening (Goodsall’s Rule)- fistulas originating anterior to a transverse line through the anus will have a straight course and exit anteriorly, whereas those originating posterior to the transverse line will begin in the midline and have a curved tract
- Fistula tract- proving/fistulography under anaesthesia

- SurgeryFistulotomy: unroof tract from external to internal opening, drainage, heal by secondary intention
- Low-lying fistula (no external sphincter involvement( 🡪 primary fistulotomy
- High-lying fistula (involves external sphincter)🡪 stages fistulotomy with Seton suture placed through tract to promote drainage, fibrosis, ↓ incontinence, delineates anatomy and to spare muscle cutting.

- Post-operative: sitz baths, irrigation, packing to ensure healing proceeds from inside to outside

- COMPLICATIONS: recurrence, fecal incontinence (rare)

### General Surgery & Gastroenterology (ANU) — portal hypertension — Gastrointestinal & Hepatobiliary Anatomy

- DEFINITION: HTN in the hepatic portal system, made up of the portal vein and its branches that drain from most of the intestine to the liver; defined as a hepatic venous pressure gradient &gt; 5 mmHg
- PATHOPHYSIOLOGY (PRESSURE= FLOW X RESISTANCE): pre-sinusoidal (portal vein thrombosis), sinusoidal (cirrhosis, alcoholic hepatitis), post-sinusoidal (RHF, hepatic vein thrombosis, constrictive pericarditis)
- COMPLICATIONS: GI bleeding (varices), ascites, encephalopathy, thrombocytopenia, renal dysfunction, sepsis
- TREATMENT: B- blockers (↓ bleeding from varicies), nitrates (vasodilator), TIPS (↓ portal venous pressure and complications such as variceal bleeding)
