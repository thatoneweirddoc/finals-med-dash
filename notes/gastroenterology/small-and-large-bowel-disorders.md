## Small & Large Bowel Disorders


### Gastroenterology Master Notes — Small & Large Bowel Disorders

#### 5.1 Malabsorption and coeliac disease

**Malabsorption** presents with steatorrhoea, weight loss, and deficiency states (iron/folate → anaemia; B₁₂; fat-soluble vitamins; osteomalacia). Causes span mucosal (coeliac, Crohn's), luminal (pancreatic insufficiency, bile-salt deficiency), and structural (short bowel, bacterial overgrowth).

**⚑ Coeliac disease** — T-cell-mediated sensitivity to **gliadin (gluten)** → **villous atrophy + crypt hyperplasia + intraepithelial lymphocytes** in the **proximal small bowel** (hence **iron/folate** deficiency). Associations: **dermatitis herpetiformis** (itchy extensor vesicles — IgA, treat with dapsone), type 1 diabetes, autoimmune thyroid, **IgA deficiency**, Down syndrome. Long-term risk: osteoporosis, hyposplenism, and **enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma (EATL)** / small-bowel adenocarcinoma.
- **Serology:** **anti-tissue transglutaminase IgA (anti-tTG) + total IgA** (to catch IgA deficiency → then use IgG-based tests). Endomysial antibody (EMA) is confirmatory. **Must be tested while eating gluten.**
- **⚑ Diagnosis:** historically duodenal **biopsy** (villous atrophy) was mandatory in adults. Current guidance now supports a **"no-biopsy" diagnosis in adults** with **anti-tTG ≥10× the upper limit of normal** *plus* a positive EMA on a second sample (mirroring the established paediatric ESPGHAN pathway) — otherwise biopsy still confirms. **HLA-DQ2/DQ8** has high negative predictive value (absence largely excludes coeliac).
- Treatment: **lifelong gluten-free diet**; pneumococcal vaccination (hyposplenism).

Other malabsorption: **bile-salt malabsorption** (terminal ileal disease → watery diarrhoea, SeHCAT test, treat with colestyramine); **small-intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO)** (hydrogen breath test, rotating antibiotics); **tropical sprue**; **Whipple's disease** (*Tropheryma whipplei* — arthralgia, weight loss, diarrhoea, PAS-positive macrophages).

#### 5.2 Inflammatory bowel disease

**Crohn's disease vs ulcerative colitis — the core discriminators:**

| Feature | **Crohn's disease** | **Ulcerative colitis** |
|---|---|---|
| Distribution | **Mouth → anus**, any part; **skip lesions**; terminal ileum classic | **Colon only**, from **rectum** proximally, **continuous** |
| Depth | **Transmural** | **Mucosa/submucosa** |
| Histology | **Non-caseating granulomas**, cobblestoning | Crypt abscesses, goblet-cell depletion |
| Smoking | **Worse** with smoking | **Protective** (paradox) |
| Complications | **Strictures, fistulae, abscesses, perianal disease**, B₁₂ malabsorption, gallstones | **Toxic megacolon**, higher **colorectal cancer** risk |
| Antibodies | ASCA+ | pANCA+ |
| Surgery | Not curative (recurs at anastomoses) | Colectomy is **curative** of the colitis |

**Shared extraintestinal manifestations** — some track disease activity, some don't:

| Manifestation | Note |
|---|---|
| Enteropathic **arthritis** (peripheral) | Tracks gut activity |
| **Erythema nodosum** | Tracks activity |
| **Pyoderma gangrenosum** | Independent of activity |
| Anterior **uveitis / episcleritis** | Eye |
| **Primary sclerosing cholangitis** | Strongly linked to **UC**; independent of activity |
| Sacroiliitis / ankylosing spondylitis | HLA-B27-linked |

**Assessing acute severe UC — Truelove & Witts** (severe = **>6 bloody stools/day** plus any systemic feature: temp >37.8°C, HR >90, Hb <105 g/L, ESR >30). This is a **medical emergency**: admit, IV fluids, **IV hydrocortisone**, VTE prophylaxis, stool chart, daily AXR (watch for **toxic megacolon**: transverse colon >6 cm — high perforation risk). Rescue therapy (**infliximab** or ciclosporin) if no response by **day 3**; **colectomy** if failing. Investigate: **faecal calprotectin** raised, endoscopy + biopsy, exclude infection/*C. difficile*.

**Medical management (⚑ the therapeutic landscape has expanded):**
- **UC:** topical/oral **5-ASA (mesalazine)** for mild–moderate (induction + maintenance); corticosteroids for flares; step up to **biologics** (anti-TNF infliximab/adalimumab/golimumab, anti-integrin **vedolizumab**, anti-IL-12/23 **ustekinumab**, anti-IL-23 **mirikizumab**), **JAK inhibitors** (**tofacitinib, upadacitinib, filgotinib**) and **S1P modulators** (**ozanimod, etrasimod**).
- **Crohn's:** 5-ASAs are less effective; use **budesonide/steroids** to induce, **thiopurines/methotrexate** and **biologics** (anti-TNF, vedolizumab, ustekinumab, risankizumab) to maintain; **smoking cessation** is essential.

#### 5.3 Irritable bowel syndrome and functional disorders

A **positive diagnosis** (not just exclusion): **Rome IV** — recurrent abdominal pain ≥1 day/week over 3 months, related to defecation and/or a change in stool frequency/form; subtypes IBS-C/-D/-M. **Exclude red flags** (age >50 new symptoms, weight loss, PR bleeding, nocturnal symptoms, family history of bowel cancer/IBD, anaemia) — check **FBC, CRP, coeliac serology, and faecal calprotectin** (normal in IBS). Management: education/reassurance, diet (**low-FODMAP** with dietitian), fibre adjustment, antispasmodics, and symptom-directed drugs (loperamide, laxatives, low-dose tricyclics, linaclotide).

#### 5.4 Diverticular disease and other colonic conditions

**Diverticulosis** (herniation of mucosa through the muscle wall at vessel points, commonest **sigmoid**; from low-fibre diet/raised intraluminal pressure) → **diverticular disease** (symptomatic) → **acute diverticulitis** (inflammation/infection: LIF pain, fever, raised inflammatory markers — "**left-sided appendicitis**"). Diagnose acutely with **CT**; classify complicated disease by **Hinchey** (I abscess→ IV faeculent peritonitis).
- **⚑ Management shift:** for **uncomplicated** acute diverticulitis in a systemically well patient, **antibiotics are no longer routinely required** (NICE NG147) — analgesia, fluids and review. Antibiotics/admission for complicated disease (abscess → drainage; perforation/peritonitis → surgery, e.g. Hartmann's).
- Complications: abscess, perforation, fistula (**colovesical → pneumaturia/faecaluria**), stricture, and **lower GI bleeding**.

**Other colonic conditions:** **angiodysplasia** (arteriovenous malformations, painless bleeding/iron deficiency, right colon, associated with aortic stenosis — Heyde's); **ischaemic colitis** (splenic-flexure watershed, "thumbprinting" on AXR, usually settles); **microscopic colitis** (chronic watery diarrhoea, normal endoscopy, abnormal biopsy — collagenous/lymphocytic; drug-associated, e.g. PPIs/NSAIDs); **radiation enteritis**.

#### 5.5 Colorectal cancer and polyps

Nearly all arise via the **adenoma–carcinoma sequence** (APC → KRAS → p53). **Hereditary syndromes** (worth spotting):

| Syndrome | Genetics | Key feature |
|---|---|---|
| **FAP** | APC (AD) | **Hundreds–thousands of polyps**, ~100% cancer → prophylactic colectomy; **Gardner** variant (osteomas, desmoids) |
| **Lynch (HNPCC)** | Mismatch-repair genes (AD) | Fewer polyps, **right-sided**, younger; also endometrial/others; **Amsterdam criteria** |
| Peutz–Jeghers | STK11 | Hamartomas + **mucocutaneous pigmentation** |
| MUTYH-associated polyposis | MUTYH (AR) | Polyposis |

**⚑ Screening (England):** **FIT (faecal immunochemical test)** home kit every **2 years**, now offered from **age 50 to 74** (the programme has been extended down from 60 to 50 — fully rolling out across the over-50s from 2025). A positive FIT → **colonoscopy**. In symptomatic patients, **FIT is also used to triage** urgent referral.

**Presentation by side:** **left-sided/rectal** → change in bowel habit, PR bleeding, obstruction, tenesmus; **right-sided** → **iron-deficiency anaemia**, weight loss, occult bleeding, later obstruction (wider lumen). **Any iron-deficiency anaemia in a man or post-menopausal woman is colorectal cancer until proven otherwise.**

**Referral/diagnosis:** 2-week-wait for red-flag features (FIT-supported); **colonoscopy + biopsy**; stage with **CT chest/abdomen/pelvis + MRI rectum** (rectal cancer) + CEA baseline. **Staging: TNM** (has largely replaced Dukes' — ⚑; Dukes A confined to bowel wall → D distant metastases). Management: **surgical resection** (the only cure) ± neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (rectal) + adjuvant chemotherapy (node-positive); palliative chemo/biologics for metastatic disease.

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators — Section 5

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Villous atrophy + iron/folate deficiency + dermatitis herpetiformis | **Coeliac disease** |
| Coeliac first-line serology | **Anti-tTG IgA + total IgA** (on gluten) |
| Adult no-biopsy coeliac criteria | **tTG ≥10× ULN + positive EMA** ⚑ |
| PAS-positive macrophages, arthralgia, weight loss | **Whipple's disease** |
| Skip lesions, transmural, granulomas, perianal disease | **Crohn's** |
| Continuous colitis from the rectum, crypt abscesses | **Ulcerative colitis** |
| Smoking **protective** | **Ulcerative colitis** |
| IBD strongly linked to PSC | **Ulcerative colitis** |
| Truelove & Witts severe UC threshold | **>6 bloody stools/day + a systemic feature** |
| Transverse colon >6 cm in acute UC | **Toxic megacolon** |
| UC rescue therapy if no response by day 3 | **Infliximab or ciclosporin** |
| Positive diagnostic criteria for IBS | **Rome IV** |
| Normal calprotectin, red-flags excluded, pain + habit change | **IBS** |
| "Left-sided appendicitis" | **Acute diverticulitis** |
| Uncomplicated diverticulitis + systemically well | **No routine antibiotics** ⚑ |
| Pneumaturia/faecaluria | **Colovesical fistula** (diverticular) |
| Painless colonic bleeding + aortic stenosis | **Angiodysplasia (Heyde's)** |
| Thousands of polyps, APC gene | **FAP** |
| Right-sided cancer, young, MMR genes, endometrial risk | **Lynch (HNPCC)** |
| Bowel-cancer screening test + age (England) | **FIT, 2-yearly, age 50–74** ⚑ |
| Iron-deficiency anaemia in an older man | **Colorectal cancer until proven otherwise** |
