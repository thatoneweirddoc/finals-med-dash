## Aortic & Peripheral Vascular Disease


### Cardiology Notes — Aortic & Peripheral Vascular Disease

#### 9.1 Aortic Aneurysm
- **Abdominal (AAA):** often asymptomatic; risk factors smoking, age, male, family history. **Screening** (single ultrasound) offered to older men in many countries. Repair generally at **diameter ≥5.5 cm**, rapid growth, or if symptomatic. **Ruptured AAA** — sudden abdominal/back pain, hypotension, pulsatile mass — surgical emergency.
- **Thoracic aortic aneurysm** — associated with connective-tissue disease.

#### 9.2 Aortic Dissection
- **Tear in the intima → blood tracks into the media**, creating a false lumen.
- **Stanford classification:** **Type A** involves the **ascending** aorta (surgical emergency); **Type B** is confined to the **descending** aorta (usually managed medically).
- **Presentation:** sudden, severe, **tearing/ripping** chest pain radiating to the **back**, **maximal at onset**; pulse/BP asymmetry between arms; may cause AR, tamponade, stroke, or limb/organ ischaemia. CXR: widened mediastinum. **Diagnosis: CT angiography** (aorta).
- **Management:** rapid control of **heart rate first, then BP** — IV **beta-blocker** (labetalol/esmolol) to a target HR ~60 and systolic ~100–120 mmHg (reduce shear stress/dP/dt), then vasodilator if needed. **Type A → emergency surgery; Type B → medical** (surgery/stenting if complicated).
- **Connective-tissue associations:** **Marfan** (fibrillin-1), Ehlers–Danlos (vascular type), Loeys–Dietz.

#### 9.3 Peripheral Arterial Disease
- **Chronic limb ischaemia:** intermittent **claudication** → rest pain → tissue loss (critical limb ischaemia). Assess with **ankle–brachial pressure index (ABPI)** (<0.9 abnormal; falsely high if calcified vessels in diabetes). Management: risk-factor control, antiplatelet + statin, supervised exercise, revascularisation for critical ischaemia.
- **Acute limb ischaemia — the 6 Ps:** **P**ain, **P**allor, **P**ulselessness, **P**erishing cold, **P**araesthesia, **P**aralysis. A surgical emergency (embolic or thrombotic) — urgent revascularisation.
- **Carotid disease:** stenosis is a stroke/TIA source; carotid endarterectomy for symptomatic significant stenosis.

#### 9.4 Venous Thromboembolism
- **DVT & PE** share risk factors (**Virchow's triad**: stasis, hypercoagulability, endothelial injury).
- **DVT:** unilateral leg swelling/pain; **Wells score** → D-dimer (if low probability) or Doppler ultrasound.
- **PE:** dyspnoea, pleuritic pain, tachycardia, hypoxia; ECG usually sinus tachycardia (classic S1Q3T3 uncommon); **CT pulmonary angiogram** is the main diagnostic test (V/Q if contrast contraindicated). **Risk-stratify:** massive/**high-risk PE** (hypotension/shock) → **thrombolysis**; otherwise anticoagulate.
- **Treatment:** **DOAC** first-line for most VTE; consider thrombolysis for haemodynamically unstable PE; IVC filter if anticoagulation contraindicated.
