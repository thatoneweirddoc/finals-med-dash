## Congenital, Inherited & Special Groups


### Cardiology Notes — Congenital, Inherited & Special Groups

##### 10.1 Congenital Heart Disease
- **Atrial septal defect (ASD):** left-to-right shunt; **fixed splitting of S2**; may present in adulthood with AF/right heart strain.
- **Ventricular septal defect (VSD):** harsh **pansystolic** murmur at the left sternal edge (smaller defect = louder); commonest congenital lesion.
- **Patent ductus arteriosus (PDA):** **continuous "machinery" murmur**.
- **Coarctation of the aorta:** **radio-femoral delay**, upper-limb hypertension, **rib notching** on CXR; associated with bicuspid valve and Turner syndrome.
- **Tetralogy of Fallot** (commonest cyanotic lesion): **VSD, overriding aorta, RV outflow obstruction, RV hypertrophy** ("boot-shaped" heart); cyanotic "tet" spells.
- **Eisenmenger syndrome:** a long-standing left-to-right shunt raises pulmonary pressures until the shunt **reverses** (right-to-left) → cyanosis; once established, shunt closure is contraindicated.

##### 10.2 Inherited Cardiovascular Disease
- **Channelopathies** (long QT, Brugada, CPVT) and **inherited cardiomyopathies** (HCM, ARVC, DCM) — cross-reference Installments 2–3; **family screening and genetic counselling** matter.
- **Familial hypercholesterolaemia:** autosomal dominant (usually LDL-receptor); tendon xanthomata, very high LDL, premature coronary disease — treat aggressively and screen relatives (cascade testing).
- **Marfan syndrome:** fibrillin-1; tall, arachnodactyly, lens dislocation, **aortic root dilatation/dissection** and MVP — needs aortic surveillance and often prophylactic root surgery.

##### 10.3 Cardiology in Special Groups
- **Pregnancy:** physiological rise in cardiac output/volume and fall in SVR unmasks or worsens disease (e.g. mitral stenosis); **peripartum cardiomyopathy**; several drugs are contraindicated (**ACEi/ARB, warfarin, spironolactone**) — labetalol, methyldopa and nifedipine are the usual antihypertensives.
- **Cardio-oncology:** anthracycline and trastuzumab cardiotoxicity, radiotherapy-related disease — baseline and serial cardiac monitoring.
- **Athlete's heart:** physiological adaptation (bradycardia, chamber enlargement, voltage criteria) that must be distinguished from HCM/ARVC.

##### C. Disease Formulation Cards

> **Hypertension** — mostly essential; screen for secondary if young/resistant. Ix: confirm with ABPM/HBPM, assess end-organ damage. Mx: lifestyle + A/C/D algorithm; target ~120–129 systolic (ESC 2024, tolerated). *Buzz:* "elevated BP" is the new ESC in-between category.

> **Aortic dissection** — intimal tear, false lumen. Sudden tearing pain to back, BP/pulse asymmetry, wide mediastinum. Ix: CT angiogram. Mx: HR then BP control (IV β-blocker); Type A → surgery, Type B → medical. *Buzz:* "maximal at onset".

> **Ruptured AAA** — pulsatile mass + back/abdo pain + hypotension. Ix: urgent imaging/theatre. Mx: emergency repair. *Buzz:* don't delay for a scan if unstable.

> **Acute limb ischaemia** — sudden 6 Ps. Embolic (AF) or thrombotic. Mx: urgent revascularisation, heparin. *Buzz:* paralysis/paraesthesia = threatened limb.

> **Pulmonary embolism** — dyspnoea, pleuritic pain, tachycardia, hypoxia. Ix: Wells → CTPA. Mx: DOAC; thrombolysis if haemodynamically unstable (massive PE). *Buzz:* S1Q3T3 (rare); sinus tachy is commonest.

> **Coarctation of the aorta** — upper-limb HTN, radio-femoral delay, rib notching; Turner/bicuspid links. Mx: repair/stent. *Buzz:* differential BP arms vs legs.
