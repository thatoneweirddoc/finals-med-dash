## Cardiac Electrophysiology


### Cardiology Notes — Cardiac Electrophysiology

#### 2.1 The contractile-cell (fast-response) action potential
Atrial and ventricular myocytes have a resting potential near **−90 mV** and a characteristic long plateau. Five phases:
- **Phase 0 — rapid depolarisation:** fast voltage-gated **Na⁺** channels open → steep upstroke.
- **Phase 1 — early repolarisation:** Na⁺ channels inactivate; transient outward **K⁺** current (I_to).
- **Phase 2 — plateau:** inward **Ca²⁺** (L-type channels) balances outward **K⁺** → sustained depolarisation. This long plateau is unique to cardiac muscle and underlies the long refractory period.
- **Phase 3 — repolarisation:** Ca²⁺ channels close, delayed-rectifier **K⁺** efflux dominates → return toward baseline.
- **Phase 4 — resting potential:** stabilised by inward-rectifier K⁺ (I_K1); the **Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase** restores gradients.

**🔗** Antiarrhythmic drug classes map onto these phases (Vaughan–Williams, Installment 5): Class I block phase-0 Na⁺; Class III block phase-3 K⁺ (prolong QT/refractoriness); Class IV block phase-2 Ca²⁺.

#### 2.2 The pacemaker (slow-response) action potential
SA- and AV-nodal cells have **no stable resting potential** and no fast Na⁺ channels. Instead:
- **Phase 4 — spontaneous diastolic depolarisation:** the "funny" current **I_f** (Na⁺ influx) plus T-type Ca²⁺ drifts the membrane up to threshold → **automaticity**.
- **Phase 0 — upstroke:** driven by **L-type Ca²⁺** influx (slower than Na⁺-driven cells).
- **Phase 3 — repolarisation:** K⁺ efflux.

The **SA node** depolarises fastest, so it is the dominant pacemaker (overdrive suppression of slower sites).

**Intrinsic escape rates:** SA node **60–100/min** · AV node **40–60/min** · His–Purkinje/ventricle **20–40/min**.

**🔗** Ivabradine blocks I_f to slow the sinus rate without affecting contractility. Autonomic tone tunes the pacemaker: vagal (ACh) slows phase-4 drift; sympathetic (noradrenaline, β1) steepens it.

#### 2.3 The conduction system and AV nodal delay
Impulse path: **SA node → atrial myocardium (internodal tracts) → AV node → bundle of His → left & right bundle branches → Purkinje fibres → ventricular myocardium**, spreading apex-to-base.

The **AV node deliberately delays** conduction (~0.1 s; the PR interval). This lets atrial contraction complete ventricular filling (the "atrial kick") *before* the ventricles contract, and protects the ventricles from very fast atrial rates.

**🔗** In AF the irregular atrial impulses are filtered by the AV node — AV-nodal blockers (β-blockers, diltiazem, digoxin) are how we rate-control. In complete heart block, a slow ventricular escape rhythm takes over.

#### 2.4 Refractory periods
During the **absolute (effective) refractory period** (phases 0–2 and early 3) the cell cannot be re-excited — this prevents **tetany** (cardiac muscle must relax to refill) and limits re-entry. The **relative refractory period** (late phase 3) needs a stronger stimulus, and can propagate abnormally.
