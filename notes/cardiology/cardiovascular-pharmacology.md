## Cardiovascular Pharmacology


### Cardiology Notes — Cardiovascular Pharmacology

##### 11.1 Antiplatelets and Anticoagulants
- **Aspirin** — irreversibly inhibits **COX-1** → less thromboxane A₂. Backbone of secondary prevention. Caution: GI bleeding/ulceration.
- **P2Y12 inhibitors** — block ADP-mediated platelet activation: **clopidogrel** (prodrug, variable metabolism), **ticagrelor** and **prasugrel** (more potent, preferred in ACS). *Prasugrel is contraindicated after prior stroke/TIA*; dose-reduce if <60 kg or ≥75 y. **Dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT)** = aspirin + a P2Y12 inhibitor, default 12 months post-ACS.
- **Anticoagulants:**
  - **Warfarin** — vitamin K antagonist (factors II, VII, IX, X). Needs **INR** monitoring; many interactions; reversed with vitamin K/PCC. Still first-line for **mechanical valves** and **moderate–severe mitral stenosis**.
  - **DOACs** — direct factor **Xa inhibitors** (apixaban, rivaroxaban, edoxaban) and the direct **thrombin inhibitor** dabigatran. Preferred over warfarin for AF and VTE; no routine monitoring; reduce dose for renal impairment/age/weight criteria; reversal agents (idarucizumab for dabigatran; andexanet alfa for Xa inhibitors).
  - **Heparins** — unfractionated (IV, monitored by APTT, quickly reversible with protamine) and **LMWH** (subcutaneous, predictable). **Fondaparinux** in NSTE-ACS.

##### 11.2 Lipid-lowering Therapy
- **Statins** (e.g. atorvastatin) — inhibit **HMG-CoA reductase** → less hepatic cholesterol synthesis, upregulated LDL-receptors. First-line; high-intensity for secondary prevention. Side effects: myalgia/myopathy (rarely rhabdomyolysis), deranged LFTs; interactions (some via CYP3A4).
- **Add-on ladder** if LDL targets not met: **ezetimibe** (blocks intestinal cholesterol absorption) → **PCSK9 inhibitors** (monoclonal antibodies — evolocumab/alirocumab) or **inclisiran** (siRNA lowering PCSK9, twice-yearly) → **bempedoic acid**. **Icosapent ethyl** for residual high triglyceride risk.

##### 11.3 Antihypertensives
- **ACE inhibitors** (ramipril) — block angiotensin I→II conversion; also raise bradykinin (→ **dry cough**, rarely angioedema). Watch K⁺ and creatinine; **contraindicated in pregnancy** and bilateral renal artery stenosis.
- **ARBs** (losartan/candesartan) — block AT₁ receptor; alternative if ACEi cough.
- **Calcium-channel blockers:** *dihydropyridines* (amlodipine — vascular selective, cause ankle oedema/flushing) vs *non-dihydropyridines* (verapamil, diltiazem — rate-limiting, negatively inotropic; **don't combine verapamil with a beta-blocker** → bradycardia/asystole risk).
- **Thiazide-like diuretics** (indapamide) — see 11.4.
- **Beta-blockers, MRAs, alpha-blockers** — see relevant sections.

##### 11.4 Diuretics
- **Loop diuretics** (furosemide, bumetanide) — inhibit the **Na⁺/K⁺/2Cl⁻ cotransporter** in the thick ascending limb; powerful; used in fluid overload/HF. Side effects: hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, hypocalcaemia, ototoxicity, gout.
- **Thiazides/thiazide-like** (bendroflumethiazide, indapamide) — inhibit the **Na⁺/Cl⁻ cotransporter** in the distal tubule; used in hypertension. Side effects: hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia, hyperglycaemia, hyperuricaemia/gout, hypercalcaemia.
- **Potassium-sparing / MRAs** (spironolactone, eplerenone — aldosterone antagonists; amiloride — ENaC blocker) — used in HF, resistant HTN, Conn's. Side effects: **hyperkalaemia**, gynaecomastia (spironolactone).
- **Diuretic resistance / sequential nephron blockade** — combining a loop + thiazide augments natriuresis in refractory oedema (watch electrolytes closely).

##### 11.5 Anti-anginals and Heart Failure Drugs
- **Nitrates** (GTN, isosorbide) — venodilators (↓ preload) and coronary dilators; tolerance develops (need a nitrate-free interval). Side effects: headache, hypotension; **avoid with PDE5 inhibitors** (sildenafil) → profound hypotension.
- **Beta-blockers** — ↓ HR, contractility, and myocardial O₂ demand; disease-modifying in HFrEF and post-MI. Caution in asthma, acute decompensated HF; don't stop abruptly.
- **Ivabradine** — inhibits the SA-node **I_f** current → slows rate without affecting contractility (sinus rhythm only).
- **Nicorandil** (K⁺-channel activator), **ranolazine** — second-line anti-anginals.
- **Heart failure "four pillars"** (ARNI/ACEi, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i) — mechanisms as above; **sacubitril/valsartan (ARNI)** adds neprilysin inhibition (augments natriuretic peptides) — do **not** combine with an ACEi (angioedema risk; 36-h washout). **SGLT2 inhibitors** — glucosuric, but their HF benefit is independent of glucose lowering.
- **Digoxin** — inhibits the **Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase** → positive inotropy and increased vagal tone (rate control). Narrow therapeutic index: **toxicity** worsened by **hypokalaemia**, causes nausea, confusion, **xanthopsia (yellow vision)**, and arrhythmias (classically bidirectional/atrial tachycardia with block); "reverse-tick"/down-sloping ST is an *effect*, not toxicity. Reverse severe toxicity with **digoxin-specific antibody (Digifab)**.

##### 11.6 Antiarrhythmics — Vaughan–Williams Classes
- **Class I — Na⁺-channel blockers** (phase 0): Ia (quinidine, procainamide), **Ib** (lidocaine — ventricular), **Ic** (flecainide — avoid in structural/ischaemic heart disease).
- **Class II — beta-blockers**: slow SA/AV nodal conduction; rate control, post-MI.
- **Class III — K⁺-channel blockers** (phase 3, prolong refractoriness/QT): **amiodarone** (broad-spectrum but multi-system toxicity — thyroid, lung fibrosis, liver, skin, corneal deposits), sotalol.
- **Class IV — non-dihydropyridine CCBs** (verapamil, diltiazem): AV nodal slowing.
- **Others:** **adenosine** (transient AV block — diagnostic/therapeutic in SVT), **digoxin**, **magnesium** (torsades).

##### 11.7 Inotropes and Vasopressors
- **Dobutamine** — β1 agonist → inotropy (cardiogenic shock/decompensated HF).
- **Noradrenaline** — α1 (+β1) → vasopressor of choice in most shock.
- **Adrenaline** — α + β; anaphylaxis, cardiac arrest, refractory shock.
- **Dopamine** (dose-dependent), **milrinone** (PDE3 inhibitor — inotrope + vasodilator, useful in RV failure/pulmonary hypertension). **Match the agent to the haemodynamic problem** (pump failure vs vasodilated shock).

##### 11.8 Prescribing Safety
- **QT-prolonging drugs** (amiodarone, sotalol, macrolides, antipsychotics, some antiemetics) → torsades risk, especially with hypokalaemia/hypomagnesaemia.
- **Bradycardia/heart block** risk when combining rate-limiting agents (β-blocker + verapamil/diltiazem, + digoxin).
- **Renal/hepatic impairment and pregnancy** — dose-adjust or avoid (ACEi/ARB, spironolactone and warfarin are contraindicated in pregnancy).

##### C. Drug-class Cards

> **Statins** — inhibit HMG-CoA reductase → ↓LDL, plaque stabilisation. First-line primary/secondary prevention. SE: myalgia/myopathy, ↑LFTs. *Buzz:* high-intensity after ACS.

> **ACE inhibitors** — block ATI→II, ↑bradykinin. Uses: HTN, HFrEF, post-MI, diabetic nephropathy. SE: dry cough, hyperkalaemia, ↑creatinine, angioedema; teratogenic. *Buzz:* check U&Es after starting.

> **Beta-blockers** — ↓rate/contractility/O₂ demand; disease-modifying in HFrEF & post-MI. SE: bradycardia, fatigue, bronchospasm; don't start in acute decompensated HF. *Buzz:* start when euvolaemic.

> **Loop diuretics** — block Na⁺/K⁺/2Cl⁻ (thick ascending limb); potent, for congestion. SE: ↓K⁺/Mg²⁺/Ca²⁺, ototoxicity, gout. *Buzz:* symptom relief, not mortality.

> **Digoxin** — Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase inhibitor → +inotropy, vagal rate control. Narrow index; toxicity ↑ by ↓K⁺. Reverse with Digifab. *Buzz:* xanthopsia.

> **Amiodarone** — Class III (multi-channel). Broad-spectrum for VT/AF. SE across thyroid/lung/liver/skin/eyes; QT prolongation. *Buzz:* baseline TFT/LFT/CXR before starting.
