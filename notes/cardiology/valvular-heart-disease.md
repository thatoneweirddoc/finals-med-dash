## Valvular Heart Disease


### Cardiology Notes — Valvular Heart Disease

For every lesion, examiners want the same five things: **cause → murmur → other signs → key investigation → when to intervene.**

#### 5.1 Aortic Stenosis
- **Cause:** age-related **calcific/degenerative** (commonest >65), **bicuspid** valve (presents younger), rheumatic.
- **Symptoms — the ominous triad ("SAD"):** exertional **S**yncope, **A**ngina, **D**yspnoea. Onset of symptoms in severe AS signals poor prognosis and the need for intervention.
- **Signs:** ejection-systolic murmur (right 2nd space) radiating to the **carotids**, **slow-rising** pulse, **narrow** pulse pressure, soft/absent S2, S4, sustained heaving apex.
- **Severe AS:** valve area <1.0 cm², mean gradient >40 mmHg, peak jet velocity >4 m/s (echo).
- **Intervention:** **symptomatic severe AS**, or **asymptomatic severe AS with LVEF <50%** → valve replacement. **SAVR** (surgical) vs **TAVI** (transcatheter) decided by age, surgical risk and anatomy — TAVI now standard for older/higher-risk patients and increasingly used across the risk spectrum.

#### 5.2 Aortic Regurgitation
- **Cause:** *acute* (infective endocarditis, aortic dissection) — a flash-pulmonary-oedema emergency; *chronic* (bicuspid, rheumatic, or **aortic root dilatation** — Marfan, connective-tissue disease, hypertension).
- **Signs:** early-**diastolic** decrescendo murmur at the left sternal edge, best sitting forward in expiration; **collapsing (water-hammer) pulse**, **wide** pulse pressure. Eponymous peripheral signs: Corrigan's (visible carotid pulsation), de Musset's (head bob), Quincke's (nail-bed pulsation), Traube's (pistol-shot femorals), Duroziez's, and the **Austin Flint** murmur (mid-diastolic apical rumble).
- **Intervention:** symptomatic severe AR, or asymptomatic with **LVEF ≤50%** or a dilating LV/aortic root.

#### 5.3 Mitral Stenosis
- **Cause:** almost always **rheumatic**.
- **Signs:** low-pitched mid-**diastolic** "rumble" at the apex (bell, left lateral position), **loud S1**, **opening snap**, **malar flush**, **tapping** non-displaced apex.
- **Complications:** **AF** (LA enlargement), pulmonary hypertension, systemic embolism.
- **Intervention:** percutaneous **balloon mitral valvuloplasty** (if suitable, non-calcified, no LA thrombus/significant MR) or surgery.

#### 5.4 Mitral Regurgitation
- **Cause:** *primary* (degenerative/**prolapse**, rheumatic, endocarditis) vs *secondary/functional* (LV dilatation, ischaemic — the valve is structurally normal).
- **Signs:** **pansystolic** murmur at the apex radiating to the **axilla**, soft S1, displaced thrusting apex, S3.
- **Acute MR** (papillary muscle rupture post-MI, endocarditis) → sudden severe pulmonary oedema — surgical emergency.
- **Intervention:** symptomatic/severe primary MR → repair (preferred) or replacement; transcatheter edge-to-edge repair for selected secondary MR.

#### 5.5 Right-sided Valves
- **Tricuspid regurgitation:** usually *functional* (RV dilatation, pulmonary hypertension); also endocarditis (IV drug use). Pansystolic murmur louder on **inspiration**, **giant v waves**, pulsatile liver, ascites.

#### 5.6 Prosthetic Valves
- **Mechanical** — durable but need **lifelong warfarin** (DOACs contraindicated); favoured in younger patients.
- **Bioprosthetic** — no long-term anticoagulation but **degenerate in ~10–15 years**; favoured in older patients.
- **Complications:** thrombosis, embolism, endocarditis, haemolysis, structural degeneration, anticoagulant-related bleeding.

#### 5.7 Rheumatic Fever & Rheumatic Heart Disease
- **Mechanism:** immune cross-reactivity (molecular mimicry) ~2–4 weeks after **group A streptococcal** pharyngitis.
- **Jones criteria** (evidence of preceding strep + 2 major, or 1 major + 2 minor). **Major = "JONES"**: **J**oints (migratory polyarthritis), **O** ♡ carditis, subcutaneous **N**odules, **E**rythema marginatum, **S**ydenham chorea. **Minor:** fever, arthralgia, raised inflammatory markers, prolonged PR.
- **Chronic RHD:** predominantly **mitral stenosis**. High burden in Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities — secondary penicillin prophylaxis is key.

### Cardiovascular (ANU) — INFECTIVE ENDOCARDITIS — Valvular Heart Disease

| Definition | Infective inflammation of the cardiac endothelium, most commonly the valvesClassification: acute vs. subacute; native vs. prosthetic valve, R-sided vs. L-sidedValve leaflet vegeation: platelet-fibrin thrombi, WBCs, bacteria |
| --- | --- |
| Epidemiology | M>F, age > 60 y/o |
| Risk Factors | Pre-existing: previous IE, pre-damaged/prosthetic heart valves, congenital defects,  ↓ immunityBacteraemia: infected peripheral venous catheters, poor dentition, surgery, IVDU, various bacterial infections, haemodialysis |
| Aetiology | Pathogen | Characteristics |
| S.aureus (45-65%) | Most common acutely, affecting previously health valvesFatal within 6 weeks (if untreated) |
| Viridans streptococci (30%) | Most common subacutely, especially in pre- damaged mitral valves and prosthetic valves≥ 60 days after surgery |
| Enterococcus (<10%) | Multiple drug resistance, following nosocomial UTIs |
| Staphylococcus epidermis | Prosthetic heart valves |
| Gram-negative HACEK | Poor dental hygiene Haemophilus, Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans, Cardiobacterium hominis, Eikenella corrodens, Kingella kingae |
| Candida species | In immunosuppressed patients |

**Pathophysiology**

- Localized infection 🡪 bacteraemia 🡪 bacterial colonisation and thrombi formation on valve leaflets and chordae tendineaeValve destruction with ↓function
- Vessel occlusion/infarction from thrombo-emboli
- Immune complex against tissue antigens (GN, Osler Nodes)

- Mitral > aortic > tricuspid > pulmonary

**Presentation**

- ACUTE IE:
- Acute onset with rapid progression (days to weeks) and ↑ constitutional Sx
- Most common: S. aureus (can be caused by GAS, S. pneumoniae, N.gonorrhoeae)
- SUBACUTE IE:
- Insidious onset with slow progression (weeks to months) and ↓ constitutional Sx
- Most common: Streptococci
- PROSTHETIC VALVE IE:
- Early (<60d) vs. late (≥ 60d)
- S.epidermidis (early onset) or S.aureus (late onset)
- SYMPTOMS
- Constitutional: fever/chills, ↑HR, malaise, arthralgia, myalgia, cough, pleuritic chest pain
- Cardiac: murmur, CHF, arrthmias
- Mnemonic: Fever, Roth’s spots, Osler’s nodes, Murmur, Janeway lesions, Anemia, Nail-bed hemorrhages (i.e. splinter hemorrhages), Emboli
- Other: neurological (seizure, paresis), PE, splenomegaly, acute renal injury

**Duke Criteria**

- 1) 2 major, 2) 1 major + 3 minor OR 3) 5 minor
- Major diagnostic criteriaTwo separate blood cultures positive for typical pathogens
- Evidence of endocardial involvement in echocardiography (valve vegetations on TTE/TOE)
- A new valvular regurgitation (worsening of a pre-existing murmur is not sufficient)

- Minor diagnostic criteriaPredisposition (underlying heart disease or IV drug abuse)
- Fever ≥ 38°C
- Vascular (major arterial emboli, pulmonary infarcts, mycotic aneurysms, Janeway lesions)
- Immunologic disorder (GN, rheumatoid factor, Osler nodes, Roth’s spots)
- Microbiology

- Only negative findings on TOE can reliably rule out endocarditis, as TTE is not sensitive enough
- Tests: FBC (normochromic/normocytic anaemia), blood culture, leucocytosis (↑ESR/CRP), echocardiography , ECG (new AV block 🡪 abscess), CXR (HF, PE)

**Differentials**

RHD, SLE, prosthetic valve thrombosis, non-bacterial thrombotic endocarditis

**Management**

![](notes/images/cardiovascular-anu/img035.jpg)

- METHOD: 1. Blood cultures, 2. Immediate empiric AHx, 3. Adapttherapy according to culture
- EMPIRICAL THERAPYNative: IV vancomycin + penicillin G/gentamicin/ceftriaxone
- Prosthetic valves: vancomycin + gentamicin + rifampin + nafcillin
- IVDU: IV vancomycin + nafcillin/cloxacillin

- TARGETED THERAPY (4 weeks in native valve, 6 weeks in prosthetic valves)
- Staphylococci: vancomycin + nafcillin + gentamicin + rifampin
- Streptococci: penicillin
- Enterococci: penicillin/vancomycin + gentamicin/streptomycin
- HACEK:ceftriaxone + ampicillin
- PROPHYLAXIS in high risk patients undergoing procedures with ↑ risk of bacteraemia (amoxicillin)
- INDICATIONS FOR SURGERY: CHF, ↑ infection, systemic emboli, prosthetic valve, fungal

**Complications**

**CHF, conduction block, new emboli** (CND, retinal, pulmonary, coronary artery, renal, limb emboli), **Abx complications** (interstitial nephritis)

**Prognosis**

**Poor prognostic factors:** CHF, prosthetic valve infection, abscess, embolisation, persistent bacteraemia, altered mental status
