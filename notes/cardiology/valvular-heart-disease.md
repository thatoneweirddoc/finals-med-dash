## Valvular Heart Disease


### Cardiology Notes — Valvular Heart Disease

For every lesion, examiners want the same five things: **cause → murmur → other signs → key investigation → when to intervene.**

#### 5.1 Aortic Stenosis
- **Cause:** age-related **calcific/degenerative** (commonest >65), **bicuspid** valve (presents younger), rheumatic.
- **Symptoms — the ominous triad ("SAD"):** exertional **S**yncope, **A**ngina, **D**yspnoea. Onset of symptoms in severe AS signals poor prognosis and the need for intervention.
- **Signs:** ejection-systolic murmur (right 2nd space) radiating to the **carotids**, **slow-rising** pulse, **narrow** pulse pressure, soft/absent S2, S4, sustained heaving apex.
- **Severe AS:** valve area <1.0 cm², mean gradient >40 mmHg, peak jet velocity >4 m/s (echo).
- **Intervention:** **symptomatic severe AS**, or **asymptomatic severe AS with LVEF <50%** → valve replacement. **SAVR** (surgical) vs **TAVI** (transcatheter) decided by age, surgical risk and anatomy — TAVI now standard for older/higher-risk patients and increasingly used across the risk spectrum.

#### 5.2 Aortic Regurgitation
- **Cause:** *acute* (infective endocarditis, aortic dissection) — a flash-pulmonary-oedema emergency; *chronic* (bicuspid, rheumatic, or **aortic root dilatation** — Marfan, connective-tissue disease, hypertension).
- **Signs:** early-**diastolic** decrescendo murmur at the left sternal edge, best sitting forward in expiration; **collapsing (water-hammer) pulse**, **wide** pulse pressure. Eponymous peripheral signs: Corrigan's (visible carotid pulsation), de Musset's (head bob), Quincke's (nail-bed pulsation), Traube's (pistol-shot femorals), Duroziez's, and the **Austin Flint** murmur (mid-diastolic apical rumble).
- **Intervention:** symptomatic severe AR, or asymptomatic with **LVEF ≤50%** or a dilating LV/aortic root.

#### 5.3 Mitral Stenosis
- **Cause:** almost always **rheumatic**.
- **Signs:** low-pitched mid-**diastolic** "rumble" at the apex (bell, left lateral position), **loud S1**, **opening snap**, **malar flush**, **tapping** non-displaced apex.
- **Complications:** **AF** (LA enlargement), pulmonary hypertension, systemic embolism.
- **Intervention:** percutaneous **balloon mitral valvuloplasty** (if suitable, non-calcified, no LA thrombus/significant MR) or surgery.

#### 5.4 Mitral Regurgitation
- **Cause:** *primary* (degenerative/**prolapse**, rheumatic, endocarditis) vs *secondary/functional* (LV dilatation, ischaemic — the valve is structurally normal).
- **Signs:** **pansystolic** murmur at the apex radiating to the **axilla**, soft S1, displaced thrusting apex, S3.
- **Acute MR** (papillary muscle rupture post-MI, endocarditis) → sudden severe pulmonary oedema — surgical emergency.
- **Intervention:** symptomatic/severe primary MR → repair (preferred) or replacement; transcatheter edge-to-edge repair for selected secondary MR.

#### 5.5 Right-sided Valves
- **Tricuspid regurgitation:** usually *functional* (RV dilatation, pulmonary hypertension); also endocarditis (IV drug use). Pansystolic murmur louder on **inspiration**, **giant v waves**, pulsatile liver, ascites.

#### 5.6 Prosthetic Valves
- **Mechanical** — durable but need **lifelong warfarin** (DOACs contraindicated); favoured in younger patients.
- **Bioprosthetic** — no long-term anticoagulation but **degenerate in ~10–15 years**; favoured in older patients.
- **Complications:** thrombosis, embolism, endocarditis, haemolysis, structural degeneration, anticoagulant-related bleeding.

#### 5.7 Rheumatic Fever & Rheumatic Heart Disease
- **Mechanism:** immune cross-reactivity (molecular mimicry) ~2–4 weeks after **group A streptococcal** pharyngitis.
- **Jones criteria** (evidence of preceding strep + 2 major, or 1 major + 2 minor). **Major = "JONES"**: **J**oints (migratory polyarthritis), **O** ♡ carditis, subcutaneous **N**odules, **E**rythema marginatum, **S**ydenham chorea. **Minor:** fever, arthralgia, raised inflammatory markers, prolonged PR.
- **Chronic RHD:** predominantly **mitral stenosis**. High burden in Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities — secondary penicillin prophylaxis is key.
