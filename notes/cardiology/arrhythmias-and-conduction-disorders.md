## Arrhythmias & Conduction Disorders


### Cardiology Notes — Arrhythmias & Conduction Disorders

##### 4.1 Mechanisms
Three broad mechanisms: **re-entry** (a circuit — most tachyarrhythmias including AVNRT, AVRT, atrial flutter, VT), **enhanced automaticity** (an ectopic focus), and **triggered activity** (afterdepolarisations — e.g. torsades).

##### 4.2 Bradyarrhythmias and Conduction Disease
- **Sinus node dysfunction (sick sinus)**: inappropriate bradycardia, pauses, tachy-brady syndrome.
- **AV block**:
  - *First-degree*: PR >200 ms, every P conducts. Benign.
  - *Second-degree Mobitz I (Wenckebach)*: progressive PR lengthening until a dropped beat. Usually benign (AV nodal).
  - *Second-degree Mobitz II*: constant PR with intermittent dropped QRS. High risk of progression to complete block → **pace**.
  - *Third-degree (complete)*: AV dissociation — P waves and QRS independent; escape rhythm. **Cannon a waves**. Needs pacing.
- **Bundle branch / fascicular blocks** (see Installment 1 for WiLLiaM MaRRoW).

**Acute bradycardia with adverse features** (shock, syncope, myocardial ischaemia, heart failure): **atropine 500 µg IV**, repeat to 3 mg; if inadequate → transcutaneous pacing / isoprenaline / adrenaline infusion; then transvenous pacing. **Pacing indications**: symptomatic bradycardia, complete heart block, Mobitz II, and symptomatic sinus node disease.

##### 4.3 Supraventricular Tachycardias
Narrow-complex, regular unless AF/flutter with variable block:
- **AVNRT** — commonest paroxysmal SVT; re-entry within the AV node.
- **AVRT** — accessory pathway (see WPW below).
- **Atrial flutter** — macro-re-entry; "sawtooth" flutter waves, atrial rate ~300, often 2:1 block giving ventricular rate ~150.
- **Focal atrial tachycardia**.

**Acute SVT management** (stable, regular narrow-complex): **vagal manoeuvres** (Valsalva, carotid sinus massage) → **adenosine 6 mg** rapid IV push, then 12 mg, then 18 mg (warn re transient chest tightness/flushing; contraindicated in asthma — use verapamil). Unstable → synchronised DC cardioversion.

**Wolff–Parkinson–White (WPW)**: accessory pathway (bundle of Kent) → **short PR + delta wave** on resting ECG. Risk: if AF occurs, rapid antegrade conduction down the pathway can degenerate to VF. **Avoid AV-nodal blocking drugs (adenosine, verapamil, digoxin, beta-blockers) in pre-excited AF** — they can accelerate pathway conduction. Definitive treatment: catheter ablation.

##### 4.4 Atrial Fibrillation — in depth
Commonest sustained arrhythmia; **irregularly irregular** pulse, absent P waves.
- **Classification**: paroxysmal (<7 days, self-terminating), persistent (>7 days), permanent.
- **Management framework (ESC 2024 "AF-CARE")**: **C** Comorbidity/risk-factor management · **A** Avoid stroke/thromboembolism · **R** Reduce symptoms (rate/rhythm) · **E** Evaluation and dynamic reassessment.
- **Rate control** (first-line for most): beta-blocker or rate-limiting CCB (diltiazem/verapamil); digoxin if sedentary/HF.
- **Rhythm control**: cardioversion (electrical or pharmacological — flecainide if no structural disease, amiodarone if there is); **catheter ablation** is now first-line rhythm control for many with paroxysmal AF. Anticoagulate for ≥3 weeks before (or exclude LA appendage thrombus on TOE) and ≥4 weeks after cardioversion.
- **Anticoagulation** — the exam centrepiece:
  - **CHA₂DS₂-VA** (ESC 2024, sex removed): **C**HF, **H**ypertension, **A**ge ≥75 (2 pts), **D**iabetes, **S**troke/TIA/thromboembolism (2 pts), **V**ascular disease, **A**ge 65–74. Anticoagulate if ≥2; consider if 1. *(The older **CHA₂DS₂-VASc** additionally scores female sex — still widely used/taught.)*
  - **HAS-BLED** estimates bleeding risk (to manage modifiable factors, *not* to withhold anticoagulation): **H**ypertension, **A**bnormal renal/liver function, **S**troke, **B**leeding history, **L**abile INR, **E**lderly >65, **D**rugs/alcohol.
  - **DOACs preferred over warfarin** — except **mechanical valves** and **moderate–severe mitral stenosis** (use warfarin).

##### 4.5 Ventricular Arrhythmias and Channelopathies
- **VT** — broad-complex, regular; monomorphic (scar-related, post-MI) vs polymorphic. Pulseless VT is a cardiac-arrest rhythm. Stable sustained VT → amiodarone / cardioversion; unstable → synchronised DC shock.
- **VF** — chaotic, no output → immediate defibrillation.
- **Torsades de pointes** — polymorphic VT on a background of **long QT**; "twisting" around the baseline. Treat with **IV magnesium sulfate**, correct electrolytes, stop offending drugs, pace/isoprenaline if bradycardia-dependent.
- **Long QT syndrome** (congenital or acquired — drugs, ↓K⁺/↓Mg²⁺/↓Ca²⁺), **Brugada syndrome** (coved ST elevation V1–V2, risk of SCD), **CPVT** — inherited causes of SCD; family screening matters.

##### 4.6 Cardiac Arrest and Sudden Cardiac Death
- **Shockable** (VF / pulseless VT): defibrillate → 2 min CPR cycles.
- **Non-shockable** (PEA / asystole): CPR, adrenaline early.
- **Adult ALS drugs**: **Adrenaline 1 mg IV**, repeated every 3–5 min. In shockable rhythms give it **after the 3rd shock** (Resuscitation Council UK/ERC) — *note: **ANZCOR (Australia/NZ) gives adrenaline after the 2nd shock*** in shockable arrests. **Amiodarone 300 mg** after the 3rd shock (further 150 mg after the 5th); lidocaine is an alternative.
- **Reversible causes — 4 Hs & 4 Ts**: Hypoxia, Hypovolaemia, Hypo/Hyperkalaemia (& metabolic), Hypothermia; Tension pneumothorax, Tamponade, Toxins, Thrombosis (coronary or pulmonary).

##### 4.7 Cardiac Devices
- **Pacemakers** — for symptomatic bradycardia/high-grade block (nomenclature e.g. DDD, VVI).
- **ICDs** — sense and terminate VT/VF (primary or secondary prevention).
- **CRT** — biventricular pacing to resynchronise (criteria in Section 3.5). CRT-D combines CRT + ICD.
- Complications: infection, lead displacement, pneumothorax, and (device-specific) inappropriate shocks.

##### B. Spot Signs — *"See this → think this"*

| Sign | Think |
|---|---|
| Irregularly irregular pulse, no P waves | Atrial fibrillation |
| Sawtooth baseline, rate ~150 (2:1) | Atrial flutter |
| Short PR + delta wave | WPW (accessory pathway) |
| Cannon *a* waves, AV dissociation | Complete heart block |
| Progressive PR lengthening → dropped beat | Mobitz I (Wenckebach) |
| Constant PR, sudden dropped QRS | Mobitz II (→ pace) |
| Polymorphic VT twisting around baseline | Torsades de pointes (→ Mg²⁺) |
| Coved ST elevation V1–V2 | Brugada syndrome |
| S3 gallop + bibasal crackles + raised JVP | Congestive heart failure |

##### C. Disease Formulation Cards

> **HFrEF (systolic heart failure)**
> *One-liner:* impaired contractility, LVEF ≤40%. *Classic:* exertional dyspnoea, orthopnoea, PND, raised JVP, displaced apex, S3. *Ix:* ↑NT-proBNP, echo (low EF). *Mx:* four pillars + loop diuretic; ICD/CRT if criteria met. *Buzz:* "start low, start all".

> **HFpEF (diastolic heart failure)**
> *One-liner:* HF symptoms with LVEF ≥50% + raised filling pressures. *Classic:* elderly, hypertensive, AF, obese, diabetic. *Mx:* SGLT2i + treat comorbidities/congestion; no strong prognostic drug list like HFrEF. *Buzz:* "stiff ventricle".

> **Acute pulmonary oedema**
> *One-liner:* acute decompensation → alveolar flooding. *Classic:* severe breathlessness, pink frothy sputum, bibasal crackles, distress, hypoxia. *Ix:* CXR bat-wing. *Mx:* sit up, O₂/CPAP, IV loop diuretic, IV nitrate. *Buzz:* LMNOP.

> **Atrial fibrillation**
> *One-liner:* disorganised atrial activity → irregular ventricular response. *Classic:* palpitations, irregularly irregular pulse, absent P waves. *Mx:* rate/rhythm control + anticoagulation by CHA₂DS₂-VA (DOAC preferred). *Buzz:* AF-CARE pathway.

> **Complete (3rd-degree) heart block**
> *One-liner:* total AV conduction failure → independent escape rhythm. *Classic:* syncope, bradycardia, cannon *a* waves, AV dissociation on ECG. *Mx:* atropine as temporiser → pacing. *Buzz:* P waves "march through" the QRS.

> **Wolff–Parkinson–White**
> *One-liner:* accessory pathway pre-excitation. *Classic:* short PR + delta wave; SVT episodes; danger in pre-excited AF. *Mx:* avoid AV-nodal blockers in pre-excited AF; ablation is curative. *Buzz:* delta wave.

> **Torsades de pointes**
> *One-liner:* polymorphic VT on long QT. *Classic:* twisting axis, syncope; drug/electrolyte trigger. *Mx:* IV magnesium, correct K⁺/Mg²⁺, stop culprit drugs. *Buzz:* "twisting of the points".
