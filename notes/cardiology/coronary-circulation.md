## Coronary Circulation


### Cardiology Notes — Coronary Circulation

The **left main** coronary artery divides into the **LAD** and **circumflex**; the **right coronary** supplies the inferior wall and (in most people) the AV node. Two features are heavily examined:
- **Coronary flow is mainly diastolic.** During systole the contracting LV compresses its own intramural vessels, so the left ventricle is perfused chiefly in **diastole**. **🔗** **Tachycardia shortens diastole** and a low diastolic pressure both reduce coronary perfusion — why fast rates and hypotension worsen ischaemia.
- **Oxygen extraction is already near-maximal at rest** (~70–80%). The myocardium cannot extract much more, so increased demand must be met by **increased flow** (metabolic hyperaemia — adenosine, hypoxia → coronary vasodilatation), tightly **autoregulated** across a range of perfusion pressures.

**Myocardial O₂ supply–demand balance:** demand rises with **heart rate, contractility, and wall tension** (preload/afterload). **🔗** Anti-anginals work by tilting this balance — β-blockers cut rate/contractility (demand), nitrates cut preload (demand) and dilate coronaries (supply).

##### C. Concept Cards

> **Fast-response action potential** — Phases 0 (Na⁺ in) → 1 (K⁺ out, transient) → 2 (Ca²⁺ plateau) → 3 (K⁺ out) → 4 (rest, −90 mV). Long plateau → long refractory period → no tetany. *Drug link:* Vaughan–Williams classes.

> **Pacemaker potential** — no stable rest; I_f drives phase-4 drift to threshold; Ca²⁺ upstroke. SA node fastest → dominant pacemaker. *Drug link:* ivabradine (I_f), autonomic tone.

> **Excitation–contraction coupling** — AP → L-type Ca²⁺ → CICR via RyR2 → Ca²⁺–troponin C → tropomyosin shift → cross-bridge cycling. Relaxation via SERCA + NCX. *Drug link:* digoxin (Na/K-ATPase → ↑Ca²⁺ → ↑inotropy).

> **Frank–Starling law** — ↑ preload (EDV/stretch) → ↑ contractile force → ↑ SV; matches output to venous return. *Clinical link:* curve is flattened in HFrEF → congestion instead of output.

> **Pressure–volume loop** — filling → isovolumetric contraction → ejection → isovolumetric relaxation. Width = SV; ESPVR slope = contractility. ↑preload widens; ↑afterload raises pressure & ↓SV; ↑contractility ↓ESV.

> **Baroreceptor reflex** — carotid sinus (CN IX) + aortic arch (CN X) → medulla → autonomic outflow. ↑BP → ↑vagal/↓sympathetic → ↓HR & vasodilate. Buffers acute BP swings (e.g. standing).

> **Coronary physiology** — LV perfused in diastole; near-maximal O₂ extraction at rest; demand set by HR, contractility, wall tension. *Clinical link:* the whole logic of angina and anti-anginal therapy.

### Cardiovascular (ANU) — Acute coronary syndrome — Coronary Circulation

| Definition | Sx due to acute myocardial ischaemia ± infarction, attributable to obstruction of coronary arteries |
| --- | --- |
| Classification |  | ST Elevation | Cardiac Biomarkers |
| STEMI | + | + |
| NSTEMI | - | + |
| Unstable Angina | - | - |

- UNSTABLE ANGINA: ACS with no biomarker changes of myocardial damage; clinically defined:  ↑ frequency, duration (> 20m), ↓ threshold of exertion, ↓ response to treatment
- Angina at rest
- New onset of severe angina
- Angina that occurs after a recent episode of MI/post-procedure (e.g. PCI, CABG)

- MI is defined by evidence of myocardial necrosis and diagnosed with biomarker changesSymptoms of ischemia

- ECG changes (ST-T changes, new BBB, pathological Q waves)
- Imaging (↓myocardial viability, wall motion abnormality, intracoronary thrombus)
- If biomarkers unattainable, cardiac Sx + new ECG changes sufficient
- NSTEMI: myocardial infarction without ST elevation or BBB
- Partial/near-complete occlusion of a coronary artery
- STEMI: clinical features of ACS in addition to either: 1) ST elevation of at least 1mm in 2 contiguous limb leads, 2) ST elevation of at least 2mm in 2 contiguous chest leads or 3) new LBBBComplete occlusion of a coronary artery

**Epidemiology**

Globally, leading cause of death for men and women

**Normal Arterial Wall**

- INTIMASingle layer of endothelial cells
- Barrier b/w circulating blood and vessel wall
- Non-thrombogenic surface with vasodilator/anti-inflammatory properties

- MEDIASmooth muscle cells and ECM
- Contractile and elastic functions
- Synthesises inflammatory mediators (IL-6, TNF-α) 🡪 leukocyte proliferation + adhesion

- ADVENTITIA

- Nerves, lymphatics and blood vessels (vasa vasorum) 🡪 nourishes arterial wall cells

**Pathophysiology of an atherosclerotic wall **

- 1) endothelial dysfunction 2) accumulation of lipids within intima, 3) leukocytes and smooth muscle cells to vessel wall, 4) foam cells, 5) ECM deposition
- FORMATION OF A FATTY STREAK (LIPID-FILLED FOAM CELLS)Arterial endothelium injury (chemical/physical) 🡪 cytokines 🡪 cell surface adhesion molecules + impaired permeability 🡪 monocytes/T lymphocytes enter subendothelial space
- LDL bind to glycoproteins 🡪 accumulate within subendothelial space 🡪 oxidation by ROS
- Monocytes differentiate into macrophages which phagocytose LDL to form foam cells
- Impaired efflux of these cells leads to local accumulation in the plaque

- PLAQUE PROGRESSION:↑ platelet to damaged endothelium 🡪 release factors that cause smooth muscle cell migration into intima 🡪 proliferation 🡪 collagen + other ECM deposition 🡪 atherosclerotic plaque (raised focal lesion with thrombogenic lipid core covered by a firm, white, fibrous cap)
- Significant lumen narrowing 🡪 tissue ischaemia 🡪 angina pectoris

- PLAQUE DISRUPTION:Plaque causes ↓ in lumen size and ↓ ability to dilate/constrict artery when needed
- Vulnerable plaque: thin cap, ↑ lipid content/macrophages/haemodynamic stresses, ↓ SMC
- Stable plaque: thick fibrous cap, small lipid core

- PLAQUE RUPTURE AND THROMBUS FORMATION:Fissure in thin fibrous cap 🡪 ACS when plaque ruptures 🡪 exposes subendothelial matrix elements 🡪 platelet and thrombus (→ direct activation of coagulation cascade and fibrin by tissue factor) 🡪 partial (UA) or complete occlusion (MI) of infarct-related artery
- Infarction can be eitherTransmural: ST elevation (STEMI)
- Sub-endocardial: ST depression (NSTEMI)

**Presentation**

- Commonly occluded coronary arteries: LAD > RCA > circumflex
- Local myocardial inflammation 🡪 irritation of cardiac plexus, T1-T4 dermatome afferents and Irritation of cardiac branches of vagus nerve 🡪  reflexive vagal responsesRetrosternal chest pain, radiating to jaw, arm or neck
- Weakness, dizziness, nausea, vomiting, palpations, syncope

- Decreased systolic function (since necrotic myocardium cannot contract as well)
- Exertional dyspnoea: Blood backing up from LV 🡪 pulmonary HTN 🡪 ↓lung compliance
- Diaphoresis: ↓CO 🡪 ↑sympathetic activity to maintain CO
- Cool skin: ↑sympathetic activity to maintain CO 🡪 generalized vasoconstriction

**Differentials**

- Need to rule out: PE, aortic dissection, pneumothorax, perforated abdominal viscus
- Consider: CHF, pericarditis, costochondritis, endocarditis, myocarditis

**Examination**

- OBSERVATIONS: ↑ HR, ↑ RR, ↓ SaO2, ↓ BP (ventricular dysfunction), HTN (anxiety, exogenous sympathomimetic stimulation)
- GENERAL: diaphoresis, cool skin (? cardiogenic shock)
- CARDIO: ↑JVP, S3 andS4 (transient LVF), carotid bruit (aortic stenosis), pulmonary oedema (LHF), ↓ pedal pulses, systolic murmur (dynamic obstruction of LV outflow tract)

**Investigations**

- 12- LEAD ECG: for all those with chest pain
- STEMI: new LBBB or ST-segment elevation of at least 1mm in 2 or more contiguous leads
- NSTEMI: ST depressions (atleast 0.5 mm) or T wave inversions (1.0 mm at least) without Q waves in 2 contiguous leads with prominent R wave or R/S ratio >1
- Isolated T wave inversions: ↑risk for MI; may represent Wellen’s syndrome (critical LAD stenosis, >2mm inversions in anterior precordial leads )
- Unstable angina: may have non-specific or transient ST depressions/elevation

![](notes/images/cardiovascular-anu/img012.jpg)

- CARDIAC BIOMARKERS:
- Troponin: ↑ within 3-12 hours from chest pain onset and peaks at 24-48 hours (thus, repeat within 6 hours). 3 proteins (I, T, C) which are situated with tropomyosin on thin actin. Troponin I and T released in myocyte necrosis and are highly sensitive (trops also present in renal failure, PE, sepsis, HF)
- CK-MB: diagnose re-infarction
- Myoglobin: more rapidly ↑ than trops/CK-MB (detected as early as 2 hours after, ↓ specific)
- BNP: prognostic value after MI
- CXR: HF, cardiomegaly
- ECHOCARDIOGRAM: systolic murmur and LV function
- EXERCISE STRESS-TEST

**Management**

ACUTE (MNEMONIC MONASH TB)

- Stabilise: ABCs, correct haemodynamic status, telemetry, 3 sets of troponins (if no ECG changes)
- Morphine
- Oxygen: if PaO2 < 90
- Nitrate: glyceryl trinitrate (sublingual/IV) → vasodilation (CI: RV and inferior infarcts as vasodilation ↑ venous return which ↑ pumping action of R. heart)
- Aspirin (COX Inhibitor) + Clopidogrel (inhibits platelet activation)
- Heparin: unfractioned heparin/LMWH
- Tests: telemetry, 12-lead ECG, IV (FBC, EUC, lipids, coags, cardiac enzymes)
- β-blockers: e.g. metoprolol (first dose IV and then oral administration; CCBs second-line)
- Thrombolysis to re-perfuse artery (only for STEMI)
- Recombinant tPA when no PCI acess within 90 minutes and thrombolysis contraindications
- Performed in a highly acute setting ≤12hours of symptom onset
- Contraindications: prior ICH, known malignant intracranial neoplasm, closed-head/facial trauma (≤3m), ischaemic stroke (≤3m), active bleeding, suspected aortic dissection
- Emergency revascularization (necessary for STEMI but optional otherwise)
- Coronary angiography (locate infarcted vessel) + PCI within 90 minutes (balloon angioplasty and stenting or atherectomy)
- Primary PCI (without prior thrombolytic therapy) and recue PCI (following failed thrombolytic therapy shown by < ½ reduction in ST elevation)
- PCI is used in medically refractive angina, NSTEMI, UA and rescue/primary for STEMI
- Balloon inflation to dilate coronary lesions 🡪 intracoronary stent to prevent restenosis (bare metal stent, drug-eluting stent)
- Coronary bypass grafting (if PCI unsuccessful or in high risk patients with multiple lesions)
- Post procedure: to ↓ ischemic complications, aspirin, heparin and consider GPIIb/IIIa inhibitors (abciximab, eptifbatide)
- Following stent implantation: dual antiplatelet therapy

| PCI | CABG |
| --- | --- |
| Less invasive, ↓ periprocedural M&M, ↓ hospitalisationIndications: single/double-vessel disease, inability to tolerate surgery | ↑ ability for complete revascularisationIndications: triple-vessel disease (≥ 70% stenosis), left main disease (≥ 50%), DM, plaque morphology unfavourable for PCIConduits include saphenous vein, left inte |

POST MI (MNEMONIC LEN SAAB C)

- Lifestyle modification, cardiac rehabilitation, regular echocardiogram (LVEF)
- Educate patient to control risk factors
- Nitrates: alleviate ischemia
- Statin: plaque stabilisation
- Aspirin
- ACE-I/ARB
- B-Blocker: metoprolol, first IV followed by oral (second-line: CCBs, aldosterone antagonists)
- Clopidogrel (dual anti-platelet therapy)

RISK STRATIFICATION: TIMI SCORE - 0-2: LOW 3-4: INTERMEDIATE 5-7: HIGH RISK

- NSTEMI or unstable angina
- Age ≥ 65
- ≥3 CAD risk factors: HTN, hyperlipidaemia, diabetes, smoker, FHx of early MI
- CAD with ≥ 50% stenosis
- ST segment deviation
- ≥ 2 anginal episodes in past 24 hours
- Aspirin in the past week
- ↑cardiac enzymes

**Complications**

- Cardiac rupture, Arrhythmia, Shock, HTN/HF, Pericarditis/PE, Aneurysm, DVT/death
- CARDIAC SHOCK: state of inadequate tissue perfusion due to cardiac dysfunction (sBP <80 mmHG/<90mmHg with inotropes, ↓ cardiac index < 2.0L/min/m2, oliguria, peripheral vasoconstriction and altered LOC)

**Prognosis**

~ 40% of people who experience an MI in a given year will die as a result
