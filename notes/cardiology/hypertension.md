## Hypertension


### Cardiology Notes — Hypertension

#### 8.1 Definitions and Measurement
- **Measure properly:** seated, rested, correct cuff; confirm raised clinic readings with **ambulatory (ABPM)** or **home (HBPM)** monitoring to catch **white-coat** (high clinic, normal out-of-office) and **masked** (normal clinic, high out-of-office) hypertension.
- **⚠️ Guideline divergence — know which your exam uses:**
  - **ESC 2024:** *non-elevated* <120/70 · **"Elevated BP" 120–139/70–89** (new category) · **Hypertension ≥140/90**. Treatment target **120–129 mmHg systolic** if tolerated (relaxed in the frail/elderly).
  - **NICE:** stage 1 = clinic ≥140/90 (ABPM/HBPM ≥135/85); stage 2 = ≥160/100.
  - **ACC/AHA (2017):** hypertension defined lower, at ≥130/80.

#### 8.2 Primary vs Secondary Hypertension
- **Primary (essential)** — ~90–95%; no single cause.
- **Secondary** — suspect if young, resistant, or with suggestive features. Causes:
  - **Renal:** parenchymal disease; **renovascular** (renal artery stenosis — atherosclerotic in older, fibromuscular dysplasia in young women).
  - **Endocrine:** **primary aldosteronism (Conn's)** — the commonest endocrine cause, look for hypokalaemia and a raised aldosterone:renin ratio; **phaeochromocytoma** (episodic headache, sweating, palpitations); **Cushing's**; thyroid/parathyroid disease.
  - **Coarctation of the aorta**, **obstructive sleep apnoea**, and drugs (NSAIDs, steroids, oral contraceptive, sympathomimetics, liquorice).

#### 8.3 End-organ Damage
Hypertension-mediated organ damage: **LV hypertrophy**/heart failure, coronary disease, **stroke** and vascular dementia, **chronic kidney disease** and albuminuria, and **hypertensive retinopathy** (Keith–Wagener grades I–IV).

#### 8.4 Management
- **Lifestyle first/always:** salt reduction, weight loss, exercise, alcohol moderation, DASH-style diet.
- **Pharmacological algorithm (A/C/D):**
  - **Step 1:** **ACEi/ARB** (**A**) — especially younger/diabetic/CKD; or a **calcium-channel blocker** (**C**) — especially older or Afro-Caribbean patients.
  - **Step 2:** combine A + C.
  - **Step 3:** add a **thiazide-like diuretic** (**D**) → A + C + D.
  - **Step 4 (resistant HTN):** add **spironolactone** (if K⁺ ≤4.5), or a beta-/alpha-blocker.
  - **Beta-blockers are no longer routine first-line** for uncomplicated hypertension (reserved for a compelling indication — angina, HF, post-MI, rate control, pregnancy).

#### 8.5 Hypertensive Emergencies
- **Hypertensive emergency** = severe hypertension **with acute end-organ damage** (encephalopathy, acute LV failure/pulmonary oedema, ACS, **aortic dissection**, acute kidney injury, eclampsia, papilloedema). **Urgency** = severely raised BP without acute organ threat.
- **Malignant/accelerated hypertension:** very high BP with grade III/IV retinopathy (haemorrhages, exudates, papilloedema) ± renal failure.
- **Principle:** in emergencies lower BP in a **controlled** way with IV agents (avoid precipitous drops → watershed infarction) — *except aortic dissection*, which needs rapid, aggressive control (see below). Uncomplicated urgency is managed with oral agents over hours–days.

**Mechanism of BP control**

CENTRAL MECHANISM

- ↓BP 🡪 ↓ arterial pressure 🡪 ↓ stretch 🡪 ↓ afferent baroreceptor firing  🡪 ↑ efferent sympathetic and ↓ efferent parasympathetic 🡪  vasoconstriction, ↑ HR,   ↑ contractility,  ↑ BP
- Carotid massage ↑ carotid sinus pressure to ↓ HR

HORMONAL MECHANISMS

- Sympathetic activation 🡪 adrenal glands release adrenaline, noradrenaline and corticosteroids (volume control) 🡪 ↑ cardiac output, ↑ peripheral vasoconstriction
- Kidneys: ↑ renin (activate RAAS) and EPO (↑ RBC formation) 🡪 ↑ blood volume

ANP/BNP

- ANP: from atrial myocytes in response to ↑blood volume and atrial pressure via cGMP. Causes vasodilation, Na+ reabsorption at the RCT and dilates afferent + constricts efferent renal arterioles (causing diuresis and “aldosterone escape” mechanism)
- BNP:  from ventricular myocytes in response to  ↑ tension. Similar physiologic action to ANP, with longer half-life. Very good negative predictive value in diagnosing HF and Tx of HF (nesiritide)

**Aetiology**

- ESSENTIAL (PRIMARY) HTN (90%) related to CO and TPR
- SECONDARY HTN (10%):
- Renal (parenchymal disease, GN, pyelonephritis, polycystic kidney)
- Endocrine (1o hyperaldosteronism, pheochromocytoma, Cushing’s, ↑thyroid, ↑PTH, ↑Ca2+, gestational)
- Vascular (coarctation of aorta, renal artery stenosis)
- Drug-induced (oestrogens/OCP, MAOIs, cocaine, amphetamine, steroids, lithium, NSAIDs, decongestants, EtOH)
- HTN is associated with structural changes, remodelling and hypertrophy in resistance arterioles
- Isolated systolic HTN associated with progressive ↓ in vascular compliance

**Risk Factors**

↑ age, male, dyslipidaemia, obesity, DM, physical inactivity, ↑ salt/fatty diet, FHx; African American > Caucasian > Asian

**Features**

- HTN URGENCY: severe (≥ 180/≥ 120 mm Hg) hypertension without acute end-organ damage
- HTN EMERGENCY: severe HTN + acute end-organ damage; types are accelerated HTN (papilloedema) and malignant HTN (causing papilloedema and vascular damage e.g. retinal haemorrhage, bulging disc, mental status change, ↑ creatinine)
- ↓ K+ is an indicator of endocrine HTN (primary aldosteronism, endogenous/exogenous glucocorticoids)

**Differentials**

White coat syndrome (from anxiety)

**Investigations (use per indication)**

- Self-measurement of BP at home
- Bloods: EUCs, BSL, lipids, endocrine tests if suspected
- 12-lead ECG
- Urine: urinalysis, urinary protein excretion (DM, CKD)
- Renovascular HTN: renal U/S, MRA/CTA
- Echocardiography: LV dysfunction?

**Treatment**

- Target: BP: <140/90 mmHG, <130/80 mmHg if DM, sBP < 150 in elderly
- Non-pharmacological: diet (↓cholesterol, saturated fats, Na+ intake ≤4g/day, no K/Mg/Ca supplementation), exercise, stress management, smoking cessation, ↓ EtOH, healthy BMI
- Pharmacological
- Begin for Grade 3, isolated systolic HTN, evidence of end-organ damage and high CVD risk
- Start with lowest dose 🡪 if it doesn’t work, change to lowest dose of another drug 🡪 if that doesn’t work, add a second agent (trial each regimen for at least 6 weeks before altering)
- Once a combination regimen is established, more convenient to use a combined preparation

**End-organ sites in progressive HTB**

- Brain: stroke, TIA, dementia, eclampsia
- Heart: CCF, dysfunctional valvular disease, AF, left atrial enlargement, LVH, aortic dissection
- Vessel: loss of capillary density (rarefaction) and loss of medium and small vessels (hypertrophy and occlusion) and large vessel changes (atherosclerosis), progressive CAD
- Eyes: retinopathy (eye pain, ↓ vision), papilloedema
- Kidney: renal glomerular ischemia (dramatic vessel hypertrophy), renal artery stenosis (larger vessel changes), albuminuria (small vessel disease)
- Aneurysm: abdominal aorta (rupture=life threatening)
