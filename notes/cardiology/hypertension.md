## Hypertension


### Cardiology Notes — Hypertension

#### 8.1 Definitions and Measurement
- **Measure properly:** seated, rested, correct cuff; confirm raised clinic readings with **ambulatory (ABPM)** or **home (HBPM)** monitoring to catch **white-coat** (high clinic, normal out-of-office) and **masked** (normal clinic, high out-of-office) hypertension.
- **⚠️ Guideline divergence — know which your exam uses:**
  - **ESC 2024:** *non-elevated* <120/70 · **"Elevated BP" 120–139/70–89** (new category) · **Hypertension ≥140/90**. Treatment target **120–129 mmHg systolic** if tolerated (relaxed in the frail/elderly).
  - **NICE:** stage 1 = clinic ≥140/90 (ABPM/HBPM ≥135/85); stage 2 = ≥160/100.
  - **ACC/AHA (2017):** hypertension defined lower, at ≥130/80.

#### 8.2 Primary vs Secondary Hypertension
- **Primary (essential)** — ~90–95%; no single cause.
- **Secondary** — suspect if young, resistant, or with suggestive features. Causes:
  - **Renal:** parenchymal disease; **renovascular** (renal artery stenosis — atherosclerotic in older, fibromuscular dysplasia in young women).
  - **Endocrine:** **primary aldosteronism (Conn's)** — the commonest endocrine cause, look for hypokalaemia and a raised aldosterone:renin ratio; **phaeochromocytoma** (episodic headache, sweating, palpitations); **Cushing's**; thyroid/parathyroid disease.
  - **Coarctation of the aorta**, **obstructive sleep apnoea**, and drugs (NSAIDs, steroids, oral contraceptive, sympathomimetics, liquorice).

#### 8.3 End-organ Damage
Hypertension-mediated organ damage: **LV hypertrophy**/heart failure, coronary disease, **stroke** and vascular dementia, **chronic kidney disease** and albuminuria, and **hypertensive retinopathy** (Keith–Wagener grades I–IV).

#### 8.4 Management
- **Lifestyle first/always:** salt reduction, weight loss, exercise, alcohol moderation, DASH-style diet.
- **Pharmacological algorithm (A/C/D):**
  - **Step 1:** **ACEi/ARB** (**A**) — especially younger/diabetic/CKD; or a **calcium-channel blocker** (**C**) — especially older or Afro-Caribbean patients.
  - **Step 2:** combine A + C.
  - **Step 3:** add a **thiazide-like diuretic** (**D**) → A + C + D.
  - **Step 4 (resistant HTN):** add **spironolactone** (if K⁺ ≤4.5), or a beta-/alpha-blocker.
  - **Beta-blockers are no longer routine first-line** for uncomplicated hypertension (reserved for a compelling indication — angina, HF, post-MI, rate control, pregnancy).

#### 8.5 Hypertensive Emergencies
- **Hypertensive emergency** = severe hypertension **with acute end-organ damage** (encephalopathy, acute LV failure/pulmonary oedema, ACS, **aortic dissection**, acute kidney injury, eclampsia, papilloedema). **Urgency** = severely raised BP without acute organ threat.
- **Malignant/accelerated hypertension:** very high BP with grade III/IV retinopathy (haemorrhages, exudates, papilloedema) ± renal failure.
- **Principle:** in emergencies lower BP in a **controlled** way with IV agents (avoid precipitous drops → watershed infarction) — *except aortic dissection*, which needs rapid, aggressive control (see below). Uncomplicated urgency is managed with oral agents over hours–days.
