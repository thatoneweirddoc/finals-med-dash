## Infective, Inflammatory & Pericardial Disease


### Cardiology Notes — Infective, Inflammatory & Pericardial Disease

#### 6.1 Infective Endocarditis
- **Organisms:** *Staphylococcus aureus* (commonest overall; acute, IV drug use, prosthetic), *Streptococcus viridans* (subacute, poor dentition), *Enterococcus*, coagulase-negative staph (prosthetic), the HACEK group, *Streptococcus gallolyticus* (bovis — look for **colorectal cancer**), and culture-negative causes (Coxiella, Bartonella).
- **Peripheral signs — "FROM JANE":** **F**ever, **R**oth spots (retinal), **O**sler nodes (painful pulps — "Ouch"), **M**urmur (new/changed), **J**aneway lesions (painless palms/soles), **A**naemia, **N**ail splinter haemorrhages, **E**mboli.
- **Modified Duke criteria** (updated by **2023 Duke-ISCVID** — added CT/**PET-CT** imaging, new microbiology, and surgical/intraoperative findings as major criteria; the classic structure is still examined):
  - **Major:** persistently positive blood cultures with a typical organism; evidence of endocardial involvement (echo vegetation/abscess/new regurgitation; now also cardiac CT/PET-CT).
  - **Minor:** predisposing cardiac lesion or IV drug use; fever ≥38 °C; **vascular** phenomena (emboli, Janeway, mycotic aneurysm, septic pulmonary infarct); **immunologic** phenomena (Osler nodes, Roth spots, glomerulonephritis); microbiological evidence not meeting a major criterion.
  - **Definite** = 2 major, *or* 1 major + 3 minor, *or* 5 minor.
- **Investigation:** **three sets of blood cultures** from different sites before antibiotics; **echo — TTE then TOE** (TOE more sensitive for vegetations, abscess, prosthetic valves).
- **Management:** prolonged **IV antibiotics** (empirical, then organism-directed, typically 4–6 weeks). **Surgery** for heart failure from valve destruction, uncontrolled/persistent infection or abscess, large/mobile vegetations with embolic risk, or prosthetic-valve involvement.
- **Prophylaxis:** now **restricted to the highest-risk patients** (prosthetic valve, previous IE, certain congenital disease) undergoing high-risk dental procedures — a big shift from historical blanket prophylaxis; know that guidance is conservative and varies by body.

#### 6.2 Pericardial Disease
- **Acute pericarditis:** causes — viral/idiopathic (commonest), post-MI/**Dressler**, uraemia, autoimmune, TB, malignancy. Features: **sharp, pleuritic** chest pain, worse lying flat and **relieved by sitting forward**, pericardial **friction rub**. ECG: **widespread saddle-shaped (concave) ST elevation + PR depression**. Management: **NSAIDs + colchicine**.
- **Pericardial effusion → cardiac tamponade:** **Beck's triad** (hypotension, raised JVP, muffled heart sounds), **pulsus paradoxus**, **electrical alternans**, globular "water-bottle" heart on CXR; echo is diagnostic (diastolic RV collapse). Treatment: **urgent pericardiocentesis**.
- **Constrictive pericarditis:** rigid/calcified pericardium restricts filling. **Kussmaul's sign**, pericardial **knock**, pericardial calcification. Distinguish from restrictive cardiomyopathy (imaging, haemodynamics).

#### 6.3 Myocarditis
Inflammation of the myocardium — often **viral** (e.g. Coxsackie B). Spectrum from mild chest pain to fulminant heart failure or life-threatening arrhythmia. Raised troponin, ECG changes; **cardiac MRI** (oedema/late gadolinium) and sometimes biopsy. Largely **supportive** care; treat HF/arrhythmia; avoid strenuous exercise during recovery.
