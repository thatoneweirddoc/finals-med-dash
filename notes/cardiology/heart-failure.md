## Heart Failure


### Cardiology Notes — Heart Failure

#### 3.1 Definitions and Classification
Heart failure is a clinical syndrome — symptoms (breathlessness, fatigue, ankle swelling) and signs (raised JVP, crackles, oedema) caused by a structural/functional cardiac abnormality producing raised intracardiac pressures or inadequate output.

**By ejection fraction** (drives treatment eligibility):
- **HFrEF** — LVEF ≤40% (reduced).
- **HFmrEF** — LVEF 41–49% (mildly reduced).
- **HFpEF** — LVEF ≥50% (preserved) — diagnosis needs *objective evidence* of raised filling pressures/structural change (e.g. raised NT-proBNP, LVH, LA enlargement, diastolic dysfunction).

**Other axes:** acute vs chronic; left vs right vs biventricular; and **high-output** failure (anaemia, thyrotoxicosis, AV fistula, beriberi, pregnancy — the heart is normal but demand outstrips supply).

**NYHA functional class** (symptom burden, reassessed often): I (no limitation) · II (slight — symptoms on ordinary activity) · III (marked — symptoms on less-than-ordinary activity) · IV (symptoms at rest).

#### 3.2 Pathophysiology
A drop in output triggers compensatory **neurohormonal activation** that is adaptive short-term but maladaptive long-term:
- **RAAS**: angiotensin II (vasoconstriction) and aldosterone (Na⁺/water retention) → afterload and congestion.
- **Sympathetic nervous system**: tachycardia, vasoconstriction, and direct myocyte toxicity.
- **Natriuretic peptides** (BNP/ANP): a counter-regulatory system (natriuresis, vasodilatation) — overwhelmed as disease progresses.
These pathways drive **adverse remodelling** (dilatation, hypertrophy, fibrosis). Every disease-modifying HF drug works by *blocking* one of these axes — which is why the "four pillars" together outperform any single agent.

#### 3.3 Causes and Precipitants
Causes: ischaemic (commonest), hypertensive, valvular, cardiomyopathic, arrhythmic (tachycardia-induced), toxic (alcohol, anthracyclines, trastuzumab), infiltrative, congenital, high-output.
**Decompensation triggers** (learn these for the "why did they deteriorate" question): arrhythmia (esp. AF), ischaemia/MI, infection, non-adherence (drugs/salt/fluid), **NSAIDs**, negatively inotropic drugs, anaemia, thyroid disease, renal dysfunction, and IV fluids.

#### 3.4 Diagnosis
- **Clinical**: congestion (orthopnoea, PND, raised JVP, crackles, oedema, S3 gallop) vs low output (fatigue, cool peripheries, hypotension).
- **NT-proBNP** — high negative predictive value; a normal value largely excludes HF and gates echo. (Use **NT-proBNP** to monitor patients on sacubitril/valsartan, which raises BNP but not NT-proBNP.)
- **Echocardiography** — defines EF, cause, and reversible pathology.
- **CXR** — the ABCDE of failure: **A**lveolar oedema (bat-wing), **B** Kerley B lines, **C**ardiomegaly, **D** upper-lobe **D**iversion, **E**ffusions.

#### 3.5 Chronic HFrEF Management — the Four Pillars
Foundational disease-modifying therapy, all four Class I, **"start low, start all"** (get all four classes on board at low dose early rather than sequential up-titration):
1. **ARNI (sacubitril/valsartan) — or ACEi / ARB** if ARNI unsuitable.
2. **Beta-blocker** (bisoprolol, carvedilol, or metoprolol succinate — start when euvolaemic).
3. **MRA** (spironolactone or eplerenone — monitor K⁺ and renal function).
4. **SGLT2 inhibitor** (dapagliflozin/empagliflozin) — benefit **regardless of diabetes**; single fixed dose, easy to start.

**Symptom control:** **loop diuretics** (furosemide) relieve congestion but do *not* improve mortality.

**Device therapy** (in addition to optimal medical therapy):
- **ICD** for primary prevention: symptomatic HFrEF, **LVEF ≤35%** despite **≥3 months** of guideline therapy, expected survival >1 year. *Not* within 40 days of MI. (Ischaemic aetiology has the strongest evidence.)
- **CRT** (biventricular pacing) for **LVEF ≤35%, NYHA II–IV, QRS ≥150 ms with LBBB** (strongest indication); weaker for QRS 130–149 ms or non-LBBB.

**Other agents:** ivabradine (sinus rhythm, HR ≥70 on max beta-blocker), hydralazine/nitrate (esp. if ACEi/ARB not tolerated), vericiguat (worsening HF), IV iron for iron deficiency, digoxin (rate control/symptoms, no mortality benefit).

#### 3.6 Acute Heart Failure / Acute Pulmonary Oedema
Emergency. Immediate management by the **LMNOP** memory aid:
- **L**asix (IV loop diuretic), **M**orphine (cautiously), **N**itrates (IV — if BP allows, reduce preload/afterload), **O**xygen (if hypoxic; **CPAP** for refractory), **P**osition (sit up).
Identify and treat the precipitant. Characterise haemodynamics as **wet/dry** (congestion) and **warm/cold** (perfusion): "wet & warm" is the commonest — diurese and vasodilate; "wet & cold" (cardiogenic shock) needs inotropes/support.

#### 3.7 Advanced Heart Failure
**Cardiogenic shock**: hypotension + hypoperfusion despite adequate filling. Manage the cause (revascularise if ischaemic), cautious inotropes/vasopressors (dobutamine, noradrenaline) as a bridge, and **mechanical circulatory support** (IABP, Impella, VA-ECMO, LVAD) in selected patients. **Transplantation** for end-stage disease; **palliative care** where appropriate.
