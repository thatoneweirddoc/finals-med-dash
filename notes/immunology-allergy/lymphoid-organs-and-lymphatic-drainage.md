## Lymphoid Organs and Lymphatic Drainage


### Immunology & Allergy Master Notes — Lymphoid Organs and Lymphatic Drainage

**Primary** (where lymphocytes mature): bone marrow (B-cell maturation) and thymus (T-cell maturation). **Secondary** (where immune responses are mounted): spleen, lymph nodes, tonsils, Peyer's patches.

**The lymph node — three zones** (a classic single-mark question):

| Zone | Contains | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Follicle (cortex)** | **B cells** | B-cell localisation and proliferation; germinal centres in active response |
| **Paracortex** | **T cells** | T-cell zone; enlarges in viral infection, underdeveloped in DiGeorge |
| **Medulla** | Reticular cells, macrophages, plasma cells | Communicates with efferent lymphatics |

**Lymphatic drainage — the two-duct rule:** the **right lymphatic duct** drains the right side of the body **above the diaphragm**; the **thoracic duct** drains everything else, emptying at the junction of the left subclavian and internal jugular veins. (This is why a left supraclavicular node — **Virchow's node** — signals abdominal malignancy.)

**Spleen.** Splenic macrophages remove encapsulated bacteria opsonised by IgM and complement.

- **Splenic dysfunction** (post-splenectomy, sickle cell autosplenectomy, coeliac disease): ↓IgM → ↓complement activation → ↓C3b opsonisation → susceptibility to encapsulated organisms.
- **Post-splenectomy blood film: Howell–Jolly bodies** (nuclear remnants — the classic finding), target cells, thrombocytosis and lymphocytosis (loss of sequestration).
- **Management:** vaccinate against pneumococcus, Hib, meningococcus (ideally **≥2 weeks pre-splenectomy**), annual influenza, and **lifelong prophylactic penicillin**.

**Thymus.** Anterosuperior mediastinum; immature T cells in the cortex, mature in the medulla. Hypoplastic in **DiGeorge** syndrome and **SCID**. **Thymoma** — associated with **myasthenia gravis** and SVC obstruction.

### Rheumatology & Immunology (ANU) — IMMUNE SYSTEM ORGANS — Lymphoid Organs and Lymphatic Drainage

- 1O organs: bone marrow (B cell maturation) and thymus (T cell maturation)
- 2o organs: spleen, lymph nodes, tonsils, Peyer’s patches
- The lymph node: Encapsulated with trabeculae and functions are nonspecific filtration by macrophages, storage of B and T cells, and immune response activation. The follicle is the site of B-cell localisation/proliferation, the medulla communicates with efferent lymphatics and contains reticular cells/macrophages and the paracortex houses T cells.

- Diagram shows where T cells, B cells, and red cells can be found
- Macrophages found nearby in spleen remove encapsulated bacteria
- ![](notes/images/rheumatology-and-immunology-anu/img001.jpg)
SPLENIC DYSFUNCTION (POST-SPLENECTOMY, SICKLE CELL): ↓ IgM → ↓ complement activation → ↓ C3b opsonization → ↑ susceptibility to encapsulated organism
- POST-SPLENECTOMY: Howell-Jolly bodies (nuclear remnants), target cells, thrombocytosis (loss of sequesteration and removal), lymphocytosis (loss of sequestration)
- Vaccinate patients undergoing splenectomy against encapsulated organisms (pneumococcal, HiB, meningococcal)

### Rheumatology & Immunology (ANU) — THYMUS — Lymphoid Organs and Lymphatic Drainage

- Located in the anterosuperior mediastinum and encapsulated
- Site of T cell differentiation/maturation (immature T cells in cortex and mature T cells in medulla)
- Hypoplastic in DiGeorge syndrome and severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID)
- Thymoma: benign neoplasm of thymus and associated with myasthenia gravis and SVC syndrome

### Rheumatology & Immunology (ANU) — antiphospholipid syndrome — Lymphoid Organs and Lymphatic Drainage

| Definition | Multisystem vasculopathy manifested by recurrent thromboembolic events, spontaneous abortions and thrombocytopenia due to pro-coagulatory antibodies |
| --- | --- |
| Aetiology | Primary: absence of other diseaseSecondary: SLE, RA, neoplasms, HIV, Hep A/B/C, bacterial infections (e.g. syphilis, Lyme disease, Tb)Catastrophic: development within 1 week of small vessel thrombotic occlusion in ≥3 organ systems with positive antiphospholipid antibody |
| Pathophysiology | Pro-coagulatory antiphospholipid antibodies which form complexes with anticoagulant proteins (protein C/S, antithrombin III), thus inactivating them Induction of a hypercoagulable state → ↑ risk of thrombosis and embolism |
