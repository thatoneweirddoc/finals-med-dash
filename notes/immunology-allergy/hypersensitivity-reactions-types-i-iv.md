## Hypersensitivity Reactions (Types I–IV)


### Immunology & Allergy Master Notes — Hypersensitivity Reactions (Types I–IV)

**Mnemonic — "ACID":** **A**llergic/Anaphylactic (I), **C**ytotoxic (II), **I**mmune complex (III), **D**elayed (IV).

| Type | Mechanism | Timing | Classic examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I — Immediate** | IgE-mediated mast cell/basophil degranulation → histamine, prostaglandins, leukotrienes | **Minutes** | Anaphylaxis, allergic rhinitis, atopic asthma, food/drug allergy, urticaria |
| **II — Antibody-mediated (cytotoxic)** | IgG/IgM bind antigen **on a cell surface** → complement activation, lysis or phagocytosis | Hours–days | Acute haemolytic transfusion reaction (ABO), Goodpasture's/anti-GBM, ITP, autoimmune haemolytic anaemia, Graves' disease and myasthenia gravis (receptor-stimulating/blocking variants), pernicious anaemia, rheumatic fever |
| **III — Immune complex** | Antibody binds **soluble circulating antigen** → complexes deposit in tissue → complement-driven inflammation | Days–weeks | SLE, post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis, serum sickness, Arthus reaction, polyarteritis nodosa (HBV) |
| **IV — Delayed, T-cell-mediated** | Pre-sensitised T cells → macrophage activation; **no antibody involved** | **48–72 hours** | Contact dermatitis (nickel), Mantoux/tuberculin test, graft-versus-host disease, type 1 diabetes, granulomatous disease |

**The discriminators that separate them:**

- **Type I vs Type IV** — both can present as a "rash after exposure". Type I is **minutes** and IgE-mediated; Type IV is **48–72 hours** and T-cell-mediated. Contact dermatitis appearing two days after a new watch strap is Type IV.
- **Type II vs Type III** — the question is **where the antigen is**. Type II: antigen is **fixed on a cell/tissue surface**. Type III: antigen is **soluble in the circulation** and the complex deposits later.
- ⚑ Some classifications place Graves' disease in a "**Type V**" (receptor-stimulating) category; most UK/Australian teaching keeps it within Type II — know both framings exist.

**Testing:** skin prick testing and specific IgE for Type I; patch testing for Type IV contact dermatitis. **Discriminator: prick = immediate/IgE; patch = delayed/T-cell.**
