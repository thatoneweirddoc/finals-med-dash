## Innate versus Adaptive Immunity


### Immunology & Allergy Master Notes — Innate versus Adaptive Immunity

| | Innate | Adaptive |
|---|---|---|
| **Speed** | Immediate (minutes–hours) | Days (needs priming) |
| **Specificity** | Pattern recognition (PAMPs via toll-like receptors) | Antigen-specific receptors |
| **Memory** | None | **Yes — the basis of vaccination** |
| **Components** | Neutrophils, macrophages, NK cells, complement, physical barriers, acute-phase proteins | T cells (cellular), B cells/antibody (humoral) |

**Acute-phase response.** The liver releases **positive** acute-phase reactants — CRP, ferritin, fibrinogen, haptoglobin, α1-antitrypsin, procalcitonin — driven mainly by **IL-6**. **Negative** acute-phase reactants fall: albumin, transferrin, antithrombin. **Discriminator:** a raised ferritin in an inflamed patient is an **acute-phase artefact, not evidence of iron overload** — check transferrin saturation.

**Complement — the three pathways converging on C3:**

- **Classical** — IgM or IgG immune complexes activate via C1.
- **Alternative** — direct activation by pathogen surfaces via C3 (no antibody needed).
- **Lectin** — mannose-binding lectin binds carbohydrate on pathogens.

**Complement functions:** opsonisation (**C3b**), membrane attack complex/cell lysis (**C5–C9**), and anaphylatoxins (**C3a, C4a, C5a**) which activate mast cells and recruit granulocytes.

*(Complement consumption as an SLE activity marker — low C3/C4 in flare — is in the MSK build Section 1.4.)*

**Cytokines worth knowing by name:** IFN-α (↑MHC I expression), **IFN-γ** (activates macrophages — the granuloma cytokine), **TNF-α** (pyrogenic, mediates septic shock), **IL-1** (fever), **IL-6** (acute-phase response). **Arachidonic acid derivatives:** prostaglandins (pain sensitisation, vasodilation) and leukotrienes (chemotaxis, exudation, bronchoconstriction — hence leukotriene antagonists in asthma).
