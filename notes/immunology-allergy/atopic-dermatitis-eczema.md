## Atopic Dermatitis (Eczema)


### Immunology & Allergy Master Notes — Atopic Dermatitis (Eczema)

Chronic, intensely pruritic, relapsing inflammatory skin disease, usually beginning in early childhood (onset typically **3–6 months**; affects ~10–15% of children, many improve with age).

**Pathogenesis:** skin barrier dysfunction (⚑ **filaggrin** loss-of-function mutations are a major genetic contributor) plus a **Th2-skewed** immune response with raised IgE.

**Distribution changes with age — the classic exam discriminator:**

| Age | Distribution |
|---|---|
| **Infantile (<2 years)** | Cheeks, face, scalp, **extensor** surfaces (nappy area typically spared) |
| **Childhood (2–12 years)** | **Flexures** — antecubital and popliteal fossae, wrists, ankles, neck |
| **Adult** | Flexures, hands, face/eyelids; lichenification from chronic scratching |

**Associated signs:** Dennie–Morgan infraorbital fold, xerosis, lichenification, post-inflammatory pigment change.

**Complications:** bacterial superinfection (*S. aureus* — weeping, golden crust) and ⚑ **eczema herpeticum** (HSV superinfection — rapidly worsening painful clustered vesicles/punched-out erosions, systemic upset; a dermatological emergency needing **systemic aciclovir**).

**Management ladder:** identify and avoid triggers → **liberal emollients** (the mainstay, used even when clear) → topical corticosteroid of potency matched to site and severity → topical calcineurin inhibitors (tacrolimus/pimecrolimus) for face/flexures → phototherapy → systemic immunosuppression or biologics (**dupilumab — anti-IL-4Rα**) ⚑ in severe refractory disease.

**Discriminator:** atopic dermatitis = **flexural and itchy**; psoriasis = **extensor, well-demarcated, silvery scale**, onset later; seborrhoeic dermatitis = greasy scale in sebaceous areas (scalp, nasolabial folds).

### Rheumatology & Immunology (ANU) — PLASMA PROTEINS ASSOCIATED WITH INFLAMMATORY REACTIONS (play a role in innate immunity) — Atopic Dermatitis (Eczema)

- ACUTE PHASE PROTEINS: liver releases 30 acute-phase reactants, including CRP, ferritin, fibrinogen, α1-antitrypsin, haptoglobin, procalcitonin, IL-6
- NOTABLE NEGATIVE ACUTE PHASE REACTANTS: albumin, transferrin, anti-thrombin
- COMPLEMENT SYSTEM: after stimulation from Ab-complexes by pathogens → compliment activation →enhance functions of antibodies and phagocytesClassical pathway: IgM or IgG binds to pathogen (via C1)
- Alternative pathway: direct activation by pathogen rather than Ab-Ag complexes (via C3)
- Causes: opsonisation (C3), bacteria lysis (C5-9) and activation of mast cells/granulocytes (C3/C4/C5)

- PRO-INFLAMMATORY CYTOKINESIFN-α: ↑MHC I expression
- IFN-β: stimulates NK cells and cytotoxic
- IFN-γ: activated macrophages
- TNF-α: pyrogenic, cytotoxic, mediates septic shock
- Interleukins: IL-1, 2, 4, 6
- Arachidonic acid derivatives: PGE2/PGI2 increases pain sensation, vasodilation, and inhibits platelet aggregation and  leukotrienes which cause chemotaxis, exudation and bronchoconstriction

Autoimmunity

DEFINITION: Immune reaction against the body’s own cells, due to a loss of immunological tolerance

PRESUMED PATHOGENESIS

- Autoreactive B lymphocytes are eliminated in the bone marrow, spleen, or lymph nodes (can be detected)
- T lymphocytes that attack the body's own cells are amended in the thymus or destroyed in peripheral lymphoid tissues (e.g., lymph nodes, adenoids, Peyer's patches) due to a lack of stimulation (usually cannot detect specific antibodies)
- If the selection mechanisms fail, this results in the immune cells attacking the body's own cells

CAUSES

- Previous infection e.g. GBS, RF through molecular mimicry
- Genetic predisposition

### Rheumatology & Immunology (ANU) — systemic-onset juvenile idiopathic arthritis (still’s disease) — Atopic Dermatitis (Eczema)

- Commonly affects children between the ages of 2–4 years
- Arthritis involving ≥ 1 joint
- Intermittent fever that lasts for at least 2 weeks with fever spikes occurring on at least 3 consecutive days
- ≥ 1 extra-articular manifestation (e.g., salmon-pink rash, lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly/hepatomegaly, serositis)
