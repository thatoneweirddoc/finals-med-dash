## Atopic Dermatitis (Eczema)


### Immunology & Allergy Master Notes — Atopic Dermatitis (Eczema)

Chronic, intensely pruritic, relapsing inflammatory skin disease, usually beginning in early childhood (onset typically **3–6 months**; affects ~10–15% of children, many improve with age).

**Pathogenesis:** skin barrier dysfunction (⚑ **filaggrin** loss-of-function mutations are a major genetic contributor) plus a **Th2-skewed** immune response with raised IgE.

**Distribution changes with age — the classic exam discriminator:**

| Age | Distribution |
|---|---|
| **Infantile (<2 years)** | Cheeks, face, scalp, **extensor** surfaces (nappy area typically spared) |
| **Childhood (2–12 years)** | **Flexures** — antecubital and popliteal fossae, wrists, ankles, neck |
| **Adult** | Flexures, hands, face/eyelids; lichenification from chronic scratching |

**Associated signs:** Dennie–Morgan infraorbital fold, xerosis, lichenification, post-inflammatory pigment change.

**Complications:** bacterial superinfection (*S. aureus* — weeping, golden crust) and ⚑ **eczema herpeticum** (HSV superinfection — rapidly worsening painful clustered vesicles/punched-out erosions, systemic upset; a dermatological emergency needing **systemic aciclovir**).

**Management ladder:** identify and avoid triggers → **liberal emollients** (the mainstay, used even when clear) → topical corticosteroid of potency matched to site and severity → topical calcineurin inhibitors (tacrolimus/pimecrolimus) for face/flexures → phototherapy → systemic immunosuppression or biologics (**dupilumab — anti-IL-4Rα**) ⚑ in severe refractory disease.

**Discriminator:** atopic dermatitis = **flexural and itchy**; psoriasis = **extensor, well-demarcated, silvery scale**, onset later; seborrhoeic dermatitis = greasy scale in sebaceous areas (scalp, nasolabial folds).
