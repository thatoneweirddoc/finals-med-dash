## Haemostasis, Thrombosis & Transfusion


### Haematology & Oncology Notes — Haemostasis, Thrombosis & Transfusion

*Sources: Hoffbrand's Essential Haematology; BSH guidelines (ITP, TTP, VTE, DIC, massive haemorrhage, transfusion); NICE NG158 (VTE), NG89; AABB 2023/2025 red-cell transfusion guidelines; SHOT (Serious Hazards of Transfusion) reports; StatPearls. Scope: the bleeding and clotting disorders, anticoagulation, and the transfusion medicine a finalist must manage acutely. TTP and the ABO transfusion reaction are emergencies — flagged throughout.*

---

#### 5.1 Approach to bleeding

**The short version**

- **Mucocutaneous + immediate** bleeding = platelet-type — thrombocytopenia, platelet dysfunction, VWD.
- **Deep + delayed** bleeding (haemarthrosis, muscle, retroperitoneal) = coagulation-type — haemophilia, DIC, warfarin, liver disease.
- Phenotype narrows the list; the **coagulation screen** (PT/APTT/fibrinogen/platelets) localises the defect.

Distinguish two bleeding phenotypes at the bedside — it points straight at the mechanism:

| Feature | **Platelet-type** (primary haemostasis) | **Coagulation-type** (secondary haemostasis) |
|---|---|---|
| Bleeding site | **Mucocutaneous** — petechiae, purpura, epistaxis, menorrhagia, gums | **Deep** — haemarthroses, muscle haematoma, retroperitoneal |
| Timing | Immediate after injury | **Delayed** (plug forms then fails) |
| Causes | Thrombocytopenia, platelet dysfunction, VWD | Haemophilia, DIC, warfarin, liver disease |

Then read the **coagulation screen** (PT/APTT/fibrinogen/platelets — see Section 3.4) to localise the defect.

#### 5.2 Thrombocytopenia & the microangiopathies

Low platelets arise from **reduced production** (marrow failure/infiltration, drugs, alcohol, B12/folate), **increased destruction** (immune/consumption), or **sequestration** (hypersplenism).

**Immune thrombocytopenia (ITP)** — isolated thrombocytopenia, autoantibody-mediated. Children: post-viral, acute, self-limiting. Adults: chronic; secondary to SLE, CLL, HIV, *H. pylori*. Diagnosis of exclusion (normal film otherwise, no other cause). **Treat only if bleeding or platelets very low** — first-line **corticosteroids ± IVIG**; second-line TPO-receptor agonists (eltrombopag/romiplostim), rituximab, splenectomy.

**Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP)** ⚑ — **a haematological emergency.** Deficiency of **ADAMTS13** (the VWF-cleaving protease; autoantibody in acquired form) → ultra-large VWF multimers → platelet-rich microthrombi → **microangiopathic haemolysis**. The **pentad** (rarely all present): **MAHA (schistocytes) + thrombocytopenia + neurological signs + renal impairment + fever.** **Coagulation screen is normal** (unlike DIC). **Do NOT give platelet transfusions** (fuels thrombosis) unless life-threatening haemorrhage. Treatment is **urgent plasma exchange** + steroids (± caplacizumab, rituximab).

**Haemolytic uraemic syndrome (HUS)** — MAHA + thrombocytopenia + **acute kidney injury**, predominantly in **children after Shiga-toxin *E. coli* O157 (bloody diarrhoea)**; renal-dominant, neurology usually milder. Mainly supportive; **avoid antibiotics** in typical STEC-HUS (may increase toxin release). Atypical HUS (complement dysregulation) → eculizumab.

**Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT)** — IgG antibodies to **platelet factor 4–heparin** complexes, 5–10 days after starting heparin (sooner if re-exposed); a **fall in platelets >50% + paradoxical thrombosis** (not bleeding). Use the **4Ts score**. Management: **stop all heparin, start a non-heparin anticoagulant** (argatroban, danaparoid, fondaparinux); **do not give platelets**; warfarin only once platelets recover.

**Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC)** — systemic activation of coagulation (sepsis, malignancy — esp. **APL**, obstetric, trauma) consuming clotting factors and platelets → simultaneous **bleeding and thrombosis**. Labs: **↓platelets, ↑PT + ↑APTT, ↓fibrinogen, ↑↑D-dimer, schistocytes.** Treat the **cause**; supportive component replacement (platelets, FFP, cryoprecipitate) only if bleeding.

#### 5.3 Inherited bleeding disorders

**Haemophilia** — **X-linked recessive**, so affects males; deep bleeding (haemarthroses, muscle bleeds). **A** = factor **VIII** deficiency (commoner); **B** ("Christmas disease") = factor **IX**. **Isolated prolonged APTT, normal PT/platelets/VWF.** Severity tracks factor level (<1% severe). Treat with **factor concentrate/recombinant factor** (or emicizumab prophylaxis for haemophilia A); desmopressin for mild A. Avoid IM injections/NSAIDs.

**Von Willebrand disease (VWD)** — **the commonest inherited bleeding disorder**; autosomal (usually dominant). VWF does two jobs — platelet adhesion **and** carrying factor VIII — so it causes **mucocutaneous (platelet-type) bleeding** and can prolong the APTT (low factor VIII). Type 1 (partial quantitative, commonest), 2 (qualitative), 3 (complete). Treat with **desmopressin (DDAVP)** ± VWF-containing concentrate; tranexamic acid for mucosal bleeds.

#### 5.4 Thrombophilia & VTE

**The short version**

- Inherited: **factor V Leiden** commonest (APC resistance); then prothrombin G20210A and protein C / protein S / antithrombin deficiency.
- Acquired: **antiphospholipid syndrome**, malignancy, pregnancy/oestrogens, nephrotic syndrome.
- APS is the one to spot — arterial *and* venous thrombosis + recurrent miscarriage, **prolonged APTT in vitro but thrombotic in vivo**, warfarin not DOAC ⚑.

**Virchow's triad** underlies venous thromboembolism (**DVT/PE**). Risk factors: surgery/immobility, malignancy, pregnancy/oestrogen, previous VTE, and **inherited/acquired thrombophilias**:

| Inherited | Acquired |
|---|---|
| **Factor V Leiden** (commonest; APC resistance) | **Antiphospholipid syndrome** |
| Prothrombin G20210A | Malignancy |
| Protein C / protein S / antithrombin deficiency | Pregnancy, oestrogens, nephrotic syndrome |

**Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS)** — arterial **and** venous thrombosis, **recurrent miscarriage**, thrombocytopenia; antibodies (lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin, anti-β2-glycoprotein-I). Paradox: **prolonged APTT in vitro but thrombotic in vivo.** Long-term **warfarin** (DOACs are inferior in triple-positive/arterial APS ⚑); aspirin + LMWH in pregnancy.

**Diagnosis of VTE:** risk-stratify (Wells score) → **D-dimer** to exclude if low probability → **Doppler USS** (DVT) or **CTPA** (PE; V/Q if contrast contraindicated). **Provoked** (transient risk factor) vs **unprovoked** VTE guides duration. **Cancer-associated thrombosis** — historically LMWH; **⚑ DOACs (edoxaban, apixaban, rivaroxaban) are now first-line for most, with caution in GI/GU tumours** (bleeding).

#### 5.5 Anticoagulation & reversal

**The short version**

- Only two drugs are routinely monitored: **UFH → APTT**, **warfarin → INR**. LMWH and DOACs need none routinely.
- Reversal is the high-yield column ⚑: **protamine** (heparin), **vitamin K + PCC** (warfarin), **idarucizumab** (dabigatran), **andexanet alfa or PCC** (apixaban/rivaroxaban/edoxaban).
- Know the target: warfarin hits **II, VII, IX, X**; dabigatran hits **IIa**; the "-xabans" hit **Xa**.

| Drug | Target | Monitoring | Reversal ⚑ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unfractionated heparin** | Antithrombin → IIa & Xa | **APTT** (or anti-Xa) | **Protamine sulfate** |
| **LMWH** (enoxaparin) | Antithrombin → mainly Xa | Usually none (anti-Xa if renal/pregnancy/extremes of weight) | Protamine (partial) |
| **Warfarin** | Vitamin-K-dependent factors II, VII, IX, X | **INR** | **Vitamin K**; **PCC** (Prothrombin Complex Concentrate) for major bleeding; FFP if PCC unavailable |
| **Dabigatran** | Direct thrombin (IIa) | None routine | **Idarucizumab** |
| **Apixaban/rivaroxaban/edoxaban** | Direct factor **Xa** | None routine | **Andexanet alfa** (or PCC) |

Key safety points: **warfarin is teratogenic** (use LMWH in pregnancy); it has an **initial procoagulant effect** (protein C falls first) → bridge with heparin/LMWH when starting for acute thrombosis; countless interactions (CYP450). **DOACs** are renally cleared (avoid/reduce in severe renal impairment) and are **contraindicated in antiphospholipid syndrome (triple-positive) and mechanical heart valves** ⚑ (warfarin only there).

#### Section 6 — Transfusion Medicine

#### 6.1 Blood groups & compatibility

**ABO** is the critical system — antibodies are **naturally occurring IgM** and cause immediate intravascular haemolysis if mismatched. **Group O = universal red-cell donor; group AB = universal recipient. Group AB plasma is the universal plasma (FFP) donor** (opposite of red cells). **RhD**: RhD-negative patients can form anti-D after exposure → matters in transfusion and in **pregnancy (haemolytic disease of the newborn; give anti-D prophylaxis).**

**Pre-transfusion testing:** **Group & save** (ABO/RhD + antibody screen, no unit assigned) for possible need; **crossmatch** (units physically/electronically matched) when transfusion is likely. Emergency: **O-negative** (or group-specific) blood before crossmatch completes.

#### 6.2 Blood components & thresholds

**The short version**

- Match component to defect: **red cells** for symptomatic anaemia/blood loss, **platelets** for thrombocytopenic bleeding or prophylaxis (<10 ×10⁹/L, pre-procedure).
- **FFP** = all clotting factors (bleeding + ↑PT/APTT, DIC); **cryoprecipitate** = fibrinogen, VWF, VIII, XIII — the one for **fibrinogen <1.5 g/L** (<2 in obstetric haemorrhage).
- Default is **restrictive**: transfuse at **Hb <70 g/L** (aim 70–90), **<80 g/L** if chronic cardiovascular disease or post-cardiac surgery; one unit at a time, reassess.
- ⚑ Exception: **acute coronary syndrome/MI — 2025 AABB suggests a liberal target ~80–100 g/L**.

| Component | Contains | Main indication |
|---|---|---|
| **Red cells** | RBCs | Symptomatic anaemia / significant blood loss |
| **Platelets** | Platelets | Thrombocytopenic bleeding or prophylaxis (e.g. <10 ×10⁹/L, or pre-procedure) |
| **Fresh frozen plasma (FFP)** | All clotting factors | Bleeding + coagulopathy (↑PT/APTT), DIC, warfarin reversal only if PCC unavailable |
| **Cryoprecipitate** | Fibrinogen, VWF, VIII, XIII | **Low fibrinogen** (<1.5 g/L, or <2 in obstetric haemorrhage), DIC |

**Restrictive transfusion is the default.** For **most haemodynamically stable inpatients, transfuse at Hb <70 g/L**, aiming for **70–90 g/L**; use **<80 g/L** for patients with **chronic cardiovascular disease** or post-cardiac surgery. **⚑ Exception — acute coronary syndrome/acute MI:** the **2025 AABB guideline now suggests a more liberal strategy (transfuse to keep Hb around 80–100 g/L)**, a shift from the older uniform restrictive threshold, based on the MINT trial and meta-analyses; this remains an area where sources differ, so state both the general restrictive rule and the acute-MI exception. Transfuse **one unit at a time and reassess** in non-bleeding patients.

#### 6.3 Transfusion reactions

**The short version**

- **Stop-immediately** list: acute haemolytic (ABO), anaphylaxis, TRALI, bacterial contamination. Febrile non-haemolytic and urticarial reactions can usually be slowed and continued.
- Fever + **loin/back pain** + hypotension + dark urine **during** the transfusion = **ABO incompatibility** ⚑ — usually a clerical error; resuscitate and check identity.
- **TACO vs TRALI** is the classic pair: TACO is **hypertensive**, raised JVP → **diurese**; TRALI is **hypotensive**, bilateral infiltrates <6 h → **support, do not diurese**.
- Anaphylaxis to a transfusion → think **IgA deficiency** with anti-IgA.

**Acute (within 24 h):**

| Reaction | Mechanism | Recognition | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acute haemolytic (ABO incompatibility)** ⚑ | IgM against wrong ABO group — usually a **clerical error** | Fever, loin/back pain, hypotension, dark urine, DIC — **during** transfusion | **STOP immediately**, resuscitate (fluids), check identity, inform lab; can be fatal |
| **Febrile non-haemolytic** | Anti-leucocyte antibodies/cytokines | Isolated fever/rigors | Slow/stop, antipyretic; usually continue cautiously |
| **Allergic / urticarial** | Antibodies to donor plasma proteins | Itch, urticaria | Antihistamine, slow rate |
| **Anaphylaxis** | Often **IgA deficiency** with anti-IgA | Hypotension, bronchospasm, angioedema | STOP, adrenaline, resuscitate |
| **TACO** (circulatory overload) | Volume overload | **Hypertension**, dyspnoea, raised JVP, pulmonary oedema | Slow/stop, **diuretics, O₂**, sit up |
| **TRALI** | Donor anti-leucocyte antibodies → pulmonary capillary leak | **Hypotension**, dyspnoea, bilateral infiltrates within 6 h | STOP, respiratory support; report |
| **Bacterial contamination** | Contaminated unit (esp. platelets, stored at room temp) | High fever, rigors, shock | STOP, cultures, broad-spectrum antibiotics |

**TACO vs TRALI** — both cause acute dyspnoea/pulmonary oedema during/after transfusion; **TACO is hypertensive and fluid-overloaded (responds to diuretics); TRALI is hypotensive with normal filling pressures (do not diurese, support ventilation).**

**Delayed:** delayed haemolytic reaction (days later, falling Hb, positive DAT), transfusion-associated GvHD (irradiate products for at-risk patients), iron overload (chronic transfusion), post-transfusion purpura.

#### 6.4 Massive haemorrhage

**Major haemorrhage protocol** — activate for loss of one blood volume in ~24 h or rapid ongoing loss. Give **red cells, FFP and platelets in a balanced ratio** (empirically ~1:1:1 until guided by results), replace **fibrinogen (cryoprecipitate) if <1.5 g/L**, give **tranexamic acid** (early, especially in trauma — CRASH-2), keep the patient warm, and correct calcium (citrate chelation), acidosis and coagulopathy (the "lethal triad": hypothermia, acidosis, coagulopathy). Reverse anticoagulants (PCC for warfarin, specific agents for DOACs). Obstetric and trauma haemorrhage have dedicated pathways.

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators

**The short version**

- Three emergencies hide in this table: **TTP** (plasma exchange, **no platelets** ⚑), **ABO haemolytic reaction** (stop, resuscitate) and **HIT** (stop heparin, non-heparin anticoagulant).
- Two pairs are where marks are lost: **TTP vs DIC** (clotting normal vs deranged) and **TACO vs TRALI** (hypertensive/diurese vs hypotensive/don't).
- Reversal agents and transfusion thresholds are the commonest single-fact asks — learn them as a block.

Split into two passes: bleeding and thrombosis first, then transfusion and reversal.

##### Bleeding & thrombosis

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Mucocutaneous bleeding + immediate | Platelet-type (thrombocytopenia/VWD) |
| Deep/delayed bleeding (haemarthrosis) | Coagulation-type (haemophilia) |
| MAHA + thrombocytopenia + neuro + renal + fever, **normal clotting** | TTP (ADAMTS13) — plasma exchange, **no platelets** ⚑ |
| MAHA + AKI in a child after bloody diarrhoea | HUS (STEC O157) |
| Platelets ↓>50% + thrombosis 5–10 days into heparin | HIT — stop heparin, non-heparin anticoagulant |
| ↓platelets + ↑PT/APTT + ↓fibrinogen + ↑↑D-dimer | DIC |
| Commonest inherited bleeding disorder | Von Willebrand disease |
| Isolated ↑APTT, X-linked, haemarthrosis | Haemophilia (A = VIII, B = IX) |
| Thrombosis + miscarriage + ↑APTT that won't correct | Antiphospholipid syndrome — warfarin (not DOAC) ⚑ |

##### Transfusion & reversal

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Universal red cell donor / universal plasma donor | O red cells / AB plasma |
| Default restrictive transfusion threshold | Hb <70 (80 if cardiac disease) |
| Acute-MI transfusion target ⚑ | More liberal (~80–100 g/L) per 2025 AABB |
| Fever + loin pain + shock during transfusion | Acute haemolytic (ABO) — STOP, resuscitate |
| Hypertensive pulmonary oedema post-transfusion | TACO — diurese |
| Hypotensive bilateral infiltrates <6 h | TRALI — support, don't diurese |
| Anaphylaxis to transfusion | Consider IgA deficiency |
| Warfarin major-bleed reversal | Vitamin K + PCC |
| Dabigatran / factor Xa DOAC reversal | Idarucizumab / andexanet alfa |
| Antidote to heparin | Protamine sulfate |

### Haematology (ANU) — DISORDERS OF PRIMARY HAEMOSTASIS — Haemostasis, Thrombosis & Transfusion

**The short version**

- Failure of the platelet plug — vessel wall, platelet number/function or VWF — so bleeding time rises and bleeding is mucocutaneous and immediate.
- **ITP** = diagnosis of exclusion, isolated thrombocytopenia (<150,000/mm3), IgG vs GpIIb/IIIa; steroids, IVIG, splenectomy if refractory.
- **HIT** = antibody to heparin–PF4 → thrombocytopenia **with thrombosis**; stop heparin, argatroban, then transition to warfarin.
- **HUS** (children, Shiga toxin, bloody diarrhoea, renal failure) vs **TTP** (adults, ADAMTS-13, ≥3/5 of the pentad, normal coags); **Bernard–Soulier** = ↓GpIb, large platelets.

### Haematology (ANU) — DISORDERS OF SECONDARY HAEMOSTASIS — Haemostasis, Thrombosis & Transfusion

**The short version**

- Failure to form an adequate **fibrin clot** → delayed bleeding, deep muscular bleeds, spontaneous haemarthroses.
- **PT** = extrinsic + common (I, II, V, VII, X); **PTT** = intrinsic + common (all but VII and XIII). **Mixing study** corrects in factor deficiency, does **not** correct with an inhibitor.
- **Haemophilia** A (VIII, XR, 80%) / B (IX, XR, 20%) / C (XI, AR, Ashkenazi) — ↑aPTT with **normal PT and platelets**.
- Acquired causes prolong **both** PT and aPTT: vitamin K deficiency and liver disease — liver disease spares **factor VIII** (also made in endothelium).

### Haematology (ANU) — MIXED PLATELET AND COAGULATION DISORDERS — Haemostasis, Thrombosis & Transfusion

**The short version**

- **VWD hits both arms** — VWF does platelet adhesion *and* chaperones factor VIII: ↑BT, normal platelets and PT, normal or ↑PTT, **↓ristocetin**.
- VWD types: 1 quantitative (80%, AD), 2 qualitative (15–20%, AD), 3 absent (~1%); acquired forms with myeloma/lymphoma. Treat with recombinant vWF, desmopressin, factor VIII, TXA; **avoid aspirin, NSAIDs, IM injections**.
- **DIC** = dysregulated plasmin and thrombin consuming platelets, factors and fibrinogen — schistocytes, ↑BT, ↑PT, ↑PTT, **↓fibrinogen, ↑D-dimer**.
- DIC management is **supportive + treat the cause**: components (platelets, plasma, cryoprecipitate) if haemorrhaging, UFH/LMWH only in the thrombotic phase in critically ill non-bleeding patients.

| VON WILLEBRAND DISEASE | DEFINITION: intrinsic pathway coagulation defect characterised by either a deficiency or disorder of vWF<br>• PATHOPHYSIOLOGY: vWF needed for platelet adhesion/aggregation and acts as chaperone for Factor VIII, therefore abnormality of VWF can affect both primary and secondary haemostasis<br>• CLASSIFICATION: Type 1 (quantitative defect; 80% of cases) → autosomal dominantType 2 (qualitative defect in activity; 15-20%) → autosomal dominantType 3 (no vWF; very rare ~1%) → complete absenceAcquired: typically associated with malignancies e.g. multiple myeloma, lymphoma, autoimmune<br>• PRESENTATION: mostly asymptomatic, ecchymosis, petechial haemorrhages, epistaxis, gingival bleeding, menorrhagia, GI bleeding, ↑bleeding during procedures<br>• LABS: normal platelets and PT, ↑BT, normal or ↑ PTT, vWF antigen (↓ factor levels), normal or ↓ factor VIII, ↓ristocetin (cofactor for vWF-platelet binding)<br>• TREATMENT: recombinant vWF, desmopressin, factor VIII concentrates, OCP in menorrhagia, anti-fibrinolytics (e.g. tranexamic acid, aminocaproic acid)<br>• AVOID: aspirin, NSAIDs, IM injections |

| DISSEMINATED INTRAVASCULAR COAGULATION | DEFINITION: Excessive, dysregulated release of plasmin and thrombin leading to intravascular coagulation and depletion of platelets, coagulation factors and fibrinogen<br>• AETIOLOGY (mnemonic STOP MAKING NEW THROMBI): Sepsis (gram ⊝)TraumaObstetric complications (amniotic fluid embolism, pre-eclampsia, placental obstruction, RPOC)acute Pancreatitis Malignancy (acute promyelocytic leukemia, pancreas, ovarian, gastric)Nephrotic syndromeTransfusion (intravascular haemolysis)<br>• LABS: schistocytes, thrombocytopenia, ↑BT, ↑PT, ↑PTT, decreased fibrinogen levels, and increased D-dimer<br>• PATHOPHYSIOLOGYHyperfibrinolysis → bleeding e.g. in leukemia, obstetric complications, aortic aneurysms, acute traumatic coagulopathyOrgan failure: ↑cytokine → hypercoagulation →consumption of platelets and coagulation factors e.g. in sepsisConsumptive: hyperfibrinolysis and hypercoagulation e.g. post-operative, obstetrics complications, tumours<br>• PRESENTATION ![](notes/images/haematology-anu/img027.jpg) <br>• TREATMENT<br>• Supportive: aggressive hydration, haemodynamic/ventilator support, RBC transfusion if severeIn haemorrhage: platelet transfusion, frozen plasma, cryoprecipitateIn thrombotic phase: UFH or LMWH in critically ill, non-bleeding patients |
