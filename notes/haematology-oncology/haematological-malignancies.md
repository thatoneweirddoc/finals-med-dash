## Haematological Malignancies


### Haematology & Oncology Notes — Haematological Malignancies

*Sources: Hoffbrand's Essential Haematology; WHO classification of haematolymphoid tumours (5th ed, 2022) & ICC; BSH guidelines; IMWG criteria (myeloma); Robbins; StatPearls; NICE. Scope: the leukaemias, lymphomas, plasma-cell disorders and myeloproliferative/myelodysplastic neoplasms — recognition, key cytogenetics, staging, and outline management. Cytogenetic translocations and film clues are the highest-yield content; drilled in the recognition summary.*

---

#### 7.1 Approach & classification

Two axes classify blood cancers: **acute vs chronic** (acute = immature **blasts**, rapid, marrow failure; chronic = more mature cells, indolent) and **myeloid vs lymphoid** (lineage). The four-box grid — **AML, ALL, CML, CLL** — plus lymphomas, myeloma and the MPN/MDS group covers finals.

**The acute-leukaemia presentation** = **marrow failure**: anaemia (fatigue/pallor), neutropenia (infection), thrombocytopenia (bleeding/bruising), ± infiltration (bone pain, hepatosplenomegaly, lymphadenopathy, gum hypertrophy in monocytic AML, CNS/testicular in ALL). **Blasts on the film** + a **hypercellular marrow packed with blasts (≥20%)** clinch acute leukaemia.

#### 7.2 Acute leukaemias

**Acute myeloid leukaemia (AML)** — mainly **older adults**. Myeloid blasts; **Auer rods are pathognomonic**; myeloperoxidase-positive. Associated with prior MDS, chemo/radiotherapy, Down syndrome. Diagnosis by marrow (≥20% blasts), flow cytometry, cytogenetics/molecular (prognostic: NPM1, FLT3, core-binding-factor translocations).

- **⚑ Acute promyelocytic leukaemia (APL, AML-M3)** is the can't-miss subtype: **t(15;17) PML–RARA**, presents with **DIC and life-threatening bleeding**. Treatment is a haematological emergency: **all-trans retinoic acid (ATRA) + arsenic trioxide** (differentiation therapy), started on clinical suspicion before genetics confirm, plus aggressive DIC support. APL is now highly curable — recognising it early saves lives.

**Acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (ALL)** — the **commonest childhood cancer** (peak 2–5 years). Lymphoid blasts (usually B-lineage, TdT-positive). Features: marrow failure + **CNS and testicular involvement** (sanctuary sites needing dedicated prophylaxis), bone pain, lymphadenopathy. High **tumour-lysis** risk on starting treatment. **Philadelphia-positive ALL** [t(9;22)] carries worse prognosis and gets a **TKI** added. Long, phased chemotherapy (induction → consolidation → CNS prophylaxis → maintenance); good paediatric cure rates.

#### 7.3 Chronic leukaemias

**Chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML)** — a myeloproliferative neoplasm defined by the **Philadelphia chromosome t(9;22) → BCR-ABL1 fusion** (a constitutively active tyrosine kinase). Middle age; often incidental **marked leucocytosis with the whole granulocyte series + basophilia**, massive splenomegaly, low **LAP score** (distinguishes from a leukaemoid reaction, which has high LAP). Three phases: **chronic → accelerated → blast crisis** (transforms to acute leukaemia). **⚑ Treatment revolutionised by TKIs** — first-line **imatinib** (or a second-generation TKI: dasatinib/nilotinib/bosutinib), with molecular monitoring of *BCR-ABL1* transcripts; life expectancy now approaches normal. Ponatinib for the resistant **T315I** mutation.

**Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (CLL)** — commonest leukaemia in **older adults in the West**; clonal mature **B lymphocytes**. Often an incidental **lymphocytosis**; **smear/smudge cells** on film; lymphadenopathy, hepatosplenomegaly. Complications: **autoimmune haemolytic anaemia/ITP**, hypogammaglobulinaemia (infections), and **Richter's transformation** to aggressive lymphoma (rapid nodal enlargement, systemic symptoms). Stage by **Binet/Rai**; many need only watchful waiting. Treatment when progressive: **BTK inhibitors (ibrutinib, acalabrutinib) and BCL-2 inhibitor venetoclax** have largely replaced chemoimmunotherapy ⚑; prognosis stratified by **del(17p)/TP53, IGHV mutation status**.

#### 7.4 Lymphomas

Lymphomas are solid tumours of lymphoid tissue, split into **Hodgkin** and **non-Hodgkin**. Both present with **painless lymphadenopathy ± B symptoms**; diagnosis needs an **excisional node biopsy** (architecture). Staged by **Ann Arbor** (I–IV; suffix **A** = no B symptoms, **B** = B symptoms present) with PET-CT.

**Hodgkin lymphoma (HL)** — **Reed–Sternberg cells** ("owl-eye" binucleate cells, CD15+/CD30+) in an inflammatory background. **Bimodal age** (young adults + elderly), EBV-associated. Clues: **contiguous nodal spread from the neck**, **alcohol-induced nodal pain**, "**B symptoms**", mediastinal mass. Nodular sclerosing is commonest. **Highly curable** with chemotherapy (ABVD) ± radiotherapy; long-term survivorship issues (secondary cancers, cardiac, infertility) drive de-escalation.

**Non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL)** — a heterogeneous group, mostly **B-cell**; often **non-contiguous/extranodal** spread:

| Type | Pace | Key feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Diffuse large B-cell (DLBCL)** | Aggressive | Commonest NHL; rapidly enlarging mass; curable with **R-CHOP** |
| **Follicular** | Indolent | **t(14;18) → BCL-2**; waxing/waning; often incurable but long survival |
| **Burkitt** | Highly aggressive | **t(8;14) → MYC**; "starry-sky" histology; **jaw mass (endemic/EBV)** or abdominal; extreme **tumour-lysis** risk |
| **MALT / marginal zone** | Indolent | Gastric MALT linked to ***H. pylori*** (may regress with eradication) |
| **Mantle cell** | Aggressive | **t(11;14) → cyclin D1** |

#### 7.5 Plasma cell & paraprotein disorders

**Multiple myeloma** — malignant clonal **plasma cells** producing a **monoclonal paraprotein (M-protein)**. Remember the end-organ damage as **CRAB**, with three added **SLiM** biomarkers that now define myeloma even without CRAB:

**⚑ IMWG diagnostic criteria** — clonal bone-marrow plasma cells **≥10%** (or biopsy-proven plasmacytoma) **plus ≥1** of:
- **CRAB:** **C**alcium raised (>2.75 mmol/L or >0.25 above ULN); **R**enal (creatinine >177 µmol/L or creatinine clearance <40 mL/min); **A**naemia (Hb <100 g/L or >20 below normal); **B**one lesions (≥1 lytic lesion on X-ray/CT/PET).
- **SLiM:** **S**ixty percent — clonal marrow plasma cells **≥60%**; **Li**ght chains — involved:uninvolved serum free light-chain ratio **≥100** (involved ≥100 mg/L); **M**RI — **>1 focal lesion ≥5 mm**.

Clinical: bone pain (lytic lesions, **"pepper-pot" skull**, fractures), hypercalcaemia, renal failure (light-chain/cast nephropathy), anaemia, recurrent infection (immunoparesis), hyperviscosity, cord compression. Investigations: **serum/urine electrophoresis + immunofixation, serum free light chains, β2-microglobulin (prognosis), skeletal survey/whole-body MRI or CT, marrow.** Film: **rouleaux**. Treatment (incurable but very treatable): proteasome inhibitors (bortezomib), immunomodulators (lenalidomide), **anti-CD38 (daratumumab)**, steroids, ± autologous stem-cell transplant; bisphosphonates for bone disease. **⚑ Note: modern imaging is whole-body CT/MRI/PET, not the old plain skeletal survey alone; a bone scan is *not* used (lesions are lytic, not osteoblastic).**

**MGUS** (monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance) — paraprotein **<30 g/L**, marrow plasma cells **<10%**, **no CRAB/SLiM**; a common, asymptomatic precursor with ~1%/year progression to myeloma — monitor.

**Smouldering myeloma** — between MGUS and myeloma (paraprotein ≥30 g/L or 10–60% plasma cells) but **no myeloma-defining event**; higher progression rate, observed closely.

**Waldenström macroglobulinaemia** — a lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma making **IgM** → **hyperviscosity** (visual disturbance, mucosal bleeding, neuro) without lytic bone lesions; MYD88 mutation; plasmapheresis for hyperviscosity.

**Amyloidosis (AL)** — light-chain deposition → nephrotic syndrome, restrictive cardiomyopathy, macroglossia, neuropathy; **Congo red apple-green birefringence** on biopsy.

#### 7.6 Myeloproliferative & myelodysplastic neoplasms

**Myeloproliferative neoplasms (MPN)** — clonal overproduction of mature myeloid cells; **JAK2/CALR/MPL** driver mutations ⚑; risk of thrombosis and marrow-failure transformation:

| MPN | Cell overproduced | Hallmark | Key point |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Polycythaemia vera** | Red cells | **JAK2 V617F (>95%)**, low EPO, aquagenic pruritus | Venesect + aspirin ± hydroxycarbamide |
| **Essential thrombocythaemia** | Platelets | Platelets ↑↑ (>450), JAK2/CALR | Thrombosis/bleeding; aspirin ± cytoreduction |
| **Primary myelofibrosis** | Fibrosis of marrow | **Tear-drop cells, leukoerythroblastic film, massive splenomegaly, dry tap** | JAK inhibitor (ruxolitinib); worst prognosis |
| **CML** | Granulocytes | **BCR-ABL1** (see 7.3) | The Philadelphia MPN |

**Myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS)** — clonal marrow **dysplasia with ineffective haematopoiesis** → **cytopenias despite a cellular marrow**, in older adults. Blasts <20% (≥20% = AML). Risk stratify (IPSS-R); risk of transformation to AML. Supportive care/transfusion, hypomethylating agents (azacitidine), transplant in selected fit patients.

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Auer rods | AML |
| **t(15;17) / PML–RARA + DIC** | APL — ATRA emergency ⚑ |
| Commonest childhood cancer, blasts + CNS/testicular | ALL |
| **Philadelphia t(9;22) BCR-ABL1** | CML (also Ph+ ALL) |
| Basophilia + massive spleen + low LAP score | CML |
| High LAP score, very high neutrophils, infection | Leukaemoid reaction (not CML) |
| Smear/smudge cells + lymphocytosis, older adult | CLL |
| CLL + rapid nodal growth + systemic symptoms | Richter's transformation |
| CLL + AAT / ITP | Autoimmune cytopenia of CLL |
| Reed–Sternberg (owl-eye) cells | Hodgkin lymphoma |
| Alcohol-induced nodal pain / contiguous neck spread | Hodgkin lymphoma |
| Commonest NHL, aggressive, R-CHOP | DLBCL |
| **t(14;18) BCL-2** | Follicular lymphoma |
| **t(8;14) MYC**, starry sky, jaw mass | Burkitt lymphoma (huge TLS risk) |
| Gastric lymphoma linked to *H. pylori* | MALT lymphoma |
| **t(11;14) cyclin D1** | Mantle cell lymphoma |
| CRAB + paraprotein + rouleaux + lytic lesions | Multiple myeloma |
| Myeloma SLiM: plasma cells / FLC ratio / MRI ⚑ | ≥60% / ≥100 / >1 focal lesion |
| Paraprotein <30, marrow PC <10%, no CRAB | MGUS |
| IgM paraprotein + hyperviscosity, no bone lesions | Waldenström macroglobulinaemia |
| Congo red apple-green birefringence | AL amyloidosis |
| JAK2 V617F + low EPO + itch after hot bath | Polycythaemia vera ⚑ |
| Tear-drop cells + dry tap + massive spleen | Myelofibrosis |
| Cytopenias with a cellular dysplastic marrow, blasts <20% | Myelodysplastic syndrome |
