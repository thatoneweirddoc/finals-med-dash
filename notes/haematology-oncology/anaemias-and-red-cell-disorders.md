## Anaemias & Red Cell Disorders


### Haematology & Oncology Notes — Anaemias & Red Cell Disorders

*Sources: Hoffbrand's Essential Haematology; BSH guidelines (iron deficiency, sickle cell, AIHA); NICE CKS (anaemia, B12/folate); StatPearls; Robbins. Scope: the MCV/reticulocyte framework and every red-cell disorder a finals student is expected to recognise and manage. Frame throughout is clue → diagnosis.*

---

#### 4.1 Approach to anaemia

Anaemia = Hb below the reference range (roughly **<130 g/L men, <120 g/L non-pregnant women**; WHO thresholds). Two orthogonal frameworks together crack almost any case:

1. **By MCV** — microcytic (<80), normocytic (80–100), macrocytic (>100 fL).
2. **By reticulocytes** — high (haemolysis/blood loss = marrow responding) vs low (production failure).

```
              MICROCYTIC (<80)          NORMOCYTIC (80–100)         MACROCYTIC (>100)
              Iron deficiency           Acute blood loss             Megaloblastic:
              Thalassaemia              Anaemia of chronic disease     B12 / folate deficiency
              Anaemia of chronic dis.   Haemolysis (retics high)     Non-megaloblastic:
              Sideroblastic             Marrow failure/infiltration    alcohol, liver, hypothyroid,
              (Lead poisoning)          CKD (↓EPO)                     myelodysplasia, reticulocytosis
```

#### 4.2 Microcytic anaemias

**Iron deficiency anaemia (IDA)** — the commonest anaemia worldwide. Film: **microcytic, hypochromic, pencil (elliptocyte) cells, anisopoikilocytosis.** Iron studies: **↓ferritin, ↓iron, ↑TIBC, ↓transferrin saturation.** Causes by age/sex: menstrual/pregnancy loss in premenopausal women; **GI blood loss in men and postmenopausal women — colorectal/gastric cancer until proven otherwise** (a 2-week-wait scope). Malabsorption (coeliac) is an under-tested cause — **check coeliac serology** in unexplained IDA. Treat the cause + oral ferrous sulfate (or IV iron if intolerant/malabsorbing); expect a reticulocyte rise within a week and Hb rise of ~20 g/L per month.

**Anaemia of chronic disease (ACD)** — usually normocytic but can be mildly microcytic; driven by **hepcidin** (inflammation traps iron in stores). **↑/normal ferritin, ↓TIBC.** Treat the underlying disease. The IDA-vs-ACD split turns on **TIBC (high in IDA, low/normal in ACD)** and ferritin.

**Thalassaemia trait** — microcytosis **out of proportion to the mild anaemia** (very low MCV with near-normal Hb and normal/raised red cell count); ferritin normal. β-thalassaemia trait → **raised HbA₂** on electrophoresis. Don't iron-load these patients.

**Sideroblastic anaemia** — defective haem synthesis; **ring sideroblasts** in marrow, raised ferritin/iron. Congenital (X-linked) or acquired (alcohol, lead, isoniazid, myelodysplasia). **Lead poisoning** gives **basophilic stippling** + microcytosis.

#### 4.3 Macrocytic anaemias

**Megaloblastic** (impaired DNA synthesis → large cells + **hypersegmented neutrophils**, mild pancytopenia):
- **B12 deficiency** — pernicious anaemia (autoimmune, anti-intrinsic-factor/parietal-cell antibodies, atrophic gastritis), ileal disease/resection, veganism, metformin. **Neurological features are the danger:** peripheral neuropathy, and **subacute combined degeneration of the cord** (dorsal columns + corticospinal tracts → sensory ataxia, spasticity, absent ankle jerks with upgoing plantars). **Replace B12 before/with folate** — giving folate alone can precipitate/worsen neurological damage.
- **Folate deficiency** — poor diet, alcohol, pregnancy, malabsorption, methotrexate/anti-folate drugs, haemolysis (increased demand). No neurology.

**Non-megaloblastic macrocytosis** — **alcohol, liver disease, hypothyroidism, myelodysplasia**, and **reticulocytosis** (young red cells are large — so brisk haemolysis raises MCV without B12/folate deficiency).

#### 4.4 Normocytic & haemolytic anaemias

Normocytic anaemia = acute blood loss, ACD, CKD (↓EPO — treat with EPO analogues once iron-replete), marrow failure, or **haemolysis**. The **haemolysis framework** is two questions:

**(a) Inherited vs acquired?** **(b) Intravascular vs extravascular?**

- **Extravascular** (spleen/liver macrophages destroy cells): **spherocytes**, splenomegaly, ↑unconjugated bilirubin, ↑LDH, ↓haptoglobin (milder). Examples: hereditary spherocytosis, warm AIHA.
- **Intravascular** (lysis in circulation): **haemoglobinuria, haemoglobinaemia, marked ↓haptoglobin, ↑LDH**, urinary haemosiderin. Examples: G6PD crisis, ABO transfusion reaction, PNH, MAHA, mechanical valve.

**Inherited:**
- **Hereditary spherocytosis** — autosomal dominant membrane defect (spectrin/ankyrin); spherocytes, **↑MCHC**, negative DAT, **osmotic fragility / EMA-binding** test; splenomegaly, pigment gallstones; folate ± splenectomy.
- **G6PD deficiency** — X-linked; **episodic** oxidative intravascular haemolysis triggered by **infection, fava beans, drugs** (dapsone, primaquine, sulfonamides, nitrofurantoin); film shows **bite cells + Heinz bodies**; enzyme assay (delay testing until after the acute episode). Manage by removing the trigger.

**Acquired:**
- **Autoimmune haemolytic anaemia (AIHA)** — **DAT-positive**:
  - **Warm (IgG)** — extravascular, spherocytes; idiopathic or secondary (**CLL, lymphoma, SLE, drugs**); first-line **steroids**.
  - **Cold (IgM)** — agglutination in cold peripheries; secondary to **Mycoplasma, EBV**, lymphoma; **avoid cold**, treat underlying cause (steroids less effective; rituximab).
- **Paroxysmal nocturnal haemoglobinuria (PNH)** — acquired PIGA mutation → loss of GPI-anchored complement regulators → **complement-mediated intravascular haemolysis, thrombosis (esp. intra-abdominal), pancytopenia**; diagnose by **flow cytometry (CD55/CD59 loss)**; treat with **eculizumab** (complement C5 inhibitor).
- **Microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia (MAHA)** — **schistocytes** from mechanical shearing across fibrin/platelet thrombi: **DIC, TTP, HUS**, malignant hypertension, mechanical valve, HELLP (covered in Section 5).

#### 4.5 Haemoglobinopathies

**Sickle cell disease (HbSS)** — autosomal recessive **β-globin point mutation (Glu→Val)**; HbS polymerises when deoxygenated → sickling, haemolysis, vaso-occlusion. Screening: **Guthrie/newborn** and antenatal. Diagnosis: **Hb electrophoresis/HPLC**.

- **Crises:** **vaso-occlusive/painful** (bones, chest), **acute chest syndrome** (dyspnoea/infiltrates/hypoxia — a leading cause of death, needs urgent transfusion), **aplastic** (**parvovirus B19** shuts off erythropoiesis), **sequestration** (splenic pooling → shock, in children), **haemolytic**.
- **Chronic complications:** **hyposplenism** (autosplenectomy → encapsulated-organism sepsis; vaccinate + penicillin prophylaxis), stroke, retinopathy, avascular necrosis (femoral head), priapism, leg ulcers, pulmonary hypertension, gallstones, chronic kidney disease.
- **Management:** hydration, analgesia (often opioids), oxygen, treat infection, transfusion/exchange for severe crises. **⚑ Hydroxycarbamide (hydroxyurea)** raises **HbF** and reduces crisis frequency — a cornerstone of long-term care; newer agents include **crizanlizumab** and **voxelotor** (availability varies ⚑), and curative options are **allogeneic stem cell transplant** and, increasingly, **gene therapy**.

**Thalassaemias** — reduced globin-chain synthesis → ineffective erythropoiesis + haemolysis, microcytic:
- **α-thalassaemia** — deletions of the 4 α-globin genes. 1–2 genes = trait/asymptomatic; 3 genes = **HbH disease** (moderate anaemia); 4 genes = **Hb Barts / hydrops fetalis** (fatal in utero).
- **β-thalassaemia** — **trait** (raised HbA₂, mild microcytosis, no treatment) vs **major** (transfusion-dependent from infancy, **skeletal/facial bone expansion, hepatosplenomegaly from extramedullary haematopoiesis**). Regular transfusion → **iron overload** → treat with **iron chelation** (desferrioxamine, deferasirox); definitive cure by transplant.

#### 4.6 Marrow failure & polycythaemia

**Aplastic anaemia** — **pancytopenia with a hypocellular marrow** and no infiltrate/fibrosis; immune-mediated, drugs (chloramphenicol, carbimazole, chemotherapy), viral (hepatitis, parvovirus), or inherited (**Fanconi anaemia**). Treat with supportive care, immunosuppression (ATG + ciclosporin), or transplant. **Pure red cell aplasia** (isolated anaemia, absent reticulocytes) — parvovirus B19, thymoma, drugs.

**Polycythaemia** (raised Hb/Hct):
- **Relative** (plasma volume ↓) — dehydration, "Gaisböck's."
- **Absolute primary — polycythaemia vera** — a myeloproliferative neoplasm; **⚑ JAK2 V617F mutation in >95%**; low EPO; itch after hot baths (aquagenic pruritus), plethora, thrombosis, splenomegaly; risk of transformation to myelofibrosis/AML. Treat with **venesection ± hydroxycarbamide + aspirin.** (Covered further in Section 7.)
- **Absolute secondary** — appropriate ↑EPO (chronic hypoxia — COPD, altitude, cyanotic heart disease) or inappropriate ↑EPO (renal/hepatic tumours). High EPO here, unlike PV.

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Microcytic + pencil cells + low ferritin | Iron deficiency |
| IDA vs ACD single discriminator | TIBC (high IDA, low/normal ACD) |
| Very low MCV, near-normal Hb, high RBC count | Thalassaemia trait |
| Raised HbA₂ | β-thalassaemia trait |
| Basophilic stippling + microcytosis | Lead poisoning / sideroblastic |
| Macrocytic + hypersegmented neutrophils | Megaloblastic (B12/folate) |
| Macrocytic anaemia + neuro signs (dorsal columns) | B12 deficiency / SACD |
| Why treat B12 before folate | Folate alone can precipitate SACD |
| Bite cells + Heinz bodies, X-linked, fava beans | G6PD deficiency |
| Spherocytes + negative DAT + ↑MCHC | Hereditary spherocytosis |
| Spherocytes + positive DAT | Warm AIHA |
| Cold agglutinins after Mycoplasma/EBV | Cold AIHA (IgM) |
| Intravascular haemolysis + thrombosis + pancytopenia, CD55/CD59 loss | PNH |
| Schistocytes | MAHA (DIC/TTP/HUS/valve) |
| Sickle + sudden aplastic crisis | Parvovirus B19 |
| Sickle drug that raises HbF | Hydroxycarbamide ⚑ |
| Pancytopenia + hypocellular marrow, no infiltrate | Aplastic anaemia |
| Polycythaemia + JAK2 + low EPO + aquagenic pruritus | Polycythaemia vera ⚑ |
| Secondary polycythaemia EPO level | High (unlike PV, where EPO is low) |
