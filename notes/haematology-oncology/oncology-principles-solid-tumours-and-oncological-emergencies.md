#### Section 8 — Principles of Oncology & Systemic Therapy

#### 8.1 Epidemiology, screening & prevention

The commonest cancers (UK/Australia): **breast, prostate, lung, bowel** dominate incidence; **lung** remains a leading cause of cancer death. **Screening** targets common cancers with a detectable pre-clinical phase and effective early treatment:

| Programme (UK) ⚑ | Population | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **Breast** | Women ~50–71, every 3 years | Mammography |
| **Bowel** | ~50/60–74 (varies by nation) | **FIT (faecal immunochemical test)** home kit ± colonoscopy |
| **Cervical** | Women 25–64 | **Primary HPV testing** (cytology if HPV+) ⚑ |

(Australia mirrors these: **BreastScreen** 50–74, **National Bowel Cancer Screening** with iFOBT 50–74, **Cervical Screening** 25–74 with 5-yearly primary HPV.) **Prostate has no national screening programme** (PSA's poor specificity). **Lung-cancer low-dose CT screening** for high-risk ever-smokers is being rolled out ⚑.

**Prevention:** smoking cessation (lung, bladder, head & neck, oesophagus), HPV vaccination (cervical, anal, oropharyngeal), hepatitis B vaccination (HCC), alcohol/obesity reduction, sun protection (melanoma). **Hereditary predisposition** worth recognising: **BRCA1/2** (breast, ovarian, prostate, pancreatic), **Lynch/HNPCC** (colorectal, endometrial — MMR genes), **FAP** (APC — colorectal), **Li–Fraumeni** (TP53 — sarcoma, breast, brain), **MEN** syndromes, **retinoblastoma (RB1)**.

#### 8.2 Diagnosis, staging & prognosis

Diagnosis needs **tissue** (Section 3.5). **Staging** describes extent — **TNM** (Tumour, Node, Metastasis) → stage groups I–IV — while **grade** describes differentiation (how abnormal the cells look). Both feed prognosis alongside **performance status** and molecular markers. **Urgent (2-week-wait) referral** pathways (NICE NG12) route red-flag presentations quickly.

#### 8.3 Treatment modalities

- **Surgery** — the main curative modality for localised solid tumours; also diagnostic, palliative (bypass, fixation), and reconstructive.
- **Radiotherapy** — ionising radiation damaging tumour DNA. **Radical** (curative), **adjuvant** (post-op), **neoadjuvant** (shrink pre-op), **palliative** (bone pain, cord compression, SVCO, bleeding). Toxicity is **acute** (mucositis, skin erythema, diarrhoea — inflammation of the irradiated field) vs **late** (fibrosis, stricture, second malignancy). Brachytherapy = internal source.
- **Systemic anticancer therapy** — reaches disseminated disease (see 8.4).

**Treatment intent:** **neoadjuvant** (before primary treatment), **adjuvant** (after, to kill micrometastases), **radical/curative**, or **palliative** (symptom control, prolong life). Naming the intent is expected in every oncology answer.

#### 8.4 Systemic anticancer therapy

| Class | Examples | Mechanism | Signature toxicity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cytotoxic chemotherapy** | (see Section 11) | Damage DNA/mitosis of dividing cells | Myelosuppression, alopecia, mucositis, N&V, infertility |
| **Targeted small molecules** | Imatinib, erlotinib/osimertinib (EGFR), TKIs | Block specific driver kinases | Rash, diarrhoea (mechanism-specific) |
| **Monoclonal antibodies** | Rituximab (CD20), trastuzumab (HER2), bevacizumab (VEGF), cetuximab (EGFR) | Block surface targets/ligands | Infusion reactions; trastuzumab cardiotoxicity; bevacizumab bleeding/HTN |
| **Immunotherapy — checkpoint inhibitors** ⚑ | Pembrolizumab/nivolumab (PD-1), atezolizumab (PD-L1), ipilimumab (CTLA-4) | Release the brakes on T cells | **Immune-related adverse events (irAEs)** — colitis, pneumonitis, hepatitis, endocrinopathies |
| **CAR-T cell therapy** ⚑ | For refractory B-cell malignancies | Engineered autologous T cells | **Cytokine release syndrome (CRS)**, neurotoxicity |
| **Hormonal therapy** | Tamoxifen, aromatase inhibitors, GnRH agonists, anti-androgens | Block hormone-driven growth | Menopausal symptoms, bone loss, thromboembolism (tamoxifen) |

**Immunotherapy is the major recent shift** — checkpoint inhibitors have transformed melanoma, lung, renal and many other cancers, but their toxicity is **autoimmune** and can affect any organ; management is steroids/immunosuppression and holding the drug, not the usual chemo supportive care. ⚑

#### 8.5 Supportive & palliative care

**Cancer pain — WHO analgesic ladder:** non-opioid (paracetamol/NSAID) → weak opioid (codeine) → strong opioid (morphine), with adjuvants (dexamethasone, gabapentinoids for neuropathic pain, bisphosphonates for bone pain). Anticipate and prescribe for **constipation** (co-prescribe laxatives with opioids), **nausea**, and breakthrough pain (~1/6 of the 24-h dose). Learn basic **opioid conversion** (e.g. oral→subcut morphine divide by 2; morphine→oxycodone roughly divide by 1.5–2). **Anticipatory/end-of-life prescribing:** subcut morphine (pain/dyspnoea), midazolam (agitation), haloperidol/levomepromazine (nausea/agitation), hyoscine/glycopyrronium (secretions). Involve palliative care early; recognise the dying patient and address holistic (psychological, spiritual, family) needs.

#### Section 9 — Common Solid Tumours

#### 9.1 Breast

Commonest cancer in women. Presentation: painless lump, nipple change/discharge, skin tethering/peau d'orange, axillary node. **Triple assessment** = **clinical exam + imaging (mammography/USS) + core biopsy** — the exam mantra. Types: **ductal (commonest) vs lobular**; in-situ (DCIS) vs invasive. **Receptor subtypes drive treatment ⚑:**

| Subtype | Marker | Treatment lever |
|---|---|---|
| **ER/PR positive** | Hormone receptor + | **Endocrine therapy** (tamoxifen pre-menopause; aromatase inhibitor post-menopause) |
| **HER2 positive** | HER2 amplified | **Anti-HER2** (trastuzumab, pertuzumab) |
| **Triple-negative** | ER−/PR−/HER2− | Chemotherapy (± immunotherapy/PARP inhibitor if BRCA); worst prognosis |

Management combines **surgery** (wide local excision + radiotherapy, or mastectomy) with **sentinel lymph node biopsy**, ± adjuvant chemo/radio/endocrine/anti-HER2. **BRCA1/2** carriers: earlier onset, higher risk, consider risk-reducing surgery and **PARP inhibitors**. Spread: bone, lung, liver, brain.

#### 9.2 Lung

Leading cancer killer; strongly smoking-related. Presentation: cough, haemoptysis, weight loss, recurrent pneumonia, or a paraneoplastic/metastatic syndrome. **Two big groups:**

**Non-small cell (NSCLC, ~85%)** — adenocarcinoma (commonest, including non-smokers), squamous (central, cavitating, **PTHrP → hypercalcaemia**), large cell. **⚑ Molecular panel guides targeted therapy in adenocarcinoma:** **EGFR** (osimertinib), **ALK** and **ROS1** rearrangements (targeted TKIs), **KRAS G12C** (sotorasib/adagrasib), **BRAF, MET, RET, NTRK**; **PD-L1** expression guides immunotherapy. Test every advanced non-squamous NSCLC.

**Small cell (SCLC, ~15%)** — central, aggressive, early metastasis, strongly smoking-related; **neuroendocrine** origin → **paraneoplastic syndromes** (SIADH, ectopic ACTH → Cushing's, **Lambert–Eaton myasthenic syndrome**). Rarely surgical; chemo ± immunotherapy ± radiotherapy.

**Paraneoplastic map (high-yield):** **squamous → PTHrP (hypercalcaemia)**; **small cell → SIADH, ectopic ACTH, Lambert–Eaton**; any → clubbing/HPOA, hypertrophic pulmonary osteoarthropathy. **Pancoast tumour** (apex) → Horner's syndrome + arm/brachial-plexus symptoms. **Mesothelioma** — pleural malignancy from **asbestos** (long latency); pleural plaques, effusion.

#### 9.3 Colorectal

Third commonest cancer. Adenocarcinoma via the **adenoma–carcinoma sequence** (APC → KRAS → TP53). Risk: age, IBD, diet, **FAP** (hundreds of polyps, APC — near-100% cancer risk, prophylactic colectomy) and **Lynch/HNPCC** (MMR genes; also endometrial). Presentation by side: **left/rectal** → change in bowel habit, PR bleeding, tenesmus, obstruction; **right** → occult bleeding/**iron-deficiency anaemia**, mass. **Screening: FIT** (see 8.1). Diagnosis: colonoscopy + biopsy; staging CT + MRI rectum + **CEA** baseline. Treatment: surgical resection ± adjuvant chemo (FOLFOX); rectal cancer often neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy. **⚑ Molecular:** **RAS/BRAF** status (anti-EGFR **cetuximab** only works in **RAS wild-type**); **MMR/microsatellite-instability (MSI-high)** predicts immunotherapy response and flags Lynch. Spread: **liver** (portal drainage) → lung.

#### 9.4 Prostate & urological

**Prostate** — commonest male cancer; adenocarcinoma of the peripheral zone. Often asymptomatic (screen-detected) or LUTS/bone pain (**osteoblastic** mets → bone scan). **PSA** raised but non-specific (BPH, prostatitis, catheter, DRE). **Gleason score / ISUP grade groups** grade aggressiveness; risk stratification guides active surveillance vs radical prostatectomy/radiotherapy vs **androgen deprivation therapy (ADT)** for advanced disease.

**Renal cell carcinoma** — "internist's tumour": haematuria, flank pain, mass (triad is late); **paraneoplastic** (polycythaemia via EPO, hypercalcaemia, hypertension, Stauffer's syndrome); left varicocele (renal vein). Surgery; TKIs/immunotherapy for advanced.

**Bladder** — **painless visible haematuria** is bladder cancer until proven otherwise (cystoscopy). Mostly **transitional cell (urothelial) carcinoma** (smoking, aromatic amines/dye/rubber; squamous with schistosomiasis). TURBT ± intravesical BCG/chemo, or cystectomy.

**Testicular** — commonest solid cancer in **young men (20–40)**; painless testicular lump. **Seminoma** vs **non-seminomatous germ cell tumours**. **Markers:** **AFP + β-hCG** (raised in NSGCT; **AFP is NOT raised in pure seminoma**), LDH. Spreads to **para-aortic nodes**. Highly curable (orchidectomy + platinum-based chemo).

#### 9.5 Upper GI & hepatobiliary

- **Oesophageal** — **adenocarcinoma** (lower third; GORD/Barrett's/obesity — rising ⚑) vs **squamous** (upper two-thirds; smoking, alcohol). Progressive dysphagia + weight loss. Poor prognosis.
- **Gastric** — adenocarcinoma; *H. pylori*, smoking, diet, pernicious anaemia; **Virchow's node**, Sister Mary Joseph nodule, Krukenberg (transcoelomic to ovary). Signet-ring/linitis plastica.
- **Pancreatic** — ductal adenocarcinoma (head): **painless obstructive jaundice + weight loss** (Courvoisier: palpable non-tender gallbladder + jaundice → not gallstones), new diabetes, thrombophlebitis migrans (Trousseau). **CA 19-9**. Usually advanced at diagnosis; Whipple if resectable.
- **Hepatocellular carcinoma** — on a background of **cirrhosis (hepatitis B/C, alcohol, NAFLD)**; **AFP**; surveillance USS in cirrhosis. Treatment by liver function/stage (resection, transplant, ablation, TACE, systemic).

#### 9.6 Gynaecological

- **Ovarian** — presents **late** with vague bloating/abdominal distension/early satiety; **CA-125**; **epithelial** (commonest), linked to **BRCA**; risk with age, nulliparity, ovulation. Surgery + platinum chemo ± PARP inhibitors (BRCA).
- **Endometrial** — **post-menopausal bleeding** is endometrial cancer until proven otherwise (TV-USS endometrial thickness → hysteroscopy + biopsy); oestrogen-driven (obesity, unopposed oestrogen, tamoxifen, PCOS, Lynch). Usually early-stage, good prognosis.
- **Cervical** — **HPV (16/18)**-driven squamous (and adeno); post-coital/intermenstrual bleeding; prevented by **HPV vaccination + screening** ⚑.

#### 9.7 Skin, CNS, head & neck, unknown primary

- **Melanoma** — UV/sun exposure; the **ABCDE** rule (Asymmetry, Border, Colour, Diameter >6 mm, Evolving). Prognosis by **Breslow thickness** (depth). **⚑ BRAF V600 mutation** → BRAF/MEK inhibitors; checkpoint immunotherapy has transformed advanced disease. Spreads widely (including brain).
- **Non-melanoma skin cancer** — **basal cell carcinoma** (commonest, "rodent ulcer", rolled pearly edge, rarely metastasises) vs **squamous cell carcinoma** (can metastasise; arises in sun-damaged skin/actinic keratosis).
- **CNS** — primary **gliomas** (glioblastoma — poor prognosis) and meningiomas, but **metastases are the commonest brain tumours** (lung, breast, melanoma, renal, colorectal); present with headache (worse in morning/on lying), seizures, focal deficit, raised ICP.
- **Head & neck** — squamous cell; smoking/alcohol, and rising **HPV-positive oropharyngeal** cancer (better prognosis).
- **Carcinoma of unknown primary** — metastatic cancer with no identifiable primary despite workup; treat by immunohistochemistry-guided likely origin.

#### Section 10 — Oncological Emergencies

#### 10.1 Neutropenic sepsis ⚑
**The most important oncology emergency.** Definition (NICE CG151): a patient on anticancer treatment with **neutrophils ≤0.5 ×10⁹/L** (some use ≤1.0 with expected fall) **and either temperature >38°C or other signs/symptoms of clinically significant sepsis.** **Management is time-critical: broad-spectrum IV antibiotics (e.g. piperacillin-tazobactam) within 1 hour — the "door-to-needle" rule — BEFORE waiting for the neutrophil count or imaging.** Full sepsis-six/cultures, assess with the **MASCC score** to identify low-risk patients. Never delay antibiotics. ⚑

#### 10.2 Tumour lysis syndrome (TLS)
Massive tumour-cell breakdown (spontaneous or on starting treatment) in **high-turnover cancers — Burkitt lymphoma, ALL, high-grade lymphomas, bulky disease**. The metabolic quartet: **↑potassium, ↑phosphate, ↑urate → ↓calcium** (and AKI from urate/phosphate crystals). **Cairo–Bishop** criteria classify laboratory vs clinical TLS. **Prevent** in at-risk patients: **IV hydration** + **allopurinol** (lower risk) or **rasburicase** (higher risk/established TLS — recombinant urate oxidase; **contraindicated in G6PD deficiency**). Treat hyperkalaemia; avoid calcium unless symptomatic; renal support if needed.

#### 10.3 Malignant spinal cord compression (MSCC) ⚑
Vertebral metastasis/collapse compressing the cord — **breast, lung, prostate, myeloma, renal**. Red flags: **new/progressive back pain (worse lying/coughing), a sensory level, limb weakness, bladder/bowel dysfunction.** **⚑ Management (NICE NG234): give dexamethasone 16 mg immediately, arrange whole-spine MRI as soon as possible and within 24 hours, and deliver definitive treatment (surgical decompression or radiotherapy) as soon as possible and within 24 hours.** Nurse flat with neutral spine alignment until stability assessed. Outcome depends on treating **before** paraplegia is established — act on suspicion.

#### 10.4 Superior vena cava obstruction (SVCO)
Obstruction of SVC flow — commonly **lung cancer, lymphoma**. Facial/neck/arm swelling, distended veins, plethora, dyspnoea, headache; **Pemberton's sign** (facial congestion on raising the arms). Usually subacute but can be an emergency (airway/cerebral oedema). Investigate with CT; treat with corticosteroids, **endovascular stenting** (fast relief), and treat the underlying tumour (radiotherapy/chemo depending on histology — get tissue if feasible first).

#### 10.5 Hypercalcaemia of malignancy
Commonest life-threatening metabolic complication of cancer. Mechanisms: **PTHrP** (squamous lung/head-neck, renal, breast — humoral), **osteolytic** bone metastases/myeloma, and **calcitriol** production (lymphoma). "**Bones, stones, groans, psychic moans**" + polyuria/dehydration. Management: **aggressive IV 0.9% saline rehydration** first, then an **IV bisphosphonate** (zoledronic acid) — or **denosumab** if refractory/renal impairment; treat the cancer. (Calcium-axis physiology cross-referenced from the endocrine build.)

#### 10.6 Other emergencies
- **Raised ICP / brain metastases** — headache, vomiting, papilloedema, focal signs, seizures; **dexamethasone** for vasogenic oedema, urgent imaging, neurosurgery/radiotherapy.
- **Catastrophic haemorrhage** (e.g. carotid blowout, tumour erosion) — often a palliative event; crisis medication.
- **Chemotherapy extravasation** — a vesicant leaking into tissue; stop infusion, aspirate, agent-specific antidote/cold or warm compress.
- **Immunotherapy toxicity (irAEs)** ⚑ — checkpoint-inhibitor colitis/pneumonitis/hepatitis/hypophysitis; recognise and treat with **corticosteroids** (not standard anti-infective/supportive care), hold the drug.
