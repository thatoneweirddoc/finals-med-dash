## Physiology, Cancer Biology & Anatomy


### Haematology & Oncology Notes — Physiology, Cancer Biology & Anatomy

*Sources: Hoffbrand's Essential Haematology; Guyton & Hall Physiology; Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease; StatPearls. Scope: the foundational biology that the clinical sections lean on — haematopoiesis, red/white cells, haemostasis, the hallmarks of cancer, and the marrow/lymphoreticular anatomy. Anatomy is written as prose + tables and paired with the anatomy SVG sheet.*

#### 1.1 Haematopoiesis

All blood cells derive from a single **pluripotent haematopoietic stem cell (HSC)** in the bone marrow, defined by two properties: **self-renewal** and **multipotency**. The HSC divides into a **common myeloid progenitor** (→ red cells, granulocytes, monocytes, platelets) and a **common lymphoid progenitor** (→ B, T and NK cells). **This myeloid/lymphoid fork is the single most useful anatomy in haematology** — it is the backbone of leukaemia and lymphoma classification.

**Sites of haematopoiesis across life** — a migration you should be able to recite:

| Stage | Primary site |
|---|---|
| 0–2 months (embryo) | Yolk sac |
| 2–7 months (foetus) | **Liver & spleen** |
| 5 months → birth → adult | **Bone marrow** |
| Adult | Axial skeleton + proximal femora/humeri (red marrow); peripheral long bones convert to yellow (fatty) marrow |

**Extramedullary haematopoiesis** (reversion to hepatic/splenic production, causing hepatosplenomegaly) is a red flag for chronic marrow stress — thalassaemia, myelofibrosis, severe chronic haemolysis. On the film it shows as a **leukoerythroblastic picture**: nucleated red cells + immature myeloid cells + tear-drop cells spilling into the blood. *Clue → cause:* leukoerythroblastic film → **marrow infiltration** (malignancy, myelofibrosis) until proven otherwise.

**Key growth factors** (each maps to a drug):

| Factor | Made by | Drives | Therapeutic analogue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Erythropoietin (EPO)** | Renal peritubular cells (in response to hypoxia via HIF) | Red cell production | Epoetin/darbepoetin (renal anaemia) |
| **Thrombopoietin (TPO)** | Liver | Platelet production | Romiplostim, eltrombopag (ITP) |
| **G-CSF** | Endothelium, macrophages | Neutrophil production | Filgrastim (post-chemo neutropenia, stem-cell mobilisation) |

#### 1.2 Red cells & haemoglobin

Erythropoiesis takes ~7 days: pronormoblast → normoblast (extrudes its nucleus) → **reticulocyte** (still contains RNA — the marker of active production) → mature biconcave erythrocyte (lifespan ~120 days, no nucleus/mitochondria, relies on glycolysis and the pentose phosphate shunt for reducing power).

**Three raw materials** the marrow needs, each with its own deficiency anaemia: **iron** (haem synthesis; deficiency → microcytic), **B12 and folate** (DNA synthesis; deficiency → megaloblastic macrocytic).

**Haemoglobin** is a tetramer of two α and two β-like globin chains, each cradling a haem (iron + protoporphyrin) that binds one O₂:

| Type | Chains | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **HbA** | α₂β₂ | ~97% of adult Hb |
| **HbA₂** | α₂δ₂ | ~2–3%; **raised in β-thalassaemia trait** |
| **HbF** | α₂γ₂ | Dominant foetal Hb; higher O₂ affinity; persists/rises in β-thalassaemia, sickle cell |

The **oxygen dissociation curve** is sigmoid (cooperative binding). *Right shift* (offloads O₂ to tissues) = ↑CO₂, ↑H⁺/↓pH (Bohr), ↑temperature, ↑**2,3-BPG**. *Left shift* (holds O₂) = the opposites, plus **HbF** and carboxyhaemoglobin. Memory hook: "**exercising muscle is hot, acidic, and CO₂-rich → curve shifts right → O₂ released**."

**Red cell breakdown:** senescent cells are removed by splenic macrophages. Haem → **biliverdin → unconjugated bilirubin** (bound to albumin, water-insoluble); globin recycled; iron recycled. Free plasma Hb is mopped up by **haptoglobin**. This is why **intravascular haemolysis** produces the pattern *↑LDH, ↓haptoglobin, ↑unconjugated bilirubin, haemoglobinuria*.

#### 1.3 White cells & immune effectors

| Cell | Lineage | Job | "Goes up with…" |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Neutrophil** | Myeloid | Bacterial/fungal phagocytosis | Bacterial infection, steroids, stress, tissue necrosis |
| **Lymphocyte** | Lymphoid | Adaptive immunity (B→antibody, T→cell-mediated) | Viral infection, CLL, pertussis |
| **Monocyte** | Myeloid | Becomes tissue macrophage; antigen presentation | Chronic infection (TB), CMML |
| **Eosinophil** | Myeloid | Parasites, allergy | **Allergy, parasites, drugs, Hodgkin, EGPA** (mnemonic: NAACP) |
| **Basophil** | Myeloid | Histamine, allergy | **CML** (basophilia is a clue), allergy |

The **differential white cell count** is a diagnostic instrument, not a footnote: a **neutrophilia with left shift** points to bacterial infection; a **lymphocytosis of mature smear cells** points to CLL; **eosinophilia** narrows to allergy/parasites/drugs/Hodgkin/vasculitis; **basophilia** flags a myeloproliferative process.

#### 1.4 Haemostasis

Haemostasis is the balance between clotting and bleeding. Two arms:

**Primary haemostasis (the platelet plug)** — endothelial injury exposes collagen; **von Willebrand factor (VWF)** bridges collagen to platelet **GpIb**; platelets activate, release ADP/thromboxane A₂, and aggregate via **GpIIb/IIIa** cross-linked by fibrinogen. *Disorders here → **mucocutaneous** bleeding: petechiae, epistaxis, menorrhagia, gum bleeding.*

**Secondary haemostasis (the coagulation cascade)** — a protease relay ending in a fibrin mesh. Two entry routes converge on a common path:

| Pathway | Factors | Measured by |
|---|---|---|
| **Extrinsic** ("tissue factor") | VII | **PT / INR** |
| **Intrinsic** ("contact") | XII, XI, IX, VIII | **APTT** |
| **Common** | X, V, II (prothrombin→thrombin), I (fibrinogen→fibrin), XIII (cross-links) | Both |

Memory hooks that pay off constantly: **"PT tests the Extrinsic; use warfarin's INR"** (VII has the shortest half-life, so PT/INR is the first to rise on warfarin). **"APTT tests the Intrinsic — think haemophilia and heparin."** **Vitamin-K-dependent factors = 2, 7, 9, 10 (+ protein C & S)** — the warfarin targets. Factor **VIII is the only clotting factor not made in the liver** (made by endothelium), so it stays normal or high in liver disease.

**Fibrinolysis:** plasminogen → **plasmin** (by tPA) dissolves fibrin, releasing **D-dimer** (a fibrin degradation product — sensitive but not specific for thrombosis).

**Natural anticoagulants** (lose these → thrombophilia): **antithrombin** (inhibits thrombin & Xa — the heparin target), **protein C & protein S** (inactivate factors Va and VIIIa; vitamin-K-dependent). **Factor V Leiden** makes factor V resistant to protein C → commonest inherited thrombophilia.

**Virchow's triad** (why thrombi form): **stasis, endothelial injury, hypercoagulability.** Every VTE risk factor slots into one of the three.

#### 1.5 Cancer biology

Cancer is a genetic disease of somatic cells: a clone acquires mutations that let it proliferate without restraint and spread. The **hallmarks of cancer** (Hanahan & Weinberg) are the exam-friendly organising frame:

- **Self-sufficiency in growth signals** (oncogene activation)
- **Insensitivity to anti-growth signals** (tumour-suppressor loss)
- **Evading apoptosis** (e.g. p53 loss, BCL-2 overexpression)
- **Limitless replicative potential** (telomerase)
- **Sustained angiogenesis** (VEGF — the bevacizumab target)
- **Tissue invasion & metastasis**
- (enabling) **Genome instability** and **tumour-promoting inflammation**
- (emerging) **Deregulated metabolism** (Warburg effect) and **evading immune destruction** (the checkpoint-inhibitor rationale)

**Three gene classes:**

| Class | Normal role | In cancer | Inheritance of mutation | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Oncogene** | Promote proliferation | **Gain of function** — one hit enough (dominant) | Usually somatic | RAS, MYC, HER2, BCR-ABL1 |
| **Tumour-suppressor gene** | Brake proliferation | **Loss of function** — **two hits** (Knudson) | Can be germline (familial cancer) | **TP53** (p53), RB1, APC, BRCA1/2 |
| **DNA-repair gene** | Fix DNA errors | Loss → mutator phenotype | Germline in Lynch, xeroderma | MLH1/MSH2 (Lynch), BRCA |

**Knudson's two-hit hypothesis:** tumour-suppressors need both alleles knocked out. In hereditary cancer syndromes (retinoblastoma, Li–Fraumeni, Lynch, FAP) the person inherits one defective allele, so a single somatic "second hit" causes cancer — explaining younger age and bilateral/multifocal tumours.

**Carcinogenesis is multistep:** *initiation* (irreversible mutation from a carcinogen) → *promotion* (clonal expansion) → *progression* (further mutations, malignant phenotype). Illustrated best by the **adenoma–carcinoma sequence** in colorectal cancer (APC → KRAS → TP53).

**Invasion & metastasis:** loss of E-cadherin, basement-membrane degradation (matrix metalloproteinases), intravasation, survival in the circulation, extravasation at a distant site, angiogenesis. Routes: **lymphatic** (carcinomas typically) vs **haematogenous** (sarcomas, and carcinomas to lung/liver/bone/brain) vs **transcoelomic** (ovarian, gastric → peritoneum, e.g. Krukenberg tumour).

**Paraneoplastic syndromes** — remote effects not due to mass/metastasis, often via ectopic hormone secretion or autoimmunity (SIADH & ectopic ACTH in small cell lung cancer, PTHrP in squamous cancers, Lambert–Eaton). High-yield because they can be the presenting clue.

**Nomenclature** — worth being crisp: **-oma** usually benign (adenoma, lipoma); **carcinoma** = malignant epithelial; **sarcoma** = malignant mesenchymal (bone, muscle, fat); **-blastoma** = embryonal (neuroblastoma); **leukaemia/lymphoma/myeloma** = haematological. Exceptions that are malignant despite "-oma": **melanoma, lymphoma, myeloma, seminoma, glioblastoma.**

#### 2.1 Bone marrow *(anatomy)*

The marrow is the factory. **Red (haematopoietically active) marrow** in adults is confined to the **axial skeleton** — vertebrae, sternum, ribs, pelvis, skull — plus the **proximal femur and humerus**. Peripheral long-bone marrow is **yellow (fatty)** and can reconvert to red under haematopoietic stress. The marrow niche pairs developing cells with stromal cells and sinusoids; only mature cells cross the sinusoidal wall into blood.

**Clinical anatomy:** the standard **bone marrow biopsy site is the posterior superior iliac spine (posterior iliac crest)** — an **aspirate** samples cells for morphology/cytogenetics/flow, while a **trephine** takes a core of intact architecture for cellularity and infiltration. The sternum can be aspirated (not trephined) but is rarely used. A **"dry tap"** (no aspirate obtained) itself suggests **fibrosis (myelofibrosis) or a packed marrow**.

#### 2.2 The lymphatic system *(anatomy)*

Lymph nodes are bean-shaped filters on the lymphatic highways. **Architecture, outside-in:** **cortex** (B-cell follicles with germinal centres) → **paracortex** (T cells) → **medulla** (plasma cells, macrophages). Afferent lymphatics enter the convex surface; a single efferent leaves the hilum. **Reactive lymphadenopathy** expands the germinal centres; **lymphoma** effaces the architecture; **metastatic carcinoma** seeds the subcapsular sinus first.

Lymph ultimately drains to the venous system via the **thoracic duct** (drains everything except the right upper body → left subclavian/IJV junction) and the **right lymphatic duct** (right head, neck, arm, thorax).

**Clinically important node groups:**

| Node | Drains / signals |
|---|---|
| **Cervical** | Head, neck; also lymphoma, nasopharyngeal/thyroid cancer |
| **Supraclavicular — Virchow's node (left)** | **"Troisier's sign"** — GI (gastric), thoracic malignancy via thoracic duct |
| **Axillary** | Breast, upper limb — central to breast-cancer staging (sentinel node) |
| **Inguinal** | Lower limb, perineum, anal canal, vulva |
| **Para-aortic** | Testicular cancer spreads here (testis descends from the abdomen, so drainage is para-aortic, **not** inguinal — a classic exam point) |

*Assessing lymphadenopathy:* rubbery/painless (lymphoma), hard/fixed/matted (carcinoma), tender/mobile (reactive/infective). Generalised lymphadenopathy → think lymphoma/leukaemia, HIV, EBV, TB, sarcoid, connective-tissue disease.

#### 2.3 Spleen *(anatomy)*

The spleen is the largest lymphoid organ, in the **left hypochondrium along the 9th–11th ribs** (so a normal spleen is not palpable). Two functional tissues: **red pulp** (mechanical filter — culls old/abnormal red cells and removes intra-erythrocytic inclusions, "pitting") and **white pulp** (immune surveillance — the body's largest collection of lymphoid tissue, key for opsonising encapsulated organisms).

**Functions:** filtration, immunity (antibody production, filtering encapsulated bacteria), reservoir (platelets — a third of the platelet mass is pooled here), and reactivation of haematopoiesis in disease.

**Hyposplenism / post-splenectomy** — loss of filtration and antibody function → **overwhelming post-splenectomy infection (OPSI)** from **encapsulated organisms** (*Strep pneumoniae, H. influenzae b, N. meningitidis*). Hence vaccinate + lifelong prophylactic penicillin. Film clues to hyposplenism: **Howell–Jolly bodies, target cells, Pappenheimer bodies, thrombocytosis.**

**Splenomegaly — the pattern matters.** *Massive* splenomegaly (crosses the midline / reaches the pelvis): **CML, myelofibrosis** (and, worldwide, chronic malaria & visceral leishmaniasis). *Moderate:* portal hypertension, lymphoma, haemolysis. **Hypersplenism** = an enlarged spleen pooling and destroying cells → cytopenias.

#### 2.4 Thymus & primary lymphoid organs *(anatomy)*

**Primary lymphoid organs** (where lymphocytes are made and educated): **bone marrow** (B cells mature here; T-cell precursors born here) and the **thymus** (T cells mature — positive/negative selection teaches self-tolerance). **Secondary lymphoid organs** (where they meet antigen): lymph nodes, spleen, MALT (tonsils, Peyer's patches).

The thymus (anterior mediastinum) involutes after puberty. **Thymic pathology is exam-relevant through its associations:** **thymoma** links to **myasthenia gravis**, red cell aplasia, and hypogammaglobulinaemia; the anterior-mediastinal mass differential is the **"4 Ts": Thymoma, Teratoma (germ cell), Thyroid (retrosternal), Terrible lymphoma.**
