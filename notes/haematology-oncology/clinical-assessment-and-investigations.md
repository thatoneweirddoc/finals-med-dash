## Clinical Assessment & Investigations


### Haematology & Oncology Notes — Clinical Assessment & Investigations

*Sources: Hoffbrand's Essential Haematology; BSH (British Society for Haematology) laboratory guidance; NICE suspected-cancer referral guidance (NG12); StatPearls; Robbins. Scope: how to take the history, read the FBC/film, interpret haematinics and the haemolysis screen, run a coagulation/thrombophilia work-up sensibly, and structure an oncology diagnosis. This is the interpretation scaffolding every later section reuses.*

---

#### 3.1 History & examination in haem-onc

**The symptom clusters that matter:**

- **Anaemia:** fatigue, dyspnoea, palpitations, pallor, angina.
- **Neutropenia:** recurrent/severe infections, mouth ulcers.
- **Thrombocytopenia/bleeding:** bruising, petechiae, epistaxis, menorrhagia, gum bleeding.
- **B symptoms** (lymphoma/leukaemia staging & prognosis): **fever >38°C, drenching night sweats, weight loss >10% over 6 months.** *Alcohol-induced nodal pain* is a rare but classic Hodgkin clue.
- **Hyperviscosity** (myeloma, Waldenström, high WCC leukaemia): headache, visual disturbance, confusion, mucosal bleeding.
- **Red-flag "cancer" symptoms:** unexplained weight loss, night sweats, bone pain, new lymphadenopathy, unexplained bruising/bleeding.

**Examination:** pallor, jaundice (haemolysis), bruising/petechiae, **lymphadenopathy** (all node groups + character), **hepatosplenomegaly**, bony tenderness (sternal tenderness in leukaemia), fundoscopy (hyperviscosity), and a full systems survey for a primary tumour.

**Performance status** drives every oncology treatment decision — learn the **ECOG/WHO** scale:

| ECOG | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Fully active |
| 1 | Restricted in strenuous activity, ambulatory, light work |
| 2 | Ambulatory, self-caring, **up >50% of waking hours**, no work |
| 3 | Limited self-care, in bed/chair **>50%** of waking hours |
| 4 | Completely disabled, bedbound |
| 5 | Dead |

Rule of thumb: **ECOG ≥3 usually precludes intensive chemotherapy.**

#### 3.2 The full blood count & blood film

**Reading an FBC** — go line by line, then think in patterns:

- **Anaemia** → classify by **MCV** (micro <80, normo 80–100, macro >100 fL) — the master framework of Section 4.
- **Pancytopenia** (all three lineages down) → think **marrow failure/infiltration** (aplasia, leukaemia, myeloma, myelofibrosis, megaloblastic anaemia), **hypersplenism**, or **peripheral destruction**. Always a bone marrow question.
- **High WCC** → is it a reactive **neutrophilia** (left shift, toxic granulation, infection), a **leukaemoid reaction** (very high neutrophils with left shift — infection/inflammation; **high LAP score**), or **leukaemia** (blasts; basophilia and **low LAP** point to CML)?
- **Thrombocytosis** → reactive (infection, inflammation, iron deficiency, post-splenectomy, malignancy) vs clonal (essential thrombocythaemia, other MPN).

**Blood-film morphology — the single highest-yield recognition table in haematology:**

| Film finding | Points to |
|---|---|
| **Microcytic hypochromic + pencil cells** | Iron deficiency |
| **Target cells** | Liver disease, thalassaemia, hyposplenism |
| **Spherocytes** | Hereditary spherocytosis or **autoimmune haemolysis** (DAT distinguishes) |
| **Schistocytes (fragments)** | **Microangiopathic haemolysis** — DIC, TTP/HUS, mechanical valve |
| **Bite cells / Heinz bodies** | G6PD deficiency (oxidative haemolysis) |
| **Sickle cells** | Sickle cell disease |
| **Tear-drop cells (dacrocytes)** | Myelofibrosis, marrow infiltration |
| **Rouleaux** (stacked coins) | High paraprotein — **myeloma**, chronic inflammation |
| **Howell–Jolly bodies** | Hyposplenism/post-splenectomy |
| **Blast cells** | Acute leukaemia |
| **Auer rods** | **AML** (pathognomonic) |
| **Smear/smudge cells** | **CLL** |
| **Hypersegmented neutrophils** | Megaloblastic anaemia (B12/folate) |
| **"Left shift" + toxic granulation** | Bacterial infection |

#### 3.3 Haematinics, haemolysis & marrow screens

**Iron studies — read them as a set, not in isolation:**

| Test | Iron deficiency | Anaemia of chronic disease | Iron overload |
|---|---|---|---|
| Serum iron | ↓ | ↓ | ↑ |
| **Ferritin** | **↓** | **↑ or normal** ⚑ | ↑↑ |
| **TIBC / transferrin** | **↑** | ↓ or normal | ↓ |
| Transferrin saturation | ↓ | ↓/normal | ↑ |

⚑ **Ferritin is an acute-phase reactant** — it rises in infection, inflammation and malignancy, so a "normal" ferritin does **not** exclude iron deficiency in an unwell patient. If in doubt, **transferrin saturation and the film** help; a very low ferritin (<15–30 µg/L) is specific for deficiency. The discriminating pair: **iron deficiency has a *high* TIBC; ACD has a *low/normal* TIBC.**

**B12 & folate** → macrocytosis, hypersegmented neutrophils; low B12 with neurological signs = pernicious anaemia/subacute combined degeneration (**check anti-intrinsic-factor antibodies**; treat B12 before/with folate to avoid precipitating cord degeneration).

**Reticulocyte count** — the "is the marrow responding?" test. **High retics** = appropriate marrow response → **haemolysis or blood loss**. **Low/inappropriately normal retics** with anaemia = a production problem (deficiency, marrow failure, ACD).

**The haemolysis screen** — order these together whenever haemolysis is suspected:

| Test | Haemolysis pattern |
|---|---|
| **LDH** | ↑ (released from lysed cells) |
| **Haptoglobin** | ↓ (consumed mopping up free Hb) — most marked in **intravascular** haemolysis |
| **Unconjugated bilirubin** | ↑ |
| **Reticulocytes** | ↑ |
| **DAT (Coombs)** | **Positive → immune** haemolysis; negative → non-immune (hereditary, mechanical, G6PD) |
| Urinary haemosiderin / haemoglobinuria | Present in intravascular haemolysis |

**Bone marrow examination** — **aspirate** (cell morphology, cytogenetics, flow cytometry, iron stores) vs **trephine** (architecture, cellularity, fibrosis, infiltration). A **dry tap** suggests fibrosis or a packed marrow.

#### 3.4 Coagulation & special tests

**Interpret the coagulation screen by which arm is prolonged:**

| Pattern | Suggests |
|---|---|
| **↑ PT (INR), normal APTT** | Factor VII deficiency; **warfarin**; early liver disease; vitamin K deficiency |
| **Normal PT, ↑ APTT** | **Haemophilia A/B**, **von Willebrand disease**, **heparin**, lupus anticoagulant (paradoxically thrombotic) |
| **Both ↑ (PT + APTT)** | Common-pathway problem: **DIC**, liver failure, vitamin K deficiency, warfarin overdose, massive transfusion |
| **Both normal, but bleeding** | Platelet-type problem (thrombocytopenia, platelet dysfunction) or mild VWD |

**Mixing study** — mix patient plasma 1:1 with normal plasma. **Corrects → factor deficiency.** **Fails to correct → an inhibitor** (e.g. lupus anticoagulant, factor VIII inhibitor).

**D-dimer** — a fibrin degradation product; **sensitive, not specific.** Useful to *rule out* VTE in a low-probability patient (a negative D-dimer excludes); a positive result has many non-thrombotic causes (infection, malignancy, pregnancy, surgery, age).

**Thrombophilia screen** — factor V Leiden, prothrombin G20210A, protein C, protein S, antithrombin, antiphospholipid antibodies. **Test sparingly and at the right time:** many results are unreliable during acute thrombosis, on anticoagulation, or in pregnancy; it rarely changes management for a first *provoked* VTE. **Antiphospholipid syndrome** is the one worth actively seeking in unexplained arterial/venous thrombosis, recurrent miscarriage, or a prolonged APTT that fails to correct.

**Protein studies** — serum **electrophoresis** (detects a monoclonal band/paraprotein), **immunofixation** (types it — e.g. IgG kappa), **serum free light chains** (κ/λ ratio — key for myeloma diagnosis and light-chain disease), and **urinary Bence Jones protein** (free light chains in urine).

#### 3.5 Oncology work-up

**Tumour markers** are aids, not diagnoses — most are neither sensitive nor specific enough to diagnose or screen (with narrow exceptions). Their real value is **monitoring treatment response and detecting recurrence** in a known cancer. Learn the map:

| Marker | Cancer | Caveats / other causes of rise |
|---|---|---|
| **PSA** | Prostate | BPH, prostatitis, catheter, DRE, ejaculation |
| **CA-125** | Ovarian (epithelial) | Endometriosis, ascites, menstruation, any peritoneal irritation |
| **CA 19-9** | Pancreatic, biliary | Cholestasis; absent in Lewis-negative people |
| **CEA** | Colorectal (monitoring) | Smoking, IBD, other GI |
| **AFP** | Hepatocellular, non-seminomatous germ cell | Pregnancy, hepatitis |
| **β-hCG** | Germ cell (choriocarcinoma, non-seminoma), gestational trophoblastic | Pregnancy |
| **AFP + β-hCG** | Testicular (non-seminoma) | Seminoma: β-hCG may rise, **AFP does not** |
| **Calcitonin** | Medullary thyroid | — |
| **Thyroglobulin** | Differentiated thyroid (monitoring) | — |

**Staging investigation set:** **CT** (chest/abdo/pelvis for most solid tumours), **MRI** (local soft-tissue/CNS/rectal/prostate staging), **PET-CT** (metabolically active disease — lymphoma staging, occult mets), **bone scan** (osteoblastic mets — prostate, breast), and site-specific endoscopy.

**Biopsy principles:** **FNA** (cytology only — cells, no architecture; good for confirming a node is malignant), **core biopsy** (histology + receptor/molecular tests — the workhorse for solid tumours), **excisional biopsy** (whole node — needed to diagnose and subtype **lymphoma**, because architecture matters). Always get tissue *before* systemic therapy.

**TNM staging** (Tumour size/depth, Node involvement, Metastasis) + **histological grade** (differentiation) together give prognosis; the **multidisciplinary team (MDT)** integrates radiology, pathology, surgery, oncology and specialist nursing to agree a plan. **NICE NG12** defines the **2-week-wait urgent referral** thresholds by symptom cluster (e.g. iron-deficiency anaemia in an older adult, dysphagia, PR bleeding with change in bowel habit, post-menopausal bleeding, haematuria).

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| B symptoms | Fever >38, drenching night sweats, weight loss >10%/6 months |
| ECOG cut-off that usually precludes intensive chemo | ≥3 (bed/chair >50% of waking hours) |
| Iron deficiency vs ACD single best discriminator | **TIBC/transferrin high in deficiency, low/normal in ACD** |
| Why a normal ferritin doesn't exclude iron deficiency | Ferritin is an acute-phase reactant ⚑ |
| Reticulocytes high in anaemia | Haemolysis or blood loss (marrow responding) |
| DAT positive | Immune haemolysis |
| Haptoglobin low + haemoglobinuria | Intravascular haemolysis |
| Isolated ↑APTT that fails to correct on mixing | Inhibitor — think lupus anticoagulant/factor inhibitor |
| Mixing study corrects | Factor deficiency |
| ↑PT + ↑APTT + ↓platelets + ↓fibrinogen + ↑D-dimer | DIC |
| Rouleaux on film | Paraprotein (myeloma) |
| Auer rods | AML |
| Smear cells | CLL |
| Tumour marker for testicular non-seminoma | AFP + β-hCG (AFP not raised in pure seminoma) |
| Biopsy needed to diagnose lymphoma | Excisional (architecture) — not FNA |
| Osteoblastic mets imaging | Bone scan (prostate, breast) |

### Haematology (ANU) — Tumour Lysis Syndrome — Clinical Assessment & Investigations

- DEFINITION: group of metabolic complications that result from spontaneous or treatment-related breakdown of cancer cells → massive release of intracellular components
- AETIOLOGY: cytotoxic treatment of ALL, AML, or NHL
- METABOLIC ABNORMALITIES
- Cells lyse, releasing K+, uric acid, PO43- (increased levels)
- PO43- binds to Ca2+ (hypocalcaemia)
- COMPLICATIONS: lethal cardiac arrhythmias (↑ K+), AKI
- TREATMENT: prevention (aggressive IV hydration, allopurinol/rasburicase, correct electrolyte abnormalities), dialysis

COMPLICATIONS

- Metastatic disease (bone &gt; liver &gt; lung &gt; brain)
- Recurrence
- Paraneoplastic: hypercalcemia of malignancy
- Treatment-associated: lymphoedema of the arm, lymphangiosarcoma, endometrial cancer from tamoxifen

PROGNOSIS

Prognostic factors: axillary lymph node status, tumour size, patient’s age, receptor status (ER/PR negative or triple-negative have poorer prognosis), histological grade and subtype (HER-2 is more aggressive)

PREVENTION

- Early-stage disease without lymph node involvement: 10-year survival rate: 70%
- Node-positive disease: high risk of recurrence
- Metastatic disease: 3-year survival rate of 48–71%
