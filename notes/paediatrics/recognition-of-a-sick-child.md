## Recognition Of A Sick Child


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Recognition Of A Sick Child

#### key learning outcomes

- Signs and behaviours which may indicate serious illness
- Triage levels
- ABC, Fluids In, Fluids Out
- Differences in infant/child care vs. adult care
- Clinical examination of a sick infant/child
- Importance of discharge and follow-up information

#### differences when caring for children

- Smaller, narrower airways- more susceptible to rapid airway obstruction
- Larger circulating blood volume per kg of body weight (80ml/kg)- relatively small losses have big consequences
- Good compensatory CVS systems- can mask serious hypovolemia until rapid decompensation
- High BMR and SA:V ratio- more prone to hypothermia and insensible fluid losses
- Immature renal function- lower ability to concentrate urine and conserve Na+ 🡪 rapid dehydration risk
- Weight and medication- accurate measures required as all meds and fluid calculations are based on weight
- Different composition of IV fluids to meet physiological needs
- Blood collection- tubes require less volume
- Excluding newborn period, all blood gas and serum electrolyte levels are the same as adults. Creatinine and Urea vary with age
- Psychological- shyness, fear of strangers. Show the child equipment and the tell what you’re going to do

#### ATS triage- INDICATORS FOR MORE URGENT TRIAGING

- ![](notes/images/paediatrics-clinical-practice-guidelines-anu/img001.jpg)
Age &lt; 3 months
- Temp: fever (&gt;39°C or &gt; 38°C if &lt; 3months), hypothermia
- Mechanism of injury: MVA, car vs pedestrian, penetrating injury, immersion, electrocution, burns
- Comorbidity: any chronic illness, prematurity
- Preceding events: apnoea, cyanosis, seizure, fluid input/output &lt; ½ normal
- Specific problems: testicular pain, bile-stained vomit, red currant jelly stool, ingestion, NV
- Rash: non-blanching petechiae or purpura
- Child protection and/or mental health issues

#### ABCFF

- Alertness/Arousal- AVPU, GCS, alert, drowsy, floppy, lethargic, irritiability, sleeping for long periods, response to stimulation
- Breathing- Apnoea, RR, Effort, Colour, Noises
- Circulation- Pulse, Colour, Bleeding/fluid loss, CRT
- Fluids In- Difficulty breastfeeding/sucking, Oral fluid type and volume (&lt;50% normal), mucous membrane (dry?)
- Fluids Out- Reduced urine output (&lt;4 wet nappies in 24 hours), anuria, excessive urination, vomiting (Coffee grounds? Bile? Blood?), stools (Blood? Mucous?)
- Other- pain (Wong Baker Faces Scale), rash (petichae/purpura), temperature, traume

#### Discharge of children from ED

- Parents understand illness and management
- Have resources, information sheet (written + verbal)
- Follow up with GP
- Know warning signs + access to transport/hospital
