## Febrile seizures


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Febrile seizures

- Definition: An event (3 months - 5 years) associated with fever, without evidence of intracranial infection or defined cause
- Aetiology- genetic, environmental, viral infection (herpes virus 6 most common, influenza, adenovirus, parainfluenze), bacterial infection less common,
- ClassificationSimple febrile seizure- generalised, lasts &lt;15 mins, not repeated in 24 hours, no previous neurological problems
- Complex febrile seizure- focal, lasts &gt; 15 minutes, repeated in a 24 -our period

- Clinical featuresHistory: fever then seizure then consciousness recovered &lt;30 minutes and ask about infectious disease, medications, immunisations, day care, surgeries, hospitalisations and family history.
- Examination: normal neurodevelopmental examination.
- Diagnosis: clinical

- PrognosisNo underlying developmental problems → No lasting neurological
- Mortality from febrile seizures is very rare
- Reoccur frequently: 32% will have additional febrile seizure, 75% within 1 year

- Risk Factors- Age &lt; 18 months, Duration of fever &lt; 1 hour before seizure onset, First degree relative previously had a febrile seizure, Temperature &lt; 40ºC
- Investigations- only if it will alter management Laboratory studies: FBC, acute phase reactants, blood culture
- CXR: If child has respiratory signs
- Lumbar puncture: Considered in young infant, toxic child, irritable child, complex febrile seizure, especially if already on antibiotic therapy
- Urine Culture: &lt; 3 months, toxic
- Non-toxic &gt; 3 months of age → Dipstick → Culture if showing leucocyte esterase/nitrites

- DifferentialEncephalitis
- Meningitis
- Epilepsy

- ManagementMost febrile seizures resolve by the time they have presented to hospital
- Evaluation: Simple vs complex, cause of fever
- To control current seizure: anticonvulsant – midazolam or lorazepam.
- To prevent recurrent seizure: antipyretic – paracetamol or ibuprofen.
- Long term antiepileptic after first febrile seizure not recommended due to potential adverse effects
- Tepid sponging is not appropriate. Unwrapping an overdressed child is appropriate
- Follow up the following day if discharged
