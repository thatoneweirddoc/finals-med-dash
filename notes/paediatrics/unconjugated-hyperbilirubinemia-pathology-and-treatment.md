## Unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia pathology and treatment


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia pathology and treatment

- ↑ preload bilirubin- ↑heme breakdown RBC, extravasation  (forcep/vacuum) with bruising, polycythemia, haemolysis
- Reduced metabolism- prematurity, hypoxia-infection, thyroid deficient, rarely (genetic abnormalities in hepatic enzymes)
- Increased post hepatic load- ↑entero-hepatic reabsorption in stasis (slow feeders), increased glucorindase in mother’s milk
- Clinical exam- skin in light (blanch with finger), jaundice progresses from the face – trunk – lower abdo/upper thighs – extremities, pallor, hepatosplenomegaly, bruising, cephalohaematoma, infection, weight loss 🡪 Do TcB check
- Investigations- SBR, rhesus/ABO incompatibility, FBC, G6PD screen, septic screen, conjugated bilirubin, serum albumin, LFTs
- Prolonged jaundice (&gt;2 weeks term babies, 3 weeks preterm)- Unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia- benign breast milk, thyroid tests, UTI; Conjugated hyperbilirubinemia- hepatitis, post-hepatic obstruction/biliary atresia
- Signs- Acute (weeks)- lethargy, poor feeding, alteration in tone, loss of reflexes, convulsion, death; Chronic (moths-years)- chorea, loss of upward gaze, sensorineural hearing impairment, mental retardation
- Hyperbilirubinemia poisons cells- interferes mitochondrial energy production, damages cell wall, reduces neurotransmission
- KERNICTERUS = bilirubin encephalopathy due to increased Hb, reduced albumin, decreased glucuronidisation, increased entero-hepatic circulation, decreased blood-brain barrier threshold
