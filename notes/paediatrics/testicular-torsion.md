## Testicular Torsion


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Testicular Torsion

- It is due excess tunica vaginalis and thus reduced fixation of the testis, causing medial/lateral rotation 🡪 vascular compromise/ischemia of the testes, threating their viability
- Incidence- bimodal (perinatal, early puberty)
- Presentation-onset of severe testicular/scrotal pain (&lt;12 hours), may radiate to lower abdomen, constant, N/V
- Examination- absent cremasteric reflex, affected testis tender, swollen, slightly elevated of because of shortening of the cord, oedematous; gangrene in 4-6 hours
- Differentials- epididymitis, testicular appendage torsion, testicular rupture, hydrocele, varicocele, systemic vasculitis, HSP, Kawasaki’s disease, testicular tumour
- Imaging- US when it doesn’t delay treatment (reduced testicular perfusion/twisting of spermatic cord)
- Management- surgical detorsion and fixation (SURGICAL EMERGENCY)
