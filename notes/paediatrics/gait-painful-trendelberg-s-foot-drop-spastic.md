## Gait- painful, trendelberg’s, foot drop, spastic


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Gait- painful, trendelberg’s, foot drop, spastic

| Painful | Normal gait= stance phase followed by swing phrase. The stance phase is shortened in an antalgic gaitFracture e.g. toddler’s fractureHistory: distressed toddler, rotational trauma, multiple presentations consider child abuseExamination: tenderness long shaft tibia, often no redness, swelling or bruising.  Investigation: x-ray lower limb – non-displaced spiral fracture long shaft tibia.Infectious process e.g. osteomyelitisHistory: sudden onset, hip most common, refusal to sit, stand, walk. Examination: tenderness, redness, swelling, refusal to weight-bearInvestigation: x-ray lower limb – soft tissue swelling, effusion, periosteal elevationMalignant process e.g. osteosarcomaHistory: gradual onset, knee most common, pain is worse at night.Examination: tenderness, lymphadenopathy, hepatomegaly, splenomegaly. Investigation: x-ray lower limb – soft tissue swelling, effusion, periosteal elevation. Osteomyelitis vs. osteosarcomaMimic each other on x-ray lower limb. Bone biopsy will differentiate.  Osteomyelitis: WBC/ESR/CRP high, joint aspiration, gram stain, culture and microscopy. Osteosarcoma: WBC/ESR/CRP normal, CT, MRI. |

| Trendelberg’s Gait | Downward pelvic tilt during swing phase caused by weakness or spasm in hip abductors, mostly the contralateral gluteus medius muscleWhile weight-bearing on the ipsilateral side, the pelvis drops on the contra-lateral side, rather than rising as is normal. With bi-lateral hip disease, this results in a waddling 'rolling sailor' gait with hips, knees, and feet externally rotated.Causes- Legg-Calve-Perthes, slipped capital femoral epiphysis, developmental dysplasia of hip, JIA involving the hip, muscle disease, neurological conditions (CP, spina bifida, spinal cord injury) |
| Foot Drop | Due to muscular (anterior/chronic compartment syndrome), neurological (fibular nerve/sciatic nerve irritation), anatomy (charcot foot) due to injury to dorsiflexors/neural pathways that supply themIn paediatrics, can be seen in cerebral palsy, Friedrich’s ataxia and hereditary spastic paraplegiaAnkle and foot dorsiflexion controlled by the tibialis anterior, extensor halluces longus &amp; extensor digitorum longusInvestigationsFasting BSL, Hb A1C, ESR, CRP, serium protein electrophoresis, BUN, creatinine, Vit B12Imaging of tibia/fibula/ankleU/S for hip/kneeMRI (?tumour/compressive mass lesion)Electromyography (?spasticity, MND, sciatic nerve palsy)TreatmentPharmocological- sympathetic blockers for painful paraesthesiasAnkle foot orthosis to provide toe dorsiflexion during swing phaseNerve stimulation- in hemiplegiaSurgical intervention |
| Spastic Gait | Stiff, foot-dragging with foot inversion as a results of CNS damage to the parts controlling movement and muscle toneSeen in upper motor neuron neurological disease (e.g., diplegic or quadriplegic cerebral palsy, stroke)Cerebral PalsyAbnormal development/damage to parts of the brain controlling movement, balance and postureRisk factors- preterm birth, twin, toxoplasmosis/rubella teratogens, head traumaNo cure- supportive, physiotherapy, medications to relax stiff muscles (e.g. botulinum, diazepam) |
