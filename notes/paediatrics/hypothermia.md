## Hypothermia


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Hypothermia

- Definition: Hypothermia occurs when T&lt;36.5°C (Mild when T36.0-36.5°C; Moderate=T32.0-36.0°C; Severe= T&lt;32.0°C)
- Why do babies lose heat so rapidly?↑surface area: body volume (thus heat loss: head production)
- Transepidermal loss-  ↑body mass: weight, skin is thin and permeable
- Immature skeletal muscle- ↓ability to shiver
- ↓thermal insulation- subcutaneous fat, body hair
- ↓ ability to move away from cold, additional warmth (e.g. drying skin),
- Immature vasomotor reflect- delayed/inadequate vasoconstriction response to cold
- Cold extauterine climate (intrauterine=38oC, extrauterine 28oC)

- Non-shivering thermogenesis from low white (energy storage, heat insulation) and brown fat (high mitochondria 🡪energy expenditure, heat expenditure). At birth, surge of catecholamines increase the metabolic rate of brown fat. Hypoxia and hypoglycemia predispose to hypothermia.
- Clinical features- Axillary temp &lt; 36.5oC, paled/mottled skin, cool/cyanotic extremities, prolonged peripheral CRT (peripheral vasoconstriction), mild (tachypnoea) 🡪 moderate (bradypnoea,↓LOC)🡪 severe (bradypnoea, apnoea, unconsciousness)
- Complications- heart/lung/kidney dysfunction; brain damage/death; impaired G&amp;D; hyperthermia
- Management- WHO 10 steps- warm delivery room, immediate drying, skin-to-skin contact, breast-feeding, bathing and weighting postponed, mother/baby together, warm transportation, warm resuscitation, training, education
