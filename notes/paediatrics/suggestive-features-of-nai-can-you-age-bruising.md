## Suggestive features of NAI- Can you age bruising?


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Suggestive features of NAI- Can you age bruising?

#### History

- Multiple visits to the hospital for trauma related injury
- Hx of untreated injuries, delay in seeking medical advice and increase concern about minor injuries
- Hx incompatible with physical findings, history vague, blaming other kids for the injury
- Hx of sibling abuse
- When did it occur, exact location, what happened, witnesses, previous Hx, developmental stage (gross motor in particular)

#### Physical Examination

- Signs of abuse and neglect: Child withdrawing or conversely very receptive of contact
- Patten of skin lesion:
- Bruises not on the front of the body and/or overlying bone
- Bruises that are unusually large or numerous
- Bruises that are clustered or patterned (patterns may include handprints, loop or belt marks, bite marks)
- Bruises that do not fit with the causal mechanism described
- Superficial InjuriesBruises: Pattern, location [medial aspect upper arm, ears, neck, feet, buttocks or torso (torso includes chest, back, abdomen, genitalia)]
- Burns: Patterns (irons, cigarettes), immersion burning (sparring of the buttocks)

Fractures: Younger child, multiple fractures on XR and bone scan (not bone dysplasia)
- Head injury: Fractures, bleeds (46% subdural), contusions, oedema
- Retinal bleeding
- Shaken Baby syndrome (exclude infections, leukaemia), It is caused by direct blows to the head, dropping or throwing a child, or shaking a child.
- Visceral injuries

NB:** **Consider disorders of coagulation

**Ageing Bruising-** It is hard to but can get an idea by assessing the colour- red  (0-2d)🡪 blue/purple (2-5d) 🡪 green (5-7d) 🡪 yellow (7-10d) 🡪 brown  (10-14d)
