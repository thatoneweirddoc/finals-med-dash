## Hirchsprung’s Disease and Chronic Constipation


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Hirchsprung’s Disease and Chronic Constipation

- Congenital aganglionosis of the bowel, leading to lack of effective peristalsis and leads to intestinal obstruction and chronic constipation)
- Aeitiology- single/multiple gene mutation (RET since gene mutation) of chromosome 10 🡪 non-functional protein that cannot transmit signals within cells
- Classification:Short: aganglionic segment includes the rectum and sigmoid colon.
- Long: aganglionic segment extends to between the hepatic flexure and the descending colon.
- Total: aganglionic segment extends to the terminal ileum.

- Clinical features- subjective fever, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation, FTT
- Red flags- bilious vomiting, explosive diarrhoea, no passage of meconium within 48 hours
- Investigation- contrast enema AXR (air-fluid levels, dilated proximal bowel), rectal biopsy (absence of ganglionic cells)
- Complications- GI perforation, bleeding, ulcers, enterocolitis
- Management- bowel irrigation/IVF/Abx (with enterocolitis), surgical excision of aganglionic section +/- colostomy
- Risks- water loss, nutrition loss, faecal incontinence
