## tics


### Paediatrics (ANU) — tics

- Sudden, repetitive, non-rhythmic movement or vocalisation involving discreet muscle groups
- Classified into motor (affecting discrete muscle groups) and phonic (involuntary sounds produced, not always vocalising)
- Simple vs complexSimple motor - Sudden brief meaningless movements; Usually one group of muscles; Blinking, head jerking, shoulder shrugging; Can vary to rest of the body
- Simple phonic- Any sound or noise; Throat clearing, sniffing, grunting
- Complex motor- Usually longer; Cluster of coordinated movements E.g. pulling at clothes, touching people, touching objects, imitating other peoples actions
- Complex phonic- Echolalia, repeating ones own words (palilalia), lexilalia (repeating read words), spontaneous speech of taboo words or phrases (coprolalia)

- Tics are not completely involuntary and can usually be supressed however patients often have increasing discomfort until tic is performed i.e. different from twitch – involuntary
- Tics may increase as a result of stress, fatigue, boredom, anxiety
- Exact reason to why they occur is not fully known- Believed to be failures in basal ganglia and frontal cortex?
- Medication generally not required; Behavioural therapy sometimes if tic is impacting on daily life
- Ritalin will make tic worse
- Need to differentiate from focal seizures 🡪 tics tend to be bilateral
- Tic DisordersTransient Tic Disorder- 5-25% of school age children; 1 + tics for 1-12months; Usually disappear within 4 months
- Chronic tic disorder- 1+ long lasting tics; Motor or vocal but not both; Onset must be before 18
- Tourettes Syndrome (spectrum disorder)- Most severe; Presence of both motor and vocal tics; Large rates of inheritance; Often associated with ADHD or OCD; Risperidone may be used; Condition can improve with maturity
