## Meningococci


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Meningococci

- Viral (more common) or bacterial (Neisseria meningitides/strep pneumoniae); Insidious/fulminant
- High risk groups- adolescents, kids &lt; 5
- Symptoms- headache, N/V, photophobia, stiff neck, LOC, non-blanching, petechial, purpuric rash, multiple organ failureIn neonates- fever, bulging fontanelle, irritable, lethargy, high-pitched cry, altered LOC, seizure apnoea
- In &gt; 3 months- fever, ↑HC, Kernig’s (can’t extend leg), Brudzinki’s (flexion at high and knee in response to neck flexion), photophobia, seizures

- Complications- septic shock, DIC, purpura fulminans, Waterhouse-Friderichsen Syndrome (septicemia, shock, purpura, adrenal hemorrhage), neurogenic pulmonary oedema, septic arthritic, conjunctivitis, pharyngitis, pericarditis, pneumonia
- Diagnosis- Clinical, CSF (GOLD STANDARD) for positive gram stain, blood culture (supportive), imaging (not routine)

- Treatment- ABCDEF, Antibiotics for 7-21 days (ampicillin + cefotaxime for N.meningitdis /HiB, vancomycin if suspect resistance or strep. pneumoniae), steroid therapy &gt; 3 months (dexamethasone), antibiotic prophylaxis to close contacts
- Prevention Subunit/conjugate vaccine for Strain and C at 12 months of age with HiB vaccine
