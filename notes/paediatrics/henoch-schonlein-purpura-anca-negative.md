## henoch schonlein purpura anca negative


### Paediatrics (ANU) — henoch schonlein purpura anca negative

- Systemic vasculitis due to deposition of IgA-containing immune complexes
- Often preceded by URTI: Mainly group A β haemolytic streptococci, plus mycoplasma, adenovirus, parvovirus, varicella, herpes simplex
- The rash is maculopapular, purpuric, palpable- symmetrically distributed, typically on buttocks, legs, extensor surfaces of arms and ankles; trunk is spared unless there is a history of trauma
- Other symptoms- fever, arthralgia (periarticular oedema), abdominal pain, glomerulonephritis (hematuria, proteinuria common 🡪 nephrotic syndrome, may progress to acute renal failure)
- IgA levels, C3 &amp; C4, ANCA (differentiate from SLE or ANCA positive vasculitis); Biopsy- IgA/C3 on blood vessel wall, IgA deposition in glomerular mesangium
- Treatment- usually resolves on its own; NSAIDS/prednisolone for analgesia; ACEi/ARB for renal involvement
- If there is renal involvement, recurrence within 6 months is ~97% and there is greater risk of persistent hypertension
