## Abdominal migraine


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Abdominal migraine

- Diagnosis of exclusion
- Consists primarily of abdominal pain (non-localised, dull, sore), nausea and vomiting
- Associated symptoms- discomfort, malaise, can have no headache. Symptoms completely go away between attacks.
- It is most common in children ages five-nine years old, but can occur in adults as well.
- Family history of migraines link
- Other conditions that should be ruled out to arrive at a diagnosis of abdominal migraine include: urogenital disorders, peptic ulcer, cholecystitis (gall bladder), duodenal obstruction, gastroesophageal reflux, Crohn’s disease, and irritable bowel syndrome. If there is any alteration in consciousness, seizure disorders should also be ruled out.
- Treatment- NSAIDs, anti-nausea medication and the triptans
