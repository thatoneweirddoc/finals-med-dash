## breath-holding


### Paediatrics (ANU) — breath-holding

- Common especially in children &lt;6yo. Most common in toddlers.
- Triggers- emotional distress, fright, minor accident 🡪 usually the biggest “clue” to suspect breath-holding
- Cause – unknown, change in usual breathing pattern, or bradycardia or both
- Natural course Apnoea and bradycardia occur (either suddenly or gradually) with cyanosis or pallor following
- The attack may terminate without loss of consciousness or progress, with the child become unconscious, limp, and sometimes briefly stiffening or jerking in response to the cerebral ischaemia
- Recovery is usually rapid
- Attacks usually cease by the third or fourth year of life

- Pathophysiology- not well understood, may have dysfunction in cardiorespiratory reflexes, iron-deficiency anaemia is an exacerbating factor
- Signs and symptomsCry and breath hold (stop breathing)
- Become listless and collapse or faint
- Lose consciousness
- Become pale or blue in colour
- Blue spells (cyanotic breath holding): are the most common. Emotional distress. They may cry or scream and turn red and then blue, especially around the lips. Child may become floppy and unconsciousness.
- Pale spells (pallid breath holding): Sudden fright or pain. Child may open their mouth as if to cry but no sound comes out, they may faint or lose consciousness and look very pale.
- May have reflex anoxic seizures 🡪 reflexing stiffening and jerking

- Treatment- lay them on their side, remove objects around them, self-resolving
