## mumps


### Paediatrics (ANU) — mumps

- ![](notes/images/crs-2-vaccines-and-preventable-diseases-anu/img003.jpg)
Mumps paramixa virus
- Mostly eradicated
- Transmission- droplet, direct contact, direct contact with saliva
- Pathogenesis- URT/Upper GIT/eye are primary sites of viral replication 🡪 spreads to local lymphoid tissue 🡪 distant sites e.g. testes, epididymis, CNS, pancreas, ovaries, parotid gland (95% symptomatic cases).
- TreatmentSupportive care (paracetamol, skin emollients, antihistamines, hydration)
- Aciclovir- moderate risk of severe disease
- Zoster immunoglobulin (ZIG)- when pregnant infection results in life-long immunity generally.

- Virus shedding in saliva begins around onset of symptoms until ~8 days
- Signs- 30% asymptomatic/mild URTI, parotid swelling in 95% (headache, malaise, myalgia, low grade fever, pain, discomfort) which decreases after 4-7 days; occasional submandibular/sublingual involvement
- Diagnosis- clinically initially, viral serology of saliva/CSF (IgM in acute, IgG using FT, ELISA)
- Investigations- ↓WBC, Normal/↑ESR/CRP, maybe ↑amylase
- Complications- meningitis, encephalitis, hearing loss, orchitis, oophoritis, pancreatitis, myocarditis, thyroiditis
- Treatment- Generally none, paracetamol,
- Prevention- Attenuated Virus; MMR at 12 months, MMRV at 18 months, no adverse effects
