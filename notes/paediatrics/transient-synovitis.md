## Transient synovitis


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Transient synovitis

- ![](notes/images/crs-6-child-with-a-limp-anu/img005.jpg)
Irritable hip: Acute inflammation of synovial membrane of hip joint, causing reactive arthritis
- Risk factors- cause unknown; related to trauma, bacterial/viral infection, higher interferon concentration, allergic reaction
- Pathophysiology- non-specific inflammation and hypertrophy of the synovial lining/membrane
- Self-limiting, most common cause of limp in children 🡪 4-8 years old
- History Acute onset of unilateral hip/groin pain (referred to anterior thigh/knee) in an otherwise healthy child
- Associated limp, antalgic gait or refusal to weight bear → Usually ambulatory
- 70% will have had a non-specific URTI 7-14 days prior to onset of symptoms
- Muscle spasms

- Examination Hip: Flexed, abducted or externally rotated if a significant effusion is present
- Often afebrile or have a low grade fever &lt; 38 degrees

- Diagnosis Diagnosis of exclusion or clinical diagnosis
- FBC, ESR, CRP, XRay should all be normal.
- US of the hip- May see joint effusion
- Must exclude septic arthritis [Kocher &lt;2]

- Treatment Symptomatic: Activity limitation and bed rest until the pain subsides
- NSAIDs and analgesics for pain relief
- Most children recovery completely within 3 weeks
- Can do US-guided joint aspiration if high clinical suspicion for septic joint

- Complications- Legg-Calve-Perthes, Coxa Magna. Hip Dysplasia
