## syncope


### Paediatrics (ANU) — syncope

- Most syncopal episodes happen at school, you may need to ring the school to get an eye witness account. Brief myoclonic jerks associated with syncope are common.
- Syncope- Transient brief loss of consciousness associated with loss of postural tone and spontaneous recovery
- Pre-syncope- Near fainting episode includes light headedness, dizziness, severe weakness, and blurry vision without loss of consciousness
- Exclusion criteria- Ill-appearing, History of heart disease or heart surgery, Significant neurological disease, or other comorbidity, Trauma preceding syncope
- Suggested approach
- Eyewitness history (NB medications) and physical examination (including BP, neurological)
- Cardiorespiratory monitoring for 30 min
- 12 lead ECG  - look for WPW, bundle-branch block, long QT (QTc &gt; 470ms), LVH ( &gt; 30 mm in V6)
- Consider pregnancy for post pubertal females
- Further investigations guided by history and examination
- If severe headache, consider neurological cause
- Red flagsSyncope with chest pain on exertion  or with swimming
- Increasing frequency of events
- ECG changes

- CausesLife-threatening-  heart disease e.g. Long QT, tetralogy of Fallot, myocarditis, cardiomyopathy, MI
- Vasovagal syncope
- Breath-holding spells
- Orthostatic hypotension
- Toxin exposure
- Hypoglycaemia
- ?Anaemia
- Arrhythmias (no- life-threatening)

- DisposalThe child may go home if symptoms have resolved and examination is normal
- If blood or other tests have been organised make sure the parents are able to access the results
- For recurrent episodes or significant parental concern, VMO paediatric referral is advised
