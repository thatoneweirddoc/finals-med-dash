## Rolandic Seizures (Benign Childhood Epilepsy with Centrotemporal Spikes on EEG)


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Rolandic Seizures (Benign Childhood Epilepsy with Centrotemporal Spikes on EEG)

- Age of onset: 3-13 years → 10-20% of all childhood epilepsies
- Most common seizure disorder of childhood; most children will out-grow (stops ~14-18)
- Clinical features:Most commonly simple partial seizure with Faxial Motor SymptomsArise from Rolandic/perisylvian sensorimotor cortex
- Facial numbness, twitching, guttural vocalisations, hypersalivation, drool, dysphasia

- 75% occur at night or on awakening (may go unnoticed)
- May have secondary generalized tonic-clonic
- Auras are common but unreported due to high incidence at night

- Investigations: MRI if atypical → Prolonged/frequent, status epilepticus, seizures when awake, atypical EEG, older age of onset, abnormal neurological exam
- Differential- other seizure disorders, other movement disorders, vasovagal syncope
- Diagnosis based on Hx and EEG to rule out anything else
- Treatment: Not usually required, often remit by 13, consider AED (valproate) if greater frequency
