## varicella (chicken pox)


### Paediatrics (ANU) — varicella (chicken pox)

- ![](notes/images/crs-2-vaccines-and-preventable-diseases-anu/img002.jpg)
 Varicella-zoster virus (herpes group of viruses)
- Ability to remain dormant in dorsal root ganglia
- Prevention (Vaccine)Live attenuated
- Free under NIPS; 18 month of age as MMRV; catch-up program for children 10-13 years

- PathophysiologySpread via direct contact with lesions/airborne spread
- Spreads to regional lymph nodes 🡪primary viraemic phase 🡪 Around days 4-6, spreads to liver/spleen/reticuloendothelial calls 🡪 Around day 9, secondary viraemic phase, mononuclear cells transport virus to skin/mucous membranes 🡪 classic vesicular rash (vesicles with high fluid levels)
- Patient’s infections prior to classic dermatological signs; infectious for at least 5 days until lesions are crusted over

- SignsFever, headache, fatigue, sore throat, tachycardia, pruritus
- Rash face to trunk to extremities. Macules develop into fluid-filled vesicles which scab.

- Diagnosis- clinical but can do PCR (most sensitive), direct fluorescent antibody testing, and latex agglutination (LA)/ELISA (positive for IgG for varicella; documents immune status0
- Complications- secondary bacterial infection (e.g. GAS, staph aureus), pneumonia, encephalitis, cerebellar ataxia, meningitis, intracranial vasculitis, hepatitis, congenital varicella syndromes (skin lesions, limb hypoplasia, neurological abnormalities when mothers infected between 8-20 weeks), neonatal varicella, herpes zoster, death (bacterial superimposition)
- Treatment-Supportive care (paracetamol, skin emollients, antihistamines, hydration)
- Aciclovir- moderate risk of severe disease
- Zoster immunoglobulin (ZIG)- when pregnant
