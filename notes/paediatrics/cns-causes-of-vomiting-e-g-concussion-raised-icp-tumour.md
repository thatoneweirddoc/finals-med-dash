## CNS Causes of vomiting e.g. concussion, raised ICP, tumour


### Paediatrics (ANU) — CNS Causes of vomiting e.g. concussion, raised ICP, tumour

#### Concussion

- Traumatically induced altered mental status with or without LOC
- Symptoms- Amnesia (retrograde, anterograde), Headache, Emotional lability, dizziness, visual disturbances, vomiting (especially if persistent), elevated ICP (worsening headache, increasing disorientation, changing LOC)
- Mechanisms of Brain Injury- coup (point of contact), contrecoup (opposite point of contact), rotational forces (shearing of axons)
- Management- cognitive and physical rest, not recommended to return to sports until complete clearing of symptoms, return of memory

#### raised icp

- Causes: head trauma, hydrocephalus, choroid plexus tumours, SOL, hepatic encephalopathy, cerebral oedema, pesudotumor cerebro
- Complications- reduced cerebral perfusion pressure, herniation of brainstem
- Symptoms- Cushing’s triad (widening pulse pressure, irregular breathing, bradycardia)
- Diagnosis- CT, MRI, LP
- Treatment- ICU, dexamethasone, CSF drainage, craniotomy (extreme)

#### Brain Tumours

- Most common types:Medulloblastomas - arise in cerebellum
- Astrocytomas - type of glioma starting in astrocytes
- Ependymomas - arise from cells lining ventricles
- Craniopharyngiomas - near pituitary glands

- Symptoms: headaches (in particular morning headaches), nausea, vomiting, macrocephaly, irritability, lethargy, drowsiness, personality changes, seizures, coma, vomiting
- Diagnosis- MRI
- Treatment- Surgery, Radiation therapy, Chemotherapy
