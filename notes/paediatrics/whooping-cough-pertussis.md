## Whooping Cough/pertussis


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Whooping Cough/pertussis

- Highly contagious, caused by gram-negative Bordatella pertussis
- Prevention- Vaccine at 2/4/6/18 months and four years; adult pertussis booster at 12-13 years. Women recommended to have vaccination when pregnant
- Droplet transmission
- Classic presentation divided into three stagesCatarrhal- Like a viral URTI (mild cough, coryza) for 1-2 weeks. Coryza remains watery, cough increases
- Paroxysmal (~2-8 weeks)- Coughing spells increase in severity 🡪 long coughs with little/no inspiratory effort 🡪 gagging, cyanosis, struggling for breath, sweating. Whoop noise due to forced inspiratory effort after cough attack. Postussive vomiting in 30-50%.
- Convalescent stage- cough subsides over weeks to months with episodic coughing + interval URTI

- Complications- apnea (almost exclusively in infants), pneumonia (most frequent), seizures, encephalopathy, death
- Diagnosis- clinical (2 weeks of cough with at least one characteristic feature of pertussis); can confirm with laboratory (PCR and culture of nasopharyngeal specimens, IgG to pertussis toxin if ≥4 months)
- Management- supportive, oxygenation, breathing treatments, mechanical ventilation as necessary
