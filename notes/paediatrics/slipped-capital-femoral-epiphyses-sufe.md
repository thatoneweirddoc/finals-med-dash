## Slipped capital femoral epiphyses/SUFE


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Slipped capital femoral epiphyses/SUFE

- ![](notes/images/crs-6-child-with-a-limp-anu/img004.jpg)
Displacement of upper femoral epiphysis from the femoral neck through the growth plate; caused by mechanical (obesity/femoral retroversion) or puberty (rapid epiphyseal growth)
- History Chronic/intermittent pain (often &gt; 3 weeks insidious onset) in the hip, increased with activity
- OR: Acute (sudden onset, severe sx, inability to weight bear)
- May present with thigh or knee pain, and altered gait
- Bilateral disease (20%), in unilateral disease, contralateral hip may be affected (30-60%)

- Examination
- Abduction and external rotation of affected leg when hip is passively flexed from extended position
- Stable slip: Able to walk or weight bear/Unstable slip: Unable to weight bear even with crutches
- Gait: Antalgic in unilateral, waddling in bilateral
- Moderate-severe: Atrophy of the upper thigh/gluteal muscles
- Anterior hip may be tender to palpation
- Diagnosis- Plain X-ray
- AP and lateral (frog-leg or cross-table lateral) views of both hips should be done
- Show posterior displacement of femoral epiphysis i.e. ice cream slipping of the cone
- Treatment Orthopaedic emergency
- Stabilisation: Of the physis with a single cannulated screw placed in the centre of the epiphysis
- Newer technique: Capital realignment (surgical) of the joint to restore near normal anatomy
