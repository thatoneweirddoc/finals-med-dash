## Pyloric Stenosis +/- electrolyte changes


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Pyloric Stenosis +/- electrolyte changes

- Definition: Hypertrophic [pyloric stenosis is due to progressive thickening of the circular muscle of the pylorus, which leads to gastric outlet narrowing
- Pathophysiology: Hypertrophy and hyperplasia of the pylorus (the 2 muscular layers – circular and longitudinal) 🡪 narrowing of gastric antrum 🡪can progress to near-complete obstruction of gastric outlet 🡪 stomach becomes markedly dilated🡪 forceful vomiting
- PresentationProgressively more forceful, projectile, non-bilous, blood-stained (10%) vomiting 🡪 becomes projectile
- Hungry after vomiting
- Weight loss/inadequate weight gain

- Examination- dehydration, low centiles on growth chart, Jaundice, gastric peristalsis, pyloric mass
- Prolonged vomitingHypochloremic metabolic alkalosis- loss of gastric HCl 🡪 chloride loss 🡪 impairs kidney’s ability to excrete HCO3-🡪prevents correction of alkalosis (EXAM Q)
- Hypokalaemia- decreased blood volume 🡪 high aldosterone levels 🡪 Na retention, K+ losses in urine
- Dehydration

- InvestigationsBloods- FBC, EUC, Acid-base, BSL, bilirubin (if jaundiced)
- Clinical- palpable thickened pylorus
- U/S- thickened pylorus muscle &gt;4mm, pyloric canal length &gt;17mm
- Upper GI contrast/Barium meal (delayed gastric emptying/narrow pyloric canal)

- ManagementElectrolyte abnormalities and dehydration
- NBM, IVF (maintenance and deficit), NGT if vomiting continues despite stopping feeds
- Repeat bloods 4-6hrly: UEC, Acid-Base and adjust for surgery
- Surgical: Pyloromyotomy or balloon dilation
