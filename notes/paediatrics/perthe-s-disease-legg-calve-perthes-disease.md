## Perthe’s disease/ legg-calve-perthes’ disease


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Perthe’s disease/ legg-calve-perthes’ disease

- ![](notes/images/crs-6-child-with-a-limp-anu/img001.jpg)
Idiopathic avascular osteonecrosis of the femoral head; occurring in ages of 2-13; M:F= 4:1
- Causes: disruption of blood flowSlipped Upper Femoral Epiphysis (SUFE)
- Coagulation abnormalities
- Trauma in the ‘predisposed’
- Synovitis of the hip
- Sickle-Cell Crisis

- History – insidious onset, majority unilateral, limp (worse with activity), pain (if present; in anteromedial thigh. knee) Trendelburg
- Examination- antalgic gait, leg length discrepancy, limited internal rotation and abduction of hip, atrophy of thighs/buttocks
- Diagnosis- bilateral hip x-ray, bone scan (↓ perfusion to femoral head)
- Treatment Non-Surgical – activity limitation, petrie casts (keeps hip in abduction/internal rotation), NSAIDs
- Surgical intervention- tenotomy (cut muscle) and/or osteotomy (cut bone) to redirect into socket. Done when &gt;50% of femoral head is damaged or unresponsive to non-surgical treatments

**4 Stages**

- Initial/avascular necrosis (several months)- inflamed area, early flattening of the top of the femoral head/fracture
- Fragmentation/resorptive (1-2 years)- resorption of dead bone; replaced with woven bone (softer) which is more likely to fracture. Femoral head has irregular, fragmented and flattened appearance
- Reossification (2-3 years)- bone density returns to normal
- Healed- Final shape reached, however femoral head can be enlarged, flattened, have a short/broad neck
