## urinary tract infection


### Paediatrics (ANU) — urinary tract infection

- Definition: A pure growth of &gt;108 CFU/litre of a single organism indicates infection on a midstream urine
- Infection of the urinary bladder (cystitis) and/or kidneys (pyelonephritis)
- Aetiology (KEEPPS)- Klebsiella, Enterococcus, E. coli, Pseudomonas, Proteus, Staphylococcus
- Risk Factors- urinary tract abnormalities (BUR, neurogenic bladder), uncircumcised males, repeated bladder catherisation, sexually active, toilet training
- Infants and young child: History- non-specific 🡪 fever, poor feeding, irritability, FTT, jaundice if &lt;28 days, vomiting
- Examination- toxic vs non-toxic, febrile, jaundice; look for external genitalia abnormalities (phimosis, labial adhesions), lower back signs of occult myelodysplasia (hair tufts -?neurogenic bladder)

- Older child:History- fever, dysuria, urgency, frequency, incontinence, haematuria, abdominal and/or flank pain
- Examination- Febrile, suprapubic/loin tenderness, palpable enlarged bladder or kidney,  HTN secondary to renal scarring from previously unrecognized or recurrent UTIs

- Investigations- SPA (preferred ), catheter specimens (growth &gt; 103 CFU/L), clean catch urine, midstream urine, dipstick urine test  (protein, blood, nitrite, and leucocyte esterase [WBC enzyme])
- ManagementAny child who is unwell or &lt; 6 months 🡪 IV antibiotics
- Blood culture and LP if &lt;4 weeks
- A shocked child will need fluid resuscitation
- Recommended antibiotics (IV) are gentamicin and benzylpennicilin

- Antibiotics- Infants and ChildrenTrimethoprim or
- Trimethoprim and sulphamethoxazole  or
- Cephalexin

- Imaging- renal ultrasound (?renal obstruction)
