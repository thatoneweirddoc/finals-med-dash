## Brain, hypoxia, hemorrhage and PVL


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Brain, hypoxia, hemorrhage and PVL

#### cerebral hypoxia

- Can progress to cerebral anoxia – complete depravation of oxygen 🡪 ultimately we want to avoid cerebral infarct
- Causes:Central Apnoea- Immaturity of respiratory centre  in brainstem results in apnoeic spells
- Hypotension – reduces blood supply to brain

#### Brain hemorrhage

- Delicate veins rupture in brain 🡪blood pools around brain 🡪 nerve cell damage
- Symptoms- anaemia, bulging fontanelles, high pitched cry, apnoea, seizures, feeding issues
- Imaging- head ultrasound
- Grading- Grade 1: Bleeding in small area of ventricle, Grade 2: Bleeding inside ventricles, Grade 3: Ventricles enlarging, Grade 4: Blood spreads around ventricles

#### Brain Periventricular leukomalacia (PVL)

- Damage to white tracts in cerebrum- Increased risk of Cerebral palsy, developmental delays
- Symptoms- Jerking, spasticity, Increased tone, Decreased power

#### Follow up

- Careful attention must be paid to development in first year of life- auditory, visual, neurodevelopmental
