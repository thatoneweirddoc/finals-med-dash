## Asthma


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Asthma

#### key learning outcomes

- Severity of an acute asthma episode
- Appropriate management of acute episode
- Reassess/respond timely to changes in child’s condition
- When to get senior medical review
- Criteria for discharge
- Information in an asthma management plan

#### overview

- History (Triad)- Cough, SOB, Audible Expiratory wheezeChest tightness, prolonged expiratory phase
- History and FHx of asthma and atopy
- Co-existing medical conditions

- Risk Factors: atopy, passive smoking, frequent bronchiolitis in infancy, prematurity
- Differentials: bronchiolitis, inhaled FB, pneumonia, transient infant wheezing, cystic fibrosis, sinusitis, milk aspiration, structural abnormality, cardiac failure with congenital heart disease, suppurative lung disease, recurrent non-specific cough (unresponsive to bronchodilator), acidosis
- Signs of respiratory distress:  marked tachy/bradycardia, dyspnoea, tachy or bradypnoea, apnoea, pallor, cyanosis, lethargy, restlessness, use of accessory muscles, nasal flaring, tracheal tug, intercostal or sternal recession
- Examination General inspection: Subcostal, intercostal, sternal or supraclavicular recession, tracheal tug (inspect and palpate), head bobbing, use of accessory muscles, nasal flaring, alertness, speech, structural abnormalities (kyphosis, scoliosis), hyperinflation, wheeze
- Obs + vitals: O2 sats RA (normally &gt; 95%), RR (20-30 for 1-5yrs, 15-25 for 5-10yrs, 15-20 for 10-16yrs)
- Chest auscultation air entry, wheeze, stridor, bronchial breath sounds, symmetry

- Most childhood asthma improves with age
- High risk: brittle asthma, Previous PICU admission, representing post D/C, Recurrent admissions, Co-existent congenital/chronic diagnosis, prematurity, Failure to respond to maximal treatment, Recent use of strong steroids

#### categories

- Infrequent Intermittent (70-75%)- isolated episodes of asthma from a few days to a week, symptom free between episodes, episodes ≥6 weeks apart, almost always triggered by an URTI, preventer not provided
- Frequent Intermittent (20-25%)- Intervals &lt; 6weeks apart, minimal/no symptoms between episodes, may benefit from a regular preventer (low-dose inhaled corticosteroid, leukotriene antagonist, often required on a seasonal basis)
- Persistent (5-10%)- Symptoms most days, disturbed sleep, early morning dyspnoea, exercise/activity intolerance, requires preventer therapy and regular review, known to paediatrician

#### assessment of severity

- Who needs to be admitted?Anyone needing oxygen: SaO2 &lt;95%

- Respiratory support (CPAP, intubation)

#### WHEN IS IT SAFE TO DISCHARGE?

- Bronchodilator needed 3rd hourly (or less frequently)
- Parents educated about asthma and follow up.
- Parents able to give medication effectively
- Written asthma education plan given
- Written bronchodilator weaning instructions given

##### Parent Education- 1. What is Asthma?

- Asthma is a chronic, reversible, inflammatory condition caused by the narrowing of the small air passages (bronchi) in the lungs. This happens when the air passages become swollen or inflamed.  Airway changes that occur are tightening of the smooth muscle, inflammation and mucus production
- Signs and symptoms of asthma- cough, breathlessness, prolonged expiratory phase and wheeze
- Discuss the differences between mild, moderate and severe acute episode of asthma versus the interval (background) pattern of asthmaInterval pattern- more than one disturbed night per week, difficulty participating in physical activities, or bronchodilator use on more than one day per week

- Common trigger factors in childhood asthma e.g. cold, stress, exercise
- Factors which can make asthma more difficult to treat (allergic rhinitis / sinusitis)

##### Parent Education- 2. Role of Reliever, Oral Steroids and Preventer Medications

- Role of reliever/oral steroids medications (acute management) and preventer medications (interval management)
- Acute Management- 4x4x4 Protocol: For use in the community where SABAs are the only available treatment. Four puffs, one puff at a time, wait four breaths after each puff, repeat after 4 minutes.
- Interval Medication Management (Preventer) Inhaled corticosteroids to prevent airway inflammation e.g. fluticasone
- Discuss the actions, potential side effects and need for spacer.

##### Parent Education- 3. Inhalation technique

- Spacer and puffer education is essential (or alternate devices)
- Demonstrate and check technique with device (use one device for all medications)
- Discuss cleaning and care of inhalation device and spacer
- Medication Delivery- Aerosol delivery to alveoli most effective via metered dose inhaler (MDI or puffer) and spacer. Only use nebulizer in severe or life threatening asthma

###### spacer

- ![](notes/images/paediatrics-clinical-practice-guidelines-anu/img009.jpg)
Small volume (until 4 years, with mouthpiece 4-6 years), Large Volume (6 years and older), Accuhaler, Autohaler Turbuhaler
- Spacer use- Prime with 6 puffs salbutamol before first use; Can be washed in warm soapy water; Rinse and re-prime before next use
- Shake the puffer and remove Cap
- Insert puffer into rubber outlet of spacer (opposite to mask)
- Place spacer mask over child’s nose and mouth, ensuring there are no gaps
- Push the puffer down once
- Keep mask on your child’s nose and mouth while they take 4-6 breaths (can look at the valve to see if they’ve taken breaths)
- Repeat until you have given all the puffs required

##### Patient Education-4. Discharge Planning

- Weaning planTo be given on discharge from hospital
- Details of medications to give over next few days and follow up
- Steroid WeaningOral prednisolone - Short course, 2mg/kg/dose (first dose only, max 50mg), Then 1mg/kg/day (3-5 days)
- Consider adrenal suppression with frequent doses (&gt;6 weekly) or long courses (&gt;10 days)

- Action plan Keep and update at each medical review
- Details of regular preventer (if needed), PRN bronchodilator and when to seek medical care

- Suggest follow-up with Local Family Doctor within 3-5 days of discharge
- Inform if follow-up is required with a Pediatrician and/or Respiratory Specialist

##### common exam qs

- Explain to a parent what asthma is
- Be able to identify the severity of an asthma and convey it to a patient
- Teach the parent how to use the spacer and prime it
- Management of mild, moderate and severe/life-threatening asthma
- Fill in an asthma management plan and weaning plan
