## Sore Throat


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Sore Throat

#### KEY LEARNING OUTCOMES

- Assessment of sore throat
- Symptoms that indicate a more serious condition
- Management
- D/C criteria

#### AETIOLOGY

##### Viral Pharyngitis

- Features- rhinorrhoea, cough (hoarse/croupy), conjunctivitis (?adenovirus), oral ulceration and vasiculation (?HSV, ?Coxsackie), skin rash
- Pathogens- Adenovirus, Rhinovirus, Enterovirus, CMV, Coxsackie, EBV, HSV, Parainfluenza, Infleunza A and B
- Stomatitis differentials- HSV, coxsackie virus, aphthous ulcers, monilial, trauma, bacterial

##### Bacterial Pharyngitis

- Group A Beta Haemolytic Streptococci (GAS) most common cause
- Complications- rheumatic fever, acute glomerulonephritis, particularly in indigenous children
- Modified Centor Score- patients &gt; 3 years, if score ≥ 3= antibiotics

#### cOMPLICATIONS

- Peritonsillar abscess- dysphagia, spasm of facial muscles, tonsil/uvula displacement to opposite side
- Obstructive apnoea- snoring, laboured breathing, apnoea (especially in sleep)
- Uvulitis- In strep infection, but also Hib, staphylococcus, pneumococcus

#### SERIOUS CONDITION?

Look for: stridor, respiratory distress, muffled voice, drooling, torticollis, asymmetric pharyngeal swelling, grey-white pharyngo-tonsillar membrane, bruising/petechial rash, toxic appearance, dehydration, lymphadenopathy &gt; 2cm, persistent fever &gt; 48 hours, trismus, hot potato voice

#### Differentials

- Epiglottitis (medical emergency)
- Peritonsillar abscess
- Diphtheria
- Leukaemia
- Severe tonsillar hypertrophy with EBV infection
- Retropharyngeal abscess
- Foreign body
- Toxic ingestion

#### MANAGEMENT AND TREATMENT

- Testing- Throat swabs, Rapid antigen testing
- Antibiotic Therapy- particularly  in GAS infection and high-groups (e.g. Indigenous)
- Symptomatic treatment- Paracetamol/Ibuprofen, Oral fluid intake
- Indications for Hospital Admission- airway obstruction, cannot tolerate oral fluids, presence of dehydration, toxicity (systemic sepsis), bruising/petechial rash
