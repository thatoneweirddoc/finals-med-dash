## GTC


### Paediatrics (ANU) — GTC

- Several motor behaviors: Generalized tonic extensionof the extremities lasting for few sec 🡪 Clonic rhythmic jerking movements (Lasts ~30 second-4 minutes) 🡪 Prolonged postictal confusion(sluggish, sleepy, confused) sometimes for hours
- Can have a focal/generalised onset
- AetiologyProvoked: reversible cause e.g. hypoglycaemia, hyperglycaemia, central nervous system infection, uraemia, hyponatraemia, hypernatraemia, toxins, medications, illicit substances.
- Unprovoked: irreversible cause i.e. epilepsy syndrome; normal neurological examination

- PathophysiologyProperties of neurones that lead to inappropriate hyperexcitability and hypersynchrony.
- Proposed mechanisms include the abnormal capability to generate intrinsic discharge bursts, increased glutamate activation and decreased GABA inhibition.
- Prevention: Provoked: avoid the cause.
- Unprovoked: sleep hygiene, avoid alcohol, avoid tobacco, avoid stress, adhere to medication.

- Clinical features: History: family history, previous central nervous system infectious or neoplastic disease or trauma, prior seizures, loss of consciousness, tonic and clonic movements, sensation of fear before the seizure, focal neurological symptoms before or after the seizure, for example, transient loss of motor or sensory function of ability to hear, see, speak or understand.
- Investigation: provoked – blood glucose, full blood count, lumbar puncture, urea and electrolytes, toxicology screen, head CT, head MRI; unprovoked – EEG, idiopathic generalised epilepsy if widespread bilateral distribution or focal onset epilepsy if focal unilateral distribution that spreads to the contralateral side.
- Differential diagnosis: vasovagal syncope – can present with convulsive activity after the loss of consciousness, however, the convulsive activity lasts less than 15 seconds and there are no neurological deficits; shorter duration; post-ictal not as significant   psychogenic seizure – can present with convulsive activity, however, the convulsive activity is waxing and waning and does not response to benzodiazepines;  EEG is normal.

- Management:
- Often self-limiting without intervention.
- Basic life support (DRSABCDE) and intravenous benzodiazepine.
- Identify and correct the cause if provoked and treat the cause if unprovoked.
- Outcome:

- Complication without treatment is status epilepticus and
- Complication with treatment is medication adverse effect or failure.
- Patient prognosis without syndrome: 60% seizure free one treatment.
- Patient prognosis with focal onset epilepsy: 50% seizure free one treatment.
- Patient prognosis with idiopathic generalised epilepsy: 70% seizure free one treatment.
