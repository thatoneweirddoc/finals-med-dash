## Viral causes of bruising


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Viral causes of bruising

#### viral haemorrhagic fever

- Severe multisystem syndrome- overall vascular system damage and body’s ability to regulate itself is impaired
- General causes- RNA viruses (w/ lipid envelope)
- Signs and symptomsDepends on the VHF agent- can be abrupt (filoviruses) or more gradual (arenoviruses)
- Fever, weakness, dizziness, muscle aches, severe diarrhoea (can be bloody) 🡪 potential shock, delirium, seizures, coma, death
- Bleeding under the skin, internal organs, bodily orifices 🡪 mouth, eyes, ears

- PathophysiologyMultiple cytokine activation🡪 Increased vascular permeability 🡪 flushing, conjunctival injection, petechial haemorrhages, accompanied with hypotension, shock, circulatory collapse

- Management- supportive, antiviral in some (e.g. Ribavirin), prevention/control difficult as there are no vaccines
- NSW VHF Contingency Plan- Crimean-Congo, Ebola viral disease, Lassa Fever, Marburg virus (life-threatening endemic to parts of Africa, the Middle East, Eastern Europe and Asia)

#### Ebola

- Severe haemorrhagic fevers- Only two members of this virus family have been identified
- Transmission- personal contact with infected body fluids, infected fruit bats/primates
- No known effective treatment – importance of transmission prevention thought isolation precautions
- Symptoms appear 2-21 days after exposure
- Fever, headache, diarrhoea, vomiting, stomach pain, unexplained bleeding or bruising, muscle pain
- A person is only contagious after Ebola symptoms begin
- After 21 days if an exposed person does not develop symptoms, they will not become sick with Ebola
- Management
- No vaccine or antiviral drug
- Symptoms and complications are treated as they appear
- People who recover develop antibodies that last for at least 10 years, possibly longer

#### others

- Filoviruses- Marburg, Ebola
- FlavivirusesFound in anthropods (ticks/mosquitos) and can occasionally infect humans
- Mosquito transmitted- Yellow Fever, Dengue Fever, Japanese encephalitis, Zika Virus (can cause microcephaly and Guillain-Barre Syndrome)
- Tick-transmitted- Tick-borne encephalitis

- Rhabdoviruses
