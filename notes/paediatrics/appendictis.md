## appendictis


### Paediatrics (ANU) — appendictis

- Acute inflammation and infection of the appendix
- Pathology- The lumen may obstruct from inspissated faeces, lymphoid hyperplasia, and/or parasites. Obstruction leads to overgrowth of bacteria and mucus secretion, which leads to oedema, venous congestion, ischaemia (gangrene), bacterial infection of the whole wall, peri‐ appendiceal inflammation and perforation
- History- most are atypical; anorexia, periumbilical pain 🡪 migration to RIF (colicky, intermittent pain), fever, vomiting
- Examination- can have positive Mcburney’s/Rovsing’s/Obturator/Iliopsoas sign, rebound tenderness, guarding
- Neonates- abdominal distension, vomiting, decreased oral intake, abdominal tenderness, sepsis, temperature instability, lethargy, irritability
- Young children (&lt;5 years)- fever, abdominal tenderness with rebound/guarding, vomiting, anorexia, irritability, grunting respiration, difficulty moving, abdominal distension, diarrhoea
- Investigations- ↑WBC, ↑neutrophils, ↑CRP, U/S (wall thickness &gt; 6mm, fixed luminal distension, faecolith, mass in RIF), CT
- Management- NBM, IVF, analgesia, IV antibiotics, appendectomy
- Complications- peritonitis, abscess
