## Red Flags for Malignancy


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Red Flags for Malignancy

**Common Types**

- Leukaemia
- Central nervous system tumours
- Lymphomas
- Neuroblastoma
- Soft tissue sarcomas
- Nephroblastoma (Wilms Tumour)

**Warning signs**

- Pallor + Bleeding- petechiae, purpura, unexplained bruises, persistent bleeding from mucocutaneous surfaces, anaemia
- Bone pain- non-localised, can wake child from night, limp
- Localised lymphadenopathy- Axillary/inguinal/cervical glands &gt;2cm, firm and non-tender; glands in supraclavicular area
- Eye changes- white reflex, recent quint, proptosis, loss/changes in vision, black eyes (CNS tumours, retinoblastoma, neuroblastoma, myeloid leukemia)
- Persistent fever/apathy/weight loss
- Unexplained neurological signs- headaches, early morning vomiting, ataxia, CN palsy, deterioration in school performance, focal convulsions, focal neurological deficits
- Unexplained mass- important sites are abdomen, testes, head, neck and limbs
- Breathing difficulty- repeated presentations of inspiratory stridor (unresponsive to croup/asthma treatment) 🡪 T-cell lymphoma 🡪 mediastinal mass which can cause SVC obstruction
