## Rubella aka ‘german measles’


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Rubella aka ‘german measles’

![](notes/images/crs-2-vaccines-and-preventable-diseases-anu/img004.jpg)

- Viral infection (rubivirus) characterised by rash, fever and lymphadenopathy
- Pathogenesis- Via inhalation of infectious particles 🡪 initial replication in nasopharyngeal cells and regional lymph nodes 🡪 in 5-7 days, viraemia. Contagious 1-2 weeks before clinically apparent until 15 days after rash onset
- Clinical features- Mild/asymptomatic, acute onset of maculopapular rash (pinpoint, pink, less red than measles, starts on face and spreads to body), mild non-exudative conjunctivitis, Forchheimer spots (enanthem on soft palate), acute thyroiditisSymptoms 1-5 days before rash- low-grade fever, sore throat, lymphadenopathy, cough, coryza, mild pink eye
- Longer duration, arthralgia, arthritis and more severe symptoms in teenagers and adults

- Diagnosis- Rubella-specific IgM antibodies
- Differential- Scarlet fever, measles, roseala
- Investigations- throat/urine/nasal specimens for viral detection via PCR
- ComplicationsPost-infection encephalitis, progressive rubella panencephalitis, hemorrhagic complications, congenital rubella syndrome (teratogenic, leading to fetal death, premature delivery, congenital abnormalities, cochlear hearing loss, developmental delay, growth retardation SGA with microcephaly, cataract, blindness, retinopathy)

- Management- Supportive (IV fluids, paracetamol, rest)
- Prevention-Live attenuated- MMR at 12 months, MMRV at 18 months
- Women screened for rubella antibodies when pregnant
