## bacterial causes of bruising


### Paediatrics (ANU) — bacterial causes of bruising

#### Meningococcemia

- Outcome of meningococcal infection 🡪 bacteraemia/septicaemia
- Signs and symptoms- prodrome of non-specific cough/headache/sore throat 🡪 URT symptoms, increasing temp, chills over next few days 🡪weakness, myalgia, headache, N/V, arthralgia, tachycardia, tachypnoea, hypotension, cool extremities
- Characteristic petechial skin rash (trunk + legs) may rapidly evolve into purpura
- Waterhouse–Friderichsen syndrome (WFS) AKA Fulminant meningoccemiaA haemorrhagic eruption, hypotension, cardiac depression, rapid enlargement of petechiae/purpuric lesions
- Due to adrenal gland failure due to bleeding into the adrenal glands

- Investigations- Blood culture, Meningococcal PCR, FBC, glucose, EUCs, LP (CSF for increased bacterial counts, low glucose, high protein, high WCC, ?PCR), skin biopsy (Gram stain and culture)
- TreatmentIV antiobiotics for 7 days- &lt;1 monoths: amp + cefotaxime, &gt;1months: ceftriaxone + penicillin
- For shock- Fluids 20ml/kg normal saline
- Supportive care- glucocorticoids (dexamethasone) for pneumococcal meningitis, protein C concentrate, fluids, O2

#### Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)

- Acquired syndrome characterised by haemorrhage and microvascular thrombosis; which can lead to end organ damage
- Caused by the systemic activation of the coagulation cascade by endothelial tissue damage (in sepsis, trauma, malignancies)
- Aetiology in older children/infants- sepsis usually as a complication of meningococcemia, trauma/tissue injury, malignancy, Kasabach merit syndrome, liver disease
- Aetiology in neonates- viral infections, systemic candidiasis, bacterial sepsis (e.g. GBS), TORCH infections, prematurity issues (NEC, respiratory distress syndrome), congenital disorders
- Risk factors for DIC in neonates- prematurity, low birth weight, low Apgar scores
- Lab findingsCoagulation factors and platelets- thrombocytopenia, prolonged PTT (extrinsic pathway activity)/aPTT (intrinsic pathway activity), decreased factor V/VIII levels
- Increased fibrin formation- low fibrinogen
- Increased fibrinolysis- D dimer increased, fibrin degradation products

- Treatment of underlying cause, replacement therapies
- Complications- Purpura fulminans, Organ dysfunction (vascular thrombosis, haemorrhage and hypoperfusion) 🡪 Renal failure, Hepatic dysfunction, Acute lung injury, Neurologic dysfunction, Adrenal failure

#### Purpura fulminans

- Acute life-threatening disorder most commonly following infection with Neisseria meningitdis but may also occur in varicella, GAS, Strep pneumoniae); result of microvascular thrombosis 🡪 tissue necrosis, skin infarction and haemorrhage.
- Causes- Congenital/acquired  protein C deficiency, Severe acute sepsis, Idiopathic
- Clinical featuresExtremely ill-appearing, fever, hypotension, bleeding. Lab evidence of DIC often present
- Erythema is followed by central areas of blue or black haemorrhagic necrosis with a surrounding erythematous border. lesions are painful and indurated.

- Treatment- treat underlying cause, fresh frozen plasma, protein C concentrate, surgical debridement of lesions, amputation of necrosed limbs/digits
