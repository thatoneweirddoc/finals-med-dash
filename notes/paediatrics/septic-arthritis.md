## Septic arthritis


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Septic arthritis

- Haematogenous spread via metaphyseal and epiphyseal circulation from pucture wound/infected skin lesions/adjacent osteomyelitis → Microbial agents in joint space →  cytokines 🡪 hydrolysis of proteoglycans and collagen 🡪 cartilage destruction 🡪 bone destruction
- Neonates often have co-existing SA and OM – usually only one joint affected
- Consider underlying illnesses e.g. immunodeficiency, sickle cell disease
- Most Common OrganismsNeonates: S. Aureus, E.coli, group B strep
- 2mths - 5 years: H. Influenzae and S Aureus
- Adolescents: N. Gonorrhoea

- Clinical featuresAcutely unwell, febrile, crying, excruciating pain, crying every time nappy removed
- Erythematous, warm, acutely tender joint with reduced ROM (pseudoparalysis),  joint effusion in peripheral joints
- Hip: Difficult in toddlers due to subcutaneous fat, may present with limp/knee painClassic sign → Flexed, abducted and externally rotated hip

- DDx - Transient synovitis (well, afebrile or mild fever, non-toxic), lyme disease, rheumatic fever, SLE
- InvestigationsLaboratory: FBC, CRP&gt;20, ESR, cultures, synovial fluid aspirate (WCC, gram stain, culture, glucose)
- Radiologic: XR (risk of fracture, widened joint, soft tissue swelling), U/S (hip effusions), MRI (osteomyelitis)
- KOCHER CRITERIA FOR SEPTIC ARTHRITIS (3 out of 4 = 93% chance of septic arthritis) Fever &gt; 38.5 C
- WBC &gt; 12,000 mm3
- Non-weight bearing on affected side
- ESR &gt; 40mm/h

- ManagementUrgent surgical drainage/washout
- IV Abx until CRP normalises, then oral Abx (total of 6 weeks) → Flucloxacillin, cephotaxen
- Splint/immobilise for 2-3 days, then encourage passive motion
