## RESPIRATORY PROBLEMS- HMD, CLD


### Paediatrics (ANU) — RESPIRATORY PROBLEMS- HMD, CLD

#### Hyaline Membrane Disease (HMD) AKA Respiratory distress syndrome

- PathogenesisDeficiency of surfactant 🡪 increased surface tension 🡪 difficulty to increase inspiratory pressure needed to inflate alveoli 🡪 atelectasis, low compliance, low functional residual capacity
- Mismatching of ventilation and perfusion 🡪 hypoxaemia (further contributions with R🡪L shunting) 🡪 respiratory/metabolic acidosis

- Clinical features- always nearly pre-term, respiratory distress, cyanosis, grunting, nasal flaring, recessions
- Investigations- X-ray for atelectasis (ground glass appearance, air bronchograms), low lung volume
- ManagementInitial- CPAP/intubation with mechanical ventilation, Fluid/metabolic management
- Preventative- Antenatal corticosteroids, assisted ventilation techniques (prevent atelectasis), endotracheal intubation and mechanical ventilation
- Surfactant therapy via endotracheal intubation
- Supportive- thermoregulation, fluids, CVS, nutrition

#### Neonatal chronic lung disease (CLD) AKA bronchopulmonary dysplasia (DBP)

- Chronic inflammation of the alveoli and airways with small airway narrowing
- Areas of atelectasis/hyper-expansion, increased airway secretions, altered lung architecture (distorted/decreased alveoli)
- Uneven airway obstruction leads to gas trapping and hyperinflation with abnormal distribution of ventilation
- Prone to lung infections, GERD, aspiration, systemic HTN, bronchospasm, progressive pulmonary HTN with or without cor pumlonale, poor growth, developmental delay and sensorineural disability
- Pathogenesis/risk factors- Prematurity, Mechanical ventilation (over-distend airways), oxygen toxicity, infection, inflammation, genetics, late surfactant deficiency
- Clinical features- Tachnypnoeic, Mild to severe retractions, scattered rates may be audible, Intermittent expiratory wheezing
- X-ray: clear lung fields, diffuse haziness and a coarse interstitial pattern (which reflect atelectasis, inflammation, and/or pulmonary oedema). Lung volumes are normal or low. In severe BPD – hyperinflation, streaky densities or cystic areas
- Cardiopulmonary function – decreased tidal volume, increased airway resistance, decreased dynamic lung compliance with increasing V/Q mismatch.
- Management- Respiratory support, mechanical ventilation, oxygen. nutrition, fluid restriction to avoid pulmonary oedema, diuretics (thiazide diuretics, loop diuretics), bronchodilators
