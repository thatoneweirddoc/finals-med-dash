## Unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia- normal physiology


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia- normal physiology

- Definition: yellowish discolouration of the skin/conjunctiva; becomes visible at serum bilirubin levels of 85-120µmol/L
- Physiology of Bilirubin Clearance/ Excretion:
- Hepatic Uptake- circulating bilirubin (bound to albumin) 🡪 liver
- Conjugation- in liver to a water-soluble form 🡪 excreted to bile (cannot be reabsorbed by intestinal mucosa)
- Biliary Excretion- In adults, bacterial enzymes break down conjugated 🡪 urobillin 🡪 excreted
- Aetiology: Neonatal Differences in Bilirubin Metabolism Increased bilirubin production - bilirubin production 2-3 x higher, more RBCs, shorter lifespan of foetal RBCs
- Decreased bilirubin clearance- relative hepatic uptake deficiency, deficiency in conjugating glucuronyl transferase
- Increase in entero-hepatic circulation of bilirubin- increased unconjugated bilirubin load 🡪 disposition

- Presentation of normal physiological jaundice- begins after 24 hours, peaks day 3, resolves ~ week1, healthy infant, slow rise in serum bilirubin &lt;85µmol/L daily (generally peaks at &lt;250µmol/L). It resolves in first 1-2 weeks after birth.
- Assessment- All babies should have hx, P/E and measurement of serum bilirubin level
- When to worry?Jaundice in the first 24 hours (always pathological, suggestive haemolysis)
- Persistence of hyperbilirubinaemia beyond 2 weeks of age
- Total serum bilirubin is &gt; hr specific 95th % according to the hour-specific Bhutani nomogram
- Rate of TB rise &gt;85µmol/L/day
- Conjugated bilirubin (suggestive of cholestasis)
- Jaundice persists for more than 14 days
