## Transient tachypnoea of the newborn


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Transient tachypnoea of the newborn

- A retrospective diagnosis implying failure of the baby to adapt to extra-uterine life- delay in clearance of normal alveolar excretion of fluid
- Presentation- within few hours of life 🡪 tachypnea, respiratory distress, increased O2 requirements (ABGs do not reflect CO2 retention)
- Risks- premature babies, elective C-section, breech delivery on unsuspecting babies who have not been permitted exposure to the normal surge of hormones, catecholamines and steroids that encourage the baby to prepare for birth
- Chest X-Ray minimal, but include peri-hilar streaking with fluid in interlobar spaces, due to engorgement of lymphatics (“wet lung” appearance). Should be performed to rule out. pneumothorax, congenital diaphragmatic hernia
- TTN usually settles in few days (24-48 hours; cot O2) but those needing more than 50% FiO2 🡪 early CPAP or ventilation
