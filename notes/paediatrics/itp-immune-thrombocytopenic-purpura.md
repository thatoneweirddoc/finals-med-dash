## ITP (Immune thrombocytopenic purpura)


### Paediatrics (ANU) — ITP (Immune thrombocytopenic purpura)

- Definition- Acquired autoimmune disorder; Antiplatelet Ab 🡪 Accelerated platelet destruction and/or impaired thrombopoiesis. It is C=Characterised by platelet count ↓ (&lt; 20 x 109/L) with normal Hb/ WCC
- AetiologyPrimary: Idiopathic autoimmune thrombocytopenic purpura; not associated with other causes/disorders)
- Secondary:  underlying causeSLE, APLS, AI hepatitis/thyroiditis
- Lymphoproliferative disorders: CLL, lymphoma
- Infections: HIV, hepatitis C, H pylori
- Myelodysplastic syndrome
- Hypo/agammaglobulinaemia
- Drugs: Heparin, penicillin, sulfamethoxazole, quinidin

- Clinical FeaturesAcute onset, between 2-5 years old, often preceding viral illness/live attenuated vaccine
- More likely to respond to therapy and resolve spontaneously
- Typical presentation: Petechiae, purpura, ecchymoses, bruising, epistaxis, gingival bleeding
- Examination: Non-palpable, non-blanching lesions, distal regions of extremities, spleen tip felt, possible gross hepatosplenomegaly
- Chronic ITP may have a more insidious onset of bruising/bleeding
- Investigations: Thrombocytopaenia (low platelets often &lt; 20 x 109 L, normal Hb (unless excessive bleeding), normal WCC; Marrow examination if suspecting malignancy

- ManagementConservative- Prevent bleeding while waiting for disease to spontaneously remit, resuscitation if required
- First line- IVIg, Anti-D in certain Rhesus+ children, Glucocorticoids (if excluded myeloproliferative disorders)
- Severe bleeding: Platelet transfusion, high-dose methylprednisolone, IVIg (IV Gammaglobulin)

- Prognosis- 80% (Spontaneous remission); 20% → Chronic disease → Consider splenectomy (&gt; 1yr duration, &gt; 5yo)
