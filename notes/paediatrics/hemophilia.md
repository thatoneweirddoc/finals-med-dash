## Hemophilia


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Hemophilia

- X-linked recessive disorder Carrier females may be affected (due to mosaicism)
- No father-son transmission; daughters of affected males have a 100% chance of being carriers

- Haemophilia A (Classical haemophilia)- absence/low factor VIII
- Haemophilia B (Christmas disease)- Deficiency of factor IX
- Factors VIII and IX crucial for thrombin generation via intrinsic pathway of coagulation. Thus haemophilia 🡪 ↓Thrombin 🡪delayed unstable clot formation 🡪 Easily dislodged 🡪 Excessive bleeding (severity and frequency)
- Clinical Features
- Spontaneous, recurrent, severe bleeding in unusual sites e.g. joints, muscles, subcutaneous
- Congenital haemophilia- MSK bleeding (hallmark), GI bleeding, hematuria
- Physical ExaminationNon-specific: Hypo activity, ↓oral intake, irritability, bulging/tense fontanelle, seizures and pallor
- Active bleeding, joint/extremity pain/swelling, decreased ROM
- Distended and painful abdomen
- Focal neurological deficits
- Excessive bruising or hematoma formation (lower extremities)
- GI or GU bleeding

- Complications- deep internal bloodying, joint damage, adverse reactions to clotting factor treatment, intracranial hemorrhage
- Investigations: FBC (rule out thrombocytopenia), LFTS, PT, APTT (increased in congenital haemophilia, Factor VIII/IX and/or Von Willebrand Factor studies), Imaging for GI bleeding
- ManagementClotting factor replacement- Recombinant factor VIII/IX infusions
- Bleeds requiring admission - Intracranial, neck/throat, compartment syndrome, iliopsoas hemorrhage, abdominal pain, persistent hematuria, severe pain
- Analgesia: Panadeine sufficient in most cases
- Do not use products containing aspirin or NSAIDS
- Lifestyle advice: Avoid contact sports, good oral hygiene, physiotherapy for joint/muscle bleeds
