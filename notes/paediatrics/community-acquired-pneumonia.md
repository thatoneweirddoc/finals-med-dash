## Community-Acquired Pneumonia


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Community-Acquired Pneumonia

#### overview

- Viral vs Bacterial Pneumonia- In viral, more insidious onset, bilater signs on auscultation, low-grade/absent fever, rhinoorhoea, sore throat, arthralgia, rash. Bacterial pneumonia usually begins with a shaking chill followed by high fever, cough and chest pain
- Atypical pneumonia- Wheeze is more often seen than in bacterial, and caused by Mycoplasma pneumoniae and Chlamydia pneumoniae
- Lower lobe pneumonia can present with abdominal pain and fever
- Differentials- bronchiolitis, URTI, cardiac failure, UTI, bacteremia, DKA

#### Investigations

- X-rays- poor indicators of aetiologic diagnosis
- FBC- Neutrophilia may occur in both bacterial and viral infection. Lymphocytosis is suggestive of pertussis. Leucopoenia may occur with a viral infection, overwhelming sepsis or Q fever
- EUCs- only of use in aiding IVI Fluids
- Blood C&amp;S- rarely positive in pneumococcus, vital to identify S Aureus
- NPA (add Pertussis if indicated)- useful in children less than 18 months, however 23% with a positive NPA have comorbid bacterial infection
- Sputum gram stain and culture- sensitivity for Strep pneumoniae
- Serology- Mycoplasma IgM appears 2-3 weeks after illness onset
- Ultrasound- useful in effusion
- Pleural fluid- US guided thoracentesis

14. MANAGEMENT OF PROCEDURAL PAIN (Prof Macdonald)

- Prof MacDonald’s Tip: EMLA takes 1 hour to work. It is usually best to put it on yourself so you can choose the veins.
- Best not to use IMI analgesia
- EMLA can be used in infants &gt; 3 months of age

#### Mild pain

- Paracetamol 15/mg kg (max 60/mg/kg day). Max dose 1 g
- OR ibuprofen 10mg/kg 6 or 8 hourly. Max 40 mg/kg/day or 400 mg dose

#### Moderate pain

- Oral oxycodone 0.1 mg/kg 6 hourly and Paracetamol 15/mg kg (max 60/mg/kg day). Max dose 1 g
- Oxycodone comes as a 1 mg/ml solution

#### Severe pain

- IVI morphine 0.1-0.2 mg/kg/IVI (requires O2 saturation monitoring)
- Intranasal Fentanyl -1-2 microgram/kg (repeat every 5 minutes. Max 4 mcg/kg or 75mcg). You should give by mucosal atomizer. Lasts only 30-60 minutes.

15. IVI FLUIDS (Prof Macdonald)

#### Children

- Maintenance-  0.9% NaCl &amp; 5% Glucose +/- 20 mmol/L KCl where there is risk of hypokalaemia
- Replacement- 0.9% NaCl &amp; 5% Glucose +/- 20 mmol/L KCl
- Resuscitation-  0.9% NaCl or Hartmann’s

#### Neonates (&lt;1 month corrected)

Day 1-3 10% Glucose

**Maintenance and Replacement**

- In SCN- 0.225% NaCl plus 10% glucose + /- 20 mmol/L KCl OR
- In EDs- 0.45% NaCl plus 10% glucose. No KCl in ED
- In Children’s Wards- 0.45% NaCl plus 10% glucose + /- 10 mmol/500 ml

**Resuscitation**

- 0.9% NaCl
