## What is the difference between possetting, projectile and reflux vomiting, and why do we worry about bilious vomiting


### Paediatrics (ANU) — What is the difference between possetting, projectile and reflux vomiting, and why do we worry about bilious vomiting

- Posseting- small amounts of milk brought back up; normal in small babies when stomach is full burping/swallowing air
- Reflux- stomach contents are regurgitated
- How to tell the difference from posseting?
- Reflux- discomfort while feeding (arching away/crying), vomiting, failure to put on weight, coughing, breathing problem
- Baby shows discomfort when feeding, such as arching away, refusing to feed and crying (reflux)
- Also may frequently vomit or spit up more than posseting
- Cough a lot including at night with no other sign of a respiratory illness
- Treatment- smaller feeds, keep baby upright after a feed, avoid tight clothing around baby’s stomach
- Complications- If reflux is very severe can result in GORD, or oesophagitis.
- What is projectile vomiting?
- Associated with gastric outlet obstruction
- Vomit that exits the mouth with such force that it is propelled over a short but significant distance.
- Nausea does not usually precede
- Causes-, stomach/bowel obstruction 🡪pyloric stenosis, Intussusception, Hirschsprung’s disease, Malrotation, foreign body injestion, raised ICP, overeating
- Why do we worry about bilious vomiting? Vomiting bile – presents as a bright yellow to dark green colour in vomitus.
- Suggests small bowel obstruction with or without abdominal distension.
- Requires the immediate involvement of a team of paediatric surgeons and neonatologists.
- NGT should be placed in immediately to decompress the stomach.
