## Seizures


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Seizures

#### key learning outcomes

- Summarise the ABCDE approach to initial assessment/management
- Prioritisation of management
- Medications used to manage
- Criteria for senior consultation

#### overview

- Definition: sudden attach of altered behaviour, consciousness, sensation or autonomic function produced by transient disruption of brain function. Most stop spontaneously
- Tonic (stiffening), Tonic-Clonic (Stiffening-jerking), Convulsion (motor accompaniments), Status epilepticus (&gt;5 minutes)

#### medical Management in an acute setting

![](notes/images/paediatrics-clinical-practice-guidelines-anu/img007.jpg)

- Midazolam or diazepam administered &lt;1hr prior to presentation should be regarded as ‘initial doses already given’
- After no response to two doses of midazolam, appropriate second line anti-epileptics should be introduced early (~ 20 mins)
- Step 1 (treatment given in the ambulance or at home should be counted as having been given as step one)- Midazolam, wait 5 mins. During the five minutes monitor the ABC, collect blood for BSL, and intervene as needed. If no IVI, do buccal/IM 🡪 Give buccal Midazolam by trickling dose over lower cheek and gum when patient is in recovery position (0.3 mg/kg)
- Step 2 (same as step 1)
- Step 3 (Maximum 20 minutes after arrival)- Phenytoin (60-80%), Leviracetam, or Phenobarbitone (respiratory depressant) for refractory status epilepticus. Mobilise anaesthetists during this time
- Step 4- Rapid Sequence Induction with thiopentone or profofol

#### Once seizure is terminated

- Position child in left Trendelenburg position
- Maintain airway (jaw thrust, chin lift, suction) and supply supplemental O2
- ECG monitoring
- Vitals – after each dose of anticonvulsant, every 15 minutes during seizure, every 30 mins after until normal consciousness
- History and examination- underlying cause, include b.p. (head injury, sepsis, meningitis, metabolic), current febrile illness, neuro state prior to seizure, trauma, Hx of epilepsy, poisons, allergies, medications, PMHx, immunisations
- Investigations- FBC, EUC, CMP, BSL, VBG,  blood culture, consider LP, cerebral imaging if focal seizure, BP
- Consider antibiotics if bacterial sepsis cannot be excluded
- Senior doctor review if compromised vitals, prolonged seizures, seizures continuing after 2 doses of benzodiazepeine, suspected serious underlying cause e.g. meningitis, metabolic abnormality, head injury
- Focused HxActivity over previous 24 hours/significant previous illness
- Current febrile illness
- Neurological state prior to seizure
- Recent trauma- ?NAI
- Current medications and allergies
- Recent immunisation
- History of epilepsy

#### Discharge criteria

- Criteria- full consciousness, clear management plan about recurrence
- Verbal and written education for care of child during seizure- seizre and epilepsy fact sheet, febrile convulsions fact sheetWhat is a seizure? by a short change in the normal electrical activity in the brain
- What is epilepsy? A condition with repeated seizures without fever or acute illness
- What is febrile convulsions? Caused by a short burst of abnormal electrical activity in the brain, caused by a fever
