## group b sepsis


### Paediatrics (ANU) — group b sepsis

- Group B Streptococcus- encapsulated gram +ve that colonizes GI and genital tracts of 15-40% of pregnant women
- Transmission- vertical transmission after labour onset/rupture of foetal membranes
- ClassificationEarly-onset GBS- within 24 hours- 6 days, primary Rx (maternal GBS colonization), sepsis/pneumonia/meningitis
- Late-onset GBS- 4-5 weeks, bacteremia without a focus/meningitis/focal infections

- Signs (subtle 🡪 septic shock), irritability, lethargy, poor feeding, temperature instability, tachycardia, poor perfusion, hypotension
- Common causes of both early and late-onset sepsis- GBS, E.coli
- Management- supportive care (ventilation, shock/ anaemia/seizure treatment, fluid/electrolyte) and anti-microbial therapy for 10 days (ampicillin + gentamicin; and penicillin g when causative organism identified)
- Prevention- Maternal screening, Intrapartum antibiotic prophylaxis (penicillin G via IV, most effective if administered at least 4 hours before delivery; continues ever 4 hours until infant is delivered), early onset
