## Infantile spasms


### Paediatrics (ANU) — Infantile spasms

- West Syndrome- Triad of infantile spasms, hysparrhythmia on EEG, mental retardation; occurs at 4-6 months of age
- Below age of 1, regardless of seizure type, Salaam spasms occur
- Signs and Symptoms:Violent flexor spasms of head, trunks, limbs (Salaam) 🡪 extension of arms (neck/trunk + limb abduction)
- 1-2 seconds of multiple bursts 20-30 spasms, often on waking but may occur many times a day
- Extensor spasms – sudden extension of neck and trunk with extension and abduction of limbs.
- May be misinterpreted as colic
- Mixed spasms most common (50%), then flexor (40%) then extensor (19-23%)

- Useful Hx: Deteriorating social interaction, growth delay (underlying brain injury), FHx of epilepsy
- Neuro exam: Abnormalities in consciousness, cranial nerve function, cognitive deficits, developmental delay
- Aetiology- many causes; tuberous sclerosis most common; believed to be due to abnormal interactions between cortex and brainstem, 2/3 have underlying CNS cause, frequent onset in infancy suggests role of immature CNS
- One theory: Reaction to stressors in immature brain → Abnormal excess secretion of corticotrophin releasing hormone → Spasms. Clinical response to ACTH and glucocorticoids which suppress CRH production
- ManagementAim for best QOL with no seizures and few adverse effects
- First line: ACTH, prednisone, vigabatrin, pyridoxine (pyridoxine is a rare treatable cause)
- Second line: Benzodiazepines, valproic acid, lamotrigine, topiramate, zonisamide, levetiracetam
- Surgical: Focal cortical resection

- PrognosisRarely have good prognosis
- Most will lose skills and develop learning disability or epilepsy
- Mental retardation (can be severe in up to 70% of patients) and autism spectrum
- Spasms may persist in adulthood (usually only 5 year) and 50-70% develop other seizure types
