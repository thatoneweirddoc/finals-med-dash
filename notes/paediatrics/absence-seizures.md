## absence seizures


### Paediatrics (ANU) — absence seizures

- Brief episodes of impaired consciousness with no aura or postictal periodTypically present as staring spells which often go unrecognized

- Typical absence seizures begin abruptly, last 5 to 15 seconds, and resolve without complication
- Stops suddenly → Staring, trance-like state → Unresponsive and unaware of surroundings
- Clinical Features: impairment of consciousness only, mild clonic components, atonic &amp; tonic components, automatisms, autonomic components
- Automatisms: Fumbling hand movements, eyelid fluttering, lip smacking, chewing motions
- Afterwards: Returns to normal, no memory of event and no lingering effects
- On EEG- Possible multiple spike-and-slow-wave complexes
- Generally precipitated by hyper-ventilation
- Atypical seizures tend to begin more slowly, last longer (few minutes), can slump/fall down
- May feel confused for a short time after regaining consciousness
- Clinical features: Changes in tone are more pronounced than those of typical, gradual onset, abrupt cessation
- May be traced to congenital/traumatic brain abnormality or metabolic renal/hepatic disorder
- Can be difficult to distinguish from complex focal → Absence usually are briefer and quicker recovery, and an EEG reading can help
- On EEG- Fast/paroxysmal activityGeneralised seizure part of:

- Childhood absence epilepsy → Onset 4-8yrs, secondary GTCS; good prognosis
- Part of Pyknolepsy – Brief episodes, multiple/day, lasting seconds
- Juvenile absence epilepsy → Adolescence onset; poorer prognenceDifferential- could be day-dreaming
- Investigations: EEG (diagnostic), MRI (usually not required, exclude structural cause)
