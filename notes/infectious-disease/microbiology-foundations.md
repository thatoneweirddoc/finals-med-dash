## Microbiology Foundations


### Infectious Disease Master Notes — Microbiology Foundations

#### 1.1 Gram-Positive Bacteria

Cocci — the clustering/chaining split is the first branch point:

| Organism | Arrangement | Key test | Notable disease |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Staphylococcus aureus* | Clusters | **Coagulase-positive** | Skin/soft tissue, endocarditis, osteomyelitis, toxic shock, food poisoning (preformed toxin) |
| Coagulase-negative staphylococci (*S. epidermidis*) | Clusters | Coagulase-negative | Line/prosthetic infections (biofilm) |
| *Streptococcus pyogenes* (Group A) | Chains | Beta-haemolytic, **bacitracin-sensitive** | Pharyngitis, cellulitis/erysipelas, necrotising fasciitis, rheumatic fever, PSGN |
| *Streptococcus agalactiae* (Group B) | Chains | Beta-haemolytic, bacitracin-resistant | Neonatal sepsis/meningitis (maternal screening) |
| *Streptococcus pneumoniae* | Pairs (diplococci) | Alpha-haemolytic, **optochin-sensitive** | CAP, meningitis, otitis media |
| Viridans streptococci | Chains | Alpha-haemolytic, **optochin-resistant** | Subacute infective endocarditis (dental procedures) |
| *Enterococcus* (faecalis/faecium) | Pairs/chains | Grows in bile/salt | UTI, endocarditis, intra-abdominal — **intrinsically resistant to cephalosporins**; VRE a major resistance concern |

**Gram-positive rods:** *Listeria monocytogenes* (neonatal/elderly/immunosuppressed meningitis, unpasteurised dairy, motile at room temp), *Clostridium* species (spore-forming anaerobes — *C. difficile* colitis, *C. perfringens* gas gangrene, *C. tetani* tetanus, *C. botulinum* botulism), *Corynebacterium diphtheriae* (pseudomembranous pharyngitis, exotoxin).

#### 1.2 Gram-Negative Bacteria

**Enterobacterales (the "coliforms"):** *E. coli* (commonest UTI/intra-abdominal pathogen), *Klebsiella pneumoniae* (UTI, pneumonia — mucoid, hypervirulent strains), *Proteus mirabilis* (**urease-producing → struvite stones**), *Salmonella*/*Shigella* (enteric), *Enterobacter*, *Citrobacter*, *Serratia* (nosocomial, AmpC-associated).

**Non-fermenters:** *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* (nosocomial, burns, CF, ventilator-associated pneumonia; intrinsically resistant to many agents — needs antipseudomonal cover), *Acinetobacter baumannii* (ICU, multidrug-resistant — CRAB a major concern ⚑).

**Fastidious Gram-negatives:** *Neisseria meningitidis* (meningococcal meningitis/sepsis, non-blanching rash), *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* (gonorrhoea), *Haemophilus influenzae* (epiglottitis, otitis media, COPD exacerbation — much rarer invasive disease since Hib vaccination).

#### 1.3 Atypical and Other Organisms

**Atypical respiratory pathogens** (no cell wall or intracellular — **don't respond to beta-lactams**): *Mycoplasma pneumoniae* (young adults, walking pneumonia, cold agglutinins), *Chlamydophila pneumoniae/psittaci*, *Legionella pneumophila* (water sources, **hyponatraemia, deranged LFTs** — legionella urinary antigen).

**Spirochaetes:** *Treponema pallidum* (syphilis), *Borrelia burgdorferi* (Lyme disease), *Leptospira* (leptospirosis — water/rodent exposure).

**Mycobacteria:** *M. tuberculosis* (see respiratory build for full TB detail), atypical mycobacteria (immunocompromised).

**Fungi:** *Candida* (mucosal/invasive, line infections), *Aspergillus* (invasive in neutropenia, ABPA in asthma/CF), *Cryptococcus neoformans* (HIV-associated meningitis), *Pneumocystis jirovecii* (HIV-associated pneumonia — Section 4).

#### 1.4 Host Defence and Sepsis Physiology

Innate vs adaptive — full immunology detail is in the immunology/allergy build; here the relevant point is the **inflammatory cascade in sepsis**: pathogen recognition (PAMPs via toll-like receptors) → cytokine release (TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6) → vasodilation, capillary leak, coagulation activation → organ hypoperfusion.

**Sepsis definitions ⚑ (Sepsis-3):**

- **Sepsis** = life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection — operationalised as a rise in **SOFA score ≥2** from baseline.
- **Septic shock** = sepsis + vasopressor requirement to maintain **MAP ≥65 mmHg** despite adequate fluid resuscitation, with **lactate >2 mmol/L**.
- ⚑ **SIRS criteria are no longer part of the formal sepsis definition** (too non-specific) but are still widely taught/used as a bedside screening trigger.
- **qSOFA** (bedside, no labs): RR ≥22, altered mentation, SBP ≤100 — ≥2 flags risk, prompts full assessment (not a diagnostic tool itself).

**Sepsis Six** (management bundle, first hour): give O₂, blood cultures, IV antibiotics, IV fluids; measure lactate, monitor urine output.

**Pathophysiology**

- Localized infection 🡪 bacteraemia 🡪 bacterial colonisation and thrombi formation on valve leaflets and chordae tendineaeValve destruction with ↓function
- Vessel occlusion/infarction from thrombo-emboli
- Immune complex against tissue antigens (GN, Osler Nodes)

- Mitral &gt; aortic &gt; tricuspid &gt; pulmonary

**Presentation**

- ACUTE IE:
- Acute onset with rapid progression (days to weeks) and ↑ constitutional Sx
- Most common: S. aureus (can be caused by GAS, S. pneumoniae, N.gonorrhoeae)
- SUBACUTE IE:
- Insidious onset with slow progression (weeks to months) and ↓ constitutional Sx
- Most common: Streptococci
- PROSTHETIC VALVE IE:
- Early (&lt;60d) vs. late (≥ 60d)
- S.epidermidis (early onset) or S.aureus (late onset)
- SYMPTOMS
- Constitutional: fever/chills, ↑HR, malaise, arthralgia, myalgia, cough, pleuritic chest pain
- Cardiac: murmur, CHF, arrthmias
- Mnemonic: Fever, Roth’s spots, Osler’s nodes, Murmur, Janeway lesions, Anemia, Nail-bed hemorrhages (i.e. splinter hemorrhages), Emboli
- Other: neurological (seizure, paresis), PE, splenomegaly, acute renal injury

**Duke Criteria**

- 1) 2 major, 2) 1 major + 3 minor OR 3) 5 minor
- Major diagnostic criteriaTwo separate blood cultures positive for typical pathogens
- Evidence of endocardial involvement in echocardiography (valve vegetations on TTE/TOE)
- A new valvular regurgitation (worsening of a pre-existing murmur is not sufficient)

- Minor diagnostic criteriaPredisposition (underlying heart disease or IV drug abuse)
- Fever ≥ 38°C
- Vascular (major arterial emboli, pulmonary infarcts, mycotic aneurysms, Janeway lesions)
- Immunologic disorder (GN, rheumatoid factor, Osler nodes, Roth’s spots)
- Microbiology

- Only negative findings on TOE can reliably rule out endocarditis, as TTE is not sensitive enough
- Tests: FBC (normochromic/normocytic anaemia), blood culture, leucocytosis (↑ESR/CRP), echocardiography , ECG (new AV block 🡪 abscess), CXR (HF, PE)

**Differentials**

RHD, SLE, prosthetic valve thrombosis, non-bacterial thrombotic endocarditis

**Management**

![](notes/images/infectious-disease-anu/img007.jpg)

- METHOD: 1. Blood cultures, 2. Immediate empiric AHx, 3. Adapttherapy according to culture
- EMPIRICAL THERAPYNative: IV vancomycin + penicillin G/gentamicin/ceftriaxone
- Prosthetic valves: vancomycin + gentamicin + rifampin + nafcillin
- IVDU: IV vancomycin + nafcillin/cloxacillin

- TARGETED THERAPY (4 weeks in native valve, 6 weeks in prosthetic valves)
- Staphylococci: vancomycin + nafcillin + gentamicin + rifampin
- Streptococci: penicillin
- Enterococci: penicillin/vancomycin + gentamicin/streptomycin
- HACEK:ceftriaxone + ampicillin
- PROPHYLAXIS in high risk patients undergoing procedures with ↑ risk of bacteraemia (amoxicillin)
- INDICATIONS FOR SURGERY: CHF, ↑ infection, systemic emboli, prosthetic valve, fungal

**Complications**

**CHF, conduction block, new emboli** (CND, retinal, pulmonary, coronary artery, renal, limb emboli), **Abx complications** (interstitial nephritis)

**Prognosis**

**Poor prognostic factors:** CHF, prosthetic valve infection, abscess, embolisation, persistent bacteraemia, altered mental status

**Presentation**

TYPICAL

- High fever and chills; malaise
- Productive cough with purulent sputum
- Pleuritic chest pain (which can project to epigastric region, particularly in children)
- Respiratory exam: ↑HR, ↑RR,  lung percussion dullness, coarse wet inspiratory crackles, bronchial breath sounds, ↑ vocal resonance/fremitus, ↓ chest expansion

ATYPICAL

- Low fever, no chills
- Non-productive, dry cough
- Dyspnoea
- Extrapulmonary: fatigue, headaches, sore throat, myalgias, eryhthema multiforme, auscultation often remarkable

**Investigations**

- BLOOD: ↑ESR, ↑CRP, BUN, leucocytosis, ↑procalcitonin, troponin/CK
- ABG: rule out respiratory failure
- SPUTUM/BLOOD CULTURE, PCR/ANTIBODY (chlamydia)
- URINE: legionella antigen, pneumococcal antigen
- CXR:
- Lobar pneumonia: extensive opacity in one lobe, air bronchogram, unilateral pleural effusion
- Bronchopneumonia: patchy infiltrates, air bronchogram
- Atypical/interstitial: diffuse reticular opacity, absent (or minimal) consolidation)
- Consider thorax CT, pleurocentesis, bronchoscopy

**CURB-65 Criteria for Hospitalisation**

- Confusion: disorientation, impaired consciousness
- Urea ≥ 20 mg/dL
- Respiratory rate ≥ 30/min
- Blood pressure: systolic BP ≤ 90 mm Hg or diastolic BP ≤ 60 mm Hg
- Age ≥ 65 years
- Interpretation
- Each finding is appointed 1 point; max. 5 points possible
- CURB-65 score ≤ 1: Outpatient
- CURB-65 score ≥ 2: Hospitalization
- CURB-65 score ≥ 4: ICU

**Treatment**

- GENERAL

- Rest with regular pulse oximetry monitoring
- Physiotherapy to encourage cough
- Supplemental O2
- IVF to avoid dehydration
- Medication: salbutamol, analgesia, antipyretics, mucolytics, anti-tussive (e.g. codeine)

- EMPIRICAL TREATMENT WITH ANTIBIOTICS

- Macrolide (azithromycin),
- Doxycline
- Fluoroquinolone (lecofloxacin, moxifloxacin)
- Antipneumococcal beta-lactam (amoxicillin ± clavulanate; ceftriaxone; cefotaxime)
- Antipseudomonal beta-lactam (tazobactam)
- Add vancomycin if MRSA suspected

**Complications**

Pleuritis, pleural effusion, empyema, sepsis, respiratory failure, ARDs, lung abscess

**Prevention**

Pneumococcal and influenza vaccine, smoking cessation
