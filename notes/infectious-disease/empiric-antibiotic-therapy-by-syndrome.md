## Empiric Antibiotic Therapy by Syndrome


### Infectious Disease Master Notes — Empiric Antibiotic Therapy by Syndrome

*The highest-yield section. Exact empiric agents vary by local antibiogram — the finals skill being tested is the **logic** (likely organism → severity → allergy/resistance risk → source control), not a single "correct" drug name.*

#### 3.1 The Empiric-Therapy Framework

Every empiric decision runs through the same four questions, in order:

1. **What organism(s) are most likely**, given the site of infection and how it was acquired (community vs hospital vs healthcare-associated)?
2. **How severe** is the presentation — does the patient need broad empirical cover now (sepsis, immunosuppression) or can narrower/oral therapy wait for cultures?
3. **What increases resistance risk** — recent antibiotics (last 3 months), recent hospitalisation, known colonisation (MRSA/ESBL), travel, care-home residence?
4. **Is there a source that needs physical control** — antibiotics alone will not cure an abscess, empyema, infected line, or obstructed/infected kidney.

Then **de-escalate**: narrow the regimen once culture and sensitivity results return — broad empirical cover is a bridge, not a destination.

#### 3.2 Respiratory *(full clinical detail — Respiratory build Section 7)*

**CAP:** severity-stratify with **CURB-65** (Confusion, Urea >7, RR ≥30, BP <90/60, age ≥65) — 0–1 = outpatient (amoxicillin ± macrolide if atypical suspected), 2 = consider admission (amoxicillin + macrolide), ≥3 = severe, IV broad-spectrum (co-amoxiclav/ceftriaxone + macrolide) ± ICU. Atypical cover added when Mycoplasma/Legionella/Chlamydophila suspected.

**HAP/VAP:** broader empirical cover reflecting nosocomial flora (Pseudomonas, MRSA, resistant Enterobacterales) — typically an antipseudomonal beta-lactam (piperacillin-tazobactam or 3rd/4th-gen cephalosporin), adding vancomycin/linezolid if MRSA risk factors.

#### 3.3 Urinary *(full clinical detail — Renal build Section 8)*

| Syndrome | Typical organism | Empiric logic |
|---|---|---|
| Uncomplicated cystitis | *E. coli* (commonest by far) | Short-course oral agent (nitrofurantoin, trimethoprim) — AWaRe Access-tier first-line |
| Pyelonephritis | *E. coli*, other Enterobacterales | Broader oral/IV cover (co-amoxiclav, ciprofloxacin, or ceftriaxone if severe) — needs tissue penetration, not just urinary concentration |
| Catheter-associated UTI | Broader flora, higher resistance | **Only treat if symptomatic** (asymptomatic bacteriuria in a catheterised patient is not treated except in pregnancy or pre-urological-procedure) |

⚑ ESBL-producing *E. coli* in UTI is rising (~15–20% in some populations) — if first-line therapy has failed or ESBL risk factors are present, escalate empirically rather than repeating the same class.

#### 3.4 Skin and Soft Tissue

| Syndrome | Organism | Empiric logic |
|---|---|---|
| Cellulitis (non-purulent) | Group A strep, *S. aureus* | Flucloxacillin (or cephalosporin/clindamycin if penicillin-allergic) |
| Purulent cellulitis/abscess | *S. aureus* (consider MRSA) | **Drainage is the treatment** — antibiotics adjunctive; add MRSA cover if risk factors |
| Necrotising fasciitis ⚑ | Often polymicrobial (Type I) or Group A strep (Type II) | **Surgical emergency** — urgent debridement, broad-spectrum antibiotics (piperacillin-tazobactam + **clindamycin** for toxin suppression) |

**Discriminator:** pain out of proportion to clinical signs, rapid spread, crepitus/skin necrosis, systemic toxicity → necrotising fasciitis; don't wait for imaging to call surgery.

#### 3.5 Intra-abdominal and Biliary *(full clinical detail — GI build)*

Typically polymicrobial (Gram-negatives + anaerobes) — empiric cover needs both (co-amoxiclav, or a cephalosporin/quinolone **plus metronidazole**). **Source control** (drainage/surgery) is as important as antibiotic choice — cholangitis and perforation are surgical/interventional emergencies first.

#### 3.6 Bone and Joint *(full clinical detail — MSK build Section 5.5)*

⚑ **Aspirate/culture before starting antibiotics** wherever possible (unless critically unwell). *S. aureus* commonest; empirical flucloxacillin (or vancomycin if MRSA risk); add ceftriaxone for *N. gonorrhoeae* in young sexually active adults.

#### 3.7 CNS *(full clinical detail — Neurology build Section 8.5)*

Bacterial meningitis — empirical therapy **must not be delayed** for imaging or LP in a septic/deteriorating patient: **ceftriaxone** (covers meningococcus and pneumococcus) ± **amoxicillin** (age <3 months, >50 years, or immunocompromised — to cover *Listeria*, which is cephalosporin-resistant) ± **dexamethasone** (reduces neurological sequelae, especially pneumococcal — give with or just before first antibiotic dose).

#### 3.8 Bloodstream/Endovascular *(full clinical detail — Cardiology build Section 6.1)*

**Sepsis of unknown source ("blind stab"):** broad empirical cover (piperacillin-tazobactam, or 3rd-gen cephalosporin + metronidazole) pending cultures and source identification, following the Sepsis Six bundle.

**Infective endocarditis (empirical):** typically vancomycin + gentamicin (or ampicillin/vancomycin + gentamicin per local protocol) to cover staphylococci, streptococci and enterococci; **three sets of blood cultures before starting antibiotics** wherever the patient is stable enough.

#### 3.9 Neutropenic Sepsis *(full clinical detail — Haem/Onc build Section 10.1)*

⚑ **Antibiotics within 1 hour**, before blood counts or imaging results are back — the exam's favourite "don't wait" scenario. Broad-spectrum antipseudomonal monotherapy (piperacillin-tazobactam) standard first-line; escalate per local protocol/MASCC risk score.

#### 3.10 Surgical/Antibiotic Prophylaxis

**Single dose** (or short course), given **within 60 minutes before incision** (timing is the exam point — too early or too late both reduce efficacy), chosen to cover expected flora (cefazolin for clean surgery, added anaerobic cover for bowel surgery). **Prolonging prophylaxis beyond the peri-operative window does not reduce infection risk** and increases resistance/C. diff risk.

##### Rapid-Recall Discriminators — Section 3

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| CAP severity score | CURB-65 |
| CAP with hyponatraemia + deranged LFTs + water exposure | *Legionella* — add atypical cover |
| Commonest UTI organism | *E. coli* |
| Asymptomatic bacteriuria in a catheterised patient | Don't treat (except pregnancy/pre-urological-procedure) |
| Purulent skin abscess — the actual "treatment" | Drainage (antibiotics adjunctive) |
| Pain out of proportion + rapid spread + crepitus | Necrotising fasciitis — urgent surgery |
| Antibiotic added for toxin suppression in nec fasc/TSS | Clindamycin |
| Intra-abdominal empiric cover needs to include | Anaerobic cover (e.g. metronidazole) |
| Before antibiotics in suspected septic arthritis | Aspirate/culture first (unless critically unwell) |
| Empirical meningitis antibiotic covering meningo + pneumococcus | Ceftriaxone |
| Added in meningitis if age <3mo/>50/immunosuppressed | Amoxicillin (Listeria cover) |
| Before starting antibiotics in suspected endocarditis | Three sets of blood cultures (if stable enough) |
| Neutropenic sepsis antibiotic timing ⚑ | Within 1 hour, before counts/imaging |
| Surgical prophylaxis timing | Within 60 minutes pre-incision, single dose/short course |
| ESBL UTI rising — what changes | Escalate empirically if risk factors/prior failure, don't repeat same class |

**Sources:** WHO AWaRe antibiotic book; NICE (CAP, UTI, meningitis, neutropenic sepsis, SSTI guidance); BNF; IDSA. Empiric agent choices illustrate the reasoning framework — defer to local antibiogram in practice.

### Infectious Disease (ANU) — empiric antibiotic theraPY — Empiric Antibiotic Therapy by Syndrome

- Choose antibiotics to cover for most likely and lethal organisms for the type of infection prior to obtaining laboratory results (usually reserved for serious infections)
- Adjust antibiotic(s) based on C&amp;S
- If causative organism identified, use antibiotic to which organism is sensitive
- If causative organism not identified, re-evaluate need for ongoing antimicrobial therapy (and continue with empiric antibiotics if indicated)
