## Sexually Transmitted Infections and HIV


### Infectious Disease Master Notes — Sexually Transmitted Infections and HIV

#### 4.1 Bacterial STIs

**The short version**
- Chlamydia is the often-asymptomatic one — doxycycline (azithromycin if pregnant/adherence concern); untreated → PID.
- ⚑ Gonorrhoea = single IM **ceftriaxone**; routine dual therapy with azithromycin is now less standard as resistance rises.
- Syphilis staging is the discriminator: painless chancre → palms/soles rash → latent → tertiary; benzathine penicillin throughout.
- Jarisch-Herxheimer hours after the first penicillin dose is cytokine release from dying spirochaetes, **not** allergy.

| Infection | Organism | Features | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chlamydia | *Chlamydia trachomatis* (intracellular) | Often asymptomatic; discharge, dysuria, PID if untreated | Doxycycline (or azithromycin if pregnant/adherence concern) |
| Gonorrhoea | *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* | Purulent discharge, dysuria; disseminated gonococcal infection (arthritis-dermatitis syndrome) | ⚑ **Ceftriaxone** (single IM dose) — resistance rising, dual therapy with azithromycin now less routine in many guidelines |
| Syphilis | *Treponema pallidum* | • Primary — **painless chancre**<br>• → Secondary — **rash incl. palms/soles**, condylomata lata, lymphadenopathy<br>• → Latent<br>• → Tertiary — gummas, cardiovascular, neurosyphilis (tabes dorsalis, general paresis) | Benzathine penicillin (single dose early, 3 weekly doses late/latent); **Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction** — fever/myalgia within hours of first dose (cytokine release from dying spirochaetes, **not an allergy**) |
| *Mycoplasma genitalium* | Atypical, intracellular | Non-gonococcal urethritis, increasingly recognised | Macrolide/quinolone depending on resistance |

#### 4.2 Viral STIs

**HSV** — genital ulcers, recurrent; aciclovir/valaciclovir for episodes and suppression. **HPV** — genital warts (low-risk types **6/11**) vs oncogenic types (**16/18** → cervical cancer); vaccination is primary prevention. **Hepatitis B/C** — sexually transmissible; full detail in the GI/hepatology build.

#### 4.3 Other

**Trichomoniasis** (*Trichomonas vaginalis*, protozoan) — frothy discharge, **"strawberry cervix"**; metronidazole. **PID** — ascending infection (chlamydia/gonorrhoea commonest), a clinical syndrome not a single organism — cervical motion tenderness, adnexal tenderness, fever; **empirical broad cover started on clinical suspicion, don't wait for swab results** (fertility risk from delay).

#### 4.4 HIV/AIDS

**Virology:** retrovirus, infects CD4+ T cells via **gp120** binding **CCR5/CXCR4** co-receptors; reverse transcriptase converts RNA to DNA for integration.

**Natural history:** seroconversion (2–4 weeks post-infection) — flu-like illness, often missed; high viral load, highly infectious → clinical latency (years, progressive CD4 decline) → AIDS-defining illness.

**Staging by CD4 count — the exam-favourite table:**

| CD4 count (cells/μL) | Risk |
|---|---|
| >500 | Usually asymptomatic |
| 200–500 | Bacterial infections, TB, shingles, oral candidiasis |
| **<200** | **PCP**, oesophageal candidiasis — AIDS-defining |
| **<100** | Toxoplasmosis, cryptococcal meningitis |
| **<50** | CMV retinitis, disseminated MAC |

**Diagnosis:** **4th-generation antigen/antibody combo test** (detects p24 antigen + antibodies — narrows the window period); confirmatory testing per local algorithm.

**ART classes:** NRTIs (tenofovir, lamivudine, abacavir — **HLA-B\*5701 screening before abacavir**), NNRTIs (efavirenz, rilpivirine), **integrase inhibitors** (dolutegravir, bictegravir — ⚑ now first-line backbone; high barrier to resistance, well tolerated), protease inhibitors (darunavir/ritonavir-boosted — less first-line now). **Standard modern regimen: 2 NRTIs + an integrase inhibitor.**

**PEP:** started **within 72 hours** of significant exposure, continued **28 days**. **PrEP:** tenofovir/emtricitabine for ongoing high-risk exposure.

##### Rapid-Recall Discriminators — Section 4

**The short version**
- Ulcer character sorts the STI: single painless → primary syphilis; palms/soles rash + lymphadenopathy → secondary; frothy + strawberry cervix → trichomonas.
- PID is treated empirically on clinical suspicion — swabs don't gate treatment.
- HIV testing = 4th-gen p24 antigen/antibody combo; CD4 <200 / <100 / <50 map to PCP, toxo + cryptococcus, CMV retinitis + MAC.
- ⚑ ART = 2 NRTIs + integrase inhibitor; HLA-B\*5701 before abacavir; PEP within 72 h for 28 days.

### Infectious Disease (ANU) — Urinary tract infection — Sexually Transmitted Infections and HIV

**The short version**
- UTI = symptoms **plus** pyuria/bacteriuria; 100,000 CFU/mL without symptoms is asymptomatic bacteriuria, not UTI.
- KEEPS organisms with *E. coli* ~80%; "complicated" is defined by the host/tract (abnormality, pregnancy, catheter, immunocompromise, pyelonephritis), not severity.
- Dipstick: leucocyte esterase + nitrites, pH >7 hints Proteus; culture reserved for complicated, >65, recurrent or suspected pyelonephritis.
- Fever or flank pain moves cystitis → pyelonephritis: IV fluids + IV antibiotics, and relieve obstruction (nephrostomy/stent).

### Infectious Disease (ANU) — Other systemic infections — Sexually Transmitted Infections and HIV

**The short version**
- Obligate/facultative intracellular cluster: *M. leprae* (leprosy), *Bartonella henselae* (cat scratch), *Rickettsia rickettsii* (Rocky Mountain spotted fever).
- Lyme = *Borrelia burgdorferi* (spirochaete) — erythema chronicum migrans, Bell's palsy, arthritis, cardiac shock.
- Toxic shock = *S. aureus* or GAS **superantigens** → mass T-cell activation and IL-1/IL-6/TNF release.

| LEPROSY (HANSEN’S DISEASE) | Mycobacterium leprae: obligate intracellular bacteria, slow-growing, survives in macrophages |
| --- | --- |
| LYME DISEASE | • Borrelia burgdorferi: spirochete bacteria<br>• Features: Bell’s palsy, arthritis, cardiac shock, erythema chronicum migrans |
| TOXIC SHOCK SYNDROME | Super antigens produced by some strains of S. aureus or GAS cause widespread T-cell activation and pro-inflammatory cytokine release (IL-1, IL-6, TNF) |
| CAT SCRATCH DISEASE | Bartonella henselae: intracellular bacteria |
| ROCKY MOUNTAIN SPOTTED FEVER | *Rickettsia rickettsii*: obligate intracellular gram negative organism |

#### HIV and AIDS

**The short version**
- Lymphotropic retrovirus: gp120 binds CD4, with CCR5 (macrophages) or CXCR4 (T cells) as the required co-receptor; RNA → DNA via reverse transcriptase, then integration.
- Transmission sexual (80%), parenteral, vertical; HIV-1 worldwide, HIV-2 almost confined to West Africa.
- Course: acute retroviral syndrome at 2–4 weeks (high viral load, highly infectious) → ~10 years latency with falling CD4 → AIDS-defining illness.
- Screen with combination p24 antigen/antibody test; confirm with HIV-1/2 differentiation immunoassay or Western blot; CD4 for staging, viral load for prognosis.
- cART classes: NRTI, NNRTI, integrase inhibitor, protease inhibitor, CCR5 inhibitor (maraviroc).

#### PYREXIA OF UNKNOWN ORIGIN

**The short version**
- 38.3°C for at least 3 weeks, still undiagnosed after 3 outpatient visits or 3 days of hospitalisation.
- Four buckets with rough weights: infection 20–40%, malignancy 10–30%, vasculitides 15–20%, miscellaneous 10–25%; 5–25% stay undiagnosed.
- Work-up: bloods/multiple cultures/PPD first, then **early CT chest-abdo-pelvis**; marrow and liver biopsy are low yield, laparoscopy and colonoscopy higher yield second-line.
- Stop unnecessary medications; a completely negative workup carries a good prognosis with fevers settling over months to years.
