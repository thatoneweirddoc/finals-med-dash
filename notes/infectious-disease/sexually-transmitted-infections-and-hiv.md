## Sexually Transmitted Infections and HIV


### Infectious Disease Master Notes — Sexually Transmitted Infections and HIV

#### 4.1 Bacterial STIs

| Infection | Organism | Features | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chlamydia | *Chlamydia trachomatis* (intracellular) | Often asymptomatic; discharge, dysuria, PID if untreated | Doxycycline (or azithromycin if pregnant/adherence concern) |
| Gonorrhoea | *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* | Purulent discharge, dysuria; disseminated gonococcal infection (arthritis-dermatitis syndrome) | ⚑ **Ceftriaxone** (single IM dose) — resistance rising, dual therapy with azithromycin now less routine in many guidelines |
| Syphilis | *Treponema pallidum* | Primary (**painless chancre**) → secondary (**rash incl. palms/soles**, condylomata lata, lymphadenopathy) → latent → tertiary (gummas, cardiovascular, neurosyphilis — tabes dorsalis, general paresis) | Benzathine penicillin (single dose early, 3 weekly doses late/latent); **Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction** — fever/myalgia within hours of first dose (cytokine release from dying spirochaetes, **not an allergy**) |
| *Mycoplasma genitalium* | Atypical, intracellular | Non-gonococcal urethritis, increasingly recognised | Macrolide/quinolone depending on resistance |

#### 4.2 Viral STIs

**HSV** — genital ulcers, recurrent; aciclovir/valaciclovir for episodes and suppression. **HPV** — genital warts (low-risk types **6/11**) vs oncogenic types (**16/18** → cervical cancer); vaccination is primary prevention. **Hepatitis B/C** — sexually transmissible; full detail in the GI/hepatology build.

#### 4.3 Other

**Trichomoniasis** (*Trichomonas vaginalis*, protozoan) — frothy discharge, **"strawberry cervix"**; metronidazole. **PID** — ascending infection (chlamydia/gonorrhoea commonest), a clinical syndrome not a single organism — cervical motion tenderness, adnexal tenderness, fever; **empirical broad cover started on clinical suspicion, don't wait for swab results** (fertility risk from delay).

#### 4.4 HIV/AIDS

**Virology:** retrovirus, infects CD4+ T cells via **gp120** binding **CCR5/CXCR4** co-receptors; reverse transcriptase converts RNA to DNA for integration.

**Natural history:** seroconversion (2–4 weeks post-infection) — flu-like illness, often missed; high viral load, highly infectious → clinical latency (years, progressive CD4 decline) → AIDS-defining illness.

**Staging by CD4 count — the exam-favourite table:**

| CD4 count (cells/μL) | Risk |
|---|---|
| >500 | Usually asymptomatic |
| 200–500 | Bacterial infections, TB, shingles, oral candidiasis |
| **<200** | **PCP**, oesophageal candidiasis — AIDS-defining |
| **<100** | Toxoplasmosis, cryptococcal meningitis |
| **<50** | CMV retinitis, disseminated MAC |

**Diagnosis:** **4th-generation antigen/antibody combo test** (detects p24 antigen + antibodies — narrows the window period); confirmatory testing per local algorithm.

**ART classes:** NRTIs (tenofovir, lamivudine, abacavir — **HLA-B\*5701 screening before abacavir**), NNRTIs (efavirenz, rilpivirine), **integrase inhibitors** (dolutegravir, bictegravir — ⚑ now first-line backbone; high barrier to resistance, well tolerated), protease inhibitors (darunavir/ritonavir-boosted — less first-line now). **Standard modern regimen: 2 NRTIs + an integrase inhibitor.**

**PEP:** started **within 72 hours** of significant exposure, continued **28 days**. **PrEP:** tenofovir/emtricitabine for ongoing high-risk exposure.
