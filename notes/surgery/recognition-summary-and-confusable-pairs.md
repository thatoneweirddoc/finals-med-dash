## Recognition Summary and Confusable Pairs


### Surgery Master Notes — Recognition Summary and Confusable Pairs

*A lookup layer over Sections 1–7. Every row is self-contained — no external context required.*

#### 8.1 Syndrome recognition — stem to diagnosis

| Stem signature | Diagnosis | Immediate next step |
|---|---|---|
| Anorexia, pain **migrating** periumbilical→RIF, RIF percussion tenderness | Appendicitis | US if child/young woman/pregnant, else CT; appendicectomy |
| RIF pain, flank tenderness, pain on passive right hip extension | Retrocaecal appendicitis (psoas sign) | Image → appendicectomy; not "renal colic" |
| Tender RIF mass after ~5 days, stable | Appendix mass/phlegmon | Non-operative; colonic imaging if >40 |
| LIF pain, fever, ↑CRP; CT sigmoid thickening, no abscess or free gas | Uncomplicated sigmoid diverticulitis | **No antibiotics** if well; colonoscopy 6–8 weeks |
| Sudden severe **constant** pain, board-like rigidity, lies still | Perforated viscus | Erect CXR (normal ≠ excluded) → CT → theatre |
| Epigastric pain then rigidity, ulcer history, free subdiaphragmatic gas | Perforated duodenal ulcer | Omental (Graham) patch + washout |
| Colicky central pain, **early** vomiting, distension, prior laparotomy | Adhesional SBO | NBM, NG, fluids + K⁺, CT; drip-and-suck ≤72 h |
| Obstruction: colic turns **constant**, fever, guarding, ↑lactate | Strangulation | Abandon conservative management — theatre now |
| Obstruction with **no previous abdominal surgery** | Non-adhesional SBO | Operate — do not trial conservative management |
| Elderly, institutionalised, constipated; coffee-bean loop **RUQ** | Sigmoid volvulus | Endoscopic decompression, then sigmoidectomy |
| Massive colonic dilatation, **no transition point**, unwell immobile | Ogilvie's | CT to exclude mechanical cause; correct K⁺/Mg²⁺; neostigmine |
| Iron-deficiency anaemia, weight loss, RIF mass, older adult | Right-sided colorectal cancer | Colonoscopy **and** gastroscopy; staging CT |
| Fresh PR bleeding, **tenesmus**, mass on PR | Rectal cancer | Colonoscopy + biopsy + **MRI rectum** (CRM) |
| Severe constant central pain, **soft non-tender abdomen**, AF | Acute mesenteric ischaemia, embolic | **Biphasic CTA now** — do not wait for lactate |
| Over 50: abdominal/back pain + hypotension ± pulsatile mass | Ruptured AAA | Unstable → **theatre, no CT**; permissive hypotension |
| Groin lump on standing, reduces lying flat, age 25 | Reducible indirect inguinal hernia | Elective tension-free mesh repair |
| Tender irreducible lump **inferolateral to pubic tubercle**, vomiting, distension | Strangulated femoral hernia + SBO | **Do not reduce**; resuscitate, emergency repair |
| Thin elderly woman, SBO, medial thigh/knee pain on hip extension | Obturator hernia (Howship–Romberg) | CT → surgery |
| Unwell, tender irreducible lump, **still passing flatus** | Richter's hernia | Resuscitate and operate |
| Day 6 post anterior resection: fever, tachycardia, new AF | Anastomotic leak | CT with IV ± rectal contrast; peritonitic → theatre |
| Day 8 post laparotomy: **salmon-pink serous discharge**, intact wound | Impending fascial dehiscence | Theatre for washout and mass closure |
| Wound **pain out of proportion**, dusky skin, crepitus, worse on antibiotics | Necrotising fasciitis | **Urgent radical debridement** + clindamycin |
| Post-op oliguria, suprapubic discomfort, palpable bladder | Urinary retention | **Bladder scan before the fluid bolus**; catheter |
| Post-op vomiting: Cl⁻ ↓, HCO₃⁻ ↑, K⁺ ↓, urine Cl⁻ <20 | Hypochloraemic hypokalaemic alkalosis | **0.9% saline + KCl**, not Hartmann's; give Mg²⁺ |
| Absent breath sounds, **hyper-resonant**, hypotension, distended neck veins | Tension pneumothorax | Needle decompression **now, no CXR**, then ICC |
| Absent breath sounds, **stony dull**, shock, **flat** neck veins | Massive haemothorax | Blood with ICC; thoracotomy per volume criteria |
| Head strike, **lucid interval**, falling GCS; biconvex clot | Extradural haematoma | Urgent non-contrast CT → neurosurgical referral |
| Hypotension, **bradycardia**, warm peripheries, after spinal injury | Neurogenic shock | Exclude bleeding; fluid then noradrenaline |
| Tibial fracture, opioid-resistant pain on passive stretch, pulses present | Compartment syndrome | Cast to skin, **limb at heart level**, fasciotomy |
| Sudden cold pale pulseless leg, AF, **normal contralateral pulses** | Embolic acute limb ischaemia | IV unfractionated heparin → Fogarty embolectomy |
| Burning forefoot pain lying flat, eased by hanging leg out | Chronic limb-threatening ischaemia | Urgent vascular referral; revascularise |
| Buttock/thigh claudication + erectile dysfunction + absent femoral pulses | Leriche (aorto-iliac occlusion) | ABI; image if revascularising |
| Tearing interscapular pain, >20 mmHg inter-arm difference, new AR | Aortic dissection | CTA; A → theatre; uncomplicated B → beta-blocker first |
| Hot red swollen **painless** foot, skin intact, redness settles on elevation | Acute Charcot | Total contact cast — not antibiotics |
| Ulcer at **medial gaiter area**, haemosiderin, pulses present | Venous ulcer | ABI first, then multilayer compression |
| **Painless** punched-out ulcer under metatarsal head with callus, diabetic | Neuropathic foot ulcer | Non-removable knee-high offloading; MDT service |
| Age 30, firm, smooth, highly mobile, painless breast lump | Fibroadenoma | Triple assessment (US + core); observe if concordant |
| Woman 40s–50s, large breast lump growing over weeks | Phyllodes | Wide local excision **≥1 cm**; no axillary surgery |
| Breastfeeding, fever and rigors, **wedge-shaped** erythematous segment | Lactational mastitis | Milk removal + di/flucloxacillin 500 mg q6h |
| Not lactating, >50, erythema ≥⅓ breast, peau d'orange | Inflammatory breast cancer | Mammography + US, **core + skin punch biopsy** |
| Unilateral scaly change **starting on the nipple**, spreading outward | Paget's of the nipple | Punch biopsy; treat underlying cancer |
| Day 1 post-thyroidectomy: perioral tingling, cramps, carpopedal spasm | Hypocalcaemia | Corrected Ca, **Mg**, PO₄, PTH; long QTc; calcium + calcitriol |
| Paroxysmal headache, sweating, palpitations on background hypertension | Phaeochromocytoma | Plasma free metanephrines; **alpha before beta blockade** |
| Changing pigmented lesion, or elevated firm growing nodule | Melanoma | **Excisional biopsy, 2 mm margin** — never shave or punch |
| Venous ulcer unhealed ~3 months, heaped rolled everted edges | Marjolin's ulcer (SCC) | **Biopsy the margin**, not the centre |

#### 8.2 Signature findings — the single feature that gives it away

| Finding | What it means |
|---|---|
| Tenderness ⅓ along ASIS–umbilicus line; LIF palpation gives RIF pain | McBurney's / Rovsing's — appendicitis |
| Pneumobilia + SBO + ectopic gallstone | Rigler's triad — gallstone ileus |
| Coffee-bean loop from pelvis pointing to RUQ | Sigmoid volvulus |
| Lines crossing the **full** lumen width | Valvulae conniventes = small bowel (incomplete haustra = large bowel) |
| Whirl sign, **absent mural enhancement**, pneumatosis, portal gas | Bowel ischaemia/strangulation |
| Transition point with collapsed distal bowel | Mechanical obstruction (none = ileus) |
| Free gas under the diaphragm, erect CXR | Perforated viscus — sensitivity only ~70–80% |
| Segmental thickening at splenic flexure (Griffiths') or rectosigmoid (Sudeck's) | Ischaemic colitis, watershed |
| Annular "apple-core" lesion | Left-sided colorectal carcinoma |
| Pink serous "salmon" discharge, intact-looking wound | Herald sign of wound dehiscence |
| Feculent or turbid brown drain output | Anastomotic leak (bilious = bile, milky = chyle, urine = ureteric) |
| CRP failing to fall by days 3–5 after colorectal surgery | Anastomotic leak until CT says otherwise |
| New AF or persistent unexplained tachycardia after bowel surgery | Earliest reliable sign of leak |
| Shoulder-tip pain, hiccups, basal effusion post-op | Subphrenic collection |
| Pain out of proportion, crepitus, bullae, worse on antibiotics; LRINEC ≥6 | Necrotising fasciitis (low score excludes nothing) |
| Fever within 24 h of surgery | Necrotising infection, malignant hyperthermia, transfusion |
| Fever in a patient on TPN | Line sepsis until proven otherwise |
| Hernia neck superomedial vs inferolateral to pubic tubercle | Inguinal vs femoral |
| Mass more tender on tensing the abdominal wall | Carnett's/Fothergill's — wall lesion, not visceral |
| Hypotension + distended neck veins + muffled heart sounds | Beck's triad — tamponade |
| Narrowed pulse pressure, normal systolic BP | Class II haemorrhage — earliest reliable marker |
| Hypertension + bradycardia + irregular respiration | Cushing's reflex — pre-herniation, not early warning |
| Ipsilateral fixed dilated pupil, contralateral hemiparesis | Uncal herniation (Kernohan's = false-localising) |
| Biconvex hyperdense clot **not crossing sutures** | Extradural haematoma |
| Crescentic collection **crossing sutures**, not the falx | Subdural haematoma |
| Tea-coloured urine, dipstick blood positive, no red cells | Rhabdomyolysis |
| Chvostek's and Trousseau's signs with long QTc | Hypocalcaemia |
| Orphan Annie nuclei, grooves, psammoma bodies on FNA | Papillary thyroid carcinoma |
| Hoarse breathy voice, weak "bovine" cough post-thyroidectomy | Unilateral recurrent laryngeal palsy |
| Pleomorphic microcalcification on mammography | DCIS |
| Tight woody tapered calf, "inverted champagne bottle" | Lipodermatosclerosis of venous insufficiency |
| Elevation pallor <20–30° plus dependent rubor | Buerger's — severe chronic limb ischaemia |
| Sharp cut-off with meniscus, little atheroma elsewhere | Embolic occlusion (tapered = thrombotic) |
| Hard gritty surface on blunt probe in a deep foot ulcer | Positive probe-to-bone — rules in osteomyelitis |
| Ketoacidosis at a near-normal BSL | Euglycaemic DKA — SGLT2i, or omitted basal insulin |

#### 8.3 Confusable pairs

| Pair | The discriminator |
|---|---|
| Extradural vs subdural haematoma | Extradural: young, middle meningeal artery, **lucid interval**, biconvex, **no suture crossing**. Subdural: elderly/anticoagulated, bridging veins, crescentic, **crosses sutures** |
| Tension pneumothorax vs massive haemothorax | Tension **hyper-resonant, veins distended**; haemothorax **stony dull, veins flat** |
| Neurogenic vs haemorrhagic shock | Neurogenic **bradycardic, warm** (sympathetic loss above T6); haemorrhagic **tachycardic, cool, clammy**. Assume bleeding |
| Direct vs indirect inguinal hernia | **Medial** to inferior epigastrics = direct (acquired, wide neck, rarely scrotal, low strangulation risk); **lateral** = indirect (congenital, narrow neck, may be scrotal) |
| Inguinal vs femoral hernia | Neck **superomedial** to pubic tubercle = inguinal; **inferolateral** = femoral. Judge by the neck, not the fundus |
| Incarcerated vs strangulated | Incarcerated **mechanical**, perfused. Strangulated **vascular**: constant pain, exquisite tenderness, no cough impulse — never reduce |
| Small vs large bowel obstruction | SBO **early vomiting, late constipation**, central loops, valvulae cross lumen, >3 cm. LBO **early constipation**, peripheral loops, >6 cm |
| Ileus vs mechanical obstruction | Ileus **quiet, continuous from surgery**, gas to rectum, **no transition point**. Obstruction **colicky, tinkling**, **transition point** |
| Adhesional obstruction vs strangulation | Simple: **colic with pain-free intervals**, soft abdomen. Strangulation: **constant** pain, fever, guarding, ↑lactate (both late) |
| Appendicitis vs mesenteric adenitis | Appendicitis: **migration**, anorexia→pain→vomiting, RIF percussion tenderness. *Sections 1–7 give adenitis no features (§3.1 only)* |
| Diverticulitis vs colorectal cancer | **CT cannot separate them** — hence interval colonoscopy 6–8 weeks after any CT-proven episode |
| Sigmoid vs caecal volvulus | Sigmoid **elderly, institutionalised**, coffee-bean **RUQ** → decompress, then sigmoidectomy. Caecal **younger**, caecum **LUQ**, SBO behind → right hemicolectomy |
| Acute mesenteric ischaemia vs ischaemic colitis | AMI occlusive SMA, **pain out of proportion**, soft abdomen, shocked → revascularise. Colitis watershed, **cramps then bloody diarrhoea**, well → conservative |
| Acute limb ischaemia — embolic vs thrombotic | Embolic **sudden**, AF, **no claudication**, **normal contralateral pulses** → **Fogarty**. Thrombotic gradual on known PAD, **contralateral pulses abnormal** → thrombolysis/bypass |
| Claudication vs CLTI vs spinal stenosis | Claudication **relieved by standing still**. CLTI **rest pain** worse flat, eased by dependency, ± tissue loss. Spinal needs **flexion/sitting** |
| Venous vs arterial vs neuropathic ulcer | Venous **medial gaiter**, shallow, haemosiderin, ABI >0.8. Arterial **punched-out, deep**, pulseless, **worse on elevation**. Neuropathic **painless**, plantar callus, ABI unreliable |
| Fibroadenoma vs phyllodes vs cancer | Fibroadenoma 15–35 y, <3 cm, mobile. Phyllodes **40–55 y, rapid, >4 cm** → excision ≥1 cm, no axilla. Cancer hard, fixed. **Most suspicious leg governs** |
| Lactational abscess vs inflammatory breast cancer | Abscess lactating, **febrile**, wedge erythema, improves by 48 h. IBC weeks-progressive, **afebrile**, ≥⅓ breast, peau d'orange, **antibiotic-unresponsive** |
| Paget's of the nipple vs eczema | Paget's **starts on the nipple**, unilateral, **no steroid response**, cancer beneath. Eczema **starts on areola, spares nipple**, steroid-responsive |
| Post-thyroidectomy hypocalcaemia vs RLN injury vs haematoma | Hypocalcaemia **12–48 h**: tingling, long QTc. RLN: **voice** — bilateral gives **stridor with near-normal voice**. Haematoma **hours**: expanding neck |
| Anastomotic leak vs ileus vs collection | Leak day 5–7: **tachycardia, new AF, fever**, CRP not falling → CT. Ileus quiet, afebrile — stubborn ileus is a leak. Collection **day 5+**: swinging fever |
| Atelectasis vs pneumonia vs PE as post-op fever | **Atelectasis does not cause fever** — hypoxia only. Pneumonia day ≥3: purulent sputum, consolidation. PE day 5+: pleuritic pain, hypoxia, new AF — **CTPA, not D-dimer** |
| Compartment syndrome vs acute limb ischaemia | Compartment: **pulses normal until late**, tense, agonising passive stretch → fasciotomy. ALI: **pulselessness early**, perishing cold → heparin, revascularise |
| Superficial vs deep partial thickness burn | Superficial partial: pink, blistered, **brisk refill**, very painful, ~2 weeks. Deep partial: blotchy red/white, **sluggish refill**, **blunted sensation**, grafted |
| Papillary vs follicular thyroid cancer | Papillary ~80%, **lymphatic to cervical nodes**, **FNA diagnostic**. Follicular ~10%, **haematogenous to bone/lung**, **FNA cannot diagnose** — needs lobectomy |
| Primary vs secondary vs tertiary hyperparathyroidism | Primary **↑Ca, ↑/inappropriately normal PTH, ↓PO₄** → surgery. Secondary **↓/normal Ca, ↑PTH** (CKD) → medical. Tertiary **↑Ca AND ↑↑PTH** → subtotal excision |
| Cellulitis vs necrotising fasciitis | Cellulitis pain **proportional**, **improves on antibiotics**. Nec fasc pain **out of proportion**, spreads over **hours**, toxic, crepitus, **worsens on antibiotics** |
| Mid-point of inguinal ligament vs mid-inguinal point | Mid-point of **ligament** (ASIS→pubic **tubercle**): deep ring 1.5 cm above. Mid-**inguinal** point (ASIS→**symphysis**): femoral artery |
| DCIS vs LCIS | DCIS **true precursor at that site**, microcalcification → excision ± RT. LCIS **risk marker, both breasts**, imaging-occult → surveillance |
| Acute Charcot vs foot osteomyelitis | Charcot **skin intact**, **redness resolves on elevation** → total contact cast. Osteomyelitis: wound, redness **persists**, ↑CRP → probe-to-bone, MRI |
| Type A vs type B aortic dissection | **A involves the ascending aorta** wherever the tear — surgery. **B does not** — uncomplicated: beta-blocker to HR ~60 **first**, then vasodilator |
| Rutherford IIb vs III limb ischaemia | IIb loss beyond toes with **weakness**, **venous Doppler audible** → immediate revascularisation. III **anaesthetic, paralysed, rigid** → primary amputation |

#### 8.4 Must-not-miss list

| Diagnosis | Feature that should raise it | Immediate action |
|---|---|---|
| Tension pneumothorax | Hyper-resonant hemithorax, hypotension, distended neck veins | Needle decompression before imaging, then ICC |
| Cardiac tamponade | Beck's triad; PEA arrest | Subxiphoid FAST → surgical decompression |
| Massive haemothorax | Stony dull hemithorax, shock, flat veins | Blood with ICC; thoracotomy >1500 mL or >200 mL/hr |
| Ruptured AAA | Abdominal/back pain + hypotension over 50 — mislabelled renal colic | Unstable → theatre, no CT; permissive hypotension |
| Type A aortic dissection | Tearing interscapular pain, inter-arm BP difference, new AR | CTA → emergency surgery |
| Acute mesenteric ischaemia | Agonising central pain, soft abdomen, AF or arteriopath | Biphasic CTA now — 6 h delay doubles mortality |
| Strangulated/closed-loop obstruction | Colic becoming constant with guarding, fever, ↑lactate | Resuscitate and operate |
| Anastomotic leak | Unexplained tachycardia or new AF after bowel surgery | CT with contrast; unstable → theatre unscanned |
| Necrotising fasciitis | Pain out of proportion, toxicity, deterioration on antibiotics | Urgent radical debridement + broad-spectrum incl. clindamycin |
| Post-thyroidectomy neck haematoma | Expanding neck swelling with distress and stridor | Open the wound at the bedside before intubation |
| Bilateral recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy | Stridor at extubation with a near-normal voice | Reintubate immediately; surgical airway if that fails |
| Extradural haematoma | Head strike, lucid interval, falling GCS | Urgent non-contrast CT + immediate neurosurgical referral |
| Intracranial bleed on anticoagulation | Any head strike on an anticoagulant, even at GCS 15 | CT regardless of decision rules; reverse without awaiting neurosurgery |
| Compartment syndrome | Opioid-resistant pain on passive stretch, pulses present | Release circumferential dressings, limb at heart level, fasciotomy |
| Acute limb ischaemia, Rutherford IIb | Paraesthesia plus weakness in a cold pulseless limb | IV unfractionated heparin; revascularise — imaging must not delay |
| Epidural haematoma or abscess | Ascending or non-regressing block, back pain, fever | Stop infusion, urgent MRI and neurosurgical referral |
| DKA in the fasted surgical patient | Nausea on an SGLT2 inhibitor, or T1DM missing basal insulin | Ketones, VBG, electrolytes whatever the BSL; VRIII with glucose/K⁺ |
| Inflammatory breast cancer | "Mastitis" not resolving on antibiotics, or non-lactating | Mammography + US, core plus full-thickness skin punch |

#### 8.6 High-yield reasoning chains

**1. Post-operative fever by day.** Days 0–2 cytokine response to injury; 3–5 catheter UTI then pneumonia; 5–7 SSI and, after bowel surgery, leak; beyond 7 collection or VTE; inside 24 h necrotising infection, malignant hyperthermia, transfusion. **Therefore** date the fever, inspect wound, cannulae and legs, send lactate and cultures; if bowel was joined, CT with contrast. Never "atelectasis": that causes hypoxia, not pyrexia.

**2. The acute abdomen by quadrant.** Visceral pain is midline by gut division (foregut epigastric, midgut periumbilical, hindgut suprapubic) and localises only once the parietal peritoneum is inflamed. **Therefore** migration is the key history item: RIF → appendicitis, LIF → diverticulitis or sigmoid cancer, epigastrium → perforated ulcer or ruptured AAA, diffuse → perforation or mesenteric ischaemia. βhCG in every woman of reproductive age.

**3. The anticoagulated head injury.** The Canadian CT Head Rule excludes anticoagulated patients — their risk is uncaptured and these bleeds evolve late. **Therefore** CT regardless of GCS, and reverse without awaiting neurosurgery: **idarucizumab 5 g** for dabigatran, **Beriplex (4-factor PCC) 50 IU/kg** for apixaban/rivaroxaban (⚑ andexanet alfa was removed from the ARTG on 20 May 2026), and **vitamin K 5–10 mg IV + Beriplex 50 IU/kg, without FFP**, for warfarin — ⚑ FFP was only required alongside the old **3-factor** Prothrombinex-VF, which Beriplex replaced in **June 2024**. Platelets are **not** routine.

**4. The burn fluid calculation** (80 kg, 30% TBSA, 1 h after injury). ANZBA: 3 mL × kg × %TBSA Hartmann's/24 h, clock from **injury**, half within 8 h. **Therefore** 3 × 80 × 30 = **7200 mL**; first half **3600 mL** over the remaining **7 h ≈ 514 mL/hr**; second half over 16 h **≈ 225 mL/hr**; urine target **40 mL/hr**.

**5. The groin lump.** The **neck** defines it — superomedial to the pubic tubercle inguinal, inferolateral femoral — and the inferior epigastric vessels separate indirect (lateral) from direct (medial). **Therefore** examine standing, find the neck, test cough impulse. Young man, reducible, asymptomatic → elective mesh repair; a femoral hernia is **never** watched; tender, irreducible, unwell → never reduce, theatre.

**6. The post-thyroidectomy patient.** Three complications compete, each with its own tempo. **Therefore** expanding neck with distress at **hours** = haematoma → open the wound at the bedside before attempting intubation; stridor at extubation with a near-normal voice = bilateral RLN palsy → reintubate; hoarse bovine cough = unilateral palsy; tingling and carpopedal spasm at **12–48 h** = hypocalcaemia → Ca, Mg, PTH, QTc.

**7. The ischaemic limb.** Six Ps in order; **paraesthesia and paralysis** mark a threatened limb, and muscle tolerates only ~4–6 h of ischaemia. **Therefore** IV **unfractionated** heparin (titratable, reversible before surgery), opioids, K⁺/CK/lactate, urgent referral. AF with normal contralateral pulses and no claudication = embolic → Fogarty; abnormal contralateral pulses with claudication = thrombotic → thrombolysis or bypass.

**8. The vomiting patient's acid–base picture and the correct fluid** (Na 138, Cl 92, HCO₃ 34, K 3.0). Gastric HCl loss strips H⁺ and Cl⁻; volume and chloride depletion force renal Na⁺ reabsorption in exchange for K⁺ and H⁺, wasting potassium and acidifying urine despite alkalosis (**paradoxical aciduria**, urine Cl⁻ <20). **Therefore** give chloride: **0.9% saline with potassium**, not Hartmann's.
