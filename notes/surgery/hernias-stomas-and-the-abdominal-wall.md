## Hernias, Stomas and the Abdominal Wall


### Surgery Master Notes — Hernias, Stomas and the Abdominal Wall

*Sources: HerniaSurge International Guidelines for Groin Hernia Management (2018) and BJS Open 2023 update, European Hernia Society, RACS/Choosing Wisely Australia, Therapeutic Guidelines: Antibiotic (Australian), WACHS Stoma Management Guideline and Australian stomal therapy nursing resources, standard surgical anatomy. Scope: groin and abdominal wall hernias, strangulation, stomas, enterocutaneous fistula, and the groin/abdominal wall lump.*

#### 4.1 Inguinal canal anatomy

**The short version**
- ~4 cm oblique canal running **inferomedially** from **deep ring to superficial ring**, above the medial half of the inguinal ligament.
- **Deep ring** = defect in **transversalis fascia**, ~1.5 cm above the **mid-point of the inguinal ligament** (ASIS→pubic tubercle). **Superficial ring** = slit in **external oblique aponeurosis**, **superolateral to the pubic tubercle**.
- **Hesselbach's triangle**: inferior epigastric vessels laterally, lateral border of rectus medially, inguinal ligament inferiorly.
- **Inferior epigastric vessels define everything**: sac **lateral** = indirect, **medial** = direct.
- Contents: **spermatic cord** (♂) / **round ligament** (♀), plus the **ilioinguinal nerve** in both.

| Wall | Composition |
|---|---|
| **Anterior** | • External oblique aponeurosis throughout<br>• Internal oblique reinforcing laterally |
| **Posterior** | • Transversalis fascia throughout<br>• Conjoint tendon and arching fibres **reinforce it medially above**, leaving bare transversalis fascia in **Hesselbach's triangle (medial and inferior)** — ⚑ **that is where direct hernias emerge** |
| **Roof** | • Arching fibres of internal oblique and transversus ("shutter mechanism") |
| **Floor** | • Inguinal ligament (in-rolled external oblique)<br>• Lacunar ligament medially |

Obliquity matters: at rest the rings do not overlie one another, and on straining the roof descends onto the floor and shutters the deep ring — so raised pressure alone rarely herniates intact anatomy.

⚑ **Mid-point of the inguinal ligament** (ASIS→pubic *tubercle*) = deep ring, 1.5 cm above. **Mid-inguinal point** (ASIS→pubic *symphysis*) = femoral artery. Two different landmarks; a standing trap.

**Contents.** In the **male**, the **spermatic cord** — rule of threes: 3 arteries (testicular, cremasteric, artery to vas), 3 fascial coverings (external spermatic, cremasteric, internal spermatic), 3 others (vas deferens, pampiniform plexus, lymphatics), plus the genital branch of genitofemoral and sympathetics. In the **female**, the **round ligament of the uterus**. In **both**, the **ilioinguinal nerve** — it enters through the lateral wall, not the deep ring, exits the superficial ring, and supplies medial thigh and root of penis/anterior scrotum or mons/labium.

The **genital branch of genitofemoral** enters via the *deep* ring (cremaster + scrotal/labial skin); **iliohypogastric** runs above the canal. These three are the nerves at risk at open repair (§4.3).

**Hesselbach's triangle** — **lateral: inferior epigastric vessels; medial: lateral border of rectus/rectus sheath; inferior: inguinal ligament.** (The original description used the pectineal line inferiorly; either is acceptable if stated consistently.) Its floor is bare transversalis fascia without the arching-fibre reinforcement above — hence **direct** hernias here.

#### 4.2 Hernia terminology

**The short version**
- **Reducible** → contents return. **Irreducible = incarcerated** → they don't, but perfusion is intact.
- **Obstructed** → lumen occluded (colic, vomiting, distension, absolute constipation); wall still perfused.
- **Strangulated** → **blood supply compromised**. Constant severe pain, exquisitely tender lump, systemic upset. Time-critical.
- **Richter's** = part of the bowel **circumference** only → ⚑ **strangulates without obstructing**; unwell patient still passing flatus with a soft abdomen.
- Incarceration is **mechanical**, strangulation **vascular**. All strangulated hernias are irreducible; most irreducible hernias are not strangulated.

| Term | Definition | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **Reducible** | Contents return spontaneously or with pressure | Elective pathway |
| **Irreducible/incarcerated** | Cannot be reduced (adhesions, narrow neck, volume) | Perfusion intact. ⚑ **Only *chronic, painless* irreducibility is non-urgent — an ACUTELY irreducible hernia cannot be told from strangulation clinically and needs emergency assessment and usually urgent operation** |
| **Obstructed** | Lumen occluded within the sac | Colic, vomiting, distension, absolute constipation |
| **Strangulated** | Venous then arterial occlusion → necrosis, perforation, sepsis | **Emergency**; never reduce |
| **Richter's** | Antimesenteric part of circumference only | Narrow-necked defects: femoral, obturator, port sites |
| **Littre's** | Sac contains a **Meckel's diverticulum** | May strangulate or fistulate |
| **Sliding** | A **retroperitoneal viscus forms part of the sac wall** (sigmoid, caecum, bladder, ovary) | Never open the sac blindly |
| **Maydl's** | "W" loop — the **intra-abdominal connecting loop** strangulates | Inspect inside the abdomen or you miss dead bowel |
| **Pantaloon / Amyand's** | Direct *and* indirect sacs straddling the vessels / sac contains the **appendix** | Repair whole myopectineal orifice / ± appendicitis |
| **Reduction en masse** | Reduced *with* its constricting neck | Still strangulated, now unseen — why force is dangerous |

#### 4.3 Inguinal hernia

**The short version**
- **Lateral to inferior epigastrics = indirect; medial = direct.**
- **Indirect** ~2/3, congenital (patent processus), any age, may reach the scrotum, narrow neck → higher strangulation risk.
- **Direct** = acquired transversalis weakness, older, wide neck, rarely scrotal, low strangulation risk.
- **Pubic tubercle**: inguinal neck **superomedial**, femoral neck **inferolateral**.
- ⚑ 2023 HerniaSurge **recommends laparo-endoscopic (TEP/TAPP) repair for primary unilateral hernia where expertise exists** — less acute and chronic pain than Lichtenstein; mesh for the majority.

| Feature | **Indirect** | **Direct** |
|---|---|---|
| Inferior epigastric vessels | **Lateral** to them | **Medial** to them |
| Route | Deep ring → canal → superficial ring | Straight through **Hesselbach's triangle** |
| Aetiology | **Congenital** — patent processus vaginalis | **Acquired** — fascial degeneration, raised intra-abdominal pressure |
| Age | Any; commonest in children/young adults | Older adults |
| Relation to cord | **Within** the cord | **Outside** the cord, pushes it aside |
| Into scrotum | **Yes** (inguinoscrotal) | Rarely — broad-based bulge |
| Neck | Narrow | Wide |
| Strangulation | **Higher** | Low |
| Deep ring occlusion test | Classically controlled by pressure | Classically still bulges medially |
| At operation | Sac from the deep ring, adherent to cord, dissected off vas/vessels | Diffuse attenuated transversalis bulge medial to the vessels, easily inverted |

⚑ **The deep ring occlusion test is unreliable** — accuracy against operative findings is little better than chance. Examination establishes *that* a hernia exists (sens ~0.75, spec ~0.96) but not reliably *which type* — and it doesn't matter, since a mesh repair covering the myopectineal orifice treats both. Know the test *and* the caveat.

##### Past exam stem — "25-year-old, groin lump appearing on standing, reducing on lying flat"

- **Reducible** (gravity-dependent, disappears supine) → no incarceration or strangulation → **elective** problem.
- **Age 25** → strongly favours **indirect** from a patent processus vaginalis.
- **Examine standing**: find the **neck** and relate it to the **pubic tubercle** (inguinal superomedial, femoral inferolateral); cough impulse; can you get above it (no in inguinoscrotal hernia, yes in hydrocele); testis palpable separately?
- **Investigations**: none routinely — clinical diagnosis. If equivocal (occult hernia, obesity, athletic groin pain), **ultrasound** first, CT/MRI for genuine doubt.
- **Answer: reducible indirect inguinal hernia → elective repair.** Watchful waiting is legitimate only for **asymptomatic/minimally symptomatic men**, and even then ~**50% cross over to surgery by ~7 years, ~68% by 10 years**, mostly for pain. A 25-year-old has decades of hernia-years ahead — repair.

**Risk factors.** Male; age; family history; previous contralateral repair; chronic cough/COPD; straining (constipation, BPH); heavy lifting; smoking and collagen disorders; prior lower abdominal incision; ascites/peritoneal dialysis; AAA. Low BMI for inguinal; obesity for incisional/umbilical.

##### Differential of a groin lump

| Diagnosis | Discriminator |
|---|---|
| **Femoral hernia** | Neck **inferolateral** to pubic tubercle; small, tense, often irreducible |
| **Lymph node** | Multiple, discrete, no cough impulse, not reducible; seek distal/systemic source |
| **Saphena varix** | Soft, bluish, **vanishes lying flat**, cough thrill, varicose veins; at the SFJ |
| **Femoral artery aneurysm** | **Expansile** (not merely transmitted) pulsation |
| **Psoas abscess** | Swelling below the inguinal ligament, spinal/hip pain, fever; TB or Crohn's |
| **Lipoma of the cord** | Soft, no cough impulse, irreducible; often an operative diagnosis |
| **Undescended/ectopic testis** | **Empty ipsilateral hemiscrotum** — always check |
| **Hydrocele of the cord** | Transilluminates, irreducible, moves with traction on the testis |

##### Management

- **Watchful waiting** for asymptomatic/minimally symptomatic men; emergencies are uncommon. Counsel crossover and red flags. ⚑ **Never** for femoral hernias, symptomatic hernias, or women.
- **Tension-free mesh repair** is standard.

| Approach | Preferred when |
|---|---|
| **Open Lichtenstein** | Local/regional anaesthesia needed (frail, cardiorespiratory disease); large inguinoscrotal hernia; hostile pre-peritoneal space (prostatectomy, pelvic radiotherapy, prior mesh); limited laparoscopic expertise; emergency groin approach |
| **Laparo-endoscopic TEP/TAPP** | **Bilateral**; **recurrence after open repair**; ⚑ **women** (avoids missing a femoral hernia, less chronic pain); early return to manual work; primary unilateral with expertise |
| **Non-mesh (Shouldice)** | Only with expertise and shared decision-making — mesh declined, contaminated field |

⚑ **Therapeutic Guidelines lists hernia repair "with or without prosthetic material" as warranting prophylaxis — cefazolin 2 g IV**, with metronidazole added only if bowel lumen entry is expected (Therapeutic Guidelines: Antibiotic; HerniaSurge) — reserve it for high-risk patients or contaminated fields.

**Complications.** Early: haematoma/seroma, urinary retention, wound infection, injury to vas/bowel/bladder, ischaemic orchitis (tender swollen testis days later, may end in atrophy). Recurrence low with mesh.

⚑ **Chronic post-herniorrhaphy groin pain (CPIP)** is the dominant modern outcome: **clinically significant in ~10–12%**, **debilitating in ~0.5–6%**. Neuropathic (nerve caught in suture, mesh or scar) or nociceptive (mesh inflammation, "meshoma", periosteal suture at the pubic tubercle). **Nerves at risk: ilioinguinal, iliohypogastric, genital branch of genitofemoral.** HerniaSurge recommends **identifying all three** at open repair; routine prophylactic neurectomy is **not** recommended, pragmatic resection of a damaged nerve is. Strongest predictors: pre-operative pain, young age. Treat with multimodal/neuropathic analgesia, blocks and pain service; neurectomy or mesh removal only in specialist units.

#### 4.4 Femoral hernia

**The short version**
- ~**3% of groin hernias**, ~**4× commoner in women** — but inguinal hernia is still the commonest groin hernia *in* women.
- ⚑ **Highest strangulation risk of any groin hernia — ~15–20%** — because the ring is narrow and rigid.
- Neck **inferolateral to the pubic tubercle**; often small, tense, no cough impulse — mistaken for a node.
- ⚑ **Never watchful waiting** — repair promptly at diagnosis; timely mesh repair, laparo-endoscopic where expertise exists.

The **femoral canal** is the medial compartment of the femoral sheath (~1.25–2 cm), containing fat and **Cloquet's node**, and exists to let the femoral vein distend. Its upper opening is the **femoral ring**:

| Border | Structure |
|---|---|
| **Anterior** | Inguinal ligament |
| **Posterior** | **Pectineal (Cooper's) ligament** |
| **Medial** | **Lacunar ligament** |
| **Lateral** | **Femoral vein** |

The sac descends through the ring, exits the **saphenous opening**, then turns **up and forwards over the inguinal ligament** — so a large one can appear to lie above it. **Judge by the neck, not the fundus.** Two ligaments and bone make an unyielding ring: hence the strangulation rate and the frequency of **Richter's** hernias here. ⚑ An aberrant obturator artery (**corona mortis**) — an obturator-to-external-iliac/inferior-epigastric anastomosis — crosses the **superior pubic ramus behind the pectineal (Cooper's) ligament**, and is at risk when the lacunar ligament is divided or tacks are placed on Cooper's ligament.

##### Past exam stem — "62-year-old man, tender irreducible groin lump, vomiting, abdominal distension"

- Tender + irreducible + **vomiting and distension** = **complicated groin hernia causing small bowel obstruction**; tenderness raises **strangulation**.
- **Most likely a femoral hernia**, despite the patient being male — complicated, obstructing, first-presentation groin hernias are disproportionately femoral (narrow rigid ring). Confirm the **neck inferolateral to the pubic tubercle**. Main alternative: incarcerated indirect inguinal hernia.
- **Immediate management** (in parallel, not sequentially): **A–E**, IV access, FBC/U&E/CRP/**lactate**/VBG, group & hold; **aggressive resuscitation** (profoundly volume-deplete from vomiting and third-spacing), catheter and urine output; **NBM + NG tube** on free drainage, analgesia and antiemetics; ⚑ **do NOT attempt reduction** (risks returning dead bowel, or *en masse* reduction with the ring); **IV antibiotics** covering enteric Gram-negatives and anaerobes per **Therapeutic Guidelines: Antibiotic**; correct electrolytes; VTE prophylaxis; **urgent surgical referral for emergency repair** (CT only if genuinely uncertain and it won't delay theatre); consent including **bowel resection and possible stoma**.
- **In theatre**: low (Lockwood) approach electively; **high/pre-peritoneal (McEvedy)** favoured in the emergency for bowel access. Reduce, assess viability, resect non-viable bowel, repair — mesh if clean, suture if contaminated.

#### 4.5 Other abdominal wall hernias

**The short version**
- **Paediatric umbilical hernias: >90% close spontaneously, most by 2 years and the great majority by 5** — repair at **4–5 years** if persistent, or if large/complicated. Incarceration risk very low.
- **Adult para-umbilical** hernias never resolve, have a narrow fibrous neck → repair.
- **Incisional hernia** is the commonest iatrogenic hernia; **wound dehiscence** is its acute in-hospital counterpart.
- **Obturator hernia** — thin elderly multiparous woman with SBO and the **Howship–Romberg sign**; diagnosed on **CT**.
- **Divarication of recti is not a hernia** — stretched linea alba, no fascial defect, no neck, cannot strangulate.

| Hernia | Features |
|---|---|
| **Umbilical (paediatric)** | Through the umbilical ring; preterm, Afro-Caribbean, trisomy 21, congenital hypothyroidism. **>90% close by 5 y**; **<1 cm** almost always closes, **≥1.5 cm** often doesn't. ⚑ Don't refer routinely before 4–5 y; taping is ineffective |
| **Para-umbilical (adult)** | Defect *adjacent* to the ring; obesity, multiparity, ascites. Narrow neck → **incarceration risk** → repair, mesh if larger |
| **Epigastric** | Linea alba **above** the umbilicus; small, pre-peritoneal fat, painful out of proportion to size, may be multiple |
| **Incisional** | Through a previous wound. Risks: **wound infection**, emergency surgery, midline incision, obesity, smoking, diabetes, malnutrition, steroids, chronic cough, poor closure. Prevent with small-bites continuous slowly-absorbable closure (suture:wound ≥4:1). Repair usually mesh |
| **Wound dehiscence** | Acute closure failure classically **day 7–10** (§2.4), heralded by **serosanguinous "salmon-pink" discharge**; full-thickness = **burst abdomen** — saline-soaked gauze over bowel, resuscitate, theatre. Same risks as incisional hernia, different timescale |
| **Spigelian** | Through the **semilunar line** at the lateral rectus border, usually **below the arcuate line**. **Interparietal** — deep to intact external oblique, so often impalpable; high incarceration risk; **US/CT** diagnoses |
| **Obturator** | Through the obturator canal. **"Little old lady's hernia"** — unexplained **SBO**. **Howship–Romberg sign**: medial thigh/knee pain on hip **extension, adduction or internal rotation**. CT diagnostic; high morbidity from delay |
| **Lumbar** | **Superior (Grynfeltt)** or **inferior (Petit)** triangle; primary, or after flank incision/iliac crest harvest |
| **Parastomal** | Around a stoma trephine; very common (§4.7) |
| **Divarication of recti** | **Not a hernia** — widened linea alba, separated rectus bellies, midline ridge on sitting up. **No defect → no neck → cannot obstruct or strangulate.** Reassure + physiotherapy; surgery cosmetic only |

#### 4.6 Strangulation — recognition and immediate management

**The short version**
- **Constant severe pain** (not colic) + **tense tender irreducible lump** + **loss of cough impulse** = strangulation until disproved.
- **Overlying skin change** — erythema → dusky discolouration → oedema — means ischaemia has reached the sac and wall: late and ominous.
- **Systemic signs** (tachycardia, fever, hypotension, raised lactate/WCC, peritonism) mark necrosis and sepsis, not an observable hernia.
- ⚑ **Never attempt to reduce a strangulated hernia.**
- **Resuscitate and operate**; investigations must not delay theatre.

The usual sequence is irreducible → obstructed → strangulated, but **Richter's** short-circuits it: pain, tenderness and systemic upset with flatus still passing. Expect leucocytosis and **rising lactate** (a normal lactate does not exclude early strangulation). Erect CXR if perforation suspected; contrast CT only if genuinely uncertain.

**Immediate management.** A–E; large-bore access; aggressive crystalloid; NBM + **NG decompression**; catheter and hourly urine output; analgesia; bloods, VBG/lactate, group & hold; **IV antibiotics per Therapeutic Guidelines: Antibiotic**; correct electrolytes and coagulopathy; early anaesthetic and surgical review; consent covering **bowel resection and possible stoma**.

**Operative.** ⚑ Expose and open the sac, **grasp and hold the strangulated contents FIRST, and only then divide the constricting ring** — release it first and necrotic bowel reduces into the abdomen unseen (the same failure mode as reduction en masse). Assess viability by **colour, peristalsis, sheen and mesenteric arterial pulsation** after warming with saline-soaked packs. **Viable** → reduce and repair. **Doubtful** → re-warm, re-inspect, consider a planned **second look at 24–48 h**. **Non-viable** → **resect**; primary anastomosis if stable and clean, **stoma** or damage control if unstable or contaminated.

Mesh is acceptable in a clean field; ⚑ in a **contaminated** field prefer suture repair or biological/absorbable mesh over permanent synthetic mesh. Always inspect proximally for **Maydl's** before closing.

#### 4.7 Stomas

**The short version**
- **Ileostomy: RIF, spouted, liquid enzyme-rich output, high volume.** **Colostomy: LIF, flush, formed faeces, low volume.** The spout delivers corrosive small bowel effluent clear of the skin.
- **Loop** = defunctioning/reversible; **end** = definitive; **double-barrel** = two ends brought out.
- **Loop ileostomy defunctions a low anterior resection; end colostomy follows Hartmann's; end ileostomy follows panproctocolectomy; ileal conduit = urostomy.**
- ⚑ **High output stoma ≈ >1500 mL/24 h on 2 consecutive days** → dehydration, Na⁺/Mg²⁺/K⁺ loss, **AKI**.
- Every stoma patient needs a **stomal therapy nurse**; pre-operative siting is a quality standard.

| Feature | **Ileostomy** | **Colostomy** |
|---|---|---|
| Site | **Right iliac fossa** | **Left iliac fossa** (transverse loop sits upper abdomen) |
| Profile | **Spouted, 2–3 cm** | **Flush** or minimally protruding |
| Why | Effluent is **enzyme-rich and alkaline** — must reach the appliance without touching skin | Formed, faeculent, non-enzymatic, far less corrosive |
| Output character | Liquid → porridge, green/khaki | Semi-formed → formed brown faeces |
| Volume | ~**500–1000 mL/24 h** adapted (initially up to 1500–2000) | ~200–600 mL/24 h; may not act daily |
| Contents | Small bowel chyme, **sodium**-rich | Colonic faeces |
| Metabolic risk | **Dehydration, Na⁺/Mg²⁺/K⁺ loss, AKI**, gallstones, renal stones | Low |
| Other issues | Food-bolus obstruction; rehydration education | Constipation, flatus, odour |

**Configuration.** **End** = single lumen, distal bowel closed off or removed (Hartmann's, APR, panproctocolectomy). **Loop** = a loop over a rod opened antimesenterically, giving functioning + mucous limbs — used to **defunction** a distal anastomosis, easily reversed. **Double-barrel** = two separate ends adjacent (Paul–Mikulicz; when anastomosis is unsafe). **Mucous fistula** = defunctioned distal limb brought out separately, draining mucus.

| Operation | Stoma |
|---|---|
| **Hartmann's** (sigmoid resection, stump closed) | **End colostomy**, LIF — reversible later |
| **Low anterior resection** | **Defunctioning loop ileostomy** — protects the anastomosis; reversed ~3–6 months |
| **Abdominoperineal resection** | **Permanent end colostomy** |
| **Panproctocolectomy** (UC, FAP) | **Permanent end ileostomy** |
| **Subtotal colectomy** (severe colitis) | **End ileostomy** ± mucous fistula; rectal stump retained |
| **Cystectomy** | **Ileal conduit (urostomy)** — spouted, RIF, urine with mucus |

**Temporary vs permanent.** Temporary stomas protect a distal anastomosis or divert while sepsis settles; reversal at **~3–6 months** once the patient has recovered, adjuvant therapy is settled and the anastomosis is shown intact (contrast enema/flexible sigmoidoscopy). Many are never reversed — say so at consent.

| Complication | Features and management |
|---|---|
| **High output** (>1500 mL/24 h × 2 days) | → **hyponatraemia, hypomagnesaemia, hypokalaemia, AKI**, malnutrition, skin damage. **Exclude a cause first**: sepsis/collection, obstruction, *C. difficile*, drug withdrawal, short bowel, recurrent disease. Then **restrict hypotonic fluid (~500–1000 mL/day)**, **glucose–electrolyte ORS with Na⁺ ≥90 mmol/L**, **loperamide before meals** (high doses often needed), **PPI**, ± codeine; IV fluid and Mg²⁺/K⁺ replacement; dietitian + stomal therapy nurse; octreotide/parenteral support if refractory |
| **Retraction** | Sinks below skin (tension, obesity, ischaemia) → leakage; convex appliance, belt; refashion if severe |
| **Prolapse** | Telescoping bowel; commonest in **loop transverse colostomy**. Reduce gently (cold compress, topical sugar for oedema); surgery if irreducible or ischaemic |
| **Parastomal hernia** | Very common, especially end colostomy; time, obesity, oversized trephine. Support garment; repair for obstruction, pain or appliance failure |
| **Stenosis** | Post-ischaemic/septic narrowing at skin or fascia; ribbon output, colic; dilate or refashion |
| **Ischaemia/necrosis** | Dusky/black stoma early post-op. Test tube + light: mucosal only → observe; **below the fascia → theatre** |
| **Skin excoriation** | Commonest problem — leakage, poor siting or fit, absent spout. Stomal therapy review, barrier products |
| **Obstruction** | Food bolus (nuts, sweetcorn, dried fruit), adhesions, parastomal hernia, stenosis. Colic + watery output, then nil; NBM, IV fluids, NG |
| **Psychosocial** | Body image, sexual function, odour/noise anxiety, withdrawal, depression, work — drives quality of life more than the technical result |

⚑ **Pre-operative siting by a stomal therapy nurse** — marked sitting, standing and lying, clear of belt line, scars, creases, bony prominences and umbilicus, on a flat 5–7 cm area in the patient's sight — is the most effective preventer of appliance failure. Australian patients get ongoing supplies via the **Stoma Appliance Scheme** through a registered stoma association.

#### 4.8 Fistulae

**The short version**
- **Fistula** = abnormal communication between **two epithelialised (or endothelialised) surfaces**; a **sinus** is a blind-ending tract to a surface.
- **Enterocutaneous fistula (ECF)** is most often **post-operative** (anastomotic leak, enterotomy); otherwise Crohn's, malignancy, radiation, diverticulitis, trauma.
- **Output**: low **<200**, moderate 200–500, **high >500 mL/24 h**.
- **FRIEND** = the factors that prevent spontaneous closure.
- **Sepsis first, nutrition and skin next, surgery last** — definitive repair deferred **≥3 months** (often 6–12).

| Letter | Factor |
|---|---|
| **F** | **Foreign body** in the tract (mesh, suture, drain) |
| **R** | **Radiation** — irradiated bowel will not heal |
| **I** | **Inflammation/IBD (Crohn's)** or ongoing **infection** |
| **E** | **Epithelialisation** of the tract |
| **N** | **Neoplasm** at the fistula site |
| **D** | **Distal obstruction** — the fistula stays the path of least resistance |
| *(HIS…)* **H** | **High output** |
| *(HIS…)* **I** | **Intestinal disruption** — >50% of bowel circumference involved |
| *(HIS…)* **S** | **Short tract (<2 cm)**, and short segment of diseased bowel |

**Management ("SNAP").**
1. **Sepsis control first** — nothing heals around undrained sepsis. CT for collections, percutaneous or operative drainage, culture-directed antibiotics per **Therapeutic Guidelines: Antibiotic**. This is what changes mortality.
2. **Nutrition, fluid and electrolytes** — **enteral where feasible** (including fistuloclysis), **parenteral** if proximal or high output; monitor albumin and trace elements.
3. **Anatomy** — once stable: **fistulogram, contrast CT or MRI** for origin, tract length, distal obstruction, residual bowel length. Planning, not urgent.
4. **Skin and effluent control** — stomal therapy input, appliances/wound managers, negative-pressure therapy, barrier films.
5. **Reduce output** — PPI, loperamide/codeine, ± octreotide (modest).
6. **Definitive surgery late** — most fistulae that will close do so within weeks of sepsis control and nutrition. Operate only after **≥3 months**, in a specialist unit.

#### 4.9 Abdominal wall and groin lumps — a framework

**The short version**
- Three questions: **where is it, does it reduce or have a cough impulse, and is it in the skin, in the wall, or coming through it?**
- Reducible with an expansile cough impulse = **hernia** until disproved.
- A groin lump in a woman is a **femoral hernia** until disproved — and it is not watched.
- Tender + irreducible + systemically unwell = **strangulation**: stop examining and resuscitate.

| Layer | Diagnoses |
|---|---|
| **Skin/subcutaneous** | Sebaceous cyst, lipoma, abscess, skin malignancy |
| **Within the wall** | Rectus sheath haematoma (anticoagulated; **Fothergill's sign** — mass persists on tensing, does not cross the midline), desmoid, endometrioma (cyclical pain in a caesarean scar), sarcoma, divarication of recti |
| **Through the wall** | Inguinal, femoral, umbilical/para-umbilical, epigastric, incisional, Spigelian, lumbar, parastomal, obturator |
| **Intra-abdominal** | Organomegaly, tumour, aneurysm, faecal loading — moves with respiration, not reducible |
| **Groin-specific** | See §4.3 |

**Carnett's test** separates wall from viscus: mark the tender point, then have the patient tense the wall (lift head/legs). **Worse or unchanged = abdominal wall**; **better = intra-abdominal**.

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators

| Cue | Answer |
|---|---|
| Sac **lateral** to the inferior epigastrics | **Indirect** inguinal hernia (medial = direct) |
| Hernia neck **inferolateral** to the pubic tubercle | **Femoral** hernia (inguinal = **superomedial**) |
| Three borders of **Hesselbach's triangle** | Inferior epigastric vessels (lateral), lateral border of rectus (medial), inguinal ligament (inferior) |
| Surface landmark of the deep ring | **1.5 cm above the mid-point of the inguinal ligament** (ASIS→pubic tubercle) |
| Part of the bowel circumference only; strangulates without obstructing | **Richter's hernia** |
| **Meckel's diverticulum** in a hernia sac | **Littre's hernia** |
| Irreducible but perfused vs blood supply compromised | **Incarcerated** vs **strangulated** |
| Groin hernia with the **highest strangulation risk (~15–20%)** | **Femoral** — repair promptly, never watchful waiting |
| Age by which >90% of paediatric umbilical hernias close | **5 years** (most by 2); repair at 4–5 y |
| Medial thigh/knee pain on hip extension, adduction, internal rotation + SBO in a thin elderly woman | **Obturator hernia — Howship–Romberg sign** |
| Midline ridge on sitting up, no fascial defect, cannot strangulate | **Divarication of recti** (not a hernia) |
| Three nerves at risk in open inguinal hernia repair | **Ilioinguinal, iliohypogastric, genital branch of genitofemoral** |
| Spouted RIF stoma, liquid enzyme-rich output | **Ileostomy** (spout protects skin); flush LIF formed output = **colostomy** |
| Stoma after **Hartmann's** vs after **panproctocolectomy** | End **colostomy** vs end **ileostomy** |
| Threshold defining a high output stoma | **>1500 mL/24 h on 2 consecutive days** |
| **FRIEND** — why a fistula won't close | Foreign body, Radiation, Inflammation/IBD/infection, Epithelialisation, Neoplasm, Distal obstruction |
| Tender, irreducible hernia in an unwell patient — the absolute rule | **Do not reduce it** — resuscitate and go to theatre |
