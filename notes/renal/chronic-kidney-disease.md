## Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)

### Finals-study-2.pdf — Renal Notes, Section 3: CKD (pages 33-38)

Cross-checked against KDIGO 2024 CKD guideline, KDIGO 2022 Diabetes-in-CKD update,

NICE NG203, and standard texts. The management section has changed substantially in recent years — SGLT2 inhibitors and finerenone are now core, not optional. Flags mark where older textbooks are out of date.

#### 3.1 Definition and Classification

##### Definition

###### CKD = abnormalities of kidney structure or function, present for ≥3 months, with

**implications for health.** Either:
- **eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m²,** OR
- **Markers of kidney damage** (albuminuria ACR ≥3 mg/mmol, urine sediment abnormalities, electrolyte abnormalities from tubular disorders, histological/structural abnormalities, or history of transplant)

The **≥3 months** criterion is what separates CKD from AKI — an isolated abnormal eGFR is not

CKD.

##### KDIGO classification — the "heat map" (CGA)

Staged by **Cause, GFR** category, and **Albuminuria** category. GFR and albuminuria together predict risk **better than either alone.**

###### G stages (eGFR mL/min/1.73 m²):

| Stag e | eGFR | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G1 | ≥90 | Normal/high (only CKD if other damage marker present) |
| G2 | 60–89 | Mildly decreased (only CKD if other marker present) |
| G3a | 45–59 | Mild–moderate |
| G3b | 30–44 | Moderate–severe |
| G4 | 15–29 | Severe |
| G5 | <15 | Kidney failure |

###### A categories (ACR):

| Category | ACR (mg/mmol) | ACR (mg/g) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | <3 | <30 |
| A2 | 3–30 | 30–300 |
| A3 | >30 | >300 |

- The **heat map** (green → yellow → orange → red) combines G and A to grade risk of progression, cardiovascular events, and death. **A worse albuminuria category shifts a** **patient into a higher-risk box even at the same eGFR** — this is the whole point of the two-dimensional system.
- **G1–G2 require a damage marker to count as CKD;** an eGFR of 75 with no albuminuria/structural abnormality is not CKD.

##### Common causes

**Diabetes and hypertension are the two commonest** (together the majority of ESKD). Then:
- Glomerulonephritis (IgA the commonest primary GN)
- ADPKD
- Obstructive uropathy, reflux nephropathy
- Renovascular disease
- Chronic tubulointerstitial disease (drugs, e.g. lithium, analgesics)

#### 3.2 Complications of CKD

##### Anaemia of CKD

- **Mechanism:** primarily **reduced EPO production** by peritubular fibroblasts; compounded by **absolute and functional iron deficiency,** uraemia-induced ↓ red cell survival, and blood loss.

- **Hepcidin** (raised in CKD due to reduced renal clearance and inflammation) blocks gut iron absorption and iron release from stores → **functional iron deficiency** (iron present but locked away).
- Typically **normochromic, normocytic.**
- **Management:** correct iron first (often **IV iron** in dialysis/non-dialysis CKD), then **erythropoiesis-stimulating agents (ESAs)** if still anaemic. Target Hb ~100–120 g/L — **do NOT normalise Hb** (higher targets increased stroke/CV events in trials, e.g. CHOIR,

TREAT).
- Newer: **HIF prolyl-hydroxylase inhibitors** (e.g. roxadustat) — oral agents that stimulate endogenous EPO; a recent addition worth naming.

##### CKD–Mineral and Bone Disorder (CKD-MBD)

The classic cascade — understand it as a chain reaction:
- 1. **↓ GFR → phosphate retention (hyperphosphataemia)**
- 2. **↓ 1α-hydroxylation → low calcitriol (active vitamin D)** → ↓ gut calcium absorption →

###### hypocalcaemia

- 3. **↑ FGF-23** (early — even before phosphate rises) suppresses calcitriol further
- 4. Hypocalcaemia + hyperphosphataemia + low calcitriol → **secondary** **hyperparathyroidism** (↑PTH, an appropriate compensatory response)
- 5. Chronic drive → **tertiary hyperparathyroidism** (autonomous PTH secretion, often with hypercalcaemia)
- **Consequences: renal osteodystrophy** (high-turnover osteitis fibrosa cystica from

↑PTH; or low-turnover adynamic bone disease), and **vascular/soft-tissue calcification** (a major driver of cardiovascular mortality).
- Memory hook: in CKD-MBD the phosphate is HIGH and calcium is LOW — the opposite of primary hyperparathyroidism.

##### Metabolic acidosis

- Failing kidneys can't excrete the daily acid load or regenerate bicarbonate → **normal-then-raised anion gap metabolic acidosis.**
- Accelerates bone demineralisation, muscle wasting, and CKD progression → treat with **oral sodium bicarbonate** when HCO₃⁻ is persistently low.

##### Hyperkalaemia

- Reduced excretion; worsened by RAAS blockade, which itself is protective — a therapeutic tension. Manage with dietary restriction and potassium binders rather than stopping renoprotective drugs where possible.

##### Cardiovascular disease

- **The leading cause of death in CKD** — patients are far more likely to die of CV disease than reach dialysis. CKD is an independent CV risk factor. Emphasise this in any exam answer on CKD complications.

##### Uraemic syndrome (advanced)

- **Pericarditis** (uraemic — a dialysis indication), **encephalopathy, peripheral** **neuropathy, pruritus, platelet dysfunction → bleeding** (despite normal platelet count/coagulation), nausea, restless legs, sexual dysfunction.

#### 3.3 Management and Slowing Progression

##### Blood pressure and RAAS blockade

- **ACE inhibitor or ARB** is first-line where there is **albuminuria** — reduces intraglomerular pressure and proteinuria, slowing progression. Titrate to the maximum tolerated dose.
- **Expected:** a creatinine rise of **up to ~25–30%** (or eGFR fall up to ~25%) after starting is acceptable and does not warrant stopping — recheck in 1–2 weeks. A larger rise suggests **renovascular disease** → investigate.
- Do **not** routinely combine ACEi + ARB (increased hyperkalaemia/AKI, no benefit).
- BP target: individualised, generally **<130/80** with albuminuria (per KDIGO, aiming for

SBP <120 in selected patients using standardised measurement).

##### SGLT2 inhibitors — now standard of care ⚑ (major recent change)

- **Dapagliflozin/empagliflozin slow CKD progression and reduce CV events and** **mortality — WITH OR WITHOUT diabetes** (DAPA-CKD, EMPA-KIDNEY).
- **Initiation threshold has been lowered to eGFR ≥20 mL/min/1.73 m²;** once started, continue until dialysis/transplant.
- Expect a small **initial "dip" in eGFR** — this is haemodynamic and benign, analogous to

ACEi.
- Side effects: mycotic genital infections, small DKA risk (euglycaemic), volume depletion.

If a textbook lists SGLT2 inhibitors as glucose-lowering drugs only, it predates this evidence.

##### Non-steroidal MRA (finerenone) ⚑

- **Finerenone** (a **non-steroidal** MRA) reduces CKD progression and CV events in **type 2** **diabetic kidney disease** with albuminuria (FIDELIO-DKD, FIGARO-DKD).
- Add on top of a RAS inhibitor when **eGFR ≥25, normal potassium, and persistent** **albuminuria (ACR ≥3 mg/mmol)** despite maximal RASi.

- **Main risk: hyperkalaemia** — monitor potassium. Differs from spironolactone/eplerenone (steroidal MRAs) in receptor selectivity and side-effect profile.

##### The "four pillars" of modern renoprotection (diabetic CKD)

RAS inhibitor + SGLT2 inhibitor + finerenone + glycaemic control (often with a **GLP-1 receptor** **agonist).** This layered approach is the current direction of travel.

##### CKD-MBD management

- **Dietary phosphate restriction** + **phosphate binders** (calcium-based, or non-calcium e.g. sevelamer/lanthanum to avoid calcium loading and vascular calcification)
- **Active vitamin D analogues** (alfacalcidol, calcitriol) for low calcitriol/secondary hyperparathyroidism
- **Calcimimetics** (cinacalcet) lower PTH by increasing calcium-sensing receptor sensitivity

— for secondary/tertiary hyperparathyroidism
- **Parathyroidectomy** for refractory tertiary hyperparathyroidism

##### Anaemia

- Iron (IV commonly) → ESAs; target homoglobin 100–120 g/L; don't over-correct.

##### Acidosis

- **Oral sodium bicarbonate** for persistent metabolic acidosis (also slows progression).

##### Other

- **Statin** for CV risk (atorvastatin 20 mg per NICE for most CKD)
- Lifestyle: smoking cessation, weight, salt restriction, exercise
- **Avoid nephrotoxins;** sick-day rules; vaccinate

##### Referral to nephrology & planning for RRT

**Refer if:** eGFR <30, ACR ≥70 mg/mmol, ACR ≥30 with haematuria, sustained rapid decline

(>25% and G-stage change, or >15 mL/min/yr), suspected genetic/rare cause, poorly controlled hypertension despite ≥4 agents, or suspected renal artery stenosis.
- Prepare for RRT well ahead: education on modalities, **fistula formation months before** **anticipated need,** transplant work-up, and **conservative (supportive) kidney care** as a valid choice for frail patients.

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Is this CKD or AKI? | CKD if ≥3 months — small echogenic kidneys, anaemia, CKD-MBD, old bloods |
| What does the heat map add over eGFR alone? | Albuminuria (A category) independently predicts risk — a worse ACR moves you to a higher-risk box at the same eGFR |
| Calcium and phosphate in CKD-MBD? | Phosphate HIGH, calcium LOW, PTH HIGH, calcitriol LOW (opposite of primary hyperPTH) |
| Commonest cause of death in CKD? | Cardiovascular disease — not ESKD |
| Drug that slows CKD with or without diabetes? | SGLT2 inhibitor (eGFR ≥20) |
| Add-on for diabetic CKD with residual albuminuria? | Finerenone (watch potassium) |
| Acceptable creatinine rise after ACEi? | Up to ~25–30%; more → suspect renovascular disease |
| Anaemia of CKD — treat what first? | Iron before ESA; don't normalise Hb |
