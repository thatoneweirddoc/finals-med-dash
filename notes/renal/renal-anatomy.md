#### Intrinsic glomerular cells (the tuft)

| Cell | Location | Function | Active agents / molecules | Clinical correlate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Endothelial cells | Innermost layer, lining glomerular capillaries | Fenestrated (70–100 nm pores) → high water/solute permeability but hold back cells; glycocalyx adds negative charge | Produce nitric oxide and tendothelin; respond to podocyte-deri ved VEGF; negatively charged glycocalyx | Endothelial injury = thrombotic microangiopathy (HUS/TTP, pre-eclampsia) |
| Podocytes (visceral epithelial cells) | Outermost layer; foot processes (pedicels) wrap the capillaries | Form the slit diaphragm — the final size/charge barrier; synthesise and maintain the GBM; structural support; secrete VEGF to sustain the endothelium | Nephrin, podocin (slit diaphragm proteins); VEGF; the PLA2R antigen sits on podocytes | Foot process effacement → minimal change/nephrotic; anti-PLA2R → membranous; can't proliferate → injury drives FSGS/glomeruloscl erosis |
| Mesangial cells (intraglomeru lar) | Central, between capillary loops | Structural support of the tuft; contractile — adjust capillary surface area and hence GFR; phagocytic — clear trapped macromolecules/i mmune complexes; lay down mesangial matrix | Contract in response to angiotensin II; relax to ANP; secrete cytokines and prostaglandins | Mesangial expansion (diabetic nephropathy, Kimmelstiel–Wilson); mesangial IgA deposits (IgA nephropathy); proliferation in MPGN |
| Parietal epithelial cells | Line the outer wall of Bowman's capsule | Form Bowman's capsule (simple squamous); act as a podocyte progenitor/stem- cell reservoir | — | Proliferate to form crescents in rapidly progressive (crescentic) GN |

#### Juxtaglomerular apparatus (vascular pole)

| Cell | Location | Function | Active agents | Clinical correlate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Juxtaglomerula r (granular) cells | Modified smooth muscle in the afferent arteriole wall | Synthesise, store, and release renin; act as an intrarenal baroreceptor (sense afferent pressure) | Secrete renin; stimulated by ↓ perfusion pressure, β1-sympathetic input, and signals from the macula densa | The trigger point of RAAS; renin ↑ in renal artery stenosis |
| Macula densa cells | Specialised cells of the thick ascending limb / early DCT where it touches its own glomerulus | Chemoreceptor — sense luminal NaCl via NKCC2; drive tubuloglomerular feedback and modulate renin release | Sensing ↑ NaCl → release adenosine/ATP (→ afferent constriction, ↓GFR); ↓ NaCl → prostaglandins/N O (→ renin release) | Why loop diuretics (block NKCC2 at the macula densa) stimulate renin; basis of tubuloglomerular feedback |
| Extraglomerular mesangial cells (Lacis / Goormaghtigh cells) | The triangle between afferent arteriole, efferent arteriole, and macula densa | Relay signals between the macula densa and the granular cells/arterioles via gap junctions; may contribute to renin secretion | Gap-junction signalling | Complete the JGA feedback circuit |

- Retroperitoneal at T12- L3 (right kidney is lower due to the liver)

- INTERNAL ANATOMY: outer capsule, cortex, medulla (columns and pyramids  minor calyces  major calyces), renal pelvis (connects to ureter)

- RENAL BLOOD FLOW

- Renal artery  5 segmental arteries (apical, upper, middle, lower, posterior)  lobar artery  interlobar arteries  arcuate artery  interlobular artery  afferent arteriole  glomerulus  efferent arteriole  vasa recta/peritubular capillaries peritubular veins  interlobular veins  arcuate veins  interlobar veins  segmental veins  renal vein  IVC

- Afferent = Arriving; Efferent = Exiting

- BLADDER:

- Detrusor muscle (distensible)

- Innervated by inferior hypogastric nerve (S2-S4) and vesical plexus

- Internal ureteral: In males, sphincter to stop black flow

- External sphincter: α motoneuron, puedenal nerve

- Pontine micturition centre: In brainstem, regulates the switching of storage of urine and voiding
