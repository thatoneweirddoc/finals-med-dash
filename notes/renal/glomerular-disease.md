## Glomerular Disease

### Finals-study-2.pdf — Renal Notes, Section 4: Glomerular Disease (pages 40-45)

Cross-checked against KDIGO 2021 Glomerular Diseases guideline and KDIGO 2025

IgAN/IgAV guideline, Robbins, and standard texts. This is the heaviest conceptual section — organise every answer around the nephrotic vs nephritic split.

#### 4.1 Framework

##### Nephrotic vs nephritic — the discriminating framework

|  | Nephrotic | Nephritic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core lesion | Podocyte injury → leaky barrier | Inflammation → glomerular breaks + hypercellularity |
| Proteinuria | Heavy (>3.5 g/day) | Mild–moderate (<3.5 g/day) |
| Haematuria | Absent/minimal | Present — red cell casts, dysmorphic RBCs |
| Albumin | Hypoalbuminaemia | Usually preserved |
| Oedema | Marked | Mild–moderate |
| Blood pressure | Usually normal | Hypertension |
| Renal function | Often preserved initially | Often impaired (↑creatinine, oliguria) |
| Other | Hyperlipidaemia, thrombophilia | — |

Mnemonic: **nephr-O-tic = prOtein; nephr-I-tic = Inflammation/hematurIa.**
- **RPGN (rapidly progressive GN)** — nephritic picture with a **rapid loss of renal** **function over days–weeks;** histology shows **crescents.** The nephrological emergency.

Causes group into three:
  - 1. **Anti-GBM** (linear IF)

- 2. **Immune complex** (granular IF — lupus, IgA, post-infectious)
  - 3. **Pauci-immune** (scanty IF — **ANCA vasculitis)**
- Overlap syndromes exist (e.g. membranoproliferative, diffuse proliferative lupus can show both nephrotic and nephritic features).

#### 4.2 Nephrotic Syndrome

Definition triad: heavy proteinuria (>3.5 g/day) + hypoalbuminaemia + oedema (± hyperlipidaemia). LM- Light microscopy, IF- immunoflorescence, EM- electron microscopy.

| Disease | Key features | Histology (LM / IF / EM) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minimal change disease | Commonest in children; steroid-responsive; can follow URTI/Hodgkin; adults too | LM normal; IF negative; EM podocyte foot process effacement |
| FSGS | Commonest primary nephrotic cause in adults (esp. Black patients); primary (permeability factor) vs secondary (HIV, obesity, reflux, heroin, sickle cell); often steroid-resistant | LM focal & segmental sclerosis; IF ±nonspecific; EM foot process effacement |
| Membranous nephropathy | Commonest primary nephrotic in older White adults; anti-PLA2R antibodies (~70% of primary); secondary: malignancy (solid tumours), SLE (class V), hepatitis B, drugs (gold, penicillamine, NSAIDs) | LM thickened GBM, "spikes"; IF granular subepithelial IgG/C3; EM subepithelial deposits |
| Diabetic nephropathy | Commonest cause of ESKD overall; progresses albuminuria → nephrotic → decline; usually with retinopathy | Kimmelstiel–Wilson nodules, mesangial expansion, GBM thickening |
| Amyloidosis / light chain deposition | Older patients; myeloma/chronic inflammation; Congo red → apple-green birefringence under polarised light | Amorphous deposits; AL (light chain) or AA (serum amyloid A) |

###### Complications of nephrotic syndrome (the "why it's dangerous" list):

- **Thromboembolism** (loss of antithrombin III, etc.) — **renal vein thrombosis** classically in membranous; VTE, PE. Consider anticoagulation if albumin is very low.

- **Infection** (urinary loss of immunoglobulins/complement) — esp. encapsulated organisms
- **Hyperlipidaemia** (hepatic compensation)
- **Oedema** — manage with salt/fluid restriction + loop diuretics; treat the underlying cause and reduce proteinuria (RAS blockade)

#### 4.3 Nephritic Syndrome

| Disease | Key features | Complement | Discriminator |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IgA nephropathy | Commonest primary GN worldwide; young adult; visible haematuria 1–2 days after URTI (synpharyngitic) | Normal | Mesangial IgA on IF |
| IgA vasculitis (HSP) | Children; palpable purpura (buttocks/legs) + arthralgia + abdominal pain + nephritis | Normal | Systemic IgA vasculitis |
| Post-streptococcal GN | Child, 1–2 weeks after throat/skin strep; oedema, hypertension, smoky urine; usually self-limiting | LOW C3 | ↑ASOT/anti-DNas e B; subepithelial "humps" on EM |
| Anti-GBM (Goodpasture) | Pulmonary–renal syndrome (haemoptysis + RPGN); antibodies to type IV collagen | Normal | Linear IgG on IF; anti-GBM antibodies |
| ANCA vasculitis | GPA (c-ANCA/anti-PR3, sinus/lung/kidney), MPA (p-ANCA/anti-MPO), EGPA (asthma, eosinophilia); RPGN | Normal | Pauci-immune on IF; ANCA positive |
| Lupus nephritis | Young woman, multisystem; ANA/anti-dsDNA positive | LOW C3 AND C4 | "Full house" IF (IgG, IgA, IgM, C3, C1q); class I–VI |
| Membranoproliferative GN / C3 glomerulopathy | Mixed nephrotic/nephritic; hepatitis C/cryoglobulins (immune-complex type) or complement dysregulation (C3 type) | LOW C3 | "Tram-track" GBM double contour |
| Cryoglobulinaemia | Often hepatitis C; purpura, arthralgia, neuropathy | Low C4 | Cryoglobulins |

**Low-complement GNs — memorise the list** (a favourite exam shortcut):
- **Post-streptococcal GN** (low C3)
- **Lupus nephritis** (low C3 **and** C4)
- **Membranoproliferative GN / C3 glomerulopathy** (low C3)
- **Cryoglobulinaemia** (low C4)
- IgA nephropathy, anti-GBM, and ANCA vasculitis are **normal-complement** — this distinction alone often cracks an SBA.

###### Lupus nephritis classes (ISN/RPS) — why the class matters:

- Class **I–II** (minimal/mesangial): supportive.
- Class **III–IV** (focal/diffuse proliferative): the aggressive forms — **immunosuppression** (steroids + mycophenolate or cyclophosphamide; belimumab/voclosporin add-on).

Biopsy class **directs treatment** — that's why every lupus nephritis patient is biopsied.
- Class **V** (membranous): nephrotic picture.
- Class **VI:** advanced sclerosis.

###### Treatment headlines:

- **ANCA vasculitis / anti-GBM:** urgent **immunosuppression** — high-dose steroids + **rituximab or cyclophosphamide; plasma exchange** for anti-GBM (and severe ANCA with pulmonary haemorrhage/dialysis-dependence). Anti-GBM is a **"treat now, biopsy** **confirms" emergency** — delay costs nephrons.
- **IgA nephropathy** ⚑ (updated 2025): **RAS blockade + SGLT2 inhibitor** as supportive backbone; **targeted-release budesonide (Nefecon)** for those at risk of progression; **sparsentan** (dual endothelin/angiotensin receptor antagonist) as an option. Systemic steroids are now used more cautiously given toxicity. If your notes only say "IgA = ACEi

± fish oil," they predate this.

#### 4.4 Inherited Glomerular Disease

| Disease | Genetics | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alport syndrome | Usually X-linked (COL4A5) — type IV collagen defect | Haematuria + progressive renal failure + sensorineural deafness + ocular (lenticonus); "basket-weave" GBM on EM |
| Thin basement membrane disease (benign familial haematuria) | AD, COL4A3/4 | Persistent isolated haematuria, benign course, normal function — the important "don't over-investigate" differential |

#### 4.5 Investigating Glomerular Disease

##### The immunology screen (know what each tests for)

- **ANA, anti-dsDNA** → SLE
- **ANCA (PR3/MPO)** → ANCA vasculitis
- **Anti-GBM** → Goodpasture
- **Complement C3/C4** → low in the GNs listed above
- **Anti-PLA2R** → primary membranous nephropathy
- **Cryoglobulins** → cryoglobulinaemia (send warm)
- **Hepatitis B/C, HIV serology** → secondary causes
- **Serum free light chains / electrophoresis / urine Bence-Jones** → myeloma/amyloid
- **ASOT / anti-DNase B** → recent streptococcal infection

##### Histology — always three modalities (the biopsy answers all three)

- 1. **Light microscopy** — pattern of injury (proliferation, crescents, sclerosis, nodules, thickening)
- 2. **Immunofluorescence** — what and where: **linear** (anti-GBM), **granular** (immune complex), **mesangial IgA** (IgAN), **full house** (lupus), **pauci-immune** (ANCA)
- 3. **Electron microscopy** — deposit location **(subepithelial** = membranous/PSGN humps; **subendothelial** = lupus/MPGN; **mesangial** = IgA) and foot process effacement (minimal change)

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators

| Clue | Diagnosis |
| --- | --- |
| Child, nephrotic, steroid-responsive | Minimal change disease |
| Older adult, nephrotic, anti-PLA2R +ve | Membranous nephropathy |
| Haematuria during a sore throat | IgA nephropathy |
| Haematuria 1–2 weeks after infection, low C3 | Post-streptococcal GN |
| Haemoptysis + AKI, linear IF | Anti-GBM (Goodpasture) |
| Sinusitis + lung nodules + AKI, c-ANCA | GPA (ANCA vasculitis) |
| "Full house" IF, low C3 and C4 | Lupus nephritis |
| Purpura on legs + abdominal pain + nephritis in a child | IgA vasculitis (HSP) |
| Haematuria + deafness + family history | Alport syndrome |
| Isolated persistent haematuria, benign | Thin basement membrane disease |

#### Nomenclature

- FOCAL: < 50% glomeruli involved

- DIFFUSE: > 50% glomeruli involved

- PROLIFERATIVE: hypercellular glomeruli

- MEMBRANOUS: GBM thickening

- PRIMARY GLOMERULAR DISEASE: 1o disease of the kidney, specifically impacting glomeruli

- SECONDARY GLOMERULAR DISEASE: systemic disease/another organ disease affecting glomeruli e.g. SLE, diabetic nephropathy

#### nephrotic syndrome

| Definition | Proteinuria (>3.5g/day), hypoalbuminaemia (<30g/L), oedema and hyperlipidaemia |
|---|---|
| Minimal change disease | Characteristics Most common in children Associated with infection, tumours e.g. Hodgkin Lymphoma Excellent response to steroid therapy LM: no changes EM: effacement of foot processes |
| Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis | Characteristics Most common cause in African Americans and Hispanics Associated with heroin, HIV, obesity and sickle cell disease Leads to ESRD if untreated and poor response to corticosteroids LM: segmental sclerosis IM: IgM and C3 inside sclerotic regions EM: effacement of foot processes |
| Membranous nephropathy | Characteristics Most common in white populations Primary: antibodies to phospholipase A2 receptor Secondary: infectious (Hep B/C), SLE, tumours, medications LM: glomerular capillary loops and basement membrane appear thickened IM: subepithelial deposits EM: “spike and dome appearance” (dense deposits) |
| Membranoproliferative GN | IG-medicated GN Associated with SLE, Hep C, monoclonal gammopathy IM: IgG deposits on basement membrane Complement-mediated: Dense deposit disease, C3 GN IM: C3 deposits on basement membrane Both EM: basement membrane thickening, GBM splitting (“train-track” appearance) No single effective treatment |
