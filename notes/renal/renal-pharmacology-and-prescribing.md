## Renal Pharmacology and Prescribing

#### 10.1 Diuretics

| Class | Site & target | Main uses | Key side effects / electrolytes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Loop (furosemide, bumetanide) | Thick ascending limb — NKCC2 | Fluid overload (HF, pulmonary oedema, CKD, nephrotic); the most powerful | Hypokalaemia, hypocalcaemia, hypomagnesaemia, metabolic alkalosis, hypovolaemia, ototoxicity, gout |
| Thiazide (bendroflumethiazide, indapamide) | Distal convoluted tubule — NCC | Hypertension, mild oedema | Hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia, hyperCALCaemia, hyperuricaemia (gout), hyperglycaemia, hyperlipidaemia |
| Potassium-sparing — ENaC blockers (amiloride, triamterene) | Collecting duct — ENaC | With loops/thiazides to spare potassium; Liddle | Hyperkalaemia |
| Aldosterone antagonists (MRA) (spironolactone, eplerenone) | Collecting duct — aldosterone receptor | Heart failure, ascites, resistant HTN, hyperaldosteronism | Hyperkalaemia; spironolactone → gynaecomastia (eplerenone doesn't) |
| Carbonic anhydrase inhibitor (acetazolamide) | Proximal tubule — carbonic anhydrase | Glaucoma, altitude sickness, alkalinisation | Metabolic acidosis, hypokalaemia |
| Osmotic (mannitol) | Proximal tubule/loop (osmotic) | Raised ICP, glaucoma | Pulmonary oedema, hyponatraemia then hypernatraemia |

Memory hook — the calcium split: **Loops Lose calcium** (hypoCALCaemia), **thiazides reTain** **calcium** (hyperCALCaemia). This is why thiazides help calcium stones and loops don't.
- **Diuretic resistance:** inadequate response to loops in HF/CKD/nephrotic. Causes: poor absorption/oedematous gut, hypoalbuminaemia, RAAS activation, distal nephron hypertrophy.
- **Sequential nephron blockade:** add a **thiazide (e.g. metolazone) to a loop** to block the compensatory distal sodium reabsorption — potent, but **high risk of profound** **hypokalaemia/hypovolaemia;** monitor closely.

#### 10.2 RAAS Blockade

| Class | Example | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ACE inhibitors | ramipril, lisinopril | ↓Ang II; dry cough (bradykinin), angioedema, hyperkalaemia, first-dose hypotension |
| ARBs | losartan, candesartan | ↓Ang II at receptor; no cough (use if ACEi cough); hyperkalaemia |
| MRAs | spironolactone, eplerenone, finerenone (non-steroidal) | Aldosterone blockade; hyperkalaemia |
| Direct renin inhibitor | aliskiren | Rarely used; avoid combining with ACEi/ARB |

- **Renoprotective** via reduced intraglomerular pressure and proteinuria — first-line in albuminuric CKD/diabetic nephropathy.
- **Expected creatinine rise** after starting: **up to ~25–30% (eGFR fall ≤25%) is** **acceptable** — recheck U&Es in 1–2 weeks. A **larger rise → suspect bilateral renal** **artery stenosis** (efferent dilatation removes the pressure maintaining GFR).
- **When to stop / hold:** significant hyperkalaemia, larger creatinine rise, acute illness/volume depletion **(sick-day rules),** pregnancy (teratogenic).
- Don't routinely combine two RAAS agents (↑AKI/hyperkalaemia).

#### 10.3 Nephrotoxins

| Drug | Mechanism of injury |
| --- | --- |
| NSAIDs | Afferent vasoconstriction (↓prostaglandins) → haemodynamic AKI; also AIN, papillary necrosis |
| Aminoglycosides (gentamicin) | Direct proximal tubular toxicity (ATN); also ototoxic; monitor levels |
| Vancomycin | ATN (dose/level-related); monitor levels |
| Contrast media | Contrast-associated AKI (see Section 2) |
| Calcineurin inhibitors (tacrolimus, ciclosporin) | Afferent vasoconstriction; acute and chronic nephrotoxicity |
| PPIs | Acute interstitial nephritis (and chronic) |
| Lithium | Nephrogenic DI, chronic tubulointerstitial nephritis |
| Chemotherapy (cisplatin, methotrexate, ifosfamide) | ATN, tubulopathy, crystal nephropathy |
| ACEi/ARB | Haemodynamic (efferent dilatation) — not toxic, but precipitate AKI in the wrong setting |

- **The "triple whammy": NSAID + ACEi/ARB + diuretic** — a classic combination causing

AKI, especially with intercurrent illness.

#### 10.4 Prescribing in Renal Impairment

##### Drugs needing dose adjustment (accumulate in renal impairment)

- **Opioids** (morphine — active metabolites accumulate → use oxycodone/fentanyl/alfentanil with care), **gabapentin/pregabalin, many antibiotics** (aminoglycosides, vancomycin, some penicillins/cephalosporins), **DOACs and LMWH** (bleeding risk), **digoxin, allopurinol, methotrexate, sulfonylureas.**

##### Drugs to avoid / stop

- **Metformin** — stop in significant impairment/acute illness **(lactic acidosis** risk; avoid if eGFR <30).

- **NSAIDs** — avoid in CKD/AKI.
- **Nitrofurantoin** — ineffective and risk of toxicity if **eGFR <45** (needs adequate renal excretion to work in the urinary tract).
- **Potassium-sparing diuretics** — caution (hyperkalaemia).

##### Sick-day rules and medication holds

- Temporarily **hold** certain drugs during acute dehydrating illness (D&V, fever) to reduce

AKI risk — commonly remembered as **"SADMANS": Sulfonylureas, ACE** inhibitors,

**Diuretics, Metformin, ARBs, NSAIDs, SGLT2** inhibitors. Restart when eating/drinking normally.

##### Drugs removed by dialysis

- **Small, water-soluble, low-protein-bound, low-volume-of-distribution** drugs are dialysable — e.g. **lithium, methanol, ethylene glycol, salicylates, metformin, some** **antibiotics (aminoglycosides, vancomycin partly).**
- **Not well dialysed:** highly protein-bound or large-volume-of-distribution drugs (e.g.

digoxin, benzodiazepines, warfarin).
- **Practical points:** time doses of dialysable antibiotics **after** dialysis; and note some toxic ingestions are treated with dialysis (see AEIOU, Section 2).

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators

| Clue | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Diuretic causing hypocalcaemia | Loop ("Loops Lose calcium") |
| Diuretic causing hypercalcaemia | Thiazide ("thiazides reTain") |
| Spironolactone side effect (not with eplerenone) | Gynaecomastia |
| ACEi side effect prompting switch to ARB | Dry cough (bradykinin) |
| Acceptable creatinine rise after ACEi | Up to ~25–30%; more → renal artery stenosis |
| Three-drug combo causing AKI | NSAID + ACEi/ARB + diuretic (triple whammy) |
| Antibiotic ineffective if eGFR <45 | Nitrofurantoin |
| Drugs to hold in acute illness | SADMANS |
| Potent but risky loop + thiazide combo | Sequential nephron blockade (watch potassium/volume) |
