## Renal Involvement in Systemic Disease

### Finals-study-2.pdf — Renal Notes, Section 9: Renal Involvement in Systemic Disease (pages 72-75)

Cross-checked against Robbins, StatPearls, KDIGO, and standard texts. Much of this cross-references earlier sections — the value here is seeing the systemic disease as the organising principle.

#### Diabetic kidney disease

- **The commonest cause of ESKD worldwide.**
- **Natural history:** glomerular hyperfiltration → **microalbuminuria (A2)** → **overt** **proteinuria (A3/nephrotic)** → declining GFR → ESKD.
- **Histology:** mesangial expansion, GBM thickening, **Kimmelstiel–Wilson nodules,** hyaline arteriolosclerosis.
- **Screening:** annual **ACR** + eGFR in all diabetics. **Retinopathy usually coexists** — its absence in a diabetic with nephropathy should prompt suspicion of an alternative diagnosis.
- **Management:** glycaemic + BP control, **RAS blockade, SGLT2 inhibitors, finerenone,**

GLP-1 agonists (the "four pillars" — see Section 3).

#### Hypertensive nephrosclerosis & renovascular disease

- **Hypertensive nephrosclerosis:** chronic hypertension → arteriolar thickening, ischaemia, glomerulosclerosis → slowly progressive CKD (second commonest cause of

ESKD). Small kidneys, modest proteinuria.
- **Renal artery stenosis: atherosclerotic** (older, vascular disease) or **fibromuscular** **dysplasia** (young women, "string of beads" on angiography).
  - Clues: **resistant hypertension, flash pulmonary oedema, AKI after starting**

**ACEi/ARB,** asymmetric kidneys, renal bruit.
  - Investigate with **duplex US, CT/MR angiography.** Revascularisation (angioplasty/stent) reserved for selected cases (trials e.g. CORAL/ASTRAL showed limited benefit over medical therapy for atherosclerotic disease).

#### Multiple myeloma and the kidney

- Multiple mechanisms — **know all four:**

- 1. **Cast nephropathy ("myeloma kidney")** — filtered **light chains** precipitate with

Tamm-Horsfall protein → tubular obstruction (the commonest, causes AKI)
  - 2. **Hypercalcaemia** → dehydration, vasoconstriction, nephrocalcinosis
  - 3. **AL amyloidosis** → nephrotic syndrome
  - 4. **Light chain deposition disease,** and hyperuricaemia
- **Red flags:** AKI/CKD + **anaemia + bone pain + hypercalcaemia + raised ESR;** **dipstick misses light chains** — send **serum free light chains, serum/urine** **electrophoresis.**
- Management: treat myeloma, aggressive hydration, treat hypercalcaemia.

#### Systemic vasculitis & connective tissue disease

- **ANCA vasculitis** (GPA/MPA/EGPA), **anti-GBM, SLE (lupus nephritis),** **cryoglobulinaemia** — all covered in Section 4. The renal presentation is usually **nephritic/RPGN;** treat with immunosuppression.
- **Scleroderma renal crisis: acute severe hypertension + AKI + microangiopathic** **haemolysis** in systemic sclerosis → treat urgently with **ACE inhibitors** (the one scenario where ACEi is the treatment for a hypertensive/renal emergency).

#### Thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA)

Triad: **microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia (MAHA) + thrombocytopenia + organ (renal)** **injury;** blood film shows **schistocytes;** ↑LDH, ↓haptoglobin, negative Coombs.

|  | HUS (typical) | Atypical HUS | TTP |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cause | Shiga-toxin E. coli O157 (post-bloody diarrhoea) | Complement dysregulation | ADAMTS13 deficiency (auto-antibody or inherited) |
| Predominant organ | Kidney (AKI) | Kidney | Neurological + renal |
| Classic group | Children | Any | Adults |
| Treatment | Supportive (avoid antibiotics in STEC) | Eculizumab (anti-C5) ⚑ | Plasma exchange (urgent) ± steroids, rituximab, caplacizumab |

- **Don't give platelets in TTP** (fuels thrombosis) unless life-threatening bleeding.
- **Pentad of TTP** (fever, MAHA, thrombocytopenia, renal, neuro) — but full pentad is uncommon; treat on the triad.

#### Sarcoidosis and the kidney

- **Hypercalcaemia/hypercalciuria** (macrophage 1α-hydroxylase → ↑calcitriol) → stones, nephrocalcinosis; **granulomatous interstitial nephritis.** Responds to **corticosteroids.**

#### Renal disease in pregnancy

- **Physiological changes:** GFR rises ~50%, so **creatinine falls** (a "normal" non-pregnant creatinine may be abnormal in pregnancy); increased renal size, physiological hydronephrosis (right > left).
- **Pre-eclampsia:** >20 weeks, **hypertension + proteinuria** ± end-organ features; podocyte injury; risk of eclampsia, HELLP; definitive treatment is **delivery.**
- **AKI in pregnancy:** pre-eclampsia/HELLP, **acute fatty liver of pregnancy,** TMA (pregnancy-associated aHUS), sepsis, haemorrhage.

#### Sickle cell nephropathy

- Medullary hypoxia/sickling → **papillary necrosis, impaired concentrating ability** **(hyposthenuria), haematuria,** and later FSGS/proteinuria and CKD.

#### HIV-associated nephropathy (HIVAN)

- Classically a **collapsing FSGS** → heavy proteinuria, rapid progression; commoner in patients of African ancestry **(APOL1** risk variants); **large echogenic kidneys.** Treat the

HIV (antiretrovirals) — cornerstone.

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators

| Clue | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Diabetic + proteinuria + no retinopathy | Reconsider — may not be diabetic nephropathy |
| Resistant HTN + flash pulmonary oedema + AKI on ACEi | Renal artery stenosis |
| Young woman, HTN, "string of beads" on angiography | Fibromuscular dysplasia |
| AKI + anaemia + bone pain + hypercalcaemia + high ESR | Myeloma — send free light chains |
| Severe HTN + AKI + MAHA in scleroderma | Scleroderma renal crisis — give ACE inhibitor |
| Child, bloody diarrhoea, AKI, low platelets, schistocytes | HUS (STEC) |
| Adult, neuro signs, MAHA, thrombocytopenia | TTP — plasma exchange, ADAMTS13 |
| Complement-mediated TMA | Atypical HUS — eculizumab |
| Hypercalcaemia + granulomatous nephritis | Sarcoidosis |
| Heavy proteinuria + large echogenic kidneys + HIV | HIVAN (collapsing FSGS) |

#### vascular diseases of kidney

- LARGE VESSEL DISEASE:

  - Acute renal artery occlusion (infarct):

  - Renal artery stenosis (ischemia)

  - Renal vein thrombosis

- MEDIUM VESSEL DISEASE:

  - Kawasaki Disease:

    - Conjunctival injection, rash (polymorphous  desquamating), adenopathy, strawberry tongue (oral muecositis), hand foot changes (oedema), fever

    - May develop coronary artery aneurysms

    - Tx: IV immunoglobulin and aspirin

  - Polyarteritis nodosa

    - Usually middle-aged men with transmural inflammation of the arterial wall with fibrinoid necrosis

    - Hepatitis B seropositivity in 30% of patients

    - Symptoms: fever, weight loss, malaise, headache, GI (abdominal pain, melena, HTN, neurologic dysfunction, cutaneous eruptions, renal damage

    - Tx: corticosteroids, cyclophosphamide

- SMALL VESSEL DISEASE:

  - HTN nephrosclerosis

  - Atheroembolic renal disease

  - Thrombotic microangiopathy

  - Scleroderma

  - Calcineurin inhibitor nephropathy

  - Haemolytic uremic syndrome (HUS)
