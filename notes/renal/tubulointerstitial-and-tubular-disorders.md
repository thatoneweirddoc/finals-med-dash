## Tubulointerstitial and Tubular Disorders

### Finals-study-2.pdf — Renal Notes, Section 5: Tubulointerstitial and Tubular Disorders (pages 47-51)

Cross-checked against Robbins, StatPearls, and standard renal physiology/clinical texts. The

RTAs and inherited tubulopathies are stable textbook content but very high-yield for data-interpretation questions — this section rewards precise memorisation of the electrolyte/urine patterns.

#### 5.1 Interstitial Disease

##### Acute interstitial nephritis (AIN)

- **Mechanism:** hypersensitivity reaction causing inflammation of the renal interstitium →

AKI.
- **Causes:**
  - **Drugs (~70%): NSAIDs, proton pump inhibitors, penicillins/cephalosporins,**

###### allopurinol, rifampicin, sulfonamides, diuretics

  - **Infection:** pyelonephritis, leptospirosis, legionella
  - **Autoimmune/systemic:** sarcoidosis, Sjögren's, SLE, **TINU** (tubulointerstitial nephritis and uveitis)
- **Classic triad** (fever, rash, eosinophilia) — present in **<10%,** so its absence doesn't exclude AIN.
- **Investigations:** AKI, **sterile pyuria, white cell casts, eosinophiluria** (poor sensitivity/specificity — no longer relied upon), mild proteinuria.
- **Management: stop the offending drug** (the key step); **corticosteroids** if no improvement or severe. Usually recovers, but can leave chronic damage.

##### Chronic tubulointerstitial nephritis

- Slowly progressive interstitial fibrosis and tubular atrophy.
- **Analgesic nephropathy:** chronic heavy use (esp. old compound analgesics with phenacetin); **papillary necrosis;** look for a "ring sign" on imaging.
- **Lithium nephrotoxicity: nephrogenic diabetes insipidus** (commonest renal effect) and chronic tubulointerstitial nephritis; monitor levels and renal function.
- Others: chronic obstruction/reflux, sarcoidosis, heavy metals, aristolochic acid, myeloma.

#### 5.2 Renal Tubular Acidoses (RTA)

All RTAs cause a **normal anion gap (hyperchloraemic) metabolic acidosis.** Distinguish by potassium, urine pH, and associated features. Normal anion gap = bicarb loss w/ compensatory hyperchloraemic; vs high anion gap = increased acids added e.g. ketoacid, lactic acid, toxins

|  | Type 1 (Distal) | Type 2 (Proximal) | Type 4 (Hyperkalaemic) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Defect | Distal H⁺ secretion fails (α-intercalated cell) | Proximal HCO₃⁻ reabsorption fails | Aldosterone deficiency/resistance |
| Serum K⁺ | LOW (hypokalaemia) | LOW (hypokalaemia) | HIGH (hyperkalaemia) |
| Urine pH | >5.5 (inappropriately high) — can't acidify urine | Variable; <5.5 once below reabsorptive threshold | <5.5 (appropriately low) |
| Causes | Autoimmune (Sjögren's, RA), hypercalciuria, amphotericin, genetic | Fanconi syndrome, myeloma, carbonic anhydrase inhibitors, Wilson's, cystinosis | Diabetes (commonest), Addison's, ACEi/ARB, NSAIDs, heparin, trimethoprim, spironolactone |
| Complication s | Nephrocalcinosis, calcium phosphate stones (alkaline urine, hypercalciuria, hypocitraturia); rickets/osteomalacia | Rickets/osteomalacia; stones uncommon | Mild acidosis |
| Treatment | Oral bicarbonate/citrate; potassium replacement | Bicarbonate (large doses needed) + potassium; thiazide | Fludrocortisone, dietary K⁺ restriction, loop diuretic; stop offending drugs |

**The urine anion gap** (Na⁺ + K⁺ − Cl⁻) — used to work out the cause of a normal-anion-gap acidosis:
- **Negative** ("neg-GUT-ive") → **GI bicarbonate loss (diarrhoea)** — kidney appropriately excreting NH₄Cl
- **Positive** → **RTA** — kidney failing to excrete ammonium

Memory hooks: Type **1** = **one** = distal = high urine pH + stones. Type **2** = proximal = Fanconi.

Type **4** = the only **hyperkalaemic** RTA = **hypoaldosteronism.**

#### 5.3 Fanconi Syndrome

- **Generalised proximal tubular dysfunction** → failure to reabsorb multiple solutes:
  - **Glycosuria (with normal blood glucose),** aminoaciduria, phosphaturia (→ hypophosphataemia → **osteomalacia/rickets),** bicarbonaturia (→ **type 2 RTA),** uricosuria, tubular proteinuria, **hypokalaemia**
- **Causes: myeloma (commonest acquired cause in adults),** drugs **(tenofovir,** ifosfamide, aminoglycosides, expired tetracyclines), heavy metals, **Wilson's disease,** **cystinosis** (commonest inherited, children), Sjögren's.
- Recognise it from the combination: glycosuria + normoglycaemia + hypophosphataemia

+ hypokalaemia + acidosis.

#### 5.4 Inherited Tubulopathies

| Disorder | "Acts like" | Defect | Key electrolytes | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bartter syndrome | Loop diuretic (furosemide) | NKCC2 in thick ascending limb | Hypokalaemia, metabolic alkalosis, normal/low BP, hypercalciuria | Presents younger/more severe; normal Mg²⁺; may have nephrocalcinosis |
| Gitelman syndrome | Thiazide | NCC in distal convoluted tubule | Hypokalaemia, metabolic alkalosis, normal/low BP, HYPOcalciuria | Hypomagnesaemia ; milder, older presentation |
| Liddle syndrome | Excess ENaC activity ("always-on" aldosterone effect) | Gain-of-fun ction ENaC | Hypokalaemia, metabolic alkalosis, HYPERTENSION, LOW renin & aldosterone | Treat with amiloride (not spironolactone) |
| Apparent mineralocorticoi d excess | Cortisol acting on MR | 11β-HSD2 deficiency (can't inactivate cortisol) | Hypokalaemia, alkalosis, hypertension, low renin/aldosterone | Also caused by liquorice excess |

###### Discriminators worth locking in:

- **Bartter vs Gitelman:** both look like diuretic abuse (hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis, normal/low BP), but **calcium separates them — Bartter = hyperCALCiuria (loop-like),**

###### Gitelman = hypoCALCiuria + hypomagnesaemia (thiazide-like).

- **Liddle vs Bartter/Gitelman:** Liddle is **HYPERtensive with LOW aldosterone** — it mimics hyperaldosteronism but the aldosterone is suppressed, and it responds to **amiloride, not spironolactone.**

##### Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus

- **Collecting duct resistance to ADH** → inability to concentrate urine → **polyuria,** **polydipsia, dilute urine despite raised plasma osmolality.**
- **Causes: lithium** (commonest acquired), **hypercalcaemia, hypokalaemia,** chronic kidney disease, inherited **(AVPR2** X-linked, or aquaporin-2).
- **Water deprivation test:** urine stays dilute after deprivation AND **fails to concentrate** **after desmopressin** (distinguishes from cranial DI, which does respond to desmopressin).
- **Management:** treat the cause; **thiazides (paradoxical antidiuresis),** **low-salt/low-protein diet, amiloride** (esp. lithium-induced).

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators

| Clue | Diagnosis |
| --- | --- |
| AKI + new PPI/NSAID/antibiotic + sterile pyuria + white cell casts | Acute interstitial nephritis |
| Normal anion gap acidosis, hypokalaemia, urine pH >5.5, stones | Type 1 (distal) RTA |
| Normal anion gap acidosis, hypokalaemia, + glycosuria/phosphaturia | Type 2 (proximal) RTA / Fanconi |
| Normal anion gap acidosis, hyperkalaemia, diabetic | Type 4 RTA (hypoaldosteronism) |
| Hypokalaemic alkalosis, normal BP, high urine calcium | Bartter syndrome |
| Hypokalaemic alkalosis, normal BP, low urine calcium + low Mg | Gitelman syndrome |
| Hypokalaemic alkalosis + hypertension + low renin/aldosterone | Liddle syndrome (treat with amiloride) |
| Polyuria, dilute urine, on lithium, no response to desmopressin | Nephrogenic DI |
| Glycosuria with normal blood glucose | Proximal tubular dysfunction (Fanconi / SGLT2 inhibitor) |

#### tubulo-interstitial disease

Acute/chronic inflammation of the renal tubules and interstitium

##### acute tubulo-interstitial nephritis

| Definition | Rapid (days to weeks) decline in renal function and accounts for 10-20% of all AKI |
|---|---|
| Aetiology | Hypersensitivity: antibiotics (B-lactams, sulphonamides, rifampin, quinolones, cephalosporins), NSAIDs, diuretics, allopurinol, PPIs, phenytoin, acyclovir, methotrexate Infectious: bacterial pyelonephritis, leptospirosis, streptococcus, legionella, CMV, EBV Immune: SLE, Sjogren’s, sarcoidosis Idiopathic Note: Pee (diuretics) Pain (NSAIDs) Penicillins and cephalosporins Proton pump inhibitors RifamPicin |
| Pathophysiology | Hypersensitivity or tubular obstruction/toxicity  inflammatory cells infiltrate interstitium |
| Presentation | Underlying disease symptoms AKI: painless haematuria Hypersensitivity: fever, rash, arthralgia Pyelonephritis: CVA tenderness, flank pain |
| Investigations | Urine: proteinuria, haematuria, pyuria, WBC casts, eosinophils (AIN) Bloods:  urea,  Cr, eosinophilia (drug reaction), metabolic acidosis (RTA), PO43-, K+, Na+ Renal biopsy definite Gallium scan:  signal=infiltrate |
| Management | Discontinue drugs and IVF Antibiotics if infectious Corticosteroids in allergic or immune disease |
| Prognosis | Recovery within 2 weeks if insult can be eliminated |

##### chronic tubulo-interstitial nephritis

| Definition | Slowly progressive renal failure, moderate proteinuria and abnormal tubular function signs |
|---|---|
| Aetiology | Persistent acute TIN UTI/chronic pyelonephritis: tumours, stones, bladder outlet obstruction, VUR Nephrotoxins Analgesic nephropathy: NSAIDs, acetaminophen Crystal-induced: acyclovir, methotrexate, cisplatin, lithium, cyclosporine Heavy metals Vascular disease Malignancies: multiple myeloma, lymphoma Immune: SLE, Sjogren’s, anti-GBM, sarcoidosis Hereditary: cystic disease, sickle cell disease |
| Pathophysiology | prostacyclin synthesis → vasoconstriction of the medullary blood vessels → papillary ischemia/necrosis, fibrosis of interstitium with tubular atrophy, mononuclear cell inflammation |
| Presentation | Painless haematuria, pyuria, fatigue, nausea Underlying aetiology- colicky pain due to papillary necrosis, multiple myeloma Renal papillary necrosis: coagulative necrosis of the renal medullary pyramids and papillae Aetiology (POSTCARDS): pyelonephritis, obstruction, sickle cell disease, Tb, cirrhosis, analgesia, renal transplant rejection, DM, systemic vasculitis Ischemia  necrosis and sloughing of the papillae  ureteral obstruction  scarring |
| Investigations | Tubular dysfunction: e.g. acidosis, electrolyte disturbances e.g. K+ Progressive renal failure: azotemia, uremia Ultrasound: shrunken kidneys with irregular contours |
| Management | Stop offending agent or treat underlying disease Supportive measures: correct metabolic disorders (Ca2+, PO43- and anaemia) Forced diuresis If necessary, plasmapheresis/dialysis |
