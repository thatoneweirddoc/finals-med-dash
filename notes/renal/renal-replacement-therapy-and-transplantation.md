## Renal Replacement Therapy and Transplantation

#### 7.1 Dialysis

##### Principles (know the two physical processes)

- **Diffusion** — solutes move down a concentration gradient across a semipermeable membrane (clears small solutes: urea, potassium). The basis of **haemodialysis.**
- **Convection / ultrafiltration** — solvent (water) dragged across by a pressure gradient, carrying solutes ("solvent drag"); removes fluid and larger middle molecules. Dominant in **haemofiltration.**
- **Osmosis** — water drawn across by an osmotic gradient (dextrose in dialysate); the basis of fluid removal in **peritoneal dialysis.**

##### Haemodialysis (HD)

- Blood pumped through a dialyser against countercurrent dialysate; usually **3× weekly,**

**~4 hours** in a unit (or home HD).
- **Vascular access — the key exam topic (in order of preference):**
  - **Arteriovenous fistula (AVF)** — **gold standard;** native artery–vein anastomosis; needs **6–8 weeks to "mature";** lowest infection/thrombosis risk, best longevity.

Complications: stenosis/thrombosis, steal syndrome (distal ischaemia), high-output cardiac failure, aneurysm.
  - **AV graft** — synthetic; used when veins inadequate; higher thrombosis/infection than fistula.
  - **Tunnelled central venous catheter** — immediate use but **highest infection** **and thrombosis risk;** last resort/bridge.
  - "Fistula first" — and **never** take blood, cannulate, or put a BP cuff on a fistula arm.
- **Complications: hypotension** (commonest — during fluid removal), cramps, arrhythmia, **dialysis disequilibrium syndrome** (rapid urea/osmolar shifts → cerebral oedema → headache, confusion, seizures — avoided by gentle initial dialysis), access problems, air embolism, anaphylaxis to membrane.

##### Peritoneal dialysis (PD)

- Uses the **peritoneum as the semipermeable membrane;** dialysate instilled via a

Tenckhoff catheter, dextrose osmotic gradient removes fluid. **CAPD** (manual exchanges) or **APD** (overnight machine).
- **Advantages:** home-based, independence, gentler haemodynamics, preserves residual function.
- **Complications:**
  - **PD peritonitis** — the big one: **cloudy effluent + abdominal pain + fever;** diagnose on effluent WCC (>100 with >50% neutrophils) and culture; usually

**Staph** (skin flora); treat with **intraperitoneal antibiotics.**
  - **Exit-site/tunnel infection,** catheter malfunction, hernias, **encapsulating** **peritoneal sclerosis** (rare, serious — bowel encased in fibrous cocoon → obstruction), membrane failure over time, hyperglycaemia/weight gain from glucose load.

##### Choosing a modality & conservative care

- Modality depends on patient factors (comorbidity, lifestyle, home support, residual function, cardiovascular stability), not just clinical criteria — a shared decision.
- **Conservative (supportive) kidney care** is a legitimate pathway for frail/multimorbid patients in whom dialysis may not improve survival or quality of life — focus on symptom control and advance care planning. **Mentioning this shows exam maturity.**

#### 7.2 Transplantation

The best treatment for ESKD for suitable patients — better survival and quality of life than dialysis.

##### Work-up and matching

- **Living vs deceased donor** (living gives better graft survival, can be pre-emptive before dialysis).
- **Matching: ABO blood group compatibility** and **HLA matching** (HLA-A, -B, -DR most important; DR mismatch matters most). **Crossmatch** (recipient serum against donor lymphocytes) must be negative — a **positive crossmatch predicts hyperacute** **rejection.**
- Placed **extraperitoneally in the iliac fossa,** anastomosed to iliac vessels and bladder; native kidneys usually left in situ.

##### Immunosuppression (and side effects — very examinable)

Typically **triple therapy:**

| Drug class | Example | Key side effects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calcineurin inhibitor | Tacrolimus, ciclosporin | Nephrotoxicity (afferent vasoconstriction — mimics rejection!), tremor, diabetes (tacrolimus), hypertension, gum hypertrophy/hirsutism (ciclosporin), neurotoxicity; narrow therapeutic window → drug-level monitoring |
| Antimetabolite | Mycophenolate mofetil | GI upset, bone marrow suppression, teratogenic |
| Corticosteroid | Prednisolone | Diabetes, osteoporosis, weight gain, infection, cushingoid |
| (mTOR inhibitor) | Sirolimus | Poor wound healing, proteinuria, pneumonitis |
| Induction agent | Basiliximab (IL-2R antibody), ATG | Infusion reactions, infection |

Key trap: **calcineurin inhibitor toxicity can look like rejection** — both cause a rising creatinine in a transplant. Biopsy + drug levels distinguish them.

##### Rejection — types and timing

| Type | Timing | Mechanism | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hyperacute | Minutes–h ours | Pre-formed antibodies (ABO/HLA) | Graft thrombosis; now rare due to crossmatching; untreatable → remove graft |
| Acute cellular | Days–mont hs | T-cell mediated | Commonest acute; treat with high-dose steroids, ATG if severe |
| Acute antibody-medi ated | Days–mont hs | Donor-specific antibodies | Treat with plasma exchange, IVIG, rituximab |
| Chronic | Months–ye ars | Immune + non-immune (interstitial fibrosis/tubular atrophy) | Gradual decline; largely untreatable — leading cause of late graft loss |

##### Long-term complications

- **Infection** (immunosuppression): **CMV** (commonest opportunistic — prophylaxis/monitoring), **BK virus** nephropathy (can mimic rejection), **PJP** (co-trimoxazole prophylaxis), fungal.
- **Malignancy: skin cancers (SCC — very high risk, sun protection essential),** **post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder (PTLD — EBV-driven).**
- **Cardiovascular disease** — leading cause of death with a functioning graft.
- **Recurrent disease** in the graft (FSGS, IgA, MPGN can recur).
- Drug toxicities, new-onset diabetes after transplant (NODAT), bone disease.

##### Delayed graft function

- Need for dialysis within the first week post-transplant, usually due to **ischaemia-reperfusion ATN** (commoner with deceased/marginal donors and long cold ischaemia time). Distinguish from rejection and CNI toxicity — often needs biopsy.

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators

| Clue | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Best dialysis access | AV fistula (matures 6–8 weeks; never cannulate/BP that arm) |
| Cloudy PD effluent + abdo pain | PD peritonitis — IP antibiotics |
| Headache/confusion/seizure at first dialysis | Dialysis disequilibrium syndrome |
| Graft fails within minutes of reperfusion | Hyperacute rejection (pre-formed antibodies) |
| Rising creatinine in transplant — rejection or drug? | CNI (tacrolimus) toxicity mimics rejection — check levels + biopsy |
| Commonest opportunistic infection post-transplant | CMV |
| Rising creatinine + viraemia mimicking rejection | BK virus nephropathy |
| Commonest malignancy after transplant | Skin cancer (SCC); also PTLD (EBV) |
