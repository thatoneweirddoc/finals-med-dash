## Renal Exam Preparation and Applied Skills

### Finals-study-2.pdf — Renal Notes, Section 11: Exam Preparation and Applied Skills (pages 83-86)

This section is applied practice rather than new content — it ties the earlier sections into the formats finals actually test: data interpretation, OSCE stations, and emergency scenarios.

#### 11.1 Data Interpretation

##### U&Es — pattern recognition

- **Urea:creatinine ratio:**
  - **High urea:creatinine (disproportionate urea)** → **pre-renal** (dehydration, GI bleed, high catabolic state) — urea is reabsorbed with water
  - **Proportionate rise** → intrinsic renal
- **AKI vs CKD on bloods:** CKD suggested by **anaemia, hyperphosphataemia,** **hypocalcaemia, raised PTH,** and (crucially) **old bloods** showing chronicity.
- **Hyperkalaemia + normal anion gap acidosis + diabetic** → think **type 4 RTA.**

##### ABG/VBG — the drill (always the same 5 steps)

- 1. pH → acidaemia/alkalaemia
- 2. Primary driver → respiratory (CO₂) or metabolic (HCO₃⁻)?
- 3. Compensation appropriate? (Winter's formula for metabolic acidosis: expected CO₂ =

1.5×HCO₃⁻ + 8 ± 2)
- 4. **Anion gap** (Na − [Cl + HCO₃⁻]); if raised → **MUDPILES**
- 5. **Delta ratio** and **osmolar gap** if indicated (mixed disorders, toxic alcohols)

##### Urinalysis panels — the discriminators (from Section 1)

- **Red cell casts + dysmorphic RBCs** → glomerulonephritis
- **Muddy-brown granular casts** → ATN
- **White cell casts + sterile pyuria** → AIN/pyelonephritis
- **Dipstick blood +ve, no RBCs** → rhabdomyolysis (myoglobin)
- **Dipstick protein −ve but heavy proteinuria** → light chains (myeloma)

##### Renal immunology panels

- **Low C3 only** → post-strep GN, C3 glomerulopathy/MPGN

- **Low C3 AND C4** → lupus nephritis, cryoglobulinaemia
- **Normal complement + pauci-immune** → ANCA vasculitis
- **Linear IF / anti-GBM** → Goodpasture
- **Anti-PLA2R** → primary membranous

##### Common traps built into questions

- A "normal" creatinine in **pregnancy** or a **muscular/cachectic** patient may hide real dysfunction
- **Trimethoprim/cimetidine** raising creatinine without true GFR change
- **Pseudohyponatraemia/hyperkalaemia** (lab artefact)
- **FeNa unreliable on diuretics** → use FeUrea
- Correcting sodium for **hyperglycaemia** before calling it true hyponatraemia

#### 11.2 OSCE Stations

##### Fluid status examination (rehearse a slick routine)

- **General:** comfortable/breathless, weight/fluid charts
- **Hypovolaemia:** dry mucous membranes, ↓skin turgor, ↓JVP, postural drop, tachycardia, cool peripheries, prolonged cap refill, low urine output
- **Hypervolaemia:** ↑JVP, peripheral/sacral oedema, lung crackles, S3, ascites
- **Always:** capillary refill, pulse, BP (lying/standing), JVP, oedema, chest, fluid balance chart, weights

##### Examining dialysis access / transplant

- **Fistula: look** (scar, aneurysm, skin changes), **feel** (thrill, warmth), **listen** (machinery bruit); comment you would **never cannulate/BP that arm**
- **Transplant: iliac fossa mass** with **hockey-stick scar;** palpate for tenderness (rejection/infection), auscultate for bruit; look for immunosuppression side effects (cushingoid, gum hypertrophy, skin lesions)

##### Counselling stations (structure: ICE → explain → check understanding → safety-net)

- **Starting dialysis:** modalities (HD vs PD), access, lifestyle impact, conservative option
- **Transplant options:** living vs deceased donor, work-up, immunosuppression and its risks
- **CKD diagnosis:** what it means, progression, modifiable factors, why BP/proteinuria control matters
- **Nephrotoxic drug avoidance:** NSAIDs, sick-day rules, over-the-counter awareness

- **Breaking bad news** (renal failure): **SPIKES** framework — Setting, Perception, Invitation,

Knowledge, Empathy, Strategy/Summary

##### Prescribing station

- **IV fluids:** choose fluid and rate for the volume state; balanced crystalloid for resuscitation; reassess
- **Hyperkalaemia:** the emergency algorithm (calcium → insulin/glucose → salbutamol → definitive removal) — see below

#### 11.3 Emergency Scenarios (know these cold)

##### Hyperkalaemia (the commonest renal emergency)

- 1. **ECG + cardiac monitor;** assess severity
- 2. **IV calcium gluconate 10% (10–30 mL)** if ECG changes — stabilises myocardium (repeat if changes persist)
- 3. **Insulin (10 units) + 25 g glucose IV** — shifts K⁺ into cells (monitor for hypoglycaemia)
- 4. **Nebulised salbutamol 10–20 mg** — adjunct shift
- 5. **Remove K⁺:** sodium zirconium cyclosilicate/patiromer; **dialysis if refractory**
- 6. **Stop** contributing drugs (ACEi/ARB, K⁺-sparing diuretics, NSAIDs); treat the cause

##### AKI with fluid overload / pulmonary oedema

- Sit up, high-flow oxygen; **IV loop diuretic** (furosemide); consider **GTN** if hypertensive;

**CPAP** if severe; **urgent dialysis/ultrafiltration** if refractory (the "O" of AEIOU)

##### Severe hyponatraemia (symptomatic — seizures/coma)

- **Hypertonic (3%) saline** in small boluses to raise sodium a few mmol/L and control symptoms
- **Cap the rise at ≤8–10 mmol/L in 24 hours** to avoid **osmotic demyelination**
- Identify and treat the cause; involve seniors early

##### Obstructed, infected kidney (urological emergency)

- **Fever + obstruction (hydronephrosis) = emergency** → **resuscitate, antibiotics, and** **urgent decompression (nephrostomy or ureteric stent)** — do not wait to remove the stone

##### Rhabdomyolysis

- **Early aggressive IV fluid resuscitation** (the key intervention); treat **hyperkalaemia;** monitor CK, U&Es, calcium; watch for **compartment syndrome** and the **polyuric**
