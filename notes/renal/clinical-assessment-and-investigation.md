## Clinical Assessment and Investigation of the Renal Patient

### Finals-study-2.pdf — Renal Notes, Section 1: Clinical Assessment & Investigation (pages 12-22)

Cross-checked against KDIGO 2012 CKD guideline, NKF/ASN 2021 eGFR Task Force,

Kidney360 (2023) fractional excretion review, StatPearls, and standard clinical texts. Units given in SI (mg/mmol) with mg/g conversions, since both appear in exams.

#### 1.1 History and Examination

##### Renal history — the symptom clusters that matter

| Symptom | What it points to |
| --- | --- |
| Oliguria (<0.5 mL/kg/hr) / anuria | AKI; anuria specifically suggests obstruction, vascular catastrophe (renal artery/vein occlusion), or severe cortical necrosis — true anuria is not typical of ATN |
| Frothy urine | Nephrotic-range proteinuria (surfactant effect of protein) |
| Haematuria — visible vs non-visible | Visible + painless → think malignancy until proven otherwise. Visible + synpharyngitic (with sore throat) → IgA nephropathy. 1–2 weeks after throat/skin infection → post-streptococcal GN |
| Oedema | Nephrotic (periorbital, worse in morning) vs cardiac/hepatic (dependent, worse in evening) |
| Uraemic symptoms | Nausea, anorexia, metallic taste, pruritus, restless legs, confusion, hiccups — late; suggests advanced CKD/need for RRT |

##### Extra-renal clues (the "why is this a systemic disease?" screen)

- **Rash** — purpuric/vasculitic (ANCA vasculitis, IgA vasculitis/HSP), malar (SLE), livedo (cholesterol emboli)
- **Arthralgia** — SLE, vasculitis, cryoglobulinaemia
- **Haemoptysis** — **pulmonary–renal syndrome:** anti-GBM (Goodpasture) or ANCA vasculitis. This is the emergency pattern to recognise.
- **Sinusitis / nasal crusting / saddle nose** — **GPA (granulomatosis with polyangiitis)**

- **Asthma + eosinophilia** — **EGPA**
- **Sensorineural deafness** — **Alport syndrome** (also lenticonus)
- **Weight loss, bone pain, anaemia** — **myeloma**

##### Drug and exposure history (always ask)

- **NSAIDs, ACEi/ARB, diuretics** — the "triple whammy" causing haemodynamic AKI
- **Aminoglycosides, vancomycin, amphotericin** — direct tubular toxicity
- **PPIs, penicillins, cephalosporins, allopurinol, NSAIDs** — acute interstitial nephritis
- **Lithium** — nephrogenic DI, chronic tubulointerstitial nephritis
- **Contrast** — recent CT/angiogram
- **Calcineurin inhibitors** (ciclosporin, tacrolimus) — in transplant patients
- Herbal/over-the-counter remedies, illicit drugs, protein/creatine supplements

##### Examination

**Fluid status assessment** (the single most examinable renal skill):
- Hypovolaemia: ↓skin turgor, dry mucous membranes, ↓JVP, postural drop, tachycardia, cool peripheries, prolonged cap refill, ↓urine output
- Hypervolaemia: ↑JVP, peripheral/sacral oedema, pulmonary crackles, S3 gallop, ascites,

↑weight
- Weight charts and fluid balance charts are more reliable than any single sign

###### Other renal-specific signs:

- **AV fistula** — inspect (scar, aneurysmal dilatation), palpate **(thrill),** auscultate **(bruit).**

Absent thrill/bruit = thrombosed fistula.
- **Transplant kidney** — palpable mass in the **iliac fossa** with an overlying hockey-stick scar (transplants are placed extraperitoneally in the iliac fossa, native kidneys usually left in situ)
- **Palpable kidneys** — bilateral, ballotable, irregular → **ADPKD.** Unilateral → tumour, hydronephrosis, compensatory hypertrophy
- **Renal bruits** — renal artery stenosis
- **Peritoneal dialysis catheter** — exit site, signs of infection
- Signs of uraemia: pallor, uraemic frost, excoriations, pericardial rub, asterixis

#### 1.2 Urine Investigations

##### Dipstick — interpretation and traps

| Finding | Interpretation | Traps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blood | Detects haem (haemoglobin peroxidase activity) | Positive with myoglobin (rhabdomyolysis) and free haemoglobin (haemolysis) — no RBCs on microscopy. Dipstick +ve blood but microscopy shows no red cells = pigment, not haematuria |
| Protein | Detects albumin mainly | Misses light chains (Bence-Jones protein) — a normal dipstick does NOT exclude myeloma. Also false +ve with concentrated urine, alkaline urine, contrast, chlorhexidine |
| Leucocytes/nitrite s | Suggests UTI | Nitrites: only Gram-negatives reduce nitrate → nitrite (false −ve with Enterococcus, Staph saprophyticus, and with frequent voiding). Sterile pyuria: TB, chlamydia, AIN, stones, treated UTI |
| Glucose | Glycosuria | If glucose in urine with normal blood glucose → proximal tubular dysfunction (Fanconi syndrome, SGLT2 inhibitor use) |

##### Urine microscopy — the casts (high-yield)

| Cast/finding | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Red cell casts + dysmorphic RBCs | Glomerulonephritis — pathognomonic of glomerular bleeding |
| White cell casts | Acute interstitial nephritis or pyelonephritis |
| Granular / "muddy-brown" casts | Acute tubular necrosis (ATN) — sloughed tubular epithelium |
| Hyaline casts | Non-specific — concentrated urine, dehydration, exercise; can be normal |
| Fatty casts / "Maltese cross" under polarised light | Nephrotic syndrome (lipiduria) |
| Broad, waxy casts | Chronic kidney disease (formed in dilated, atrophic tubules) |
| Crystals | Uric acid (tumour lysis), calcium oxalate (ethylene glycol — envelope-shaped), cystine (hexagonal) |
| Eosinophiluria | Historically for AIN — poor sensitivity and specificity; no longer recommended to rely on it |

##### Reading it the other way — disease → expected urinalysis pattern

The casts table above works from finding to diagnosis. Exams more often hand you a disease (or a vignette) and expect you to predict the urine. Learn it in both directions.

| Condition | Dipstick | Microscopy | Protein quantification | Key discriminator |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-renal AKI | Bland (may be trace protein) | Hyaline casts only | Minimal | FeNa <1%, urine osm >500, concentrated urine |
| Acute tubular necrosis | Trace–1+ protein, ± blood | Muddy-brown granular casts, renal tubular epithelial cells | Mild (<1 g/day) | FeNa >1–2%, urine osm ~300 (isosthenuric) |
| Acute interstitial nephritis | Protein +, blood +, leucocytes +, nitrites −ve | White cell casts, sterile pyuria, ± eosinophiluria | Mild (usually <1 g/day) | Sterile pyuria + drug history (PPI, NSAID, penicillin, allopurinol); ± rash, fever, eosinophilia |
| Nephritic syndrome (any GN) | Blood +++, protein + to ++ | Red cell casts + dysmorphic RBCs | Sub-nephrotic (<3.5 g/day) | Red cell casts = glomerular bleeding; plus hypertension, oliguria, oedema |
| Nephrotic syndrome | Protein ++++, blood −ve/trace | Fatty casts, oval fat bodies, "Maltese crosses" (polarised light) | >3.5 g/day; ACR >220 mg/mmol | Heavy proteinuria + hypoalbuminaemia + oedema + hyperlipidaemia |
| IgA nephropathy | Blood ++ (often visible haematuria) | Red cell casts, dysmorphic RBCs | Variable, usually sub-nephrotic | Synpharyngitic — haematuria during the sore throat (1–2 days), normal complement |
| Post-streptococ cal GN | Blood ++, protein + | Red cell casts, dysmorphic RBCs | Sub-nephrotic | Haematuria 1–2 weeks AFTER infection; LOW C3, ↑ASOT/anti-DNase B |
| Lower UTI / cystitis | Leucocytes +, nitrites +, blood ± | Pyuria, bacteriuria, no casts | Nil/trace | No casts — casts mean the kidney is involved |
| Pyelonephritis | Leucocytes +, nitrites +, blood ± | White cell casts, pyuria, bacteriuria | Nil/trace | White cell casts + fever + loin pain = upper tract |
| Rhabdomyolysi s | Blood +++ | No red cells on microscopy | Mild | Dipstick–microscopy mismatch; ↑↑CK, ↑K⁺, ↑PO₄³⁻, ↓Ca²⁺, myoglobinuria (tea-coloured urine) |
| Myeloma / cast nephropathy | Protein often NEGATIVE or low | Bland, ± casts | PCR high but ACR low — the gap is the clue | Dipstick misses light chains. Send urine electrophoresis / serum free light chains; ↑Ca²⁺, anaemia, bone pain |
| Renal stones | Blood ++, ± leucocytes | RBCs, crystals (uric acid, calcium oxalate, cystine) | Nil | Colicky loin-to-groin pain; non-contrast CT KUB |
| Urological malignancy | Blood ++ (painless, visible) | Isomorphic (normal-shape d) RBCs, no casts | Nil | Painless visible haematuria + smoking/age → cystoscopy + CT urogram |
| Diabetic nephropathy | Protein + → ++++ (progressive) | Bland — relatively acellular sediment | Progressive: A2 → A3 → nephrotic | Bland sediment + long diabetes duration + retinopathy (its absence should prompt a rethink) |
| Fanconi syndrome | Glucose + with normal blood glucose, protein + | Bland | Low-grade tubular proteinuria | Glycosuria with normoglycaemia + hypophosphataemia, hypokalaemia, type 2 RTA, aminoaciduria |
| Ethylene glycol poisoning | ± blood | Envelope-sha ped calcium oxalate crystals | Nil | Raised anion gap AND osmolar gap metabolic acidosis |

**The three mismatch patterns worth memorising** (each is a classic exam trick):
- 1. **Dipstick blood positive, microscopy shows no RBCs** → **myoglobin** (rhabdomyolysis) or free haemoglobin (haemolysis)
- 2. **Dipstick protein negative/low, but heavy proteinuria on PCR** → **light chains** **(myeloma)** — dipstick detects albumin only
- 3. **Pyuria with negative culture (sterile pyuria)** → **AIN, TB, chlamydia, stones, or**

###### recently treated UTI

##### Quantifying proteinuria

- **ACR (albumin:creatinine ratio)** — preferred for **screening and CKD staging,** more sensitive at low levels; the KDIGO standard.
- **PCR (protein:creatinine ratio)** — captures **non-albumin proteins,** so preferred when **light chains/myeloma** or tubular proteinuria is suspected.
- Spot early-morning sample avoids the need for a 24-hour collection (which is prone to collection error).

###### KDIGO albuminuria categories (A1–A3):

| Categor y | ACR (mg/mmol) | ACR (mg/g) | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | <3 | <30 | Normal to mildly increased |
| A2 | 3–30 | 30–300 | Moderately increased (old term: "microalbuminuria") |
| A3 | >30 | >300 | Severely increased (old term: "macroalbuminuria") |

- **Nephrotic-range proteinuria** = **>3.5 g/24h,** corresponding roughly to **PCR >300–350** **mg/mmol** or **ACR >220 mg/mmol** (KDIGO note: nephrotic syndrome usually implies albumin excretion >2200 mg/24h).
- Terms "micro-" and "macroalbuminuria" are **deprecated** by KDIGO in favour of A1–A3

— worth using the modern terms in exams.

##### Urine electrolytes and fractional excretion

**FeNa (fractional excretion of sodium)** = (urine Na × plasma creatinine) / (plasma Na × urine creatinine) × 100

| FeNa | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| <1% | Pre-renal AKI — tubules working, avidly reabsorbing sodium |
| >1–2% | ATN — tubules damaged, can't reabsorb sodium |

###### Critical caveats (this is where exams catch people):

- **FeNa is unreliable if the patient is on diuretics** (diuretic forces natriuresis → FeNa

>1% despite hypovolaemia). **Use FeUrea instead** (<35% suggests pre-renal), because urea is reabsorbed proximally and is less affected by loop/thiazide diuretics.
- **FeNa can be <1% in non-pre-renal states:** contrast nephropathy, pigment nephropathy (rhabdomyolysis), acute glomerulonephritis, hepatorenal syndrome, cardiorenal syndrome, early obstruction.
- FeNa was validated in **oliguric** patients — less reliable in non-oliguric AKI and in CKD.

###### Other urine electrolyte uses:

- **Urine sodium** in hyponatraemia: <20 mmol/L → extra-renal loss/hypovolaemia; >20–40 mmol/L → renal loss, SIADH, diuretics, adrenal insufficiency
- **Urine anion gap** (Na + K − Cl) in normal anion gap metabolic acidosis: **negative** → GI bicarbonate loss (diarrhoea, appropriate NH₄⁺ excretion); **positive** → **renal tubular** **acidosis** (failure to excrete NH₄⁺)

##### Urine osmolality

- **Concentrated urine (>500 mOsm/kg)** with AKI → **pre-renal** (tubules can still concentrate; ADH working)
- **Isosthenuric urine (~300 mOsm/kg, i.e. same as plasma)** → **ATN** (lost concentrating ability)
- In **hyponatraemia:** urine osm <100 mOsm/kg → appropriately dilute (primary polydipsia, low solute intake/"beer potomania"); urine osm >100 → inappropriately concentrated

(SIADH, hypovolaemia, diuretics)

- In **diabetes insipidus:** dilute urine despite raised plasma osmolality — the water deprivation test discriminates cranial from nephrogenic

#### 1.3 Blood Tests and Estimating Renal Function

##### eGFR equations

- **CKD-EPI 2021 (creatinine)** is the current standard — this is the **race-free refit,** recommended by the NKF/ASN Task Force after they concluded race is a **social, not** **biological, construct** and should not appear in eGFR equations.
- Compared with the old 2009 race-based equation: for the same creatinine, the 2021 equation gives a **lower eGFR in Black patients** and a **higher eGFR in non-Black** **patients.**
- **MDRD** is older and less accurate at higher GFRs — largely superseded.
- **Cockcroft–Gault** estimates creatinine clearance (not eGFR), uses actual body weight, and is still used for some **drug dosing** (e.g. DOACs) — worth knowing it's a different beast.

##### Limitations of eGFR — when it lies

- **Not valid in AKI / non-steady state.** eGFR assumes a stable creatinine. In rapidly rising creatinine, true GFR is far lower than the eGFR suggests (the equation lags behind reality).
- **Extremes of muscle mass:** overestimates GFR in the cachectic, elderly, amputees, and those with muscle-wasting disease; underestimates in bodybuilders and those on creatine supplements.
- **Diet:** vegan/low-protein diets lower creatinine; a large cooked-meat meal transiently raises it.
- **Not validated** in pregnancy, children, or acute illness.
- Indexed to **1.73 m² body surface area** — misleading at extremes of body size.

##### Cystatin C — when and why

- A protein produced by **all nucleated cells at a constant rate,** freely filtered — so it is **independent of muscle mass,** which is its main advantage.
- **Use it as a confirmatory test when creatinine is likely to be unreliable:** extremes of muscle mass, amputees, cirrhosis, malnutrition, or to confirm eGFR near a clinical decision threshold. NKF specifically suggests it for **eGFRcr 45–59 with ACR <30 mg/g** (i.e. is this really CKD?).
- The **CKD-EPI 2021 creatinine–cystatin C combined equation is the most accurate** of the available equations.

- **But cystatin C has its own confounders: smoking, obesity, inflammation, thyroid** **disorders, and corticosteroids** all affect it.

##### Creatinine pitfalls (drugs that raise creatinine without lowering GFR)

- **Trimethoprim, cimetidine, dolutegravir, cobicistat, fenofibrate, ritonavir** — these **block tubular secretion of creatinine,** producing a rise in serum creatinine with **no** **true fall in GFR.** Recognising this avoids an unnecessary AKI workup.
- Conversely, in advanced CKD, tubular secretion of creatinine means **creatinine** **clearance overestimates true GFR.**

#### 1.4 Imaging and Biopsy

##### Renal ultrasound — first-line

- **The key question: is there obstruction?** US is the first-line test to exclude post-renal

AKI (look for **hydronephrosis).** Do it within 24 hours in AKI of unknown cause.
- **Kidney size:**
  - **Small (<9 cm), echogenic, poor corticomedullary differentiation** → **chronic** disease (irreversible)
  - **Normal or enlarged** → acute process, or specific causes of large kidneys:

###### diabetic nephropathy (early), amyloid, HIV-associated nephropathy,

###### myeloma, ADPKD, infiltration

  - **Asymmetry (>1.5–2 cm difference)** → renovascular disease or unilateral pathology
- **Caveat:** hydronephrosis may be **absent** in early obstruction, volume depletion, or **retroperitoneal fibrosis/encasing malignancy** (the ureters are trapped and cannot dilate).

##### Other imaging

| Modality | Use |
| --- | --- |
| CT KUB (non-contrast) | Gold standard for renal stones — no contrast needed |
| CT with contrast | Renal masses, staging RCC. Weigh contrast-associated AKI risk |
| CT / MR angiography | Renal artery stenosis, vascular anatomy. MRA avoids iodinated contrast, but gadolinium is contraindicated in severe CKD (risk of nephrogenic systemic fibrosis) |
| DMSA | Static scan — renal cortical scarring and differential function (e.g. reflux nephropathy) |
| MAG3 | Dynamic scan — renal perfusion, drainage, and obstruction (± furosemide to assess drainage) |
| Micturating cystourethrogram (MCUG) | Vesicoureteric reflux in children |

Memory hook: **DMSA** = **Damage/scarring** (static); **MAG3** = **Movement/drainage** (dynamic).

##### Renal biopsy

###### Indications:

- Nephrotic syndrome in an adult (in children, presumed minimal change → trial steroids first, biopsy only if steroid-resistant)
- Nephritic syndrome / suspected glomerulonephritis / rapidly progressive GN
- AKI of unknown cause where pre-renal and post-renal have been excluded
- Unexplained CKD with normal-sized kidneys
- Systemic disease with renal involvement (SLE — the class determines treatment)
- Transplant dysfunction (to diagnose and classify rejection)

###### Contraindications:

- **Uncorrected bleeding diathesis / anticoagulation / thrombocytopenia** (the main one)
- **Uncontrolled hypertension**
- **Single functioning kidney** (relative — risk of losing the only kidney)
- **Small, shrunken kidneys** — will show end-stage fibrosis only; high risk, no useful answer
- Active UTI, hydronephrosis, uncooperative patient

###### Complications:

- **Bleeding** — the dominant risk: haematuria (visible in ~10%), perinephric haematoma, rarely requiring **transfusion, embolisation, or nephrectomy**
- **Arteriovenous fistula** formation
- Pain, infection, damage to adjacent organs
- Death (rare, <0.1%)

###### What the histology answers — always three modalities:

- 1. **Light microscopy** — architecture: proliferation, crescents, sclerosis, nodules, inflammation
- 2. **Immunofluorescence** — what is deposited and where: linear IgG (anti-GBM), granular (immune complex), mesangial IgA (IgA nephropathy), "full house" (lupus), **pauci-immune** (ANCA vasculitis)
- 3. **Electron microscopy** — deposit location (subepithelial/subendothelial/mesangial), podocyte foot process effacement (minimal c hange), GBM thinning/splitting (Alport,

TBMD)

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators

| Clinical question | The test that answers it |
| --- | --- |
| Is this an obstruction? | Renal ultrasound |
| Is this pre-renal or ATN? | FeNa (<1% pre-renal), urine osmolality, muddy-brown casts — but check for diuretics first, then use FeUrea (<35%) |
| Is this glomerular bleeding? | Red cell casts + dysmorphic RBCs |
| Is this AKI or CKD? | Small echogenic kidneys, anaemia, hyperphosphataemia, hypocalcaemia, previous bloods → CKD |
| Dipstick blood but no RBCs? | Myoglobin (rhabdomyolysis) or haemoglobin |
| Normal dipstick protein but suspect myeloma? | Dipstick misses light chains — send urine electrophoresis / serum free light chains, use PCR not ACR |
| Creatinine up but patient well? | Check for trimethoprim/cimetidine (blocked tubular secretion) |
| Is the eGFR trustworthy? | Not in AKI, not at extremes of muscle mass — consider cystatin C |
