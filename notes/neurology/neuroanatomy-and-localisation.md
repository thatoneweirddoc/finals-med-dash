## Neuroanatomy and localisation


### Neurology Master Notes — Neuroanatomy and localisation

#### 2.1 Cerebral cortex

| Lobe | Key functions | Lesion syndrome |
|---|---|---|
| Frontal | Motor (precentral gyrus), Broca's area (dominant inferior frontal), executive function, saccades (frontal eye field), continence | Contralateral weakness, expressive aphasia, disinhibition/apathy, primitive reflexes, gaze deviation **towards** the lesion |
| Parietal | Primary sensory (postcentral gyrus), spatial awareness (non-dominant), calculation/praxis (dominant) | Cortical sensory loss, **neglect** (non-dominant), **Gerstmann syndrome** (dominant: acalculia, agraphia, finger agnosia, left–right disorientation), inferior quadrantanopia |
| Temporal | Auditory cortex, Wernicke's area (dominant superior temporal), memory (hippocampus), Meyer's loop | Receptive aphasia, amnesia, **superior** quadrantanopia ("pie in the sky"), complex partial seizures |
| Occipital | Vision | Homonymous hemianopia with macular sparing, cortical blindness (Anton syndrome if denied) |
| Insula/cingulate | Autonomic, pain affect, motivation | Akinetic mutism (bilateral cingulate) |

**Language.** Broca's = non-fluent, effortful, comprehension intact, patient frustrated. Wernicke's = fluent but empty/paraphasic, comprehension impaired, patient unaware. Conduction aphasia (arcuate fasciculus) = fluent, comprehension intact, **repetition disproportionately impaired**. Global aphasia = all impaired (large dominant MCA territory). Left hemisphere is dominant in ~95% of right-handers and ~70% of left-handers.

#### 2.2 Arterial supply and vascular territories

The **circle of Willis** joins anterior (internal carotid) and posterior (vertebrobasilar) circulations: anterior communicating artery between the two ACAs; posterior communicating arteries linking each ICA to its PCA.

| Artery | Territory | Signature deficit |
|---|---|---|
| Anterior cerebral | Medial frontal and parietal | **Leg > arm** weakness, abulia, urinary incontinence, grasp reflex |
| Middle cerebral | Lateral hemisphere | **Face and arm > leg** weakness, aphasia (dominant) or neglect (non-dominant), contralateral homonymous hemianopia, eyes deviate to the side of the lesion |
| Posterior cerebral | Occipital, medial temporal, thalamus | Homonymous hemianopia **with macular sparing**, alexia without agraphia, thalamic pain syndrome |
| Lenticulostriates (MCA branches) | Internal capsule, basal ganglia | **Pure motor lacunar stroke** — dense equal weakness of face/arm/leg with no cortical signs |
| Basilar | Pons, midbrain, cerebellum | Reduced consciousness, quadriparesis, cranial nerve signs, locked-in syndrome |
| PICA | Lateral medulla | **Lateral medullary (Wallenberg) syndrome** |
| AICA | Lateral pons, inner ear | Lateral pontine syndrome **with deafness** |
| Anterior spinal | Medial medulla | Contralateral hemiparesis + tongue deviation to lesion + loss of vibration |

**Watershed (borderzone) infarction** — between ACA/MCA and MCA/PCA territories, caused by global hypoperfusion (cardiac arrest, severe hypotension, critical carotid stenosis). Classic picture: **"man-in-a-barrel"** proximal arm and thigh weakness with preserved distal power, plus transcortical aphasia.

**Venous:** superior sagittal sinus, transverse → sigmoid → internal jugular; deep system via internal cerebral veins → vein of Galen → straight sinus. Cortical vein or sinus thrombosis causes infarcts that **cross arterial territories and are often haemorrhagic** — the discriminator.

#### 2.3 Internal capsule, thalamus, basal ganglia

The **internal capsule** packs the entire corticospinal and corticobulbar output into a few millimetres, which is why a small lacunar infarct there causes a dense hemiplegia with no cortical signs. The **posterior limb** carries motor fibres (face → arm → leg, anterior to posterior); the genu carries corticobulbar fibres; the retrolenticular part carries the optic radiation.

**Thalamus** — the relay for essentially everything except olfaction. VPL (body sensation), VPM (face sensation), LGN (vision), MGN (hearing), VL/VA (motor from cerebellum and basal ganglia). Thalamic stroke → contralateral hemisensory loss of all modalities, sometimes followed by **Déjerine–Roussy** central post-stroke pain.

**Basal ganglia** — caudate + putamen (striatum), globus pallidus, subthalamic nucleus, substantia nigra.

#### 2.4 Brainstem and classic syndromes

**Rules that solve most brainstem questions:**
1. **Crossed signs** = brainstem. Ipsilateral cranial nerve deficit + contralateral long-tract (motor or sensory) deficit.
2. **Which cranial nerve is involved tells you the level** — midbrain III/IV, pons V/VI/VII/VIII, medulla IX/X/XI/XII.
3. **Medial** structures (motor, medial lemniscus, hypoglossal) are supplied by paramedian branches; **lateral** structures (spinothalamic, spinal trigeminal, sympathetic, nucleus ambiguus) by circumferential arteries.

| Syndrome | Vessel/level | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Weber** | Midbrain, PCA branch | Ipsilateral CN III palsy + contralateral hemiparesis |
| **Benedikt** | Midbrain tegmentum | Ipsilateral CN III palsy + contralateral ataxia/tremor (red nucleus) |
| **Millard–Gubler** | Ventral pons | Ipsilateral CN VI and VII palsy + contralateral hemiparesis |
| **Wallenberg (lateral medullary)** | PICA/vertebral | Ipsilateral: facial pain/temperature loss, Horner's, ataxia, dysphagia and hoarseness (nucleus ambiguus), vertigo/nystagmus. **Contralateral: body pain/temperature loss. Face and body dissociated, and no limb weakness** |
| **Medial medullary (Déjerine)** | Anterior spinal | Contralateral hemiparesis + contralateral vibration loss + **tongue deviates towards the lesion** |
| **Locked-in** | Basilar, ventral pons | Quadriplegia + anarthria, **vertical eye movements and blinking preserved**, fully aware |
| **Internuclear ophthalmoplegia** | MLF | Failure of adduction on the side of the lesion + nystagmus of the abducting eye; convergence intact. **Bilateral INO in a young adult = MS until proven otherwise** |

> **Hook for Wallenberg:** "*Don't pick a (PICA) horse that can't eat*" — Horner's, ataxia, dysphagia/hoarseness, crossed sensory loss, and **no weakness**.

#### 2.5 The cranial nerves

| CN | Name | Function | Exits skull via | Key lesion sign |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | Olfactory | Smell | Cribriform plate | Anosmia (early PD, frontal meningioma, head injury) |
| II | Optic | Vision | Optic canal | Field defect localised by site (see 2.6) |
| III | Oculomotor | All extraocular muscles except LR/SO; levator; parasympathetic pupil | Superior orbital fissure | "Down and out", ptosis, **mydriasis if compressive** |
| IV | Trochlear | Superior oblique | Superior orbital fissure | Vertical diplopia worse looking down/in; head tilt away |
| V | Trigeminal | Facial sensation, muscles of mastication | V1 SOF, V2 foramen rotundum, V3 foramen ovale | Sensory loss, jaw deviates **towards** weak side |
| VI | Abducens | Lateral rectus | Superior orbital fissure | Failure of abduction; **false localising sign in raised ICP** |
| VII | Facial | Facial muscles, taste anterior ⅔, stapedius, lacrimation/salivation | Internal acoustic meatus → stylomastoid foramen | LMN: whole side including forehead. UMN: forehead spared |
| VIII | Vestibulocochlear | Hearing, balance | Internal acoustic meatus | Sensorineural loss, vertigo |
| IX | Glossopharyngeal | Taste posterior ⅓, gag afferent, parotid | Jugular foramen | Loss of gag sensation |
| X | Vagus | Palate, pharynx, larynx, parasympathetic | Jugular foramen | Uvula deviates **away** from lesion, hoarseness |
| XI | Accessory | SCM, trapezius | Jugular foramen | Weak shoulder shrug and head turn to opposite side |
| XII | Hypoglossal | Tongue | Hypoglossal canal | Tongue deviates **towards** the lesion (LMN) |

> **Hook:** *"Uvula runs away from the lesion, tongue points to it."*

**Third nerve palsy — the exam's favourite discriminator.** Parasympathetic pupillary fibres run on the *outside* of the nerve, so:

- **Pupil-involving ("surgical")** — compressive: posterior communicating artery aneurysm, uncal herniation, tumour. Painful. **Urgent CT angiography.**
- **Pupil-sparing ("medical")** — microvascular infarction of the nerve core: diabetes, hypertension. Usually recovers in ~3 months.

#### 2.6 Visual pathway and pupils

Retina → optic nerve → **optic chiasm** (nasal fibres cross) → optic tract → lateral geniculate nucleus → optic radiations (**Meyer's loop through temporal lobe carries superior field**; parietal fibres carry inferior field) → occipital cortex.

| Lesion site | Field defect |
|---|---|
| Optic nerve | Monocular visual loss / central scotoma |
| Chiasm | **Bitemporal hemianopia** (pituitary adenoma, craniopharyngioma) |
| Optic tract | Contralateral homonymous hemianopia (incongruous) |
| Temporal (Meyer's loop) | Contralateral **superior** quadrantanopia |
| Parietal radiation | Contralateral **inferior** quadrantanopia |
| Occipital cortex | Congruous homonymous hemianopia **with macular sparing** |

**Pupillary pathways.** Light reflex: retina → optic nerve → pretectal nucleus → *bilateral* Edinger–Westphal nuclei → CN III → ciliary ganglion → sphincter pupillae (hence the consensual response). **Relative afferent pupillary defect (Marcus Gunn)** = optic nerve or severe retinal disease (classically optic neuritis) — the swinging-torch test shows paradoxical dilatation. **Argyll Robertson pupil** (neurosyphilis) — small, irregular, accommodates but does not react ("prostitute's pupil"). **Holmes–Adie** — dilated, poorly reactive, slow tonic constriction, absent ankle reflexes, benign, young women.

**Horner's syndrome** (ptosis, miosis, anhidrosis, enophthalmos) localises along a three-neurone chain:

| Order | Path | Causes | Anhidrosis pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| First (central) | Hypothalamus → cord T1 | Stroke (Wallenberg), cord lesion, MS | Whole side of body |
| Second (preganglionic) | T1 → superior cervical ganglion, over lung apex | **Pancoast tumour**, thyroid/neck surgery, trauma | Face and neck |
| Third (postganglionic) | Along ICA to orbit | **Carotid dissection**, cluster headache, cavernous sinus | Little or none (medial forehead) |

*Painful Horner's with neck pain in a young person = carotid dissection until proven otherwise.*

#### 2.7 Cerebellum

Anatomy: two hemispheres and midline vermis; three peduncles (superior — output to red nucleus/thalamus; middle — input from contralateral pontine nuclei; inferior — input from cord and inferior olive). Functional divisions: **vestibulocerebellum** (flocculonodular — balance, eye movements), **spinocerebellum** (vermis — gait, trunk), **cerebrocerebellum** (hemispheres — limb coordination and planning).

Signs — **DANISH**: Dysdiadochokinesia, Ataxia, Nystagmus (fast phase towards lesion), Intention tremor, Slurred/scanning speech, Hypotonia/heel–shin incoordination.

Discriminator: **cerebellar ataxia does not worsen much on eye closure**; **sensory (dorsal column) ataxia does — a positive Romberg test.**

#### 2.8 Spinal cord

The cord runs from the foramen magnum to the **conus medullaris at L1/L2 in adults** (L3 in neonates); below that the **cauda equina**. There are cervical and lumbar enlargements. Lumbar puncture is therefore performed at **L3/4 or L4/5**, below the conus.

**Tracts to know in cross-section:** dorsal columns posteriorly (gracile medial = legs, cuneate lateral = arms), lateral corticospinal tract laterally, spinothalamic tract anterolaterally.

**Blood supply:** one **anterior spinal artery** (supplies the anterior two-thirds — corticospinal and spinothalamic) and two posterior spinal arteries (dorsal columns), reinforced by radicular arteries including the **artery of Adamkiewicz** (usually T9–T12, left-sided). Anterior spinal artery occlusion → paraplegia and loss of pain/temperature **with preserved vibration and proprioception**.

| Cord syndrome | Cause | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Complete transection | Trauma | Loss of all modalities below, UMN signs below, LMN at level, sphincter loss |
| **Brown-Séquard** (hemisection) | Trauma, tumour, MS | Ipsilateral weakness + dorsal column loss; contralateral pain/temperature loss |
| **Anterior cord** | ASA occlusion, aortic surgery | Weakness + pain/temperature loss; **vibration/proprioception spared** |
| **Central cord** | Hyperextension in cervical spondylosis; syringomyelia | **Arms > legs** weakness; "cape" dissociated sensory loss |
| **Posterior cord** | B12 deficiency, tabes dorsalis | Loss of vibration/proprioception, sensory ataxia, positive Romberg |
| **Subacute combined degeneration** | B12/copper deficiency | Dorsal columns + corticospinal: **absent ankle jerks with extensor plantars** |
| **Conus medullaris** | Tumour, trauma | Early symmetrical sphincter dysfunction, mixed UMN/LMN, saddle anaesthesia |
| **Cauda equina** | Central disc prolapse, tumour | Asymmetric **LMN** leg weakness, saddle anaesthesia, late retention |

**Key sensory levels:** C4 shoulder tip, T4 nipple, T10 umbilicus, L1 inguinal, S2–S4 perianal.

#### 2.9 Roots, plexuses and peripheral nerves

| Root | Motor | Reflex | Sensory |
|---|---|---|---|
| C5 | Shoulder abduction | Biceps | Lateral arm |
| C6 | Elbow flexion, wrist extension | Supinator | Thumb |
| C7 | Elbow extension, wrist flexion | Triceps | Middle finger |
| C8 | Finger flexion | — | Little finger |
| T1 | Finger abduction | — | Medial arm |
| L2/3 | Hip flexion, adduction | — | Anterior thigh |
| L4 | Knee extension, ankle dorsiflexion (inversion) | Knee | Medial shin |
| L5 | Great toe extension, ankle dorsiflexion, hip abduction | — | Dorsum of foot, first web space |
| S1 | Plantarflexion, eversion | Ankle | Sole, lateral foot |

**Brachial plexus:** roots C5–T1 → trunks (upper, middle, lower) → divisions → cords (lateral, posterior, medial) → branches. **Erb's palsy** (C5/6, upper trunk) = "waiter's tip" adducted internally rotated arm; **Klumpke's** (C8/T1) = claw hand ± Horner's.

| Nerve | Roots | Motor loss | Sensory loss | Classic cause |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Median | C6–T1 | Thumb abduction/opposition (LOAF muscles) | Radial 3½ digits, palmar | Carpal tunnel |
| Ulnar | C8–T1 | Interossei, adductor pollicis (**Froment's sign**), claw hand | Ulnar 1½ digits, dorsal and palmar | Elbow compression |
| Radial | C5–T1 | **Wrist drop**, finger extension | First dorsal web space | Spiral groove ("Saturday night palsy") |
| Axillary | C5/6 | Shoulder abduction | Regimental badge | Shoulder dislocation, humeral neck fracture |
| Common peroneal | L4–S1 | **Foot drop**, eversion | Lateral shin, dorsum foot | Fibular neck compression |
| Tibial | L4–S3 | Plantarflexion, inversion | Sole | Tarsal tunnel |
| Femoral | L2–4 | Knee extension | Anterior thigh, medial calf (saphenous) | Pelvic surgery, haematoma |
| Lateral cutaneous of thigh | L2/3 | None | Lateral thigh | **Meralgia paraesthetica** |

**Foot drop discriminators:** common peroneal palsy — weak dorsiflexion **and eversion**, inversion spared, ankle jerk intact. L5 radiculopathy — weak dorsiflexion **and inversion and hip abduction**, often with back pain. Sciatic — all of the above plus weak plantarflexion and absent ankle jerk.

**Median vs ulnar claw paradox:** the *higher* the ulnar lesion, the *less* the claw ("ulnar paradox"), because flexor digitorum profundus to the ring and little fingers is also denervated.

#### 2.10 Autonomic nervous system

Sympathetic outflow is **thoracolumbar (T1–L2)**, short preganglionic/long postganglionic, mostly noradrenergic. Parasympathetic outflow is **craniosacral (CN III, VII, IX, X and S2–S4)**, long preganglionic/short postganglionic, cholinergic. The vagus supplies viscera to the splenic flexure; S2–S4 ("**S2, 3, 4 keeps the pee off the floor**") supply the distal colon, bladder and genitalia.

**Bladder control:** parasympathetic S2–S4 (detrusor contraction, pelvic nerve), sympathetic T10–L2 (relaxation and sphincter tone), somatic S2–S4 pudendal (external sphincter). A **suprapontine or cord lesion** gives a spastic, small-capacity, reflex bladder with urge incontinence; a **cauda equina/conus lesion** gives a flaccid, atonic bladder with retention and overflow — the discriminator that matters in acute back pain.

Autonomic failure appears in MSA, Parkinson's, diabetes, amyloidosis and GBS: postural hypotension, anhidrosis, gastroparesis, erectile dysfunction, fixed heart rate.
