## Neurological Assessment


### Neurology Master Notes — Neurological Assessment

*Sources: Kumar & Clark *Clinical Medicine*; Macleod's Clinical Examination; Blumenfeld *Neuroanatomy through Clinical Cases*; NICE guidance (head injury NG232, suspected neurological conditions NG127, stroke NG128); Merck Manual Professional; StatPearls. Scope: how to take the history, localise, examine, and choose and interpret investigations.*

---

#### 3.1 The history — four questions that do most of the work

1. **Where is the lesion?** Driven by the *pattern* of deficit (see 3.2).
2. **What is the pathology?** Driven by the *time course*.
3. **Why this patient?** Risk factors, family history, drugs, occupation, travel.
4. **How disabling is it?** Function, driving, work, safety.

**Time course is the single most useful piece of information in neurology:**

| Onset and evolution | Pathology suggested |
|---|---|
| Seconds — maximal at onset | Vascular (ischaemic or haemorrhagic stroke), seizure, syncope |
| Seconds–minutes with a spreading "march" | Seizure (seconds), **migraine aura (5–60 min, spreads slowly)** |
| Minutes–hours | Infection, inflammation, metabolic, migraine |
| Days–weeks | Inflammatory/demyelinating, infective (TB, abscess), subdural |
| Weeks–months, progressive | Tumour, degenerative, chronic compressive |
| Months–years | Neurodegenerative, hereditary |
| Relapsing and remitting | MS, migraine, epilepsy, periodic paralysis |
| Fatigable / diurnal | Myasthenia gravis |

> **Hook:** *Stroke is sudden, seizure is fast and stereotyped, migraine aura creeps, tumour crawls.*

#### 3.2 The localisation algorithm

Work top-down. The key first split is **UMN versus LMN**, then the distribution.

| Level | Clues that put the lesion here |
|---|---|
| **Cortex** | Higher function involved — aphasia, neglect, apraxia, cortical sensory loss (astereognosis, agraphaesthesia), seizures, homonymous field defect |
| **Subcortical/internal capsule** | Dense, equal face/arm/leg deficit **without** cortical signs |
| **Brainstem** | **Crossed signs**; cranial nerve palsies with contralateral long-tract signs; diplopia, vertigo, dysarthria, dysphagia, reduced consciousness |
| **Cerebellum** | Ataxia, nystagmus, dysmetria, intention tremor — ipsilateral, no weakness |
| **Spinal cord** | A **sensory level**, bilateral signs, sphincter disturbance, UMN below and LMN at the level |
| **Root** | Dermatomal pain and sensory loss, myotomal weakness, lost reflex, worse on cough/strain |
| **Plexus** | Multiple roots/nerves in one limb, pain, patchy |
| **Peripheral nerve** | Individual nerve territory (mononeuropathy), or length-dependent glove-and-stocking (polyneuropathy) |
| **NMJ** | **Fatigable**, no sensory loss, normal reflexes, ocular/bulbar predominance |
| **Muscle** | **Proximal symmetrical** weakness, no sensory loss, reflexes preserved until late, raised CK |

**Weakness pattern shortcut:**

| Pattern | Think |
|---|---|
| Proximal symmetrical | Myopathy |
| Distal symmetrical + sensory loss | Polyneuropathy |
| Fatigable, ocular/bulbar | Myasthenia gravis |
| Asymmetric, mixed UMN and LMN, no sensory signs | **Motor neurone disease** |
| Ascending, areflexic, days | Guillain–Barré syndrome |
| Hemiparesis | Contralateral hemisphere or brainstem |
| Paraparesis with sensory level | Spinal cord |
| Paraparesis, flaccid, asymmetric, saddle anaesthesia | Cauda equina |

#### 3.3 Examination essentials

**Sequence:** inspection (posture, wasting, fasciculations, tremor, involuntary movements) → tone → power (MRC 0–5) → reflexes → coordination → sensation → gait.

**MRC power grades:** 0 none; 1 flicker; 2 movement with gravity eliminated; 3 against gravity; 4 against resistance but reduced; 5 normal.

**Gait is the highest-yield single observation** and is often the OSCE opener:

| Gait | Description | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| Hemiplegic | Circumduction, flexed arm | Stroke |
| Spastic paraparetic ("scissoring") | Stiff, adducted legs | Cord lesion, cerebral palsy, HSP |
| Ataxic (cerebellar) | Broad-based, unsteady, falls **towards the lesion** | Cerebellar disease, alcohol |
| Sensory ataxic | Stamping, watches feet, **Romberg positive** | Dorsal column disease, B12, tabes |
| Parkinsonian | Small shuffling steps, reduced arm swing, festination, difficulty turning | Parkinson's disease |
| High-stepping | Foot drop | Peroneal palsy, L5 root, peripheral neuropathy |
| Waddling | Trendelenburg, pelvis drops | Proximal myopathy |
| Apraxic ("magnetic", feet stuck) | Wide-based, cautious, upright | **Normal pressure hydrocephalus**, frontal disease |
| Antalgic | Short stance on painful side | Pain |
| Functional | Inconsistent, excessive effort, improves with distraction | Functional neurological disorder |

**Signs worth having a stock answer for:** pronator drift (subtle pyramidal weakness), Hoffmann's sign (cervical myelopathy), Lhermitte's phenomenon (electric shock down spine on neck flexion — cervical cord: MS, cord compression, B12), Uhthoff's phenomenon (heat-induced worsening — MS), Froment's sign (ulnar), Trendelenburg (gluteus medius/L5), Gowers' sign (proximal myopathy in children).

#### 3.4 Assessing consciousness

**Glasgow Coma Scale (3–15)** — always report the three components, not just the total.

| Eye opening (4) | Verbal (5) | Motor (6) |
|---|---|---|
| 4 spontaneous | 5 orientated | 6 obeys commands |
| 3 to voice | 4 confused | 5 localises to pain |
| 2 to pain | 3 inappropriate words | 4 withdraws from pain |
| 1 none | 2 incomprehensible sounds | 3 abnormal flexion (decorticate) |
| | 1 none | 2 extension (decerebrate) |
| | | 1 none |

**GCS ≤8 = consider intubation** (loss of airway protective reflexes). Decorticate (flexion) implies a lesion above the red nucleus; decerebrate (extension) implies a lower brainstem lesion and is the worse sign.

**Brainstem reflexes to test in coma** (each tests a specific level): pupillary light (midbrain), corneal (pons — V afferent, VII efferent), oculocephalic/"doll's eyes" and oculovestibular caloric (pons/midbrain, MLF), gag/cough (medulla), respiratory pattern.

**Pupils in coma:**

| Pupils | Localisation/cause |
|---|---|
| Pinpoint, reactive (needs magnifier) | **Pontine** haemorrhage; **opioids** |
| Midposition, fixed | Midbrain lesion |
| Unilateral fixed dilated | **Uncal herniation / CN III compression** |
| Bilateral fixed dilated | Severe hypoxic-ischaemic injury, atropine, deep barbiturate, brain death |
| Normal and reactive with reduced consciousness | Favours **metabolic/toxic** cause |

> **Hook:** *Metabolic coma spares the pupils; structural coma does not.*

#### 3.5 Neuroimaging — choosing the right test

| Situation | First test | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Suspected acute stroke | **Non-contrast CT head immediately** | Excludes haemorrhage before thrombolysis; early ischaemic signs (hyperdense MCA, loss of grey–white differentiation, insular ribbon) |
| Stroke being considered for thrombectomy | **CT angiography** ± CT perfusion | Detects large-vessel occlusion; perfusion selects late-window patients |
| Thunderclap headache | Non-contrast CT (sensitivity >98% if within 6 h) then **LP at ≥12 h** if CT negative | Xanthochromia |
| Head injury with NICE criteria | CT head within 1 h (or 8 h for delayed criteria) | Surgical haematoma |
| Suspected MS, cord lesion, posterior fossa, epilepsy workup, tumour | **MRI** | Far better soft-tissue and posterior fossa resolution |
| Suspected cord compression / cauda equina | **Urgent MRI whole spine** | CT misses cord and disc detail |
| Suspected venous sinus thrombosis | **CT venogram or MR venogram** | Plain CT often normal; "empty delta sign" only sometimes |
| Suspected carotid dissection | CT/MR angiography with fat-saturated sequences | Mural haematoma, "string sign" |

**MRI sequences worth naming:** **DWI/ADC** (restricted diffusion = acute infarct within minutes; also abscess, and cortical ribboning in CJD), **FLAIR** (periventricular demyelination, "Dawson's fingers"; DWI–FLAIR mismatch dates a stroke as <4.5 h ⚑ and is used to select wake-up strokes for thrombolysis), **T2\*/SWI** (microbleeds, haemosiderin, cavernoma), **T1 with gadolinium** (BBB breakdown — active plaque, tumour, abscess, meningeal enhancement).

**Contrast patterns:** ring enhancement — think **abscess (thin, smooth, restricts diffusion centrally), glioblastoma (thick, irregular), metastasis (multiple, grey–white junction), toxoplasmosis, tuberculoma, demyelination (incomplete/open ring)**.

#### 3.6 Lumbar puncture and CSF

**Contraindications / when to CT first:** focal neurological deficit, GCS <10 or falling, new seizure, papilloedema, immunocompromise, age >60 — and always if signs of raised ICP. Also avoid in coagulopathy, platelets <50, local sepsis. Remember: **suspected bacterial meningitis — give antibiotics first, image second, LP third if delayed.**

| Parameter | Normal | Bacterial | Viral | TB / fungal | SAH | MS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Appearance | Clear | Turbid | Clear | Fibrin web | Xanthochromic | Clear |
| Opening pressure | 10–20 cmH₂O | ↑ | Normal/↑ | ↑↑ | ↑ | Normal |
| White cells | <5/mm³ | **>1000, neutrophils** | 10–1000, **lymphocytes** | 100–500, lymphocytes | RBC ± | Normal or mild lymphocytosis |
| Protein | 0.15–0.45 g/L | **>1 g/L** | <1 g/L | **>1–5 g/L (very high)** | ↑ | Normal or mildly ↑ |
| Glucose (CSF:serum) | >0.6 (≥⅔) | **<0.4 (very low)** | Normal | **Low** | Normal | Normal |
| Extra | — | Gram stain, PCR | Viral PCR (HSV, enterovirus) | AFB, TB PCR, CrAg, India ink | **Xanthochromia at ≥12 h**, RBC does not fall between bottles | **Unmatched oligoclonal bands** |

**Albuminocytological dissociation** — high protein with normal cell count — is the classic Guillain–Barré finding, though it may be normal in the first week (present in ~50% at 1 week, ~75% by 3 weeks), so a normal early CSF does not exclude GBS.

**Traumatic tap vs SAH:** in a traumatic tap the red cell count **falls markedly** from bottle 1 to bottle 4 and there is no xanthochromia; in SAH the count stays constant and xanthochromia (from bilirubin, needing ~12 hours to form) is present on spectrophotometry.

#### 3.7 Neurophysiology

| Test | What it shows | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **EEG** | Cortical electrical activity | Classifying epilepsy (not diagnosing "is this a seizure?" — a normal interictal EEG never excludes epilepsy), non-convulsive status, encephalopathy (triphasic waves), **HSV encephalitis (temporal periodic discharges)**, **CJD (periodic sharp wave complexes)**, 3 Hz spike-and-wave in absence epilepsy |
| **Nerve conduction studies** | Velocity and amplitude | Demyelinating (↓velocity, conduction block, prolonged F-waves) vs axonal (↓amplitude) neuropathy; entrapments |
| **Repetitive nerve stimulation** | NMJ safety factor | **Decrement** in myasthenia, **increment** in LEMS |
| **EMG** | Muscle electrical activity | Denervation (fibrillations, positive sharp waves, large polyphasic units) vs myopathy (small short polyphasic units); myotonic discharges |
| **Evoked potentials** | Conduction in a sensory pathway | Visual evoked potentials show **delayed P100** after optic neuritis — evidence of a previous subclinical lesion |

#### 3.8 Blood tests, antibodies and specialist investigations

**Baseline in most presentations:** FBC, U&E, LFT, glucose/HbA1c, calcium, CRP/ESR, TFT, B12 and folate, lipids. Add on pattern:

| Test | When |
|---|---|
| **CK** | Proximal weakness — very high in inflammatory myopathy/rhabdomyolysis |
| ESR (urgent) | ?Giant cell arteritis — but do not delay steroids for the result |
| **AChR antibody**, then **MuSK** | Myasthenia gravis (AChR positive in ~85% generalised, ~50% ocular) |
| **Anti-ganglioside** (GQ1b) | Miller Fisher variant of GBS |
| **Aquaporin-4 (AQP4-IgG)**, **MOG-IgG** | NMOSD and MOGAD — must be checked before calling it MS ⚑ |
| **Oligoclonal bands (CSF, unmatched in serum)** | MS |
| **Anti-NMDAR, LGI1, CASPR2, Hu, Yo, Ri, Ma2** | Autoimmune/paraneoplastic encephalitis |
| Copper, caeruloplasmin, 24 h urinary copper, slit lamp | **Wilson's disease** in any young person with movement disorder or unexplained tremor plus liver disease |
| Genetic testing | Huntington's (CAG repeat), CMT, SCA, Friedreich's, mitochondrial |
| HIV, syphilis serology | Almost any unexplained neurology |
| Paraprotein/immunofixation | Neuropathy of unclear cause |

#### 3.9 Red flags and the never-miss list

**Headache red flags (SNOOP4):** **S**ystemic features/secondary risk (fever, cancer, immunosuppression, pregnancy), **N**eurological signs, **O**nset sudden (thunderclap), **O**lder (new headache >50 — think GCA), **P**apilloedema, **P**ositional/**P**ostural, **P**recipitated by Valsalva, **P**rogressive/pattern change.

**Back pain red flags:** bilateral sciatica, saddle anaesthesia, bladder or bowel dysfunction, reduced anal tone, progressive neurological deficit, thoracic pain, age <20 or >55, cancer history, fever, night pain, unexplained weight loss, steroid use, IV drug use.

**The never-miss diagnoses, and the test that catches each:**

| Diagnosis | Immediate action |
|---|---|
| Bacterial meningitis | Blood cultures then **immediate ceftriaxone** ± amoxicillin; dexamethasone; LP when safe |
| Subarachnoid haemorrhage | Non-contrast CT; LP ≥12 h if negative |
| Acute ischaemic stroke | Immediate CT and reperfusion assessment — time is brain |
| Cord compression / cauda equina | Emergency MRI whole spine; dexamethasone if malignant |
| Giant cell arteritis | Immediate high-dose steroid, then temporal artery ultrasound/biopsy |
| Raised ICP with herniation | Head up 30°, osmotherapy, neurosurgical referral |
| Status epilepticus | Benzodiazepine now; treat the cause |
| Guillain–Barré / myasthenic crisis | **Serial FVC** — not oximetry |
| Wernicke's encephalopathy | IV thiamine **before** glucose |
| Herpes simplex encephalitis | Empirical IV aciclovir on suspicion |
| Temporal arteritis of the eye / acute angle closure | Same-day ophthalmology |
| Carotid/vertebral dissection | Angiography; antithrombotic |

#### Rapid-Recall Discriminators — Section 3

| Clue / question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Deficit maximal at onset | Vascular event |
| Symptoms spreading over 20–30 minutes with visual zig-zags | Migraine aura |
| Symptoms spreading over seconds, stereotyped, positive phenomena | Seizure |
| Fatigable diplopia and ptosis worse in the evening | Myasthenia gravis |
| Sensory level on the trunk | Spinal cord lesion |
| Cortical sensory loss / neglect / aphasia | Cortical lesion |
| Dense hemiparesis with completely normal higher function | Internal capsule lacune |
| Pinpoint pupils in coma | Pontine haemorrhage or opioids |
| Unilateral fixed dilated pupil in coma | Uncal herniation |
| Reactive pupils with deep coma | Metabolic/toxic cause |
| GCS threshold for airway protection | ≤8 |
| Which is worse — decorticate or decerebrate? | Decerebrate (extension) — lower brainstem |
| CSF: >1000 neutrophils, protein >1, glucose <0.4 ratio | Bacterial meningitis |
| CSF: lymphocytes, very high protein, low glucose | TB (or fungal) meningitis |
| CSF: high protein, normal cell count | Albuminocytological dissociation — GBS |
| CSF: unmatched oligoclonal bands | Multiple sclerosis |
| Red cells constant across bottles + xanthochromia | Subarachnoid haemorrhage |
| Red cells falling across bottles, no xanthochromia | Traumatic tap |
| Minimum interval from headache onset to LP for xanthochromia | 12 hours |
| Delayed P100 on visual evoked potentials | Previous optic neuritis |
| Periodic lateralised discharges over the temporal lobe on EEG | HSV encephalitis |
| Periodic sharp wave complexes + cortical ribboning on DWI | Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease |
| 3 Hz generalised spike-and-wave | Childhood absence epilepsy |
| MRI sequence positive within minutes of infarction | DWI (with ADC correlate) |
| DWI positive but FLAIR negative | Stroke likely <4.5 h old ⚑ |
| Monitoring parameter in GBS/myasthenic crisis | Forced vital capacity |
