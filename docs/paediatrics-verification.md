# Paediatrics notes — key-fact verification, 15 Aug 2026

The 32,000-word Paediatrics master was re-checked by **eight independent adversarial
reviewers**, one per block, each instructed to find errors rather than confirm the
work, and to verify every number against a current Australian primary source rather
than from memory — RCH Melbourne CPGs, Queensland Clinical Guidelines / CHQ, ANZCOR,
ASCIA, the Australian Immunisation Handbook, RHDAustralia, KDIGO 2025, Safer Care
Victoria, AIFS and the state child-protection statutes.

**Result: no DANGEROUS findings survived verification.** The high-risk algorithm
content — neonatal resuscitation, paediatric ALS, DKA, status epilepticus,
anaphylaxis dosing, febrile neutropenia — was accurate throughout, including the
ANZCOR-vs-ERC adrenaline timing that this project has caught wrong before.
**58 corrections were applied**, almost all in the WRONG or MINOR band.

That is a different profile from O&G, where four errors were found and three were
dangerous. The difference is that Paediatrics was written against the RCH and
Queensland guidelines from the start rather than assembled from mixed sources.

---

## Superseded — these were teaching the wrong current answer

1. **CAH newborn screening.** The notes said CAH is *not* on the Australian newborn
   bloodspot panel and that male CAH is therefore detected clinically in salt-wasting
   crisis. **21-hydroxylase CAH is now a target condition** on the national panel.
   That was a whole exam answer pointing the wrong way.
2. **Orchidopexy timing.** Given as 12–18 months and flagged as "earlier than older
   texts" — the current Australian recommendation is **6–12 months**, so the flag was
   pointing backwards at the older figure.
3. **Nephrotic hypoalbuminaemia.** <25 g/L is the old ISKDC number; **KDIGO 2025 uses
   <30 g/L**, and KDIGO 2025 is already cited as a source in the same section.
4. **Maternal HIV as an absolute contraindication to breastfeeding.** ASHM no longer
   treats it that way — formula remains the recommendation, but breastfeeding is a
   viable choice under shared decision-making.
5. **AIHW substantiated-abuse ranking.** Neglect was named the commonest category;
   **emotional abuse is (~55%)**, with neglect second (~21%).

## Dangerous-adjacent — would change a management answer

6. **Pre/post-ductal saturation gradient.** Stated as **>10%**; the CCHD screening
   threshold is **>3%**. A 5–9% gradient is a classic coarctation presentation and
   the note's rule missed it.
7. **Dexamethasone in meningitis.** Two separate sections implied that giving it
   after antibiotics have started loses the benefit / that the window is 4 hours.
   RCH allows it **up to 12 hours** after the first dose. As written it would have a
   candidate withhold an indicated drug at 6–8 hours.
8. **TMP–SMX in pertussis.** Age floor given as "not <1 month"; it is **>6 weeks**.
   That gap is the sulfonamide/kernicterus boundary.
9. **Ipratropium in acute asthma.** "3 doses only, then stop" — three doses is the
   **first-hour burst**; both CHQ and the Australian Asthma Handbook continue it
   4–6 hourly for up to 24 hours if needed.
10. **Orbital cellulitis.** Keyed ceftriaxone + flucloxacillin. The Australian
    regimen is cefotaxime or ceftriaxone, **with vancomycin only if MRSA is
    suspected** — the note added a drug that isn't used and omitted the actual
    decision point.
11. **Mandatory reporting.** "Australian doctors are mandatory reporters in every
    jurisdiction" is true of the profession but not of every category of harm — **WA
    mandates sexual abuse only**; Vic, ACT and Qld mandate physical and sexual. Only
    NSW, NT, SA and Tas cover all four. Also corrected: **midwives are not a listed
    mandated category in Queensland**.
12. **NT consent age.** The notes gave the NT a statutory age of 16. **Only SA and
    NSW have statute on minors' consent**; the NT runs on common-law *Gillick*.

## Internal contradictions — one of the two versions was wrong

13. **Complex febrile convulsion.** The prose said "recurs within the same illness"
    (correct); the table said "recurs within 24 h" (wrong). A second seizure on day 3
    of the same illness is complex.
14. **AVPU.** Prose said below "V"; the critical-signs table said ≤"V", which would
    class a child responding to voice as critical.
15. **Epididymo-orchitis age.** The age row said post-pubertal while the management
    row told you to image the urinary tract in a *prepubertal* boy. It is bimodal.
16. **Faltering growth.** Two rows of the same table each claimed to be the majority
    cause, and the categories overlapped.
17. **Neonatal gentamicin.** §13.1 asserted the mg/kg dose doesn't fall in neonates
    and pointed at gentamicin as the example; §13.6 gives 5 mg/kg neonatally against
    7.5 mg/kg at 1 month–10 years.
18. **Walking red flag** appeared at both 18 months and 2 years in the same table.

## The false discriminator

19. **Bronchiolitis oxygen threshold.** The notes taught "Australian target 90% vs
    NICE/UK 92% — a clean discriminator", bolded, flagged, and duplicated into the
    Rapid-Recall table. **NICE also uses 90%** for infants over 6 weeks; 92% applies
    only under 6 weeks or with underlying disease. There is no Australia/UK split.
    This is the most instructive finding of the pass — a wrong fact presented as a
    high-yield exam discriminator propagates into every question drawn from it.

## Developmental milestones — a systematic shift

The Section 1 red-flag table was misaligned against the CHQ Red Flags guide it cites:
sitting unsupported was placed at 9 months (WHO median **5.9 months**; 9 months is
essentially the 99th centile, which is why *not* sitting then is a flag), "no clear
words" was pulled forward from 2 years to 18 months, daytime continence was flagged
at 3 years against CHQ's 5, and several 18-month and 5-year items sat one row early.
All realigned to CHQ and the RACGP Red Book Appendix 3A.

## Smaller corrections applied

Delivery-room temperature ≥26 °C for the very preterm (not 23–25); cord clamping
≥30 s below 34 weeks; ASCIA autoinjector bands (the 7.5 kg threshold was inverted —
150 µg is standard for 7.5–20 kg, and devices are *not* usually recommended below
7.5 kg) and the 4–6 y / 7–10 y age bands; RANZCO ROP guideline is 2021 not 2025;
paracetamol suspension labelled 5–12 y not "6+"; amoxicillin max 500 mg in AOM and
cefalexin max 750 mg in cystitis (the 1 g caps were imported from other indications);
Kawasaki aspirin 5 mg/kg as RCH actually states it, and the AHA infant echo rule
(≤6 months, ≥7 days); West syndrome 3–7 months with "can occur at any age"; DMD
1 in 5,000; vWD types 2N and 3 are recessive; ITP ~80% resolved at **12** months not
6; RCH specifies **amikacin** in febrile neutropenia; the SA two-doctor rule (the
written requirement attaches to the second practitioner only); the Jack test is
great-toe dorsiflexion, not tiptoeing; nebulised budesonide is second-line, not
obsolete; deep nasal suction is *not* recommended in bronchiolitis; pertussis
exclusion is the earliest of three criteria, not two.

## Gaps filled

- **Age-specific Hb lower limits and the ferritin <20 µg/L cut-off** — §8.1 defined
  no anaemia threshold at all.
- **Tumour lysis syndrome** — entirely absent from §8.5 despite ALL and mediastinal
  T-cell disease being covered. Added with the biochemistry, rasburicase/G6PD rule,
  and the "don't give calcium for asymptomatic hypocalcaemia" trap.
- **Haemophilia A = VIII, B = IX** — the pairing appeared three times without ever
  stating which factor went with which letter.
- **Cefazolin** as RCH's current empirical choice in osteomyelitis, alongside the
  Therapeutic Guidelines flucloxacillin.

## Standing rule, restated

Every system built through the pipeline gets an independent adversarial key check
before it counts. The failure mode is not an obviously wrong number — it is a
confident, well-formed, plausibly-sourced fact, and the bronchiolitis "discriminator"
above is exactly what that looks like.
