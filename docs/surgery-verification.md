# Surgery — build and verification, 16 Aug 2026

5 of every 100 real exam questions, and previously no system at all — the surgical
content was scattered inside Gastroenterology, so it was neither findable nor
rankable on the dashboard.

**8 sections, 39,450 words.** Built through the v2 pipeline, one agent per section,
each opening with a short-version summary and closing with a Rapid-Recall
Discriminators table, `⚑` on anything changed from older texts or where Australian
practice diverges. Medical detail on pancreatitis, biliary disease, hepatology,
peptic ulcer, GI bleeding and IBD stays in Gastroenterology and is cross-referenced
rather than duplicated.

Then **five independent adversarial reviewers**, instructed to find errors rather
than confirm the work. **41 corrections applied.**

---

## The dangerous ones

1. **Sepsis antibiotic timing.** The notes tied the 60-minute rule to *shock*, and
   allowed up to 3 hours where shock was absent. The ACSQHC Sepsis Clinical Care
   Standard ties 60 minutes to **recognition of infection-related organ dysfunction —
   i.e. sepsis, shock or not**. The 3-hour limit applies only where there is genuine
   diagnostic uncertainty about whether infection is present. As written, a patient
   with recognised sepsis and a normal blood pressure could have waited three hours.

2. **"Incarcerated hernia — a warning, not an emergency."** Only *chronic, painless*
   irreducibility is non-urgent. An **acutely** irreducible hernia cannot be
   distinguished from strangulation clinically. The section's own worked example
   (tender irreducible groin lump → theatre) contradicted this line two pages later.

3. **Warfarin reversal in traumatic intracranial haemorrhage.** Given as vitamin K
   plus Prothrombinex "with FFP only if PCC unavailable". The Australasian consensus
   for critical-organ bleeding is vitamin K1 5–10 mg IV **plus** Prothrombinex-VF
   50 IU/kg **plus** FFP 150–300 mL — all three together, because **Prothrombinex-VF
   is a 3-factor PCC lacking adequate factor VII**. The notes also wrote
   "prothrombinex/4-factor PCC" as if they were the same product.

## Wrong in a way that changes the answer

4. **Cefazolin 30 mg/kg (max 2 g)** was given as the adult surgical prophylaxis dose.
   That is the **paediatric** formula; the adult dose is a flat **2 g**. Applied to an
   adult it under-doses anyone under about 67 kg.

5. **Hernia mesh prophylaxis.** Section 4 asserted that elective open mesh repair in a
   low-risk patient needs no prophylaxis, and I had propagated that into Section 1 as
   a flagged exam pearl while resolving a cross-section contradiction. Therapeutic
   Guidelines lists **hernia repair with or without prosthetic material as YES**,
   cefazolin 2 g. Both sections corrected — a reminder that harmonising two sections
   makes them consistent, not correct.

6. **Posterior wall of the inguinal canal** described as "weakest laterally-to-medially
   where direct hernias emerge" — which is not a statement of anything, and reads as
   "direct hernias emerge laterally", the classic wrong answer. It is bare transversalis
   fascia **medially and inferiorly**, in Hesselbach's triangle.

7. **Strangulated hernia operative sequence** had the ring released *before* the
   contents were controlled. Reversed: grasp and hold the strangulated bowel first,
   then divide the constriction — otherwise necrotic bowel reduces into the abdomen
   unseen, the same failure mode as reduction en masse.

8. **AAA surveillance intervals** were every band one step too tight and labelled
   "Australian practice", with the discrepancy against SVS presented as a legitimate
   guideline conflict — so the error was self-justifying. ANZSVS publishes no
   surveillance schedule; RACGP recommends **ESVS**. Rebuilt as a three-column
   comparison. The knock-on: the worked exam answer for an incidental 4.2 cm AAA said
   6-monthly ultrasound, and contradicted the note's own SVS row. It is **annual**.

9. **Asymptomatic carotid stenosis.** The notes allowed intervention "in selected
   higher-risk patients at centres with complication rates <2–3%". The Australian
   Stroke Foundation guideline says plainly that endarterectomy or stenting **should
   not be performed** for asymptomatic stenosis. The symptomatic window was also given
   as 6 months (the NASCET/ESVS trial window) where the Australian guideline says
   **<3 months**.

10. **Compression and ABI.** The "≥0.8 full, 0.5–0.8 reduced, <0.5 contraindicated"
    ladder is a UK convention presented as Wounds Australia. Wounds Australia
    authorises compression **above 0.8** and refers below it; international guidelines
    put the hard contraindication anywhere from <0.5 to <0.8.

11. **INR ≤1.4, not <1.2**, for neuraxial blockade. No Australian or ASRA source
    supports 1.2, and using it would cancel safe lists.

12. **Paediatric fasting.** "Formula 4 h" was stated as a blanket paediatric rule. Under
    ANZCA PG07 the 4-hour rule applies to **infants under 12 months**; over 12 months
    formula counts as a **solid at 6 hours**.

13. **Breast imaging age threshold.** The notes gave 35, then hedged with "some units
    use 40, adding mammography only if the ultrasound is indeterminate". Cancer
    Australia has no such band: under 35 ultrasound first, **35 and over mammography
    *and* ultrasound**. Hedge deleted.

14. **Laparoscopic lavage in Hinchey III** described as "no longer recommended".
    WSES says not recommended *as routine practice*, with an individualised role in
    selected cases — a stronger claim than any guideline makes.

## Smaller corrections

Corona mortis crosses the superior pubic ramus behind Cooper's ligament, not the
lacunar ligament; needle decompression is the **5th ICS** (ATLS 10th ed — I had
harmonised it to "4th or 5th" earlier in the build, which the reviewer corrected
back); Canadian CT Head Rule uses **≥3 feet / 5 stairs**, not >1 m (that is NICE);
CRASH-3 **enrolled** GCS ≤12 or CT-positive TBI, with GCS 9–15 being the benefit
subgroup, not the entry criterion; Hartmann's chloride is **111**; metronidazole
prophylaxis goes in within **120 min**, not 60; prophylaxis may continue post-op in
defined settings but **never beyond 24 h**; VRIII triggers are >12 rapidly rising,
>12 for an hour despite correction, or >15 (the ">10 twice" rule is
cardiothoracic/ICU-specific); mastitis is **5 days only if rapid resolution, otherwise
10**, and a woman with 5 days of untreated symptoms has already failed the 48-hour
window — image now; "not lactating, >50" is a weak discriminator for inflammatory
breast cancer, which has a *younger* median age than other breast cancers and occurs
in pregnancy; two in three Australians develop skin cancer by **70**, not 75; the
BCC/SCC 4/6/10 mm margin tiers are NCCN, where Cancer Council Australia gives 2–3 mm
(3–4 mm if aggressive subtype) and a 2–10 mm range for SCC; ultrasound is done in
**every** patient with a thyroid nodule whatever the TSH.

## Gap filled

**Abdominal compartment syndrome** was absent from both the acute abdomen and
abdominal wall sections despite damage control and massive resuscitation being
covered. Added with the examinable numbers: intra-abdominal hypertension **IAP
≥12 mmHg**, ACS **>20 mmHg with new organ dysfunction**.

## What survived

The arithmetic — which was the highest-risk content and the reason a reviewer was
told to re-do every step — is **clean**. Both worked burn calculations were
recomputed independently and every step was correct, including the deduction for
elapsed time and the anchoring of the clock to the time of injury rather than
presentation. The ANZBA **3 mL/kg/%TBSA** figure was confirmed as current Australian
practice against NSW ACI and VicBurns, as were the urine output targets, the Rule of
Nines arithmetic, and the ANZBA referral criteria item for item.

Also verified clean: haemorrhagic shock classes (nothing shifted between columns),
the full GCS, NEXUS and the Canadian C-Spine Rule stated separately without
conflation, the cord syndromes, the embolic-vs-thrombotic limb ischaemia table
(correctly oriented, not inverted), thyroid cancer spread patterns (papillary
lymphatic, follicular haematogenous — the classic swap, not made here), the MEN
syndromes, the Fifth International Workshop parathyroidectomy criteria verbatim,
melanoma margins by Breslow, the Alvarado score, the WSES mesenteric ischaemia
proportions (the *current* ones, not the superseded split), the Australian
1-6-11-year post-resection colonoscopy schedule, the NBCSP request-only 45–49 rule,
the eviQ Lynch and FAP schedules, the deep ring versus mid-inguinal point trap, and
the DOAC interruption table including the worked apixaban-before-hemicolectomy answer.

## Not verified — treat as unchecked

Therapeutic Guidelines is paywalled and blocks automated fetching. The intra-abdominal
sepsis regimen (amoxicillin + gentamicin + metronidazole) could not be confirmed
